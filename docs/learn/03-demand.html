Demand: Who Buys, Why, and for How Long

需求：誰買、為什麼買、還能買多久

What You'll Learn這一課學什麼
  • Break a market's demand engine into four drivers — penetration, replacement cycles, price, and new use cases — and name which one is doing the work in a given story.拆解一個市場的需求引擎，分辨滲透率、換機週期、單價、新用途這四個驅動力，並指出眼前的成長故事到底靠哪一個撐起來。
  • Compute a market size two ways — top-down and bottom-up — and decide what to do when the two numbers disagree by more than 20%.用自上而下與自下而上兩條路徑各算一次市場規模，並判斷當兩個數字差超過兩成時該怎麼處理。
  • Calculate a 5-year demand multiple and classify where an industry sits using a rough penetration-ceiling rule of thumb.算出五年需求倍數，並用滲透率天花板的粗略門檻，判斷一個產業還剩多少跑道。
  • Tell structural, cyclical, and one-off demand apart, and trace any demand line back to who signs the check and what funds it — the two things that decide how long it can last.分辨結構性、週期性、一次性三種需求來源，並把任何一條需求線追回到「誰簽支票、那筆錢從哪來」——這兩件事決定了它還能撐多久。
  • Explain, with an example, why strong demand growth does not by itself mean a stock is worth buying.舉例說明，為什麼需求成長強勁本身，並不代表一檔相關股票就值得買。

The Same Market, Two Numbers, One Missing Explanation同一個市場，兩個數字，差在一個沒人講的環節

In 2024, several research desks tried to size the same market: how much money would be spent, five years out, on the specialized chips that power large-scale artificial-intelligence computing. The estimates were not close. Some put the number at roughly three times today's spending; others, using a narrower definition of what counts, landed closer to today's level with modest growth on top. Every analyst involved was working in good faith, reading the same quarterly earnings calls, the same capital-spending announcements from the handful of companies that actually buy this hardware. Nobody was lying. The gap came from somewhere else entirely.

2024年前後，好幾家研究機構試著估算同一個市場：五年後，全球在支撐大型人工智慧運算的專用晶片上，一年會花掉多少錢。算出來的數字完全對不上。有些估計未來規模大約是現在的三倍；另一些用比較窄的定義去算「這個市場」到底包含什麼，算出來的數字只比現在略高一點。參與估算的分析師都不是在瞎掰，讀的是同一批公司法說會、同一批資本支出公告——這幾家真正在買這類硬體的雲端巨頭，數字是公開的。沒有人在說謊。落差是從別的地方冒出來的。

The gap came from how each analyst built the number. A market size five years out is never a fact you look up — it is a chain of assumptions you build, link by link, starting from something you can actually observe today. Two people can start from the exact same observable fact and, three multiplications later, land a third apart, simply because they made different choices about what counts, what growth rate to assume, and which year to call "today." Learning to see that chain — and to catch the moment where two independent chains should agree but do not — is what this lesson is about.

落差其實是從「這個數字怎麼被算出來的」這個環節冒出來的。五年後的市場規模，從來不是一個查得到的事實，而是一條從今天觀察得到的東西出發、一環扣一環算出來的假設鏈。兩個人可以從完全相同的、今天就觀察得到的事實出發，經過三次乘法之後，答案卻能差到三分之一，原因只是他們在「這個範圍算不算進去」「用哪個成長率」「哪一年算是『現在』」這幾個選擇上做了不同決定。學會看清這條鏈——尤其是抓出「兩條獨立的鏈本來應該對得上、卻對不上」的那個時刻——就是這一課要教的事。

The Demand Engine: Who Buys, and Why需求引擎：誰在買，為什麼買

Revenue for almost any product can be broken into a small number of moving parts: how many buyers exist, how often each one buys, and how much each purchase costs. Growth in the top line comes from exactly four places, and telling them apart is the single most useful habit in this lesson.

幾乎任何一項產品的營收，都能拆成幾個會動的零件：有多少買家、每個買家多久買一次、每次買多少錢。營收成長只會從四個地方冒出來，分辨清楚是這一課最有用的一個習慣。

1. Penetration — new buyers who never bought this before. The penetration rateThe share of a potential customer base that currently owns or uses the product — in plain terms, how much of the available room has already been filled. is the share of a potential customer base that already owns the product. When penetration is low, growth comes almost entirely from first-time buyers, and it tends to be the fastest, most explosive kind of growth an industry ever sees — because the pool of "people who don't have one yet" is still large.

一、滲透率——從來沒買過的新買家。滲透率潛在客群裡已經擁有或使用這項產品的比例，白話說就是這個空間已經被填了多少。指的是潛在客群裡已經擁有這項產品的比例。滲透率低的時候，成長幾乎全部來自第一次購買的新買家，這種成長通常是一個產業一生中最快、最爆發性的階段——因為「還沒有的人」這個池子還很大。

2. Replacement — existing owners buying again. Once penetration is high, growth shifts to how often existing owners trade in the old unit for a new one — a replacement cycleThe average time between when an existing owner buys and when they buy the next one — shorter cycles mean more frequent repeat revenue from the same customer base.. This is a completely different growth engine: it depends on wear, obsolescence, or a compelling enough upgrade, not on finding anyone new.

二、換機——既有客戶重複購買。滲透率一旦拉高，成長的重心就會轉移到既有用戶多常把舊的換成新的——也就是換機週期既有用戶這次購買到下次購買之間的平均間隔，週期愈短，代表同一批客群能貢獻愈頻繁的重複營收。。這是完全不同的一台成長引擎：它靠的是產品耗損、過時，或是升級誘因夠強，而不是找到新的人。

3. Price — the same unit sold for more (or less). Revenue can grow purely because the average selling price rises, with unit volume flat or even falling. This can be genuine — a real upgrade in specifications, a shift to premium tiers — or it can be nothing more than a price hike dressed up as growth.

三、單價——同一件東西賣得更貴（或更便宜）。營收也可能純粹因為平均售價上升而成長，出貨量持平甚至下滑。這可能是真實的：規格真的升級了、客群往高階產品移動；也可能只是一次漲價，被包裝成成長的樣子。

4. New use case — a reason to buy that did not exist before. Occasionally an entirely new application expands the pool of potential buyers itself — a product originally bought for one purpose finds a second purpose, resetting how large the addressable market can even be.

四、新用途——一個原本不存在的購買理由。有時候一個全新的應用場景，會直接擴大潛在買家的池子本身——一項原本因為某個用途而被買的產品，找到了第二個用途，重新設定了整個潛在市場能有多大。

The false signal to watch for here is easy to state and easy to miss: revenue growth driven mostly by price, with volume barely moving, gets read as demand strength. What makes this worth checking rather than assuming is that the answer often comes out the opposite way from the story everyone tells. Micron — one of the three companies that make most of the world's DRAM, the memory chip inside nearly every computer, phone, and server — reported fiscal 2017 revenue up about 64%, to roughly $20.3B. That year is remembered as a memory "super cycle," and super cycles are usually described as pure price events. Micron's own 10-K splits the number for you: DRAM net sales rose about 80% that year, made up of average selling price per gigabit up about 19% and gigabits sold up about 52%. Multiply the two halves — 1.19 × 1.52 ≈ 1.81 — and the 80% comes back out. Volume, not price, did most of the work in the year everyone remembers as a price story.

這裡要留意的假訊號說出來很簡單，實務上卻很容易漏看：營收成長主要靠漲價、出貨量幾乎沒動，卻被讀成需求強勁。真正值得動手查、而不是憑印象假設的理由是：答案常常跟大家講的故事相反。美光（Micron）是全球三家主要DRAM廠之一（DRAM就是幾乎每一台電腦、手機、伺服器裡都有的記憶體晶片）。它2017財年營收年增大約64%，來到約203億美元。那一年被記成記憶體的「超級循環」，而超級循環通常被描述成純粹的漲價事件。但美光自己的10-K年報就把這個數字拆好了：那一年DRAM營收成長大約80%，其中每GB平均售價漲了大約19%，出貨位元數則增加了大約52%。把兩半乘起來——1.19 × 1.52 ≈ 1.81——正好把80%還原回來。在這個大家都記成漲價的年份，真正出力最多的其實是出貨量，不是價格。

That is why the habit matters more than any single case: the split is disclosed, so you never have to guess. To find it, look for volume and price reported separately — many hardware companies break out units shipped, bits shipped, or average selling price in the annual report's management discussion or in quarterly earnings materials. Where a company does not, market-research firms publish industry-wide price indices, and trade associations in hardware-heavy industries (chips, cars, appliances) publish unit shipment data, both independent of any single company's own reporting. The red flag is revenue growth running at more than roughly twice the disclosed volume growth for two or more quarters in a row.

所以重點不是這個案例本身，而是這個習慣：拆解的數字是公開揭露的，你根本不需要用猜的。找法是看公司有沒有把「量」跟「價」分開報——很多硬體公司會在年報的管理階層討論、或每季法說材料裡，單獨列出出貨台數、出貨位元數或平均售價。公司沒報的話，市場研究機構會公布整個產業的價格指數，晶片、汽車、家電這類硬體產業的公會也會公布整體出貨量，兩者都獨立於任何單一公司的自報數字之外。警訊是：營收成長率連續兩季以上，超過公司自己揭露的出貨量成長率大約兩倍。

Takeaway一句話收斂

Before trusting a growth number, ask which of the four engines is actually running — new buyers, repeat buyers, price, or a new use case each imply a very different story about how long the growth can last.在相信一個成長數字之前，先問是哪一台引擎在運轉。新買家、回頭買家、漲價、新用途——這四台引擎對「這個成長能撐多久」給的答案，完全不一樣。

Where to Find This in Public Filings公開資料在哪裡看：實際動手的步驟
  1. 1
    Open the company's 10-K, Item 7 (Management's Discussion & Analysis). Search the text for "units," "shipments," or "subscribers." Hardware and device companies frequently disclose unit volume in this section or a supplementary table even when the press release only leads with a dollar figure — that gap between the two is exactly the volume-versus-price split this lesson keeps coming back to.打開公司10-K年報的第7項（管理階層討論與分析，MD&A），搜尋「units」「shipments」或「subscribers」等字樣。硬體與裝置公司經常會在這一節或附表裡揭露出貨量，即使新聞稿標題只放了一個金額數字——兩者之間的落差，正是這一課一再回頭處理的「量」與「價」之分。
  2. 2
    Pull up the earnings call transcript — free on the company's own investor-relations site, or via free transcript archives most large brokerages and financial media sites maintain — and search for "average selling price," "ASP," or "pricing." When price rather than volume is doing the work in a quarter, management almost always says so explicitly in response to an analyst question, because analysts ask this question specifically.找出當季法說會逐字稿——公司自己的投資人關係網站上通常有免費全文，多數大型券商與財經媒體網站也維護免費的逐字稿檔案庫——搜尋「average selling price」「ASP」或「pricing」等字樣。當一季的成長主要是漲價、不是出貨量在撐，管理層幾乎都會在回答分析師提問時明講，因為分析師本來就會特別問這個問題。
  3. 3
    For customer concentration: 10-K Item 1 (Business) or the notes to the financial statements disclose any single customer above roughly 10% of revenue — a threshold companies are required to flag. Then check that buyer's own separate 10-K (if it is a public company) to see whether it records the purchase as capital expenditure or operating expense — that tells you which budget it is competing against, and how easily it can be cut.要查客戶集中度：10-K第1項（業務說明）或財務報表附註，會揭露是否有單一客戶佔營收超過大約一成——這是公司被要求主動揭露的門檻。接著去查那個買家自己的10-K（如果它也是上市公司），看它把這筆採購記成資本支出還是營運費用——這會告訴你這筆錢在跟哪一類預算搶額度，以及它有多容易被砍掉。
  4. 4
    For channel inventory and cyclicality: search a hardware company's MD&A for "days sales outstanding," "channel inventory," or "weeks of supply." For chip-specific coverage, industry associations such as the Semiconductor Industry Association publish monthly, industry-wide billings and inventory data free of charge — independent of any single company's own reporting, which is exactly the kind of cross-check this lesson asks for.要查通路庫存與週期性：在硬體公司的MD&A裡搜尋「days sales outstanding」「channel inventory」或「weeks of supply」。針對晶片產業，半導體產業協會（SIA）之類的公會會免費公布每月、涵蓋整個產業的出貨與庫存數據——不受任何單一公司自身財報的影響，這正是這一課要求的那種交叉查證。
  5. 5
    Normal range and red flag: revenue growth running at more than roughly twice the growth rate of the company's own disclosed unit or shipment growth, for two or more consecutive quarters, is a specific, checkable signal that price — or channel stockpiling — is doing most of the work. That is the moment to go read the next earnings call transcript specifically for management's own explanation, rather than accepting the headline revenue number at face value.正常範圍與警訊：如果營收成長率連續兩季以上，超過公司自己揭露的出貨量成長率大約兩倍，這是一個具體、可查核的訊號，代表漲價——或通路囤貨——正在做大部分的工作。這時候該做的，是去讀下一次法說會逐字稿，專門找管理層自己怎麼解釋，而不是照單全收頭條營收數字。
Hands-On: How Fast Is "Fast"?動手做：多快算「快」？

NVIDIA's own 10-K filings report Data Center segment revenue of about $15,005M in fiscal 2023, about $47,525M in fiscal 2024, and about $115,186M in fiscal 2025. Compute the year-over-year growth rate for each of the two most recent years.

NVIDIA自己的10-K申報，資料中心部門營收在2023財年大約是150.05億美元，2024財年大約475.25億美元，2025財年大約1,151.86億美元。算出最近這兩年，各自的年增率。

  1. FY2024 growth = (47,525 − 15,005) ÷ 15,005 ≈ %2024財年成長率 ＝ (47,525 − 15,005) ÷ 15,005 ≈ %
  2. FY2025 growth = (115,186 − 47,525) ÷ 47,525 ≈ %2025財年成長率 ＝ (115,186 − 47,525) ÷ 47,525 ≈ %

Two consecutive years above 140% growth is rare enough that the three-bucket framework from earlier in this lesson is the right next move, not celebration. The growth is not primarily cyclical restocking (there is no prior inventory drawdown this reverses) and it is not obviously one-off (there is no expiring subsidy or mandate behind it) — the closest fit is structural demand tied to a genuine platform shift (training and running AI models on accelerated computing instead of general-purpose servers), cross-checked in this lesson's own worked example against roughly $228B of calendar-2024 capital spending from just four buyers. But structural does not mean permanent at this rate: this lesson's own rule about penetration ceilings applies here too — growth this fast eventually meets either a capacity constraint or a deceleration once the earliest, easiest-to-convert buyers are largely converted. The number to watch going forward is not whether Data Center revenue keeps growing, but whether the growth rate itself compresses toward the underlying capex growth rate of the four buyers named above.

連續兩年成長率都超過140%，罕見到值得先套用這一課前面教的三桶分類架構，而不是急著慶祝。這波成長不太像典型的週期性補庫存（沒有前一段庫存去化在反轉），也不明顯是一次性（背後沒有正在到期的補貼或強制法規）。比較接近的是結構性需求，且跟一次真正的平台轉換綁在一起——訓練與執行AI模型改用加速運算、而不是泛用型伺服器。這一課自己的示範例子裡，也拿大約2,280億美元、來自僅僅四家買家的2024日曆年資本支出去交叉核對過。但「結構性」不代表能一直維持這個速度：這一課自己講的滲透率天花板規則，在這裡一樣適用——成長快到這個程度，遲早會撞上產能限制，或是隨著最早、最容易轉換的買家大多轉換完畢而開始放緩。接下來真正該盯的數字，不是資料中心營收還會不會繼續成長，而是這個成長率本身，會不會開始往上面那四家買家自己的資本支出成長率收斂。

Who Writes the Check, and What Funds It誰簽支票，那筆錢從哪裡來

Naming the growth engine tells you what is moving. The next two questions decide how much of that movement you should believe: who actually pays, and where the money they pay with comes from. These sound like details. They are usually the difference between a demand line that survives a bad year and one that vanishes in a single quarter.

說出成長引擎是哪一台，只告訴你什麼東西在動。接下來的兩個問題，才決定這個動作有多少值得相信：真正付錢的是誰，以及那筆錢從哪裡來。這兩件事聽起來像細節，但它們往往就是「一條需求線能撐過一個壞年頭」與「一條需求線在一季之內消失」的差別。

Start with the fact that the buyer and the user are frequently not the same person. A hospital device is chosen by a physician, paid for by an administrator out of a capital budget, and reimbursed — or not — by an insurer who never touches it. Enterprise software is used by employees and paid for by a technology budget owner who is judged on cost. When the payer and the user differ, demand is governed by the payer's constraint, not the user's enthusiasm: a product users love can stall for years because the person with the checkbook is under a spending freeze, and a mediocre product can sell steadily because it sits inside a budget line that renews automatically.

先從一個事實開始：買的人跟用的人，常常不是同一個人。一台醫療設備是醫師挑的，由行政主管從資本預算裡付錢，最後由一個從來不會碰到這台機器的保險支付方決定給不給付。企業軟體是員工在用，付錢的卻是一個被用成本來考核的技術預算負責人。當付錢的人跟使用的人不是同一個人，需求就由付錢那一方的限制決定，而不是使用者的熱情：一個使用者很愛的產品，可能因為握有支票簿的人正在凍結支出而卡住好幾年；一個平庸的產品，也可能因為它掛在一條會自動續約的預算科目底下而穩定地賣。

Then ask what funds the payment, because funding sources have very different half-lives. Ranked from most durable to least: money out of the buyer's own operating cash flow (a supermarket restocking shelves — the purchase repeats because the buyer's own customers keep coming); money out of a discretionary capital budget (a data-center operator ordering servers — real money, but a plan that a board can revise at one meeting); money out of someone else's balance sheet (a start-up buying equipment with funds it raised, or a customer buying on credit the seller itself extended); and money out of a policy pool (a subsidy or mandate with a written end date). The same $1B of revenue means something different depending on which of those four it came from, and a great many demand forecasts never ask.

接著問：付這筆錢的資金從哪裡來，因為不同的資金來源，半衰期差很多。從最耐久到最脆弱大致排下來：來自買方自己的營運現金流（超市補貨上架——這筆採購會一直重複，因為買方自己的客人一直上門）；來自可自由裁量的資本預算（資料中心業者訂伺服器——是真金白銀，但這個計畫董事會開一次會就能改）；來自別人的資產負債表（新創公司拿募來的錢買設備，或客戶用賣方自己提供的信用賒帳買）；以及來自政策資金池（有明確落日條款的補貼或強制規定）。同樣是10億美元營收，來自這四種來源的哪一種，意義完全不同，而非常多的需求預測從來沒問過這個問題。

The clean warning case is vendor financing, and the telecom equipment bust of 2000–2001 is the version worth memorizing. Through the late 1990s, equipment makers including Lucent and Nortel booked enormous revenue growth selling network gear to new carriers — and helped those carriers pay for it by lending them the money, or guaranteeing the debt they borrowed elsewhere. On the income statement it read as demand. Mechanically it was the seller funding its own order book. When capital markets closed to those carriers in 2000–2001, the orders did not slow gradually the way genuine end demand does; they stopped, and the loans went bad at the same time, so the same event hit revenue and the balance sheet together. Lucent's own annual report shows the size of it: total revenue of about $28.9B in fiscal 2000 fell to about $21.3B in fiscal 2001 and about $12.3B in fiscal 2002 — less than half in two years, visible directly on the income statement with no adjustment needed. The profit line moved further than the revenue line, which is the part worth noticing: gross margin fell from about $11.7B in fiscal 2000 to about $1.6B in fiscal 2002, because the fixed cost of factories and engineers stayed while the revenue funding them left. Both Lucent and Nortel lost roughly 90%-plus of their peak share price over the following two years. This is a teaching case, not a comment on any company today.

最乾淨的警示案例是「賣方融資」，而2000到2001年的電信設備崩盤，是最值得記住的那個版本。1990年代後期，包括朗訊（Lucent）與北電（Nortel）在內的設備商，靠著把網路設備賣給新成立的電信業者，做出驚人的營收成長——同時也借錢給這些業者、或替它們在外面借的債務作保，來幫它們付得出這筆錢。在損益表上，這讀起來就是需求。但機制上，這是賣方在替自己的訂單簿出錢。當資本市場在2000到2001年對這些電信業者關上大門，訂單並沒有像真實終端需求那樣慢慢降溫，而是直接停掉，同時放出去的貸款也一起變成呆帳——同一個事件把營收與資產負債表一起打穿。朗訊自己的年報就把規模寫得很清楚：總營收從2000財年大約289億美元，掉到2001財年大約213億美元，再掉到2002財年大約123億美元——兩年不到剩下一半，而且直接寫在損益表上，不需要任何調整就看得到。更值得注意的是，利潤掉得比營收更兇：毛利從2000財年大約117億美元，掉到2002財年大約16億美元，因為工廠與工程師這些固定成本還在，養活它們的營收卻走了。接下來兩年，朗訊與北電的股價都從高點跌掉大約九成以上。這是教學案例，不代表對現在任何一家公司的看法。

The counter-example matters just as much, because the obvious lesson — "concentrated demand is bad, fragmented demand is safe" — is wrong as stated. A single payer that is well capitalized and buying something non-discretionary can be far steadier than millions of individual buyers who each defer a purchase in a recession: a national health system reimbursing a treatment patients cannot go without will keep paying through a downturn that guts discretionary consumer spending. The real question is not how many buyers there are. It is how discretionary the purchase is for whoever writes the check, and how securely that person's own funding holds up. Customer concentrationThe share of a company's revenue coming from its largest few customers — high concentration means demand can change through a handful of decisions rather than a broad shift in end usage. raises the speed at which demand can change, not automatically the probability that it will.

反例同樣重要，因為那個看起來很順的教訓——「需求集中很危險、需求分散很安全」——照字面講是錯的。一個資本雄厚、而且買的東西是非買不可的單一支付方，可以比幾百萬個在景氣差時各自把採購往後延的散戶買家穩定得多：一套替病人給付「不能不用」療法的國家醫療體系，會在一場砍光可選消費支出的衰退中繼續付錢。真正的問題不是買家有幾個，而是這筆採購對簽支票的那個人來說有多可有可無，以及那個人自己的資金有多穩。客戶集中度一家公司營收裡來自最大幾家客戶的比例——集中度高，代表需求可以靠少數幾個決定就改變，而不需要終端使用出現全面性的轉變。提高的是需求可以改變的「速度」，不是自動提高它「會改變」的機率。

How to see this from public filings: annual reports disclose when a single customer accounts for more than roughly a tenth of revenue, and most companies break revenue down by end market or geography somewhere in the same document. Read the customer's side too — if the buyers are listed companies, whether they book the purchase as capital expenditure or as an operating cost tells you which budget it competes in, and their own cash flow statements tell you whether they are paying out of earnings or out of raised money. The false signal to watch for is a revenue line growing fast into a customer base that is itself loss-making and recently financed: that is not end demand yet, it is someone else's cash being converted into your revenue, and it lasts exactly as long as their access to funding does.

要從公開申報資料看出這一點：年報會揭露是否有單一客戶佔營收超過大約一成，多數公司也會在同一份文件裡把營收按終端市場或地區拆開。也要讀客戶那一邊——如果買家是上市公司，它們把這筆採購記成資本支出還是營運費用，會告訴你這筆錢在跟哪一類預算搶額度；它們自己的現金流量表則會告訴你，它們是拿盈餘在付，還是拿募來的錢在付。要留意的假訊號，是一條快速成長的營收線，客戶群本身卻是虧錢、而且剛募到一大筆資金的公司：那還不叫終端需求，那是別人的現金正在被轉換成你的營收，而它能持續多久，完全等於那些人拿得到資金的時間長度。

Takeaway一句話收斂

Demand funded by the buyer's own recurring cash flow can be projected. Demand funded by a capital budget, by raised capital, or by a subsidy has an expiry date somewhere — find it before you extrapolate.靠買方自己的循環性現金流付錢的需求，可以往前推估。靠資本預算、靠募來的資金、靠補貼付錢的需求，某個地方一定有到期日——在你把它往未來外推之前，先把那個日期找出來。

Sizing the Market Two Ways拆解市場規模：兩條路都要走

The number everyone reaches for first is TAMTotal Addressable Market — the total revenue a product or service category could theoretically capture if it fully served every potential buyer. (Total Addressable Market): the total revenue this category could capture if it fully served every potential buyer. There are two structurally different ways to build that number, and a serious analysis does both, on purpose, because they fail in opposite ways.

大家第一個會抓的數字是TAMTotal Addressable Market，潛在市場總規模——一項產品或服務類別，如果服務到每一個潛在買家，理論上能拿到的總營收。（潛在市場總規模）：這個類別如果服務到每一個潛在買家，理論上能拿到的總營收。要算出這個數字，有兩條結構上完全不同的路，一份認真的分析會刻意兩條都走，因為它們會用不同的方式失準。

Top-downStarting from a large, macro-level number and multiplying it down by a series of share assumptions until you reach the specific market you care about. starts big and multiplies down. Take a large, observable macro number — total spending by the handful of companies that dominate a category — and multiply it by a chain of share assumptions until it narrows down to the specific slice you care about. This method is fast and works even for markets too new to have reliable bottom-up data, but every multiplication is a place where an optimistic assumption can quietly compound.

自上而下從一個較大、總體層級、可觀察到的數字出發，用一連串佔比假設一路乘下去，最後收斂到你真正關心的那個具體市場。：從大數字往下乘。找一個較大、觀察得到的總體數字——例如主導這個類別的少數幾家公司合計的支出——再用一連串佔比假設一路乘下去，收斂到你真正關心的那個具體區塊。這個方法算得快，即使市場太新、沒有可靠的由下而上數據也能用，但每一次乘法，都是一個樂觀假設可以悄悄複利放大的地方。

Bottom-upAdding up known, specific data points from the companies actually inside the value chain — the sum of what real buyers already say they will spend, or real sellers already say they will earn. starts small and adds up. Instead of starting from a macro number, add together known, specific data points from the companies actually inside the value chain — consensus revenue estimates for every named supplier in the category, or disclosed spending plans from the actual buyers. For a year far enough out that analyst estimates no longer reach, the equivalent inputs are each supplier's disclosed capacity plans and stated long-range targets. This method is grounded in real, reported figures, but it systematically undercounts anything not yet public: private companies, emerging entrants, and categories too new for analysts to have built a model for yet.

自下而上把價值鏈裡實際存在的公司，已知、具體的數據一筆筆加總起來——加總真實買家已經說要花的錢，或真實賣家已經說要賺的營收。：從小數字往上加。不從總體數字出發，而是把價值鏈裡真實存在的公司，已知、具體的數據一筆筆加總——把這個類別裡每一家有名有姓的供應商的營收共識加起來，或是把實際買家已經揭露的支出計畫加起來。如果要算的那一年遠到分析師的估計還構不著，對應的輸入就換成各家供應商公開的產能計畫與自己講出來的長期目標。這個方法立足於真實、已申報的數字，但會系統性地漏掉任何還沒公開的部分：未上市公司、新進入者，以及新到分析師都還沒建立模型去追蹤的類別。

The rule that follows from this is a hard one, not a soft suggestion: when the two paths land more than roughly 20% apart, that gap itself must be explained before either number can be trusted. A gap that large is usually one of three things — double counting (the top-down number accidentally includes spending the bottom-up number also counts separately, under a different label), a scope mismatch (the two paths are quietly measuring different things — one includes surrounding hardware and services, the other counts only a narrower core product), or a genuine omission (the bottom-up list is missing a category of supplier that is not yet covered by public analyst estimates). Silently picking whichever number is more convenient, without stating which of the three explains the gap, is the single most common way a market-size number ends up misleading the reader who trusts it.

由此而來的規則是硬性的，不是可有可無的建議：當兩條路徑算出來的數字相差超過大約兩成，這個落差本身就必須先被解釋清楚，兩個數字才有資格被相信。落差這麼大，通常是三件事之一。第一，重複計算：自上而下的數字，不小心把自下而上已經用不同名目算過一次的支出又算了一次。第二，範圍不一致：兩條路徑其實在量不同的東西——一邊把周邊硬體與服務也算進去，另一邊只算最核心的產品本身。第三，真的有遺漏：自下而上的清單漏掉了某一類供應商，因為分析師的估計還沒覆蓋到那裡。什麼原因都不說明，直接偷偷挑一個比較方便的數字用，是市場規模數字最後誤導讀者的最常見方式。

A real case shows both paths at once. In NVIDIA's quarterly results release of May 2023, its founder and chief executive described "a trillion dollars of installed global data center infrastructure" that would shift from general-purpose to accelerated computing — a top-down anchor for the size of the base that could shift toward accelerated computing, not a bottom-up forecast of NVIDIA's own revenue. Cross-checking that anchor from the bottom up means adding what the buyers themselves disclosed spending: in calendar 2024, Alphabet reported capital expenditures of about $52.5B, Amazon about $83.0B, and Meta about $37.3B, and Microsoft's quarterly disclosures imply a comparable figure of roughly $55B for the same year (a sum of quarterly numbers, not a single reported annual figure, and one that likely understates total spend because it excludes some equipment obtained through finance leases) — together, close to $228B from just four buyers in a single year, against a roughly $1T installed base. That comparison does two things: it makes the trillion-dollar top-down number checkable against real, reported spending instead of a marketing anchor, and it shows NVIDIA's own Data Center segment revenue accelerating from about $15.0B in fiscal 2023 to about $47.5B in fiscal 2024 to about $115.2B in fiscal 2025 — growth this fast is unusual enough that the discipline from this lesson (checking it against the underlying spend of a handful of concentrated buyers, not just trusting the growth rate) matters more here, not less.

有一個真實案例同時示範了這兩條路。在NVIDIA 2023年5月的季度財報新聞稿裡，創辦人兼執行長說全球有「1兆美元的現有資料中心基礎設施」，會從泛用型運算轉向加速運算——這是一個自上而下的錨點數字，用來描述有多大一塊基礎有可能轉向加速運算，不是NVIDIA自己營收的由下而上預測。要交叉核對這個錨點，可以由下而上加總買家自己揭露的支出。2024日曆年，Alphabet申報的資本支出大約525億美元，Amazon大約830億美元，Meta大約373億美元。微軟的季度揭露換算下來，同一年大約也有550億美元——這是把幾季數字加起來得出的估計，不是官方單一年度數字，而且可能還低估了實際支出，因為它排除了部分透過融資租賃取得的設備。光是這四家買家一年加起來，就接近2,280億美元，對照大約1兆美元的現有裝機基礎。這個對照做了兩件事：讓這個兆美元的自上而下數字，可以拿真實、已申報的支出去查核，而不是只當成一個行銷錨點；同時也顯示NVIDIA自己的資料中心部門營收，從2023財年大約150億美元，加速到2024財年大約475億美元，2025財年大約1,152億美元——成長快到這種程度，正是這一課教的紀律（拿它去對照少數幾家集中買家的真實支出，而不是照單全收成長率本身）派上用場的地方，而不是可以放鬆的地方。

Worked Example: Two Paths to the Same TAM逐步示範：兩條路算同一個 TAM
  1. 1
    Build the top-down chain — and state up front which year you are sizing. Here it is Year 5, not today. Suppose the handful of companies that dominate purchases of a specialized computing chip are, on their stated build-out plans, spending roughly $600B a year on capital equipment by Year 5 (a teaching figure, not anyone's actual forecast), that about 35% of that budget goes toward this specific category of infrastructure, and that — because a dollar of chip spend pulls in roughly another third of a dollar of surrounding networking and packaging hardware counted under the same category — an attach multiplier of about 1.33x applies on top.先搭出自上而下這條鏈——而且開頭就要講清楚你算的是哪一年。這裡算的是第五年，不是今年。假設主導這種專用運算晶片採購的少數幾家公司，依照它們公開的擴建計畫推算，到第五年時合計一年的資本支出大約是6,000億美元（這是教學用的假設數字，不是任何人真正的預測），其中大約35%投入這一類特定的基礎設施；而因為每一塊錢的晶片支出，會拉動大約再三分之一塊錢、算進同一個類別裡的周邊網路與封裝硬體支出，所以還要再乘上大約1.33倍的附加倍數。
  2. 2
    Multiply it out: $600B × 35% × 1.33 ≈ $280B. Call this the top-down Year-5 base case.把三個數字乘起來：600 × 35% × 1.33 ≈ 280（十億美元）。把這個數字當成自上而下算出的第五年基準情境。
  3. 3
    Now build the bottom-up path independently, for the same Year 5: list every named supplier that actually sells into this category, and add up what each one's own disclosed capacity plans and long-range revenue targets imply it could ship that year. Suppose that sum comes to roughly $210B. Note the bias baked into this path before you compare anything: a bottom-up list can only contain companies that already exist and already publish plans.接著獨立地搭出自下而上這條路，算同一個第五年：把真正在賣這一類產品的每一家有名有姓的供應商都列出來，加總它們各自公開的產能計畫與長期營收目標所隱含的那一年出貨金額。假設加總起來大約是210（十億美元）。在拿兩個數字比較之前，先記住這條路天生的偏誤：自下而上的清單，只裝得下今天已經存在、而且已經公布計畫的公司。
  4. 4
    Compute the gap: |280 − 210| ÷ 280 ≈ 25%. That clears the roughly-20% threshold — this gap cannot be waved away.算出落差：|280 − 210| ÷ 280 ≈ 25%。這已經超過大約兩成的門檻——這個落差不能隨便帶過。
  5. 5
    Diagnose the gap rather than average it away. Here the likely explanation is scope: the top-down chain's "attach multiplier" pulls in facility power, cooling, and networking spend that the bottom-up supplier list mostly does not capture, since those dollars flow to different companies analysts have not yet mapped into this category. That is a scope mismatch, not double counting and not a guaranteed omission.診斷落差，而不是把兩個數字平均了事。這裡比較可能的解釋是範圍不一致：自上而下那條鏈裡的「附加倍數」，把機房電力、冷卻、網路這些支出也算了進去，而自下而上的供應商清單大多沒有涵蓋到這些——因為這些錢流向的是分析師還沒被歸進這個類別的其他公司。這是範圍不一致，不是重複計算，也不代表一定有遺漏。
  6. 6
    Keep both numbers, with the explanation attached, instead of forcing a single false-precision figure. Treat $210–280B as the honest range for the Year-5 estimate, and note that the bottom-up figure lands close to a 75%-realization bear case of the top-down number ($280B × 0.75 = $210B) — when two independently-built paths converge on a plausible downside like that, it is a real reason for more confidence, not a coincidence to ignore.把兩個數字連同解釋一起保留下來，而不是硬湊出一個假裝很精確的單一數字。把210到280（十億美元）當成第五年估計值的誠實區間，並且留意到自下而上算出的數字，剛好落在自上而下「實現率75%」的悲觀情境上（280 × 0.75 ＝ 210）——當兩條獨立算出來的路徑，這樣收斂到一個說得通的下檔數字，這是可以增加信心的真實理由，不是可以忽略的巧合。
  7. 7
    Finally, compute the 5-year demand multiple against this year's estimated TAM of roughly $140B: $280B ÷ $140B = 2.0x. Then immediately convert it back into an annual path, because a multiple hides how ordinary or how heroic it is: doubling over five years is about 15% compound growth a year (1.15 to the fifth power is roughly 2.0). Stated that way, the forecast becomes checkable — if the market grows 4% next year, the chain is already off its path, and you will know that within four quarters instead of in five years.最後，用今年估計的TAM（大約140十億美元）當分母，算出五年需求倍數：280 ÷ 140 = 2.0x。算完立刻把它換算回每年的成長路徑，因為一個倍數會把「這件事到底算平凡還是算英勇」藏起來：五年翻一倍，等於每年大約複合成長15%（1.15的五次方大約是2.0）。這樣講出來，這個預測就變得可以查核了——如果明年市場只成長4%，這條鏈就已經偏離軌道，而你會在四季之內知道，不必等五年。
  8. 8
    One condition would make this whole reconciliation unreliable, and it is worth naming before trusting step 6's convergence: if the 1.33x attach multiplier from step 1 is not a stable structural ratio but is itself inflated by the same shortage-driven pricing this lesson warns about elsewhere — say, a temporary component shortage pushing up the price of the networking and packaging hardware counted in that multiplier — then both the top-down and bottom-up numbers could be quietly capturing the same distorted price spike from two different angles. The "convergence" that looked reassuring in step 6 would then just be two paths agreeing on the same inflated starting point, not two independent confirmations. The check is to ask, separately, whether the attach multiplier itself has moved much over the last few years — a multiplier that has been stable for a decade is far more trustworthy than one that recently jumped.有一個條件會讓這整套對帳失去可信度，值得在相信第6步「兩者收斂」之前先點名：如果第1步用的1.33倍附加倍數，並不是一個穩定的結構性比率，而是本身也被這一課別處提醒過的那種短缺式漲價撐高的——比如某種零組件短缺，把算進這個倍數裡的網路與封裝硬體價格暫時推高——那麼自上而下與自下而上這兩個數字，很可能只是從兩個不同角度、悄悄捕捉到同一波失真的價格暴衝。第6步看起來讓人安心的「收斂」，其實只是兩條路徑一起認同了同一個被墊高的起點，而不是兩個獨立的確認。查證的方法是另外去問：這個附加倍數本身，過去幾年有沒有大幅變動過——一個十年來都很穩定的倍數，遠比一個最近才突然跳升的倍數值得信任。

Two habits are doing the real work in that example, and both are cheap. The first is writing the target year on the number, every time — a great many market-size arguments fall apart the moment someone asks whether the big figure is this year's or Year 5's, because the person quoting it has quietly used it as both. The second is converting the multiple into an annual rate, which turns an unfalsifiable story into a claim that next year's data can contradict.

上面這個例子真正在起作用的是兩個習慣，而且兩個都很便宜。第一個是每次都把「這是哪一年的數字」寫在數字旁邊——很多市場規模的論述，只要有人問一句「這個大數字是今年還是第五年的」就會當場垮掉，因為引用的人早就把它同時當成兩者在用。第二個是把倍數換算成年化成長率，這會把一個無法被推翻的故事，變成一個明年的資料就能反駁的主張。

Three Kinds of Demand, Three Levels of Trust三種需求，三種可信度

Not all demand growth deserves the same amount of trust, because it does not all come from the same place. Sorting a growth number into one of three buckets — before doing anything else with it — changes how far into the future you are allowed to project it.

不是所有的需求成長都值得同等程度的信任，因為它們根本不是從同一個地方冒出來的。在對一個成長數字做任何事之前，先把它歸進三個桶子的其中一個——這會決定你能把它往未來投射多遠。

Structural demand comes from penetration climbing a genuine S-curve, or from an aging population, or from a technology becoming permanently cheaper than the alternative it replaces. It is the most predictable kind, because it follows a slow-moving, well-documented logic — but "predictable" does not mean "fast": structural trends often unfold over a decade or more, and mistaking their pace for a cyclical sprint is its own error.

結構性需求來自滲透率沿著一條真正的S曲線往上爬，或是來自人口老化，或是來自一項技術永久性地比它取代的舊技術更便宜。這是最可預測的一種需求，因為它遵循一套變化緩慢、有據可查的邏輯——但「可預測」不等於「快」：結構性趨勢常常要花上十年以上才走完一個階段，把它的節奏誤認成週期性的短跑，是另一種常見的錯誤。

Cyclical demandDemand that swells because buyers are restocking depleted inventory or replacing worn-out equipment on a roughly regular schedule — it eventually reverses because it borrows from future purchases. comes from a restocking cycle — buyers rebuilding inventory that ran low, or a wave of equipment hitting the end of its useful life at roughly the same time. It is timeable in a rough sense (restocking waves tend to follow a prior period of shortage; equipment replacement waves follow a prior installation boom by roughly the equipment's typical lifespan) but treacherous in magnitude, because the same mechanism that inflates the upswing also inflates the downswing once buyers realize they over-ordered.

週期性需求因為買家在補庫存、或設備大約在同一時間到達汰換年限而膨脹的需求——最終會反轉，因為它其實是向未來的購買提前借的。來自補庫存週期——買家把降到偏低的庫存補回來，或是一批設備大約在同一時間到達使用年限盡頭。這種需求在粗略意義上是可以抓時間點的（補庫存潮通常跟在前一段短缺之後；設備汰換潮通常跟在前一波安裝高峰之後、間隔大約等於設備的常見使用年限），但幅度很難拿捏，因為讓上升段膨脹的同一個機制，一旦買家發現自己下單下太多，也會讓下降段同樣膨脹。

Micron's own numbers make the up-and-down mechanism concrete, and they are the mirror image of the split shown earlier in this lesson. Fiscal 2018 (ended August 2018) closed as Micron's then-record year, with revenue of about $30.4B. One year later, fiscal 2019 revenue fell to about $23.4B — a drop of roughly 23%. Micron's own filing names the cause: the decline was primarily pricing, with DRAM sales down about 28% on average selling prices down roughly 30%. So the up-leg was mostly volume and the down-leg was mostly price, in the same company two years apart. Nothing about global demand for computers, phones, or servers fell 23% in a single year; that swing is the cyclical mechanism itself — price moving through a concentrated industry whose new capacity arrives in large, slow lumps and then all at once.

美光自己的數字，把這種一漲一跌的機制講得很具體，而且正好是這一課前面那組拆解的鏡像。2018財年（2018年8月結束）是美光當時的歷史新高，全年營收大約304億美元。才過一年，2019財年營收就跌到大約234億美元，跌幅大約23%。美光在年報裡自己點名了原因：這次下跌主要是價格造成的——DRAM營收下滑大約28%，其中平均售價跌了大約三成。所以同一家公司，相隔兩年，上升段主要靠量，下降段主要靠價。全球對電腦、手機、伺服器的需求，並沒有在一年內少掉23%；這個擺盪本身就是週期機制：價格在一個高度集中的產業裡上下甩動，而這種產業的新產能總是又慢又大塊地一次到位。

One-off demand comes from a subsidy, a mandate, a disaster, or some other event with a known or knowable expiration. It is the least predictable of the three in one specific sense — not that the event itself is unpredictable, but that almost nobody accurately predicts what happens to demand the day after the event ends. The safest default assumption is that one-off demand reverts once its trigger disappears, unless there is separate, specific evidence that it converted buyers into structural ones along the way.

一次性需求來自補貼、法規強制規定、天災，或其他有明確或可推算到期日的事件。這是三者裡最難預測的一種，難就難在一個特定的地方——不是事件本身難以預測，而是幾乎沒有人能準確預測「事件結束後那一天」需求會發生什麼事。最安全的預設假設，是一次性需求會在觸發它的事件消失後打回原形，除非有另外、具體的證據顯示它在過程中真的把買家轉化成了結構性買家。

The false signal here is a familiar one: a company reporting order growth that suddenly jumps from single digits to 30%+ can look, on the surface, like a structural inflection. The way to check is to look one layer past the order number, at how much inventory sits in the channel — measured in weeks of supply — at the same time. If channel inventory weeks are also climbing sharply, buyers are very likely stockpiling ahead of an expected shortage or a price increase, not consuming more in any real sense, and that kind of order surge tends to mean-revert within a year or two rather than compound.

這裡的假訊號是一個熟悉的模式：一家公司公布訂單成長忽然從個位數跳到30%以上，表面上看起來很像結構性轉折。查證的方法是往訂單數字後面再看一層，看同一時間通路庫存（用庫存周數衡量）是不是也在往上飆。如果通路庫存周數同步飆升，買家很可能是在預期短缺或漲價之前提前囤貨，而不是真的消耗更多——這種訂單暴增，通常會在一兩年內均值回歸，而不是持續複利下去。

There is a mechanical reason cyclical demand looks so much more violent the further upstream you sit, and it is worth understanding rather than memorizing. Most companies do not sell to the end user; they sell to someone who sells to someone who does. Their demand is derived demand, and every layer in between holds inventory. When end consumption dips 5%, the retailer, seeing slightly slower sell-through, also decides to carry two fewer weeks of stock; the distributor does the same; the component maker at the far end sees orders drop not 5% but something closer to 30%, because it absorbs the real decline and every layer's inventory correction at the same moment. This amplification — orders swinging far harder than the consumption underneath them — is the bullwhip effectThe tendency of order volatility to amplify at each step upstream in a supply chain, because every layer adjusts its own inventory in the same direction at the same time., and it runs in both directions.

週期性需求愈往上游看愈劇烈，背後有一個機械性的原因，值得理解而不是背下來。多數公司並不直接賣給終端使用者，而是賣給「賣給終端使用者的那個人」。它們面對的是衍生需求，而中間的每一層都握有庫存。當終端消費下滑5%，零售商看到銷售略微變慢，順手把庫存水位少壓兩週；經銷商也做同樣的事；站在最上游的零組件廠看到的訂單下滑，就不是5%，而是接近30%，因為它在同一個時點吸收了真實的下滑，加上每一層的庫存修正。這種放大效應——訂單的擺盪遠比底下的實際消費劇烈——就是長鞭效應供應鏈上每往上游一層，訂單波動就被放大一次的現象，因為每一層都在同一時間往同一個方向調整自己的庫存。，而且它兩個方向都會發生。

The consequence for analysis is specific and useful: an upstream supplier's order growth does not measure end demand — it measures end demand plus the rate of change of everyone's inventory. So the discipline is to go and find the end-consumption number itself (retail sell-through, vehicle registrations, subscriber additions, whatever the physical unit of the end market happens to be) and treat the order book as a leveraged, delayed echo of it. Semiconductors ran the full loop in public between 2021 and 2023: shortage, then double-ordering by customers hedging against allocation, then record order books, then a correction in which orders fell far harder than consumption of the end devices ever did — because that correction was mostly inventory unwinding, not people abandoning their phones and cars.

這件事對分析的意義很具體也很有用：上游供應商的訂單成長，量到的不是終端需求，而是終端需求「加上」所有人庫存的變化率。所以紀律是自己去找終端消費本身的數字（零售端實際賣出量、汽車掛牌數、訂閱淨增，看那個終端市場的實體單位是什麼），再把訂單簿當成它一個加了槓桿、又有時間落後的回音。2021到2023年的半導體，把這整個迴圈公開跑了一遍：先是短缺，接著客戶為了搶配額而重複下單，訂單簿創新高，然後進入一輪修正——訂單下滑的幅度遠大於終端裝置的實際消費曾經下滑的幅度，因為那輪修正主要是庫存在退潮，不是人們不用手機、不開車了。

🚴 War Story戰爭故事

Peloton is the cleanest recent case of one-off demand mistaken for structural growth, and it involves a real capital decision, not just a stock chart. Total revenue jumped from about $1,825.9M in fiscal 2020 (its year ends in June) to about $4,021.8M in fiscal 2021 — growth of roughly 120% in a single year — while connected-fitness subscriptions went from about 1.09 million to about 2.33 million, as pandemic lockdowns closed gyms and pulled years of at-home fitness adoption into a few months. Reading that 120% as a new structural growth rate, Peloton paid about $412.0M on 1 April 2021 for Precor, a commercial fitness-equipment maker, to build U.S. manufacturing capacity; in the same annual report it also described plans to invest about $400M in a new U.S. factory of its own. Both commitments were sized against a demand level that had a nameable trigger and no evidence of permanence.

Peloton是最近最乾淨的一個案例，示範一次性需求被誤認成結構性成長，而且牽涉到一個真實的資本決策，不只是股價走勢圖。總營收從2020財年大約18.259億美元（它的會計年度在6月結束），跳升到2021財年大約40.218億美元，單一年度成長大約120%；同期間連網健身訂閱數從大約109萬戶增加到大約233萬戶。原因是疫情封城關掉了健身房，把原本要好幾年才會發生的居家健身普及，壓縮進幾個月內。把這120%讀成新的結構性成長率，Peloton在2021年4月1日付了大約4.12億美元買下商用健身器材廠Precor，用來建立美國本土製造產能；同一份年報裡，它還說要再投入大約4億美元蓋一座自己的美國工廠。這兩筆承諾的規模，都是照著一個「有明確觸發事件、又沒有任何證據顯示會持久」的需求水準抓出來的。

By fiscal 2022, revenue had fallen to about $3,582.1M, down roughly 11% from the prior year, as gyms reopened and the pull-forward reversed. The signal was visible before the reversal showed up in revenue: subscriber engagement metrics and average monthly workouts per subscriber — both disclosed quarterly, and both easy for any outside reader to track over several quarters — had already begun softening while headline revenue was still being reported as growing, because a subscriber who bought the bike during lockdown but works out less each month is a warning about the durability of the initial surge, not yet a warning that shows up in revenue itself. The market's own assessment moved faster and further than the revenue line, and you can read that from the filings rather than from a chart: the cover page of every 10-K states the market value of shares held by outside investors on a fixed date. Peloton's cover pages put that figure at about $39.7B on 31 December 2020 and about $10.7B on 31 December 2021 — roughly 73% lower, twelve months apart, while the revenue decline for the year in between was about 11%. The mechanism to take from this: a demand surge with a known, nameable trigger — here, mandatory gym closures — deserves the one-off classification from the moment the trigger is identified, regardless of how many consecutive quarters of strong growth follow, because the capital spent treating it as structural (a factory acquisition, in this case) cannot be un-spent once the trigger disappears. This example is used here purely to teach the mechanism, not as a view on any specific company today.

到了2022財年，營收跌到大約35.821億美元，比前一年下滑大約11%，因為健身房重新開放，先前被提前拉走的需求開始反轉。訊號其實在營收反轉之前就已經看得到：訂閱戶的使用度指標，以及平均每位訂閱戶每月的運動次數，這兩個數字都會按季揭露，任何外部讀者都能連續好幾季追蹤。在頭條營收數字都還在成長的時候，這兩個數字就已經開始走軟——因為一個在封城期間買了車、但每個月動得愈來愈少的訂閱戶，代表的是「這波暴增有多耐久」的警訊，還不是會馬上反映在營收上的警訊。市場對這家公司的評價，動得比營收數字更快也更多，而且這件事可以從財報本身讀出來，不必看股價圖：每一份10-K年報的封面，都會寫出某個固定日期時、由外部投資人持有的股票市值。Peloton的封面數字是：2020年12月31日大約397億美元，2021年12月31日大約107億美元——相隔十二個月少了大約七成三，而中間這一個會計年度的營收只下滑大約11%。從這個案例該學到的機制是：一個有明確、講得出名字的觸發事件的需求暴增——這裡是強制關閉的健身房——從觸發事件被指認出來的那一刻起，就該歸類成一次性，不管接下來連續幾季都還在強勁成長，因為當時拿去因應「這是結構性」判斷所花的資本（這裡是收購一座工廠），一旦觸發事件消失，是花出去就收不回來的。這個案例純粹是教學用途，不代表對現在任何一家特定公司的看法。

Think first: why can two analysts start from the same public numbers and land a market size a third apart?先想一下：為什麼兩個分析師用同一批公開數字，算出來的市場規模卻能差三分之一？

A market-size number is not a single fact, it is the product of a chain of choices: what counts as inside the category (a wide or narrow scope), what base year is being multiplied forward, and what growth rate is assumed for each link in the chain. None of those choices is stated on the label of the final number. Two analysts using identical raw data but different scope definitions, or different assumed realization rates, will multiply their way to different answers without either one making an arithmetic mistake — which is exactly why the number needs a visible derivation chain, not just a headline figure.

一個市場規模數字不是單一事實，而是一連串選擇乘出來的結果：什麼算進這個類別裡（範圍抓寬還是抓窄）、用哪一年當基期往前乘、每一環假設用哪個成長率。這些選擇都不會寫在最終數字的標籤上。兩個分析師就算用完全一樣的原始資料，只要範圍定義不同、或假設的實現率不同，一路乘下去就會得到不同答案，而且兩邊都沒有算錯任何一步——這正是為什麼這個數字需要附上看得見的推導鏈，而不是只丟一個頭條數字。

Seeing a headline like "this market will triple in five years" and immediately searching for a stock to buy is a shortcut worth resisting. Ask first whether the number comes from a single top-down chain with no bottom-up cross-check — an unusually large multiple with no independent second path behind it is often a sell-side marketing figure more than a research conclusion.看到「這個市場五年內會成長三倍」這種標題就急著找相關股票，是一個值得抵抗的捷徑。先問這個數字是不是只有一條自上而下的算式、沒有任何自下而上的交叉核對——一個異常大的倍數，如果背後找不到一條獨立走出來的第二條路，通常比較像是行銷數字，而不是研究結論。

The S-Curve and the Penetration CeilingS 曲線與滲透率天花板

Adoption of almost anything new follows a shape, not a straight line — a S-curveThe typical shape of adoption over time: slow at first (early adopters only), steep in the middle (the mass market adopts quickly), then flattening again as the pool of remaining non-buyers shrinks.. Adoption starts slow, because only the most eager early buyers show up first. It then steepens sharply as the mass market catches on and buying becomes normal, ordinary behavior. Finally it flattens again — not because the product stopped being good, but because the pool of people who still do not have one keeps shrinking, and each additional buyer is harder to find than the last.

幾乎任何新東西的普及過程，走的都是一個形狀，不是一條直線——這就是S 曲線普及率隨時間變化的典型形狀：一開始很慢（只有最早期的嘗鮮者），中段陡峭（大眾市場快速跟上），最後又趨平（因為還沒買的人愈來愈少）。。一開始成長很慢，因為只有最積極的早期買家會先出現。接著會急遽變陡，因為大眾市場跟上了，購買變成一件普通、日常的事。最後又會趨平——不是因為產品變差了，而是因為還沒有這項產品的人愈來愈少，每多找一個新買家都比上一個更難。

A rough, useful rule of thumb for reading where an industry sits on that curve: look at projected penetration five years out. Below roughly 35%, treat the runway as long and still mostly ahead of you. Between roughly 35% and 70%, the fastest part of the curve is likely behind you, and growth is shifting from new buyers toward replacement and price — watch closely for deceleration. Above roughly 70%, assume the curve has mostly flattened unless there is concrete evidence of a second curve starting — a new use case, a price drop that unlocks an entirely different customer segment, or a geography that has barely started while the reference market is already saturated.

一個粗略但好用的判斷法則，可以用來看一個產業目前在這條曲線的哪個位置：看五年後預估的滲透率。大約35%以下，可以把跑道看成還很長，大部分成長還在前面。大約35%到70%之間，曲線最陡的那段很可能已經走過了，成長的重心正從新買家轉向換機與單價——這時候要密切留意成長是不是在放緩。大約70%以上，除非有具體證據顯示第二條曲線正在啟動——新用途、降價解鎖了完全不同的客群、或是某個地區才剛起步而參考市場早已飽和——否則應該假設這條曲線大致已經走平。

The clean positive case is a market still in its steep middle section, where a rising penetration rate is genuinely pulling in buyers who never owned the product before, and unit shipments confirm it (shipments and penetration both rising together, not diverging). The false ceiling — the counter-example worth remembering — is a market that looks saturated by the headline penetration number but is quietly building a second curve: a price collapse in a key component can suddenly make a product affordable to an entirely new income segment that the original S-curve never counted, effectively resetting the denominator. Reading "penetration over 70%" as an automatic sell signal, without checking for that possibility, is how a genuine second-wave opportunity gets missed.

乾淨的正面案例，是一個還處在陡峭中段的市場，滲透率上升確實在拉進從沒買過這項產品的新買家，而且出貨量同步印證了這件事（出貨量與滲透率一起往上走，而不是彼此背離）。值得記住的反例，是一個看起來已經飽和的市場，其實正在悄悄醞釀第二條曲線：某個關鍵零組件的價格大幅下跌，可能突然讓一整個原本被排除在最初S曲線之外的收入級距客群買得起這項產品，等於重新設定了分母。把「滲透率超過70%」直接當成賣出訊號、卻沒有查證這種可能性，正是真正的第二波機會被錯過的原因。

Apple is the named case with numbers on both sides of that idea. iPhone unit sales peaked in fiscal 2015 at about 231 million and have never returned to that level; iPhone net sales were about $155.0B in fiscal 2015 and about $201.2B in fiscal 2024, roughly 1.3x in nine years — the flat top of a penetration curve, exactly as this section predicts. Underneath it a second curve was already running: Services net sales grew from about $24.3B in fiscal 2016 to about $96.2B in fiscal 2024, close to 4x, sold to the installed base that the first curve had built. State the mechanism precisely, because the general form is what you want to carry away: when a hardware S-curve flattens, the installed base is not zero — it is the largest it has ever been, and it becomes the denominator of a new penetration question, namely how many of those owners can be sold something recurring. Anyone who wrote the company off when unit growth stopped was answering the previous question well and the current one not at all.

蘋果是這件事的具名案例，而且兩邊都有數字。iPhone出貨量在2015財年見頂，大約2.31億支，之後再也沒回到那個水準；iPhone銷售額2015財年大約1,550億美元，2024財年大約2,012億美元，九年只有大約1.3倍——這就是滲透曲線走平之後的頂部，跟這一節講的一樣。但在它底下，第二條曲線早就在跑：服務業務的銷售額從2016財年大約243億美元，成長到2024財年大約962億美元，接近四倍，而客戶就是第一條曲線幫忙裝上去的那批既有用戶。這個機制值得講精確一點，因為要帶走的是它的一般形式：當硬體的S曲線走平，既有用戶的數量並不是零，而是史上最大——它會變成一個新滲透率問題的分母，也就是「這些既有用戶裡，有多少人可以被賣一個會持續付費的東西」。在出貨量停止成長時就把這家公司判出局的人，是把上一個問題答得很好，卻完全沒有回答當下這個問題。

How to see this from public data: penetration figures for consumer categories are usually published by industry trade associations or national statistics agencies; for business-to-business categories, a company's own investor materials often state what share of its addressable customer base has adopted a product, and year-over-year change in that figure is itself informative. Where no direct penetration figure exists, unit shipment growth decelerating sharply while the installed base (cumulative units sold) keeps compounding is a reasonable proxy for "penetration is getting high."

要從公開資料看出這一點：消費性產品的滲透率數字，通常由產業公會或官方統計機構定期公布；企業對企業的類別，公司自己的投資人資料經常會揭露有多少比例的潛在客戶已經採用這項產品，這個數字的年增變化本身就有參考價值。如果找不到直接的滲透率數字，可以退而求其次，用「出貨量成長明顯放緩、但累計安裝量（歷年累積賣出的總數）還在持續墊高」當作滲透率已經偏高的替代訊號。

Hands-On: Size the Second Curve動手做：把第二條曲線量出來

Use the two numbers from Apple's own annual reports quoted above: Services net sales of about $24.3B in fiscal 2016 and about $96.2B in fiscal 2024 — eight years apart. Work out the multiple first, then convert it into an annual rate, which is the habit this lesson keeps asking for.

用上面引自蘋果年報的兩個數字：服務業務銷售額2016財年大約243億美元、2024財年大約962億美元，相隔八年。先算出倍數，再把倍數換算成年化成長率——這正是這一課一直要求的那個習慣。

  1. Multiple over eight years = 96.2 ÷ 24.3 ≈ x八年倍數 ＝ 96.2 ÷ 24.3 ≈ 倍
  2. Compound annual rate (%) = that multiple to the power of 1÷8, minus 1 ≈ %年複合成長率（%）＝ 該倍數開八次方再減1 ≈ %

Roughly 19% a year for eight years is a strong number, and notice what it is not: it is not the 40%-plus that "nearly 4x" feels like at first glance. That gap between how a multiple feels and what it implies annually is why the conversion is worth doing every single time. Then ask the question the arithmetic cannot answer — whether this second curve has its own ceiling, since it is sold to a fixed installed base and its own penetration rises with every year it grows.

八年每年大約19%是很強的數字，但也要看清楚它不是什麼：它不是「將近四倍」乍看給人的那種四成以上。倍數給人的感覺和它真正隱含的年增率之間的落差，正是每一次都值得動手換算的理由。算完之後，再問一個算術回答不了的問題：這條第二曲線自己有沒有天花板？因為它賣的對象是一批數量固定的既有用戶，它每多成長一年，自己的滲透率也就更高一分。

Strong Demand Does Not Mean the Profit Sits Here需求強不代表利潤留在這一環

Here is the counter-intuitive part, and it is worth sitting with: a genuinely strong, well-documented, structurally growing market can still be a terrible place for shareholders to put money, because demand and profit captureWhether a company in a value chain actually keeps the economic value created by strong end-market demand, versus watching it get competed or negotiated away. are two entirely separate questions. Solar panel manufacturing is the clean textbook case: global demand for solar panels grew for well over a decade in a genuinely structural way, driven by falling costs and climate policy — and yet, for most of that period, panel manufacturers as a group earned razor-thin or negative margins, because the manufacturing step itself became commoditized, with too many factories (many built with state-supported capital) chasing the same buyers. The demand story was completely true. The investment case for owning a panel maker was, for long stretches, false.

這裡是反直覺的地方，值得花點時間想清楚：一個真實、有憑有據、結構性成長的市場，仍然可能是股東放錢進去最糟糕的地方之一，因為需求跟利潤捕獲價值鏈裡的一家公司，是不是真的把終端需求強勁所創造出來的經濟價值留在自己手上，還是眼睜睜看著它在競爭或議價中被別人拿走。是兩個完全獨立的問題。太陽能板製造是教科書等級的乾淨案例：全球太陽能板需求在超過十年的時間裡，確確實實走出一條結構性成長曲線，動力來自成本持續下降與氣候政策。然而在這段期間的大多數時候，太陽能板製造商作為一個群體，賺到的毛利薄到近乎於零、甚至是負的——因為製造這個環節本身被高度商品化，太多工廠（不少還有國家資本挹注）在搶同一批買家。需求的故事完全屬實。持有一家太陽能板製造商股票的投資理由，在很長一段時間裡卻是錯的。

First Solar is the named exception worth studying in detail, because its own 10-K numbers show the mechanism directly. Unlike most panel makers, First Solar does not use polysilicon at all — its panels use a thin-film cadmium telluride process, stated plainly in its own 10-K filings, which sidesteps the crystalline-silicon supply chain where the commoditization above played out. Its gross margin moved from about 25.0% in fiscal 2021, down to a trough of about 2.7% in fiscal 2022 (before a U.S. policy shift), then up to about 39.2% in fiscal 2023 and about 44.2% in fiscal 2024 — a recovery that tracks the Section 45X domestic-manufacturing tax credit under the Inflation Reduction Act, not a change in underlying solar demand, which stayed strong throughout. Solar demand did not create that margin swing; a specific policy change plus a technology choice that avoided the commoditized part of the chain did.

第一太陽能（First Solar）是值得細看的具名例外，因為它自己10-K申報裡的數字，直接示範了這個機制。跟大多數太陽能板廠不同，第一太陽能完全不使用多晶矽——它的面板用的是薄膜碲化鎘製程，這一點在它自己的10-K裡寫得很清楚，也讓它繞開了上面商品化最嚴重的那條結晶矽供應鏈。它的毛利率從2021財年大約25.0%，一路跌到2022財年低點大約2.7%（那是美國政策轉向之前），接著回升到2023財年大約39.2%、2024財年大約44.2%——這段回升，對應的是《降低通膨法案》裡第45X條「本土製造」稅額扣抵，不是終端太陽能需求本身的變化，因為需求在這整段期間其實一直很強勁。不是太陽能需求造出了這段毛利率的劇烈起伏，是一個具體的政策轉向，加上一個從一開始就避開商品化那段供應鏈的技術選擇，才造出來的。

The reverse also happens: a supplier sitting at a genuine bottleneck within an otherwise commoditized value chain can capture windfall profits during a demand surge even though its own end market looks unglamorous — a lithium producer during a battery-material shortage, or a specialized equipment maker whose product every panel factory needs regardless of how thin the panel makers' own margins are. The lesson is not "avoid strong-demand industries." The lesson is that a demand number, however well built, cannot by itself tell you where in the value chain the money actually lands — that question needs a completely different kind of analysis, which the next lesson takes up directly.

反過來的情況也會發生：在一條大致商品化的價值鏈裡，一個真正卡在瓶頸位置的供應商，即使自己的終端市場看起來一點都不性感，也可能在需求暴增期間賺到意外之財——例如電池原料短缺期間的鋰生產商，或是不管太陽能板廠自己的毛利多薄、每一家工廠都非買不可的特定設備商。這裡的教訓不是「避開需求強勁的產業」，而是：不管一個需求數字算得多嚴謹，它本身都無法告訴你這筆錢最後會落在價值鏈的哪一個環節——那是一個完全不同種類的分析，下一課會直接接著處理。

Where this tool stops working: everything in this lesson answers one question — how big can this market get, and how fast. It says nothing about who inside that market keeps the profit (the next lesson's job), nothing about whether new capacity will show up fast enough to erase any pricing power the growth briefly creates (the lesson after that), and nothing about whether the current stock price has already priced in the growth story you just built. A well-built demand case is necessary before buying almost anything — it is never sufficient on its own.

這個工具在哪裡會失靈：這一課教的所有東西，都只回答一個問題——這個市場能長多大、長多快。它沒有告訴你這個市場裡誰把利潤留在自己手上（下一課的任務），沒有告訴你新產能會不會快到把這波成長暫時創造出來的議價力磨掉（再下一課的任務），也沒有告訴你現在的股價是不是已經把你剛剛算出來的成長故事反映進去了。一個算得紮實的需求論點，是買進幾乎任何東西之前的必要條件——但它本身從來不是充分條件。

Takeaway一句話收斂

A true, well-documented demand story tells you the industry is worth studying. It does not tell you who captures the profit, whether supply will erase the opportunity, or whether the price already assumes the good news — three separate questions this course answers next.一個真實、有憑有據的需求故事，告訴你這個產業值得研究。但它沒有回答三件事：誰把利潤留住、供給會不會把這個機會磨平、股價是不是早就假設了這個好消息。這三個問題彼此獨立，接下來幾課會一個一個處理。

📱 War Story戰爭故事

When the first iPhone shipped in 2007, global smartphone penetration was still in the low single digits, and smartphones were a small, premium-priced slice of a handset market still dominated by simple feature phones. Over the next several years, that changed at a pace few forecasters got right: by around 2013, smartphones had overtaken feature phones in global handset shipments, and in developed markets like the United States and much of developed Asia, penetration climbed into the 60-70%+ range by around 2015. That eight-year stretch is close to a textbook S-curve — slow start, steep middle, and a top that was clearly visible by the mid-2010s.

2007年第一代iPhone上市時，全球智慧型手機滲透率還在個位數低段，智慧型手機只是整個手機市場裡一小塊高單價、被簡易型功能手機主導的區塊。接下來幾年，變化的速度快到沒幾個預測者猜對：大約到2013年前後，智慧型手機在全球手機出貨量上已經超越功能手機；在美國與大部分發展成熟的亞洲市場，滲透率到2015年前後已經爬升到大約六成到七成以上。這段大約八年的區間，幾乎是教科書等級的S曲線——慢啟動、陡中段，而且頂部在2010年代中期已經清楚可見。

What happened next is the part suppliers geared for continued volume growth got wrong: once penetration in mature markets sat that high, the growth engine mechanically had to shift from "new buyers" to "replacement," and replacement cycles themselves lengthened — the typical gap between an owner's upgrades stretched from roughly two years toward three years or more, as improvements between generations became less compelling relative to the price of a new device. Global smartphone unit shipments, which had compounded at a rapid clip for most of the prior decade, plateaued and then declined in parts of 2017-2019 even as the installed base kept growing — a company or supplier still modeling demand off the old S-curve slope during that stretch was modeling the wrong engine. This example is used here purely to teach the mechanism, not as a view on any specific company today.

接下來發生的事，是那些還在按照舊有出貨量成長速度規劃產能的供應商沒料到的：一旦成熟市場的滲透率站上這個高度，成長引擎就必然要從「新買家」轉向「換機」，而換機週期本身也拉長了——用戶升級的典型間隔，從大約兩年拉長到三年甚至更久，因為新一代機種帶來的改善，相對於新機價格已經不那麼有吸引力。全球智慧型手機出貨量在前十年大部分時間都以很快的速度成長，卻在2017到2019年間的部分時段停滯甚至下滑，即使累計安裝量還在增加。如果一家公司或供應商在這段期間，還在用舊的S曲線斜率推估需求，那它推的其實是一台已經換檔的引擎。這個案例純粹是教學用途，不代表對現在任何一家特定公司的看法。

Apple's own 10-K numbers are the cleanest instance of this on record for a single company: iPhone unit sales fell from about 231.2 million units in fiscal 2015 to about 211.9 million units in fiscal 2016 — a decline of roughly 8%, and iPhone net sales fell from about $155.0B to about $136.7B, roughly 12% — the first year-over-year decline in iPhone volume since its 2007 launch. That one data point, disclosed in Apple's own unit-sales table, is the mechanism from this lesson landing on a single, named company rather than an industry aggregate. Apple named the cause in the same document: the decline was attributed primarily to a lower rate of iPhone upgrades than the year before, plus difficult conditions in several major markets. The replacement-cycle engine was not something an outsider had to infer — the seller wrote it down.

蘋果自己10-K裡的數字，是這件事在單一公司身上留下的最乾淨紀錄：iPhone出貨量從2015財年大約2.312億支，掉到2016財年大約2.119億支——跌幅大約8%；iPhone淨銷售額則從大約1,550億美元，掉到大約1,367億美元，跌幅大約12%——這是iPhone自2007年上市以來，出貨量第一次年減。這一個數字就寫在蘋果自己揭露的出貨量表格裡，讓這一課教的機制落在一家有名有姓的公司身上，而不是停留在產業加總層級。蘋果在同一份文件裡自己說明了原因：這次下滑主要來自當年度的iPhone換機率低於前一年，加上幾個主要市場的景氣不佳。換機週期這台引擎，不需要外部的人去猜——賣方自己把它寫下來了。

The signal was public well before unit shipments actually turned down. That lengthening replacement cycle — the stretch from roughly two years toward three — was not a hidden number: consumer-research surveys tracking how long owners kept their existing phone before upgrading were publishing that trend for a couple of years before the shipment decline showed up in anyone's income statement. An analyst who watched unit growth alone had no warning until the quarter it happened. An analyst who was also watching the replacement-cycle number had roughly a two-year head start on the same conclusion — which is the general point this lesson keeps returning to: the leading indicator for a penetration-ceiling problem is almost never the growth number itself, it is the pace at which existing owners choose to repeat.

這個訊號其實在出貨量真正轉跌之前，早就公開露出來了。那段拉長中的換機週期——從大約兩年拉向三年——不是一個藏起來的數字：追蹤用戶換機前平均用了多久的消費者調查，在出貨量下滑真正反映在任何一家公司損益表之前，就已經連續好幾年公布這個趨勢。只看出貨量成長的分析師，直到真正發生的那一季才會被嚇到；同時也在看換機週期數字的分析師，大約提早了兩年就能得出同一個結論——這正是這一課一直在強調的重點：滲透率天花板問題的領先指標，幾乎從來不是成長數字本身，而是既有用戶「多快願意重複購買」這件事的節奏。

🔌 War Story戰爭故事

In 2021, global electric-vehicle sales surged, and a wave of forecasts extrapolated the prior two years' acceleration in a straight line, projecting global EV penetration into the 30%-plus range by the mid-2020s. By 2024, the picture had split in two along a line most of those forecasts had not drawn: in the United States and much of Europe, EV sales growth slowed sharply — several automakers publicly pushed back electrification timelines and scaled back planned EV production — while in China, EV penetration kept climbing with barely any deceleration, crossing well above a third of new car sales.

2021年，全球電動車銷量出現爆發性成長，一批預測機構把前兩年的加速走勢直接線性外推，推算到2020年代中期，全球電動車滲透率會突破三成以上。到了2024年，畫面沿著一條大多數這類預測都沒畫出來的線分裂成兩半：在美國與歐洲大部分地區，電動車銷售成長明顯放緩——好幾家車廠公開下修電氣化時程、縮減原訂的電動車產能計畫；而在中國，電動車滲透率幾乎沒有減速地持續攀升，滲透率遠遠超過新車銷售的三分之一。

The lesson is not "EV demand forecasts were wrong" — it is that "global EV penetration" was never really one S-curve to begin with. Charging infrastructure density, government incentive design, local battery-supply-chain cost structure, and consumer price sensitivity differ enough by geography that treating the whole world as a single adoption curve hid two very different stories under one blended number. A demand analysis that stopped at the global aggregate would have missed both the deceleration and the continued strength sitting right next to each other. This example is used here purely to teach the mechanism, not as a view on any specific company today.

這裡的教訓不是「電動車需求預測全部錯了」，而是「全球電動車滲透率」從一開始就不是單一一條S曲線。充電基礎設施密度、政府補貼設計、當地電池供應鏈成本結構、消費者對價格的敏感度，這些因素因地區而異的程度，大到把全世界當成一條單一的普及曲線來看，會把兩個非常不同的故事藏在同一個混合數字底下。一份只看全球加總數字的需求分析，會同時漏看放緩與持續強勁這兩件事，即使它們就並排發生在旁邊。這個案例純粹是教學用途，不代表對現在任何一家特定公司的看法。

This is also a useful case for naming the condition under which the four-driver framework taught earlier in this lesson would itself give the wrong answer if applied carelessly. Someone modeling "EV penetration" as a single structural-demand line, using one global growth rate, would have been mechanically wrong the moment they picked that scope — not because structural demand was the wrong bucket, but because the unit of analysis was wrong: the correct unit here was closer to "EV penetration, by country, conditioned on that country's charging density and incentive regime," and the geography-level divergence was already visible in publicly tracked charging-station build-out data and incentive-program terms well before the 2024 sales data confirmed it. A framework applied at the wrong level of aggregation can be internally consistent and still mislead.

這個案例也適合用來說明：這一課前面教的四個驅動力架構，如果套用得不夠仔細，在什麼條件下自己會給出錯誤答案。如果有人把「電動車滲透率」當成單一一條結構性需求曲線來建模、只用一個全球成長率，那麼從選定這個範圍的那一刻起，判斷在機制上就已經錯了。不是因為「結構性需求」這個桶子選錯，而是分析單位本身選錯了：這裡正確的分析單位，應該更接近「按國家拆分、並且以該國充電密度與補貼制度為條件的電動車滲透率」。地區之間的分歧，其實在充電站建置的公開追蹤數據與各國補貼條款裡，早在2024年銷售數字證實之前就已經看得到。一個套用在錯誤加總層級上的架構，可以自成一套邏輯，卻仍然會誤導判斷。

Think first: why can penetration keep rising for years past the point most people call "saturated"?先想一下：為什麼滲透率會在大多數人喊出「已經飽和」之後，還能繼續往上爬好幾年？

"Saturated" is usually measured against one specific customer segment or one specific price point, not against every possible future variant of the product. Once a key cost component drops far enough, or a new use case appears, the effective denominator of "how many people could plausibly buy this" quietly grows — a second, distinct S-curve begins layered on top of the first one, and total penetration against the new, larger denominator can keep climbing for years even though penetration against the old denominator genuinely topped out.

「飽和」通常是相對於某一個特定客群、或某一個特定價位帶去衡量的，不是相對於這項產品未來每一種可能形態的總和。一旦某個關鍵成本零件降到夠低，或是出現新的用途，「還有多少人可能會買」這個分母就會悄悄變大——一條第二條、獨立的S曲線，會疊加在第一條之上開始展開，而對照這個更大的新分母，總滲透率還能再爬好幾年，即使對照舊分母來看，滲透率確實已經到頂。

⚠
When a company's revenue growth jumps in the same year it closed an acquisition, assume none of the jump is demand until you have separated the two. Bought revenue and demand growth look identical on the top line and mean opposite things: one says more buyers want the product, the other says the seller wrote a cheque. Most annual reports give you the split somewhere — an organic-growth figure, a pro-forma comparison, or a note showing the revenue the acquired business contributed since the closing date. If none of those exist, treat the growth rate as unusable and go to unit volumes instead.當一家公司營收成長跳升的那一年，剛好也完成了一樁併購，在你把兩者分開之前，先假設這個跳升裡沒有任何一塊是需求。買來的營收跟需求帶來的成長，在營收那一行長得一模一樣，意思卻相反：一個是更多買家想要這個產品，另一個是賣方開了一張支票。多數年報都會在某個地方給你拆解——有機成長率、備考（pro forma）對照，或是附註裡「被併購事業自交割日起貢獻的營收」。如果三樣都找不到，就把這個成長率當成不可用，改去看出貨量。
⚠
When a market-size number counts every buyer who could theoretically want the product, subtract the ones who cannot actually buy it before using the figure. A hospital that has no budget line for the device, a country where the product is not approved for sale, a customer whose existing contract runs another six years — all of them sit inside a headline market size and none of them can place an order this cycle. The usable number is always smaller than the theoretical one, and the honest way to present it is two figures with the reason for the gap written between them, not one big figure with an asterisk.當一個市場規模數字，把「理論上可能想要這項產品」的買家全部算進去時，先扣掉那些其實買不了的，再拿這個數字來用。一家沒有編列這項設備預算的醫院、一個這項產品尚未取得銷售許可的國家、一個現有合約還要再跑六年的客戶——他們都被算在頭條市場規模裡，但這一輪誰都下不了單。可用的數字永遠比理論數字小，而誠實的呈現方式是把兩個數字都寫出來、中間交代清楚差在哪裡，而不是給一個大數字再加一個星號註解。

How This Connects這一課接到哪裡

The demand engine and the 5-year multiple built in this lesson are only half the picture — the next lesson takes the same growth rate and asks whether new capacity can show up fast enough to absorb it, which determines whether demand strength ever turns into pricing power at all. Whether the profit from that demand actually lands inside the company you are studying, rather than a supplier or a customer further down the chain, is answered by tracing the value chain in the lesson on the profit pool. The penetration and replacement-cycle rhythm measured here also becomes one of three proxies used to locate an industry's position in the capital cycle. And the question this lesson deliberately leaves open — how much of a strong demand story the market has already priced in — is not answerable with the tools built here; it waits for the lesson on price and expectations.

這一課建立的需求引擎跟五年需求倍數，只是整幅圖的一半——下一課會拿同一個成長率，去問新產能能不能快到把它吸收掉，這決定了需求強勁會不會真的變成議價力。這股需求最後帶來的利潤，究竟是留在你正在研究的這家公司，還是流向更上游的供應商或更下游的客戶，要靠利潤池那一課沿著價值鏈追蹤才能回答。這裡量到的滲透率與換機節奏，也會變成後面用來定位資本週期位置的三個代理指標之一。而這一課刻意留下沒有回答的問題——一個強勁的需求故事，市場已經反映了多少——並不是這裡建立的工具能回答的，要等到價格與市場預期那一課。

Knowing how big a demand pool is and how fast it is growing tells you nothing about whether new supply can chase it fast enough to keep prices, and margins, intact — that tension is where the next lesson begins.

知道一個需求池有多大、成長多快，並不能告訴你新增的供給會不會追得夠快，把價格跟利潤率一起追掉——這股拉扯，正是下一課要開始處理的問題。
