A Moat Must Show Up in the Numbers

護城河要對得上數字

What You'll Learn這一課學什麼
  • Judge whether a moat is real from the level AND the stability of ROIC across a full cycle, not from one good year.從整個景氣週期的 ROIC 水準與穩定度（不是單一好年冬）判斷護城河是否真實存在。
  • Run a pricing-power test on gross margin: when input costs rise, does the company pass the cost through without losing volume?對毛利率做定價權測試：當成本上漲時，公司能不能把漲價轉嫁出去而不流失銷量。
  • Read market share and price/margin trends together to tell real pricing power apart from buying share with discounts.把份額趨勢和價格／毛利率趨勢疊在一起看，分辨真定價權與花錢買份額。
  • Use a rival's income statement as evidence — and understand why "the whole industry is making money" can mean the opposite of a moat.用對手的損益表當證據——並理解「整個產業都在賺錢」有時代表的其實是反面訊號。
  • Recognise when this toolkit does not apply, and connect "moat is real" to the separate question of "is the price already too high."辨認這套工具什麼時候不適用，並把「護城河是真的」和「價格是不是已經太貴」這兩個問題分開處理。

In 1972, Blue Chip Stamps — a company Warren Buffett and Charlie Munger controlled and which was later merged into Berkshire Hathaway — paid $25 million for See's Candies, a California boxed-chocolate maker. That year See's had about $30 million in sales and just under $5 million in pretax profit, and the whole business needed only about $8 million of capital to operate. So it was already earning roughly 60% pretax on the capital actually invested in it, and the $25 million price was about three times its net tangible assets. On the surface it looked like an ordinary regional candy business — not fast-growing, not high-tech, selling a product nobody strictly needed. What made a price well above asset value worth paying was a single number the two of them kept coming back to: See's could raise the price of a box of chocolates almost every year, and unit sales barely moved. Munger later said learning to notice that number — not the story about the brand, the number itself — was one of the most important lessons of his investing life.

1972 年，由巴菲特與蒙格控股、後來併入波克夏海瑟威的藍籌印花公司，用 2500 萬美元買下了美國加州的巧克力製造商喜詩糖果（See's Candies）。那一年喜詩的營收約 3000 萬美元、稅前獲利略低於 500 萬美元，而整門生意只需要約 800 萬美元的資本就跑得動——換算下來，它當時的稅前投入資本報酬率就已經高達約 60%，而 2500 萬美元的買價大約是淨有形資產的三倍。表面上這只是一家普通的地區型糖果公司——不是高成長，不是高科技，賣的東西也沒人非買不可。願意付一個明顯高於資產價值的價格，關鍵在於一個他們兩人反覆提到的數字：喜詩幾乎每年都能調漲一盒巧克力的價格，銷量卻幾乎不掉。蒙格後來說，學會盯著這個數字——不是故事本身，是數字本身——是他投資生涯裡最重要的體悟之一。

That single observation is the whole subject of this lesson. The previous lesson named five possible sources of a moat — cost advantage, intangible assets, switching costs, network effects, efficient scale. Naming the source is a hypothesis. This lesson is about the evidence that turns the hypothesis into a conclusion you can act on: what number, over what stretch of time, under what stress test, actually proves a moat is there — and what number looks exactly like proof but isn't.

這個觀察正是這一課的主題。上一課列出了護城河可能的五個來源——成本優勢、無形資產、轉換成本、網路效應、有效規模。指出來源只是一個假說。這一課要談的是把假說變成可以下判斷的證據：用哪個數字、看多長的時間、做什麼樣的壓力測試，才能真正證明護城河存在——以及哪個數字看起來像證據，其實不是。

The First Piece of Evidence: ROIC That Is High AND Stable第一道證據：ROIC 要高，還要穩

The mechanism is simple: a moat's job is to stop competitors from bidding away a company's excess return. If it is doing that job, the company's return on invested capitalPost-tax operating profit divided by the capital actually invested in the business — plant, inventory, receivables, minus non-interest-bearing operating liabilities. See Lesson 2 for the full build. should sit meaningfully above its cost of capital not just this year, but across good years and bad ones — because a real structural barrier does not take a year off. A company earning 25% ROIC in a boom year and 4% in the following downturn is telling you its "edge" evaporates exactly when competitors get desperate, which is when a moat is supposed to matter most.

機制其實很簡單：護城河的工作是擋住競爭者把超額報酬搶走。如果它真的在發揮作用，公司的投入資本報酬率（ROIC）稅後營業利益除以真正投入到生意裡的資本——廠房設備、存貨、應收帳款，減去不計息的營運負債。完整算法見第 2 課。就應該不只在今年、而是在好年冬和壞年冬都明顯高於資金成本——因為真正的結構性障礙不會偶爾放假。一家公司在景氣高峰 ROIC 25%、隔年景氣反轉就掉到 4%，這其實是在告訴你，它的「優勢」正好在競爭者最狠的時候消失了，而護城河最該派上用場的時刻，正是那個時候。

The evidence to pull: five to ten years of ROIC (not just net margin — a company can pad net margin with financial leverage that has nothing to do with a moat), covering at least one industry-wide downturn or cost shock. Two things to read off it: the average level (is it durably above a reasonable cost of capital — a rough working range of 8-10% for most industrial and consumer businesses), and the spread between the best and worst year. A tight spread around a high average is the signature of a real, defended edge. A wide spread — even if the average looks fine — is the signature of a business riding a cycle, not defending a position.

要拉出來看的證據：五到十年的 ROIC。不要用淨利率——淨利率可以靠借錢（財務槓桿）墊高，跟護城河沒有關係。這段期間還要涵蓋至少一次產業性的景氣反轉或成本衝擊。然後看兩件事。第一是平均水準：這幾年有沒有一直高於合理的資金成本？多數工業與消費型公司的資金成本，粗略落在 8% 到 10%。第二是落差：最高的那一年減掉最低的那一年，差多少。平均高、落差小，代表這個優勢真的有人在守。落差很大，就算平均看起來不錯，代表的是這家公司在搭景氣的順風車，不是在守自己的位置。

The false signal to watch for: a single spectacular year driven by a one-off event — a competitor's factory fire, a temporary shortage, a currency windfall, a one-time asset sale booked through operating income. All of these can print a great ROIC number without a single thing changing about the company's actual competitive position. The tell is that the number does not repeat, and there is no mechanism (a patent, a contract, a customer switching cost) you can point to that would make it repeat.

要提防的假訊號：由一次性事件推高的某一個爆亮年份——競爭對手工廠失火、暫時性缺貨、匯率意外的順風、或是一次性資產出售被算進營業利益裡。這些都可能印出漂亮的 ROIC 數字，而公司實際的競爭地位其實一點都沒變。破綻在於這個數字不會重複出現，而且你也找不到任何具體機制（一項專利、一份長約、一個客戶轉換成本）可以解釋它為什麼會重複。

A real pair of numbers makes the level-and-stability test concrete, and both are pulled straight from 10-K filings. O'Reilly Automotive, an auto-parts retailer, reported operating margin of about 22% in 2021, about 21% in 2022, about 20% in 2023 and about 19% in 2024 — four years that included a sharp cost-inflation shock and a slowdown in discretionary car spending, and the number barely moved. United States Steel, over almost the same stretch, went from an operating loss of roughly 7% of revenue in 2020, to about 23% in 2021 at the peak of a post-pandemic steel-price spike, back down to about 16% in 2022 and about 6% in 2023 once steel prices retreated — a swing of roughly 30 percentage points top to bottom. (Operating margin is not the same number as ROIC, but neither company was making large swings in its invested-capital base over this period, so the shape of the two lines tells close to the same story an ROIC series would.) Same four years, same backdrop of inflation and a cooling economy for both — one company's return barely noticed the weather, the other's swung by 30 points. That is the level-and-stability test decided by an actual comparison, not a definition.

用一組真實數字把「水準與穩定度測試」講清楚，兩組數字都直接來自公開的 10-K 年報。汽車零件零售商 O'Reilly Automotive，2021 年營業利益率約 22%、2022 年約 21%、2023 年約 20%、2024 年約 19%——這四年剛好經歷了一次劇烈的成本通膨衝擊、以及消費者在非必要汽車支出上的放緩，這個數字幾乎沒有明顯移動。美國鋼鐵公司（United States Steel）在幾乎同一段期間，2020 年營業利益率是約 -7%（虧損），2021 年在疫情後鋼價飆漲的高峰衝到約 23%，2022 年回落到約 16%，2023 年鋼價回落後掉到約 6%——高低相差約 30 個百分點。（營業利益率不完全等於 ROIC，但這兩家公司在這段期間投入資本的規模都沒有劇烈變動，所以這兩條線呈現的形狀，和真正的 ROIC 序列會講的故事很接近。）同樣的四年、同樣面對通膨和景氣降溫的大環境——一家公司的報酬率幾乎沒有感覺到天氣變化，另一家公司卻上下擺盪了 30 個百分點。這正是水準與穩定度測試用一組真實比較給出的答案，不是一句定義。

Takeaway重點

A moat shows up as a level AND a shape: durably above cost of capital, with a narrow spread across a full cycle. One great year is a data point, not a moat.護城河同時展現在水準和形狀上：持續高於資金成本，而且在整個景氣循環中落差要小。單一個好年冬只是一個數據點，不是護城河。

The Pricing Power Test: Does Gross Margin Hold When Costs Rise?定價權測試：成本上漲時，毛利率守不守得住？

ROIC tells you the outcome. Gross margin behaviour tells you the mechanism — specifically, whether the company can pass a cost increase through to its customers without losing meaningful volume. Call this the pricing power testWhether a company can raise price to fully offset a cost increase without a proportional drop in unit volume — the cleanest single test of real pricing power.. A business with genuine pricing power sees its input costs go up 10% and its gross margin barely moves, because it raises price roughly in step and its customers keep buying anyway. A business without it sees the same cost shock eat straight into the margin, because either it cannot raise price (no leverage over the customer) or it can, but volume collapses the moment it does (the customer switches).

ROIC 告訴你結果。毛利率的行為則告訴你機制——具體來說，公司能不能把成本上漲轉嫁給客戶，而不明顯流失銷量。這就是定價權測試公司能不能透過漲價完全抵銷成本上漲，同時銷量不會等比例掉——這是測試真實定價權最乾淨的單一方法。。真正有定價權的公司，遇到成本上漲 10%，毛利率幾乎不動，因為它幾乎同步把價格調上去，客戶照樣買單。沒有定價權的公司，遇到同樣的成本衝擊，毛利率會直接被吃掉——不是漲不了價（對客戶沒有籌碼），就是漲了價、但銷量在漲價的那一刻就垮了（客戶轉單）。

The evidence to pull: find a period when an input cost genuinely rose industry-wide — a commodity spike, a wage inflation cycle, a tariff, a shipping cost surge — and compare gross margin before and after across the company and its closest peers. A company that holds its margin (or gives up only a point or two) while peers give up five to ten points has just demonstrated pricing power in the one setting where it actually matters: under stress, not in a calm year when nobody needed to test it.

要拉出來看的證據：找一段某個投入成本真的全產業一起上漲的期間——原物料飆漲、工資通膨循環、關稅、運費暴漲——比較那段期間前後，這家公司和它最直接的同業毛利率各自怎麼變。如果一家公司只掉一兩個百分點，同業卻掉了五到十個百分點，這就是在唯一真正重要的情境下——壓力測試下，不是沒人需要測試的平靜年份——證明了自己有定價權。

The false signal here runs in both directions. A margin that holds up can be pricing power — or it can be a temporary cost tailwind unrelated to the customer relationship (a raw-material contract locked at an old price, a currency move, a one-off supplier rebate) that will reverse next year. A margin that falls can look like "no moat" — or it can be a company deliberately absorbing cost to protect long-term share, a strategic choice rather than a competitive failure. The distinguishing question is always the same: is there a specific, nameable reason (a patent, a certification requirement, a switching cost, a contract) the company could raise price without losing the customer — or did the margin simply happen to hold this one time?

這裡的假訊號會往兩個方向都出現。毛利率守住了，可能是真正的定價權——也可能只是跟客戶關係完全無關的暫時性成本順風（用舊價格鎖住的原物料合約、匯率變動、供應商一次性回饋），下一年就會反轉。毛利率下滑，看起來像「沒有護城河」——也可能是公司刻意吸收成本以守住長期份額的策略選擇，不是競爭上輸掉了。分辨的關鍵問題永遠一樣：有沒有一個具體、講得出名字的理由（一項專利、一個認證門檻、一個轉換成本、一份長約），讓公司可以漲價而不流失客戶——還是這次毛利率守住，只是剛好而已。

A real failed test is worth sitting with, because "raised price" and "passed the pricing power test" are not the same event. Clorox, the household-products maker, did raise list prices repeatedly during fiscal 2022 (year ended June 2022) to fight a sharp jump in input, freight and manufacturing costs. Its gross margin fell anyway — from about 44% in fiscal 2021 to about 36% in fiscal 2022, an 8-point drop, according to its 10-K filings — before recovering to about 39% the following year as costs eased and the price increases fully caught up. The price increases were real and did eventually work; they simply did not work fast enough to protect the margin in the year the cost shock actually hit. That gap between "raised price" and "protected margin" is exactly what the test is built to catch — a company can be doing the right thing and still fail the test for a year, which is why the test should be read as a full before-and-after window, not a single announcement.

有一個真實的「測試沒過」案例值得細看，因為「漲價了」和「通過了定價權測試」根本是兩件事。家用品廠商高樂氏（Clorox）在 2022 財年（截至 2022 年 6 月）確實多次調漲產品售價，用來對抗投入成本、運費和製造成本的劇烈上漲。但根據其 10-K 年報揭露，毛利率還是掉了——從 2021 財年的約 44%，掉到 2022 財年的約 36%，跌了 8 個百分點，隔年成本壓力緩解、漲價效果完全發酵後，才回升到約 39%。這些漲價本身是真實的，最後也確實奏效了；只是奏效的速度不夠快，來不及在成本衝擊發生的那一年守住毛利率。「漲價了」和「守住毛利率」之間的這道落差，正是這項測試想抓的東西——一家公司可能做了正確的事，卻還是在某一年沒通過測試，這也是為什麼這項測試該看的是完整的「前後對照窗口」，不是單一次的漲價公告。

Where To Find This Yourself, In a Real 10-K公開資料在哪裡看：一份真實 10-K 的實際位置

Every US-listed company's 10-K is free to read (US companies file a 10-K annually; a company based outside the US that lists in the US usually files an equivalent 20-F instead). Here is exactly where each number in this lesson lives.

每一家美國上市公司的 10-K 年報都是公開免費的（美國公司每年提交 10-K；總部在美國以外、在美國掛牌的公司通常改提交性質相近的 20-F）。以下是這一課用到的每個數字，具體會出現在哪裡。

  1. 1

    For ROIC inputs: the income statement (usually called "Consolidated Statements of Operations" or "Consolidated Income Statements") gives operating income. A few pages later, the balance sheet gives property/plant/equipment, inventory, receivables and non-interest-bearing operating liabilities like accounts payable — everything Lesson 2 uses to build invested capital.

    要算 ROIC 的原料：損益表（通常叫「合併損益表」）給你營業利益。翻幾頁之後，資產負債表會給你廠房設備、存貨、應收帳款，和應付帳款這類不計息的營運負債——第 2 課用來組出投入資本的所有材料都在這裡。

  2. 2

    For a gross-margin history: the same income statement's "cost of revenue" (or "cost of sales") and "net revenues" lines, one filing per year — pull five consecutive years of 10-Ks to build the five-year table this lesson keeps asking for.

    要算毛利率的歷史走勢：同一張損益表上的「營業成本」和「營收淨額」，一份 10-K 抓一年——連續抓五年份的 10-K，就能組出這一課反覆要求的五年表。

  3. 3

    For the peer comparison: pull the closest competitor's 10-K for the same fiscal years, and read the same line items. Watch the fiscal year-end date closely — a company whose fiscal year ends in June and one whose year ends in December are not measuring the same twelve months, and lining them up as if they were will misdate exactly which quarter absorbed a shared cost shock.

    要做同業比較：找出最直接競爭對手同一段年份的 10-K，讀同樣的科目。要特別留意會計年度的結束月份——一家會計年度在 6 月結束、一家在 12 月結束，兩者根本不是同一段十二個月，硬把它們對齊在同一列，會搞錯是哪一季真正吸收了同一次成本衝擊。

  4. 4

    For share and pricing evidence, which almost never sits inside the 10-K's financial statements themselves: check Item 1 ("Business"), where management describes its own view of competitive position, and the earnings-call transcript (usually posted free on the company's own investor-relations site) — analysts ask about price and volume directly almost every quarter, and management's answer is often more candid there than in the filing.

    份額和定價的證據幾乎不會出現在 10-K 的財務報表本身：要看第一部分（「業務」章節），管理層在這裡會自己描述對競爭地位的看法；還有法說會逐字稿（通常公司自己的投資人關係網站就有免費全文），分析師幾乎每一季都會直接問價格和銷量，管理層在這裡的回答往往比正式文件裡更直白。

  5. 5

    Rough normal ranges to hold in your head while reading: ROIC durably above roughly 8-10% cost of capital is a reasonable working bar for most industrial and consumer businesses; a peer gross-margin gap under about 3 points is close to noise; a P/E premium under about 50% above the peer median rarely needs a special justification. None of these are hard laws — they are starting points for asking "does this number need an explanation," not a pass/fail line.

    讀資料時可以放在腦中的粗略正常區間：ROIC 持續高於資金成本約 8% 到 10%，對多數工業與消費型公司是合理的判斷門檻；同業毛利率落差在約 3 個百分點以內，接近雜訊；本益比溢價在同業中位數的約 50% 以內，通常不太需要特別解釋。這些都不是硬性規定——它們是用來問「這個數字需不需要解釋」的起點，不是及格／不及格的分界線。

◆ War Story 戰爭故事

Costco, membership fees, 2011-2024. Costco has raised its US and Canada membership fee only a handful of times in its history — about 2011 (base tier from $50 to $55), again around 2017 (from $55 to $60), and again in September 2024 (base tier from $60 to $65, executive tier from $120 to $130), each increase roughly five years apart. Each time, the same question got asked: would members quit rather than pay more? Each time, the renewal rate — the single cleanest volume signal a membership business has — stayed at roughly 90% in North America, barely moving. That is a pricing power test passed in public, repeatedly, over more than a decade, on a product (a membership card) that has no patent, no brand mystique, and no switching cost beyond mild inconvenience. The moat here is not the fee — it is the value gap between what members pay and what they save, wide enough that a $5 increase does not change the decision.

好市多（Costco），會員年費，2011 年至 2024 年。好市多在美國與加拿大市場的會員年費，歷史上只調漲過寥寥幾次——大約在 2011 年（基本會員從 50 美元調到 55 美元）、2017 年前後再調一次（55 美元調到 60 美元）、以及 2024 年 9 月最近一次（基本會員從 60 美元調到 65 美元、行政會員從 120 美元調到 130 美元），每次相隔大約五年。每一次調漲前，市場都問同一個問題：會員會不會乾脆退卡不續？每一次結果都一樣——續卡率，也就是會員制生意最乾淨的銷量指標，在北美始終維持在約 90% 左右，幾乎沒有明顯波動。這是一個公開、重複、跨越超過十年的定價權測試，測試的產品是一張會員卡——沒有專利、沒有品牌光環、除了小小的不方便之外也沒有真正的轉換成本。這裡的護城河不是年費本身，而是會員繳的錢和省下來的錢之間那道夠寬的價值落差，寬到一次五美元的漲價根本不會改變會員的決定。

The public-data tell here is specific, not a vague sense that "Costco seems strong": the company discloses its membership renewal rate every quarter, and a reader tracking that one line since long before the 2024 increase would have watched it sit near 90% for years — the evidence was sitting in plain sight before the announcement, not discovered after the fact. The boundary worth naming: this test would read very differently if a fee increase ever landed alongside a visible cut to what membership actually delivers — fewer warehouses, a shrinking product selection, or a well-publicized service failure. Pricing power measured by a stable renewal rate assumes the value side of the trade is held constant; a company that raises price while quietly eroding the benefit is not running the same test, and a renewal rate that starts slipping after a cut like that should be read as the moat narrowing, not as noise.

這裡的公開資料訊號很具體，不是一句籠統的「好市多看起來很強」：這家公司每一季都會揭露會員續卡率，一個從 2024 年那次漲價之前就開始追蹤這條數字的讀者，早就會看到它多年維持在接近 90% 的水準——證據在公告之前就已經攤在陽光下，不是事後才被發現的。這裡也有一個值得點名的邊界：如果漲價同時伴隨會員實際拿到的東西明顯縮水——賣場數量減少、商品選擇變少、或一次公開的服務事故——這項測試讀出來的結果會完全不同。用穩定的續卡率來衡量定價權，前提是交易裡「價值那一邊」維持不變；一家一邊漲價、一邊悄悄削減會員權益的公司，測的根本不是同一個測試，如果在那之後續卡率真的開始下滑，該解讀成護城河正在變窄，而不是雜訊。

Read Share and Price Together, Never One Alone份額和價格要一起看，不能只看一個

Market share going up is usually presented as good news by itself. It isn't — not until you check what happened to price and margin at the same time. There are exactly two ways to gain share: customers choose you at a similar or better price because you offer more value (a real moat expanding), or you pay for the share with lower prices, richer promotions, longer payment terms, or heavier marketing spend — buying shareGaining market share by paying for it with discounts, promotions, easier terms or heavy marketing, instead of winning it at a similar price. The signature is share rising while price or gross margin falls at the same time. rather than earning it. Both produce the same headline — "share up" — and look identical on a single chart. They are opposite stories about the business.

市佔率上升，通常單獨被當成好消息報導。其實不然——除非你同時檢查同一段時間價格和毛利率發生了什麼事。拿到份額，本質上只有兩種方式：客戶在價格差不多甚至更高的情況下還是選你，因為你提供了更多價值（護城河真的在變寬）；或者你用比較低的價格、更豐厚的促銷、更長的付款條件、或更重的行銷費用把份額買回來——這叫買份額用折扣、促銷、放寬條件或重砸行銷把市佔率買下來，而不是在價格相當的條件下贏過來。它的特徵是份額上升的同時，價格或毛利率同步下滑。，不是靠自己贏來的。這兩種情況都會產出同一個標題——「份額上升」——單看一張圖幾乎分辨不出來。但它們是兩個完全相反的故事。

The evidence to pull: plot market share and average selling price (or gross margin, if unit price is not disclosed) on the same timeline, three to five years. Share up together with price/margin flat or up is the signature of real competitive strength — the company is winning on something other than price. Share up while price/margin falls is the signature of buying share — sustainable only as long as the company is willing to keep funding the discount, and reversible the moment it stops (which is usually when the market notices margin trouble and forces a stop).

要拉出來看的證據：把市佔率和平均售價（如果沒揭露單價，就用毛利率代替）畫在同一條時間軸上，看三到五年。份額上升，同時價格／毛利率持平或上升，是真正競爭優勢的特徵——公司靠的是價格以外的東西在贏。份額上升，同時價格／毛利率下滑，是花錢買份額的特徵——這種成長只要公司還願意繼續補貼折扣就能延續，一旦公司停止補貼（通常是市場開始擔心毛利、逼公司踩剎車的時候），就會反轉。

The boundary case worth naming: buying share is not automatically a mistake. A young company deliberately trading margin for share early in a market's life, with a credible plan to raise price later once switching costs or network effects lock customers in, is a legitimate strategy — Amazon Web Services and many subscription software businesses have run some version of this. The test is whether there is an explicit, checkable mechanism that will let pricing power appear later. Without one, "we're investing in growth" is simply a slower way of describing a business with no moat.

值得特別點出的邊界情況：花錢買份額不代表一定是錯誤的決定。一家年輕的公司在市場發展早期，刻意用毛利率換份額、並且有可信的計畫在客戶被轉換成本或網路效應綁住之後再把價格拉回來，這是合理的策略——亞馬遜的雲端業務和許多訂閱制軟體公司，某種程度上都走過這條路。判斷的關鍵在於：有沒有一個明確、可以檢驗的機制，能讓定價權在之後真的出現。如果沒有，「我們正在投資成長」只是換一種比較慢的說法，來描述一家沒有護城河的生意。

A real case shows what it looks like when discounting fails on both counts at once — it neither protects the moat nor even buys the share it was supposed to buy. GoPro, the action-camera maker, saw its gross margin fall from about 45% in 2014 to about 42% in 2015, about 39% in 2016 and about 33% in 2017, as it cut prices to move a backlog of camera inventory through a period of slowing demand and a costly product recall. If that had been a pure trade of margin for share, the playbook described above, it would at least have grown the top line. It did not: revenue fell from about $1.39 billion in 2014 to about $1.18 billion in 2017, a decline of roughly 15%, according to its 10-K filings. The price cuts gave up margin and still did not hold the customer base — a reminder that "buying share" is a bet, not a formula, and the bet can simply lose: discounting a product that is losing relevance for reasons unrelated to price (a shrinking use case, better substitutes, a damaged launch) can shrink margin and revenue together.

有一個真實案例，示範了「靠降價買份額」同時在兩件事上都失敗的樣子——它既沒有守住任何優勢，甚至連原本想買的份額都沒買到。運動攝影機廠商 GoPro 的毛利率，從 2014 年的約 45%，掉到 2015 年約 42%、2016 年約 39%、2017 年約 33%，原因是它在需求放緩、加上一次代價不小的產品召回事件期間，靠降價去消化堆積的相機庫存。如果這真的只是「用毛利率換份額」的標準劇本，至少應該換到營收成長。但事實並非如此：根據其 10-K 年報，營收從 2014 年約 13.9 億美元，掉到 2017 年約 11.8 億美元，跌了約 15%。降價賠掉了毛利率，卻連客戶基礎都沒守住——這提醒了一件事：「花錢買份額」是一個賭注，不是一條公式，這個賭注完全可能輸掉：對一個因為與價格無關的原因正在失去吸引力的產品（使用情境在萎縮、有更好的替代品、一次搞砸的新品上市）降價，可能會讓毛利率和營收同時縮水。

Could a reader have caught GoPro earlier than the 2017 margin trough? Yes, and the year to look at is 2015, not 2017. In 2015 gross margin had already slipped from about 45% to about 42% — but revenue still grew hard, from about $1.39 billion to about $1.62 billion. That is what a discount that is working looks like: you give up margin, and the volume actually shows up. The following year the trade stopped paying. Revenue fell about 27% to roughly $1.19 billion in 2016 while gross margin fell again to about 39% — both lines down in the same year, printed in the 2016 annual report, a full year before the 2017 trough. So the practical rule is a calendar rule: the moment you identify share being bought with price, write down one question and check it at the next annual report — did the volume arrive? A discount that buys volume is a strategy you can still argue with. A discount that buys nothing is a product losing relevance for reasons price cannot fix, and exactly one year of data separates the two.

如果不想等到 2017 年毛利率見底才發現問題，讀者最早能在哪一年看出來？答案是 2015 年，不是 2017 年。2015 年 GoPro 的毛利率已經從約 45% 滑到約 42%——但營收還在猛漲，從約 13.9 億美元漲到約 16.2 億美元。這才是「折扣有效」的樣子：你讓出毛利，銷量真的進來了。隔一年，這筆交易就不划算了：2016 年營收掉了約 27%、剩下約 11.9 億美元，毛利率又掉到約 39%——兩條線在同一年一起往下，而且就印在 2016 年的年報裡，比 2017 年那個谷底早了整整一年。所以這裡的實用規則是一條「行事曆規則」：一旦你判斷某家公司在用降價買份額，就寫下一個問題，等下一份年報出來時去對答案——銷量到底有沒有進來？買到銷量的折扣，是一個還可以拿出來討論的策略；什麼都沒買到的折扣，代表這個產品正在因為降價救不了的理由失去吸引力。分辨這兩者，只需要一年的資料。

Three boundary conditions decide whether that read is right, and all three are checkable before you conclude anything. First, was it the industry or the company? If every close peer also reported falling revenue that year, you are reading a cycle, not a moat — which is why the next section makes lining up the rival's numbers a required step rather than an optional one. Second, is the revenue decline deliberate? A company walking away from a low-margin product line or a low-margin customer will also show revenue falling — but its margin should go UP, not down. Revenue down with margin up is the fourth combination described below, and it means close to the opposite thing. Third, is the currency doing the work? For a company that sells mostly abroad, a strong home currency can pull reported revenue down in a year when unit volume actually grew; check the constant-currency revenue growth or the units-shipped disclosure before reading a revenue decline as lost customers. Miss any one of these three and the same pair of falling lines will lead you to the wrong conclusion.

這個判讀對不對，由三個邊界條件決定，而且三個都能在下結論之前先查。第一，掉的是這家公司，還是整個產業？如果同期每一家直接同業的營收也在掉，你讀到的是景氣循環，不是護城河——這正是為什麼下一節要把「把對手的數字排出來」列為必做步驟，而不是選配。第二，營收下滑是不是刻意的？一家公司主動退出低毛利產品線或低毛利客戶，營收同樣會掉——但它的毛利率應該往上，不是往下。營收降、毛利升，是下面要講的第四種組合，意思幾乎是相反的。第三，是不是匯率在作怪？一家主要在海外銷售的公司，在本國貨幣走強的年份，銷量其實在成長，帳面營收卻可能是掉的；把營收下滑讀成流失客戶之前，先看一下以固定匯率計算的營收成長率，或是出貨量的揭露數字。這三個條件只要漏掉一個，同樣兩條往下的線就會把你帶到錯誤的結論。

There is a fourth combination, and it is the one most often mis-read as bad news: share falling while price and gross margin hold or rise. That is the fingerprint of a company deliberately walking away from business it cannot price properly — the discount tender, the customer who will switch for two percent — and keeping the part of the market that pays for what it does well. Apple has run a version of this in smartphones for more than a decade: its share of global handset units has mostly sat somewhere in the high teens to low twenties, far behind the Android makers on volume, while most industry estimates put its share of the whole industry's operating profit somewhere in the region of two-thirds to four-fifths. Unit share fell for years as cheap phones flooded the low end; profit share did not follow it down. A moat getting narrower but deeper is a completely different situation from a moat being taken away, and the two are indistinguishable if share is the only line you track. The check is simple: when share falls, ask what happened to gross margin and to the profit pool share in the same period — if both held, the company chose this.

還有第四種組合，而它最常被誤讀成壞消息：份額下滑，但價格和毛利率守住甚至上升。這是一家公司主動放掉「它沒辦法好好定價的生意」的指紋——那些殺價的標案、那些為了 2% 差價就跳槽的客戶——只留下願意為它的長處付錢的那一塊市場。蘋果在智慧型手機上已經這樣做了十幾年：它在全球手機出貨量的份額，多數時候落在 15% 到 20% 出頭之間，出貨量遠遠落後安卓陣營的廠商，但多數產業研究機構的估計都認為，它拿走了整個產業約六到八成的營業利益。低價手機淹沒入門市場的那幾年，蘋果的出貨份額確實在掉，利潤份額卻沒有跟著掉。「護城河變窄但變深」和「護城河正在被搶走」是完全不同的兩件事，而如果你只追蹤份額這一條線，這兩者長得一模一樣。檢查方式很簡單：份額下滑時，去看同一段期間毛利率和利潤份額怎麼走——如果兩者都守住了，那是這家公司自己選的。

The fourth combination has its own failure mode, and it needs naming because "we chose to walk away from that business" is the easiest sentence a management team can say. Two checks separate a deliberate retreat from a slow eviction. First, read operating profit in absolute dollars, not as a percentage. A company defending a premium niche should still be growing the profit it keeps; a company being pushed up-market shows a rising margin percentage sitting on a shrinking profit number — the percentage improves precisely because the part that disappeared was the low-margin part. Second, ask where the abandoned volume went. If a competitor is using that volume to fund better products and is visibly climbing toward your segment, today's clean margin is an early stage of losing the whole market rather than evidence of discipline. Both checks use the same public numbers as the rest of this lesson; the only difference is refusing to accept a percentage on its own.

第四種組合也有它自己的失敗模式，必須點名，因為「那塊生意是主動放掉的」是管理層最容易講出口的一句話。用兩個檢查就能分辨「主動撤退」和「被慢慢趕出去」。第一，看營業利益的絕對金額，不要只看百分比。真正守住高階市場的公司，留下來的那塊獲利金額應該還在成長；被往高階擠壓的公司，則會出現「毛利率往上、獲利金額往下」——百分比之所以變好，正是因為消失的那一塊本來就是低毛利的那一塊。第二，問放掉的銷量跑去哪裡了。如果是某個競爭對手接走了這些量，拿去養出更好的產品，而且看得出來正一步步往你的區間爬上來，那今天這條漂亮的毛利率，其實是失去整個市場的早期階段，不是紀律的證據。這兩個檢查用的都是這一課一路在用的公開數字，唯一的差別是：不接受「只看百分比」的說法。

Takeaway重點

Share up + price/margin up = real strength. Share up + price/margin down = rented, not earned — unless there is a named mechanism that will flip it later. Share down + price/margin up = a narrower but deeper moat, often a deliberate choice. Share on its own tells you nothing.份額升＋價格或毛利率升＝真實力。份額升＋價格或毛利率降＝租來的份額，不是贏來的——除非有一個講得出名字的機制，之後能把它翻轉回來。份額降＋價格或毛利率升＝護城河變窄但變深，通常是主動的選擇。份額這條線單獨看，什麼都說明不了。

Read the Rival's Income Statement Too看對手的損益表

Here is the counterintuitive part. Most people treat "the whole industry is making a lot of money" as reassuring — everyone is doing well, so this must be a good place to be. It should make you more suspicious, not less. A moat is, by definition, a company-specific advantage that its closest rivals cannot copy. If a company's two or three closest competitors are earning similarly high ROIC in the same period, that high return is much more likely a feature of the industry — a temporary supply shortage, a capex supercycle, a favourable regulatory window, a one-off demand surge — than a feature of any single company's competitive position. Genuine moat evidence looks like a gap: the company earns durably high returns while its capable, well-funded rivals with similar assets and similar customers cannot replicate it, quarter after quarter, cycle after cycle.

這裡是最反直覺的一段。多數人看到「整個產業都很賺錢」會覺得安心——大家都過得不錯，這裡應該是個好地方。但這其實應該讓你更提高警覺，而不是放心。護城河的定義，本來就是「競爭對手學不來」的個別優勢。如果一家公司最直接的兩三個競爭對手，在同一段期間也賺到類似的高 ROIC，那麼這個高報酬更可能是產業本身的特徵——暫時性的供給短缺、資本支出超級週期、有利的監管窗口、一次性的需求爆發——而不是任何單一公司競爭地位的特徵。真正的護城河證據，看起來應該是一道落差：公司持續賺到高報酬，而條件相似、資金同樣充裕、客戶群也相似的競爭對手卻學不來，一季又一季、一個循環又一個循環都是如此。

The evidence to pull: line up the two or three closest competitors' ROIC, operating margin, and gross margin next to the company's own, for the same years. Three patterns to look for. First, the company is consistently well ahead of capable rivals with similar assets — this is the actual signature of a moat. Second, everyone in the group moves up and down together, roughly in sync — this is an industry cycle or tailwind, and it tells you almost nothing about any single company's durability. Third, the company is consistently ahead of a group that is itself structurally weak (undercapitalised, poorly run, or locked out of the best customers for reasons unrelated to the leader's product) — this is a real edge, but a narrower one than it looks, because it may only hold against weak rivals, not against a well-funded new entrant.

要拉出來看的證據：把最直接的兩三個競爭對手的 ROIC、營益率、毛利率，和公司自己的數字排在同一段年份旁邊比較。要留意三種型態。第一種，公司持續大幅領先資產條件相似的競爭對手——這是護城河真正的特徵。第二種，整組公司大致同步一起升一起降——這是產業循環或順風，幾乎說明不了任何單一公司的持續性。第三種，公司持續領先一群本身體質就比較弱的對手（資金不足、經營不善、或因為與領先者產品無關的原因被排除在最好的客戶之外）——這也是真的優勢，但比看起來要窄，因為它可能只在對付弱對手時有效，換成資金充裕的新進入者未必成立。

The false signal to watch for is the mirror image of the standard reading: analysts and management teams routinely present "the whole sector is thriving" as evidence that the leading name's moat is proven, when it is usually the opposite — proof that the return is available to whoever is in the industry right now, and will very likely compress for everyone once the cycle turns or new capacity arrives.

這裡要提防的假訊號，剛好是常見解讀的反面。分析師和管理層很常把「整個產業都很興旺」拿來證明龍頭的護城河已經確立，但真相通常相反：這件事證明的是「現在只要待在這個產業，誰都拿得到這個報酬率」。等到景氣反轉，或是新產能開出來，這個報酬率很可能每一家一起被壓縮。

A real pair of numbers makes this concrete, and shows why the timing of the comparison decides the answer. Take the two largest listed pure-play chip foundries, TSMC and UMC: same industry, overlapping customers, the same demand cycle, and both publishing gross margin every quarter. 如果只看 2022 那一欄，聯電看起來就像一家剛剛證明了自己有護城河的公司：毛利率兩年內拉高超過 20 個百分點、資本報酬率創新高，當時也不乏「成熟製程產能已經被結構性重新定價」的說法。但把同一家公司放在同業旁邊比較，故事就整個反過來。真正帶著資訊的數字是「落差」，而這個落差最窄的時候，恰好就是景氣循環的最高點——缺貨那年只剩 15 個百分點，而前一個平常年份大約有 31 個百分點。

That is the counterintuitive mechanism worth carrying away: a shortage lifts the weakest capable producer the most, because when everything is sold out even ordinary capacity gets priced as if it were scarce. The boom is therefore the worst moment to measure a moat — it compresses the very gap you are trying to read. When the cycle turned, UMC gave back roughly 12 points of gross margin while TSMC gave back about 4, and the gap widened back toward its structural level. The reason is nameable and checkable, which is what separates a moat conclusion from a story: on leading-edge nodes a customer has few or no alternatives, so price is set by what the technology is worth to that customer; on mature nodes several fabs can make the same wafer, so price is set by whoever has idle capacity that quarter.

這裡有一個值得帶走的反直覺機制：缺貨對「體質較弱但還能量產」的廠商拉抬幅度最大，因為當所有產能都賣光時，連普通的產能都會被當成稀缺品定價。所以景氣高峰其實是測量護城河最糟的時點——它會把你想量的那道落差壓扁。等到循環反轉，聯電毛利率吐回約 12 個百分點，台積電只吐回約 4 個百分點，落差又拉回接近它的結構水準。而這個落差之所以存在，理由是講得出名字、也查得到的，這正是「護城河的結論」和「一個故事」的分野：在先進製程上，客戶幾乎找不到替代選擇，價格由這項技術對客戶值多少錢決定；在成熟製程上，好幾家廠都能做同一片晶圓，價格由那一季誰手上有閒置產能決定。

The practical rule that follows: measure the peer gap in the worst year of the cycle, not the best. If you can only get three years of data, make sure one of them is a bad one — a comparison built entirely out of good years will systematically overstate the weaker company and understate the difference between them.

由此得到一條可以直接用的規則：要在景氣最差的那一年量同業落差，不是在最好的那一年量。如果你手上只拿得到三年資料，至少確保其中一年是壞年冬——完全用好年份拼出來的比較，會系統性地高估體質較弱的那一家，也會系統性地低估兩家之間真正的差距。

The TSMC/UMC pair shows the gap narrowing in a good year. It is just as instructive to see a real gap widen in a bad one, which is the pattern that actually confirms a moat rather than merely warning against a boom-year read. O'Reilly Automotive and Advance Auto Parts are two of the largest auto-parts retail chains in the US, selling largely the same categories to overlapping customers. In fiscal 2021 — a strong year for the industry, as pandemic-era used-car demand pushed more people to maintain older vehicles — O'Reilly's operating margin ran about 22% against Advance Auto Parts' about 7%, a gap of roughly 15 points. By fiscal 2023, as freight and labor costs stayed elevated and consumers pulled back on discretionary auto spending, O'Reilly's margin held at about 20% while Advance Auto Parts' collapsed to well under 1%, according to both companies' 10-K filings — the gap had widened to roughly 20 points. The two companies sell into the same industry and faced the same cost pressures; one absorbed them and one did not, and the gap grew precisely when conditions got harder, which is the opposite of what the TSMC/UMC pattern showed and exactly what a real, defended edge should look like.

台積電和聯電這組例子，示範的是落差在好年冬變窄。同樣有教育意義的是看一個真實的落差在壞年冬變寬——這個型態才是真正確認護城河存在，而不只是提醒你別在景氣高點下判斷。O'Reilly Automotive 和 Advance Auto Parts 是美國規模數一數二的汽車零件連鎖零售商，賣的品類大致相同、客戶群也重疊。2021 財年——對這個產業算是好年冬，疫情期間二手車需求上升，讓更多人選擇維修保養舊車——O'Reilly 的營業利益率約 22%，Advance Auto Parts 約 7%，落差約 15 個百分點。到了 2023 財年，運費和人力成本持續維持高檔、消費者又縮減非必要的汽車支出，根據兩家公司的 10-K 年報，O'Reilly 的利益率守在約 20%，Advance Auto Parts 卻崩到遠低於 1%——落差擴大到約 20 個百分點。這兩家公司身處同一個產業、面對同樣的成本壓力，一家扛住了、一家沒扛住，而且落差正好在情況變難的時候變大，這和台積電／聯電那組例子呈現的型態正好相反，也正是一個真實、守得住的優勢該有的樣子。

Takeaway重點

The moat is not in either company's margin — it is in the gap between them, measured in a bad year. Everyone looks defensible at the top of a shortage.護城河不在任何一家公司的毛利率裡，而在兩家之間的落差裡，而且要在壞年冬量。在缺貨的高峰，每一家看起來都很有護城河。

◆ War Story 戰爭故事

Warren Buffett and USAir, 1989. In 1989, Berkshire Hathaway put about $358 million into a convertible preferred stake in USAir — at the time a leading US domestic carrier, with a strong regional route network that looked, on paper, like a defensible position. It was not. The US airline industry had (and largely still has) a structural problem no single carrier's route map can fix: seats are close to a commodity, capacity is easy to add back once demand returns, and whenever one airline cuts fares to fill planes, rivals must match or lose volume — a dynamic that keeps returns pinned near or below the cost of capital across the whole industry, almost regardless of how well any one airline is run. Buffett later summarised the industry-level evidence he had failed to weigh: from the Wright brothers to the end of the twentieth century, the cumulative net profit of all US airlines added together was, in his words, roughly zero. USAir suspended the dividend on Berkshire's preferred in 1994, and Berkshire wrote the holding down to about $90 million — around a quarter of what it paid. What rescued the position was nothing Buffett had identified in advance: an industry-wide fare recovery in the mid-1990s revived the airline, the accumulated dividends were eventually paid, and the preferred was redeemed in 1998. He has been blunt that the outcome was luck rather than judgement, and has repeatedly cited the episode — half-jokingly proposing a hotline for investors tempted to buy airline stocks — as a reminder that revenue growth and industry prominence are not evidence of a moat when the whole industry's return on capital tells a different story.

巴菲特與全美航空（USAir），1989 年。1989 年，波克夏投入約 3.58 億美元買下全美航空的可轉換特別股——當時是美國國內主要航空公司之一，擁有強大的區域航線網路，紙上看起來像是一個守得住的位置。事實並非如此。美國航空業當時有一個結構性問題，而且沒有任何一家航空公司的航線圖解得開（這個問題到今天很大程度上仍然存在）。座位近乎是一種商品。需求一回升，產能就很容易補回來。只要有一家航空公司降價把飛機填滿，其他家就必須跟進，不然就會流失載客量。這個動態幾乎不管哪一家經營得多好，都會把整個產業的資本報酬率，壓在資金成本附近或以下。巴菲特後來用一句話總結了他當時漏掉的產業層級證據：從萊特兄弟到二十世紀結束，全美國所有航空公司加總起來的累計淨利，用他的話說大約是零。全美航空在 1994 年暫停支付波克夏這批特別股的股息，波克夏把這筆投資減記到約 9000 萬美元，只剩當初投入金額的四分之一左右。最後救回這個部位的，不是任何他事前看出來的東西：1990 年代中期整個產業的票價反彈救活了這家航空公司，積欠的股息陸續補付，特別股在 1998 年被贖回。巴菲特自己講得很直接——這個結局靠的是運氣，不是判斷。他多次提起這件事，甚至半開玩笑地建議該有一支專線電話，勸阻想買航空股的投資人，提醒大家：當整個產業的資本報酬率說的是另一個故事時，營收成長和產業知名度並不能證明護城河存在。

The public-data signal was not hidden in 1989 — it just required looking at the industry rather than the single carrier. The route network and market position that made USAir look defensible were company-specific facts; the near-zero cumulative industry profit Buffett later cited was an industry-wide fact, sitting in every US carrier's own reported results going back decades, available to anyone who compared them the way this lesson's "read the rival's P&L" section describes, before making the investment rather than after. The boundary worth naming is that this is not an eternal law of the airline business: the wave of US airline mergers between roughly 2008 and 2016 that produced four large network carriers controlling most of the domestic market did coincide with a period of materially better and steadier industry returns on capital than the fragmented, hyper-competitive market Buffett bought into in 1989. Fewer, larger competitors with more capacity discipline can weaken the exact commodity-competition mechanism that broke USAir — which is itself a lesson in capital-cycle thinking (Lesson 6): the same structural diagnosis can flip when the number of competitors changes, and a stale read of "airlines never earn their cost of capital" would have been wrong to hold onto forever.

1989 年當時，這個公開資料訊號其實並沒有被藏起來——只是需要看的是整個產業，不是單一一家航空公司。讓全美航空看起來守得住的航線網路和市場地位，是這家公司自己的事實；巴菲特後來引用的、整個產業幾乎為零的累計獲利，則是整個產業層級的事實，早就攤在每一家美國航空公司自己公布的財報裡，回溯好幾十年，任何人只要照這一課「看對手的損益表」這節教的方法去比較，在投資之前、不是之後，就能看到。這裡也有一個值得點名的邊界：這不是航空業一條永恆不變的鐵律。大約 2008 年到 2016 年間，美國航空業經歷了一波併購潮，最後形成四大網路型航空公司主導多數國內市場，這段期間整個產業的資本報酬率，確實比巴菲特 1989 年買進時那個分散、過度競爭的市場，明顯更好也更穩定。競爭者數量變少、規模變大，紀律性地控制運力，可以削弱正是當年打垮全美航空的那個商品化競爭機制——這本身就是資本週期思維（第 6 課）的一個教材：同一個結構性診斷，會在競爭者數量改變時翻轉，如果一直死抱著「航空業永遠賺不到資金成本」這句話不放，遲早也會錯。

The Revenue-Minus-Operating-Income Gap營收成長減營益成長的落差

Lesson 2 introduced reading the income statement for signs a headline number is misleading. Here is the specific version worth turning into a habit for moat evidence: compare revenue growth year-over-year against operating income growth year-over-year, and treat the difference as a single diagnostic — the revenue-minus-operating-income gapRevenue growth rate minus operating income growth rate, in percentage points. Positive and widening means each new dollar of sales is costing more to win; negative means margin is expanding as the company grows.. When operating income grows slower than revenue, the gap is telling you margins are compressing — the company is having to spend more, discount more, or absorb higher costs for every extra dollar of sales. As a rough working scale: a gap under roughly 3 percentage points is normal noise; 3 to 7 points is worth a specific explanation; above 7 points, in most industries, something structural is going on with cost or pricing, and it deserves to be treated as a warning rather than filed away.

第 2 課教過怎麼從損益表看出頭條數字可能有誤導性。這裡是值得養成習慣、專門拿來當護城河證據用的具體版本：把營收年增率和營業利益年增率放在一起比較，兩者相減就是一個單獨的診斷指標——營收減營益落差營收年增率減去營業利益年增率，單位是百分點。落差為正且持續擴大，代表每一塊新增營收要花更多成本才拿得到；落差為負，代表公司一邊成長、毛利一邊在擴張。。當營業利益成長得比營收慢，這個落差就是在告訴你毛利正在被壓縮——公司每多賺一塊錢營收，就得多花錢、多打折、或吸收更多成本。粗略的判斷區間：落差在大約 3 個百分點以內算正常雜訊；3 到 7 個百分點值得找出具體原因；超過 7 個百分點，多數產業裡通常代表成本或定價出現了結構性的問題，該當成警訊處理，不該就這樣放過。

The mechanism worth remembering: revenue growth alone is a demand story, not a moat story — a company can grow revenue fast while its competitive position quietly deteriorates, if every dollar of growth is coming cheaper and cheaper. A moat that is actually intact should let operating income grow at least as fast as revenue over time, because the company is not having to give up economics to keep growing. A widening negative gap, sustained over several quarters rather than one, is one of the more reliable early-warning reads available from public numbers — often visible well before it shows up in the ROIC trend, because ROIC is a slower-moving, more averaged number.

值得記住的機制是：單看營收成長只是一個需求的故事，不是護城河的故事——如果每一塊新增的營收都是用越來越便宜的代價換來的，公司完全可能一邊營收快速成長、一邊競爭地位悄悄惡化。真正還在的護城河，長期下來應該讓營業利益至少跟得上營收的成長速度，因為公司不需要犧牲經濟效益來維持成長。持續好幾季（不是單一季）擴大的負向落差，是公開數字裡相對可靠的早期警訊之一——而且通常比 ROIC 趨勢更早出現，因為 ROIC 是一個變動比較慢、比較平均化的數字。

What it looks like in practice: a company grows revenue 20% and operating income 21% — the gap is negative, margin is expanding, and whatever it is doing to grow is not costing it economics. The following year it grows revenue 20% again, but operating income only 8% — a 12-point gap. Nothing in the revenue headline changed; the entire change is in what each dollar of that revenue now costs to win, and that is precisely the thing a moat is supposed to control. The false signal to guard against runs the other way: a gap this wide can also be a deliberate, bounded investment cycle — a retailer in the middle of a store-opening programme, a software company hiring sales staff a year ahead of a product launch — where the spending is a choice and the payoff is meant to arrive later. Three questions separate the two: did management name the spend in advance, is it bounded in time, and did the gap actually close on the schedule they gave? A gap that keeps widening past the promised date, with a fresh explanation each quarter, is margin loss being described as investment.

實際看起來是這樣：一家公司營收成長 20%、營業利益成長 21%——落差是負的，代表毛利在擴張，它為了成長所做的事並沒有犧牲經濟效益。隔年營收一樣成長 20%，營業利益卻只成長 8%——落差 12 個百分點。營收的頭條數字完全沒變，變的全部是「現在每拿到一塊錢營收要付出多少代價」，而這正好就是護城河應該替你控制住的東西。這裡要提防的假訊號往反方向走：這麼大的落差也可能是一段刻意的、有期限的投資期——一家零售商正在展店、一家軟體公司提前一年擴編業務團隊準備新產品上市——這種支出是選擇，回報本來就設定在後面才出現。用三個問題可以分開這兩者：這筆支出管理層事前有沒有講明白？有沒有明確的期限？落差有沒有照他們給的時間表收斂回去？如果過了承諾的時間點落差還在擴大，而且每一季都有一個新版本的解釋，那就是毛利流失被講成了投資。

The gap read in the other direction is just as informative, and gets mis-read as good news far more often. When operating income grows much faster than revenue — say revenue up 4% and operating income up 25% — that can be operating leverage on a largely fixed cost base, which is genuinely what a moat business looks like as it scales. It can also be harvesting: cutting research, marketing, maintenance capital spending or headcount to manufacture this year's margin out of next decade's competitiveness. The two are almost indistinguishable for about two years, then diverge permanently. The check is to look at what fell in absolute dollars, not as a percentage of sales: if research and selling costs shrank outright while revenue grew, this year's profit came out of the future, and the moat evidence points down even though the margin line points up.

這個落差往反方向讀同樣有資訊量，而且更常被誤當成好消息。當營業利益成長遠快於營收——例如營收只成長 4%、營業利益卻成長 25%——這可能是固定成本結構下的營運槓桿，一家有護城河的公司在放大規模時本來就長這樣。但它也可能是在「收割」：砍研發、砍行銷、砍維護型資本支出、砍人力，把下一個十年的競爭力做成今年的毛利率。這兩件事在頭兩年幾乎分不出來，之後就永久分岔。檢查方法是去看「絕對金額」掉了什麼，而不是看佔營收的百分比：如果營收在成長，研發費用和銷售費用的絕對金額卻是縮的，那今年的獲利是從未來挪過來的——毛利率那條線往上，護城河的證據其實往下。

A real example shows just how wide this gap can get without the revenue line giving any warning at all. Meta Platforms, the social-media company, reported revenue of about $117.9 billion in 2021 and about $116.6 billion in 2022 — a decline of roughly 1%, barely visible on a headline chart. Operating income over the same two years fell from about $46.8 billion to about $28.9 billion, a decline of roughly 38%, according to its 10-K filings — a gap of well over 35 percentage points between the two growth rates in a single year. The revenue line alone would have told a reader almost nothing was wrong; the operating-income line, and the gap between the two, told the real story months before the company itself announced a formal cost-cutting push the following year. This is close to the largest, cleanest version of the diagnostic this section describes: a nearly flat top line hiding a collapsing profit engine underneath it.

有一個真實案例，示範了這個落差可以大到什麼程度，而營收這條線本身完全不會發出任何警訊。社群媒體公司 Meta Platforms，2021 年營收約 1179 億美元，2022 年約 1166 億美元——只跌了約 1%，光看頭條圖表幾乎看不出異狀。但根據其 10-K 年報，同一段兩年期間，營業利益從約 468 億美元掉到約 289 億美元，跌了約 38%——單一年之內，兩個成長率之間的落差超過 35 個百分點。單看營收這條線，讀者幾乎看不出任何問題；營業利益這條線、以及兩者之間的落差，卻早在這家公司隔年正式宣布大規模撙節措施的好幾個月前，就已經講出了真正的故事。這幾乎是這一節在講的診斷方法裡，最極端、也最乾淨的一個版本：幾乎持平的頭條營收數字，底下藏著一台正在崩潰的獲利引擎。

Takeaway重點

Revenue growth is a demand fact. The gap between revenue growth and operating income growth is the moat fact — in both directions, and it moves earlier than ROIC does.營收成長講的是需求。營收成長和營益成長之間的落差，講的才是護城河——而且兩個方向都要讀，它比 ROIC 更早動。

A Moat With Nowhere to Reinvest, and the Peer-Premium Stress Test沒地方投的護城河，以及同業溢價壓力測試

A high, stable ROIC proves the moat exists. It does not automatically mean the company is a compounding machine — that depends on whether it has anywhere left to put new capital at anything close to that same return. A company earning 28% ROIC that reinvests almost nothing, funds no new capacity or acquisitions, and returns nearly all its free cash flow through buybacks and dividends is a cash cowA business with a real, defended high return but limited room to redeploy new capital at that same rate — value comes from the cash it distributes, not from compounding growth., not a compounder — the moat is real, but the growth engine has stalled. That is not a bad outcome for an investor (a well-run cash return machine can be a perfectly good holding), but it is a different kind of holding than a business that can keep reinvesting at 28% for another decade, and the two should never be valued the same way.

高而穩定的 ROIC，證明了護城河存在。但這不代表這家公司就是一台複利機器——那要看它還有沒有地方，能把新增的資本投入到接近同樣報酬率的地方。一家 ROIC 28% 的公司，如果幾乎不再投資、不擴產也不併購、幾乎把所有自由現金流都拿去回購和發股利，這是一台現金牛生意本身有真實、守得住的高報酬，但幾乎沒有空間把新增資本用同樣的報酬率再投入——價值來自它發放出來的現金，不是來自複利成長。，不是複利機——護城河是真的，但成長引擎已經停了。對投資人來說這不算壞結果（一台經營良好的現金回饋機器，完全可以是一個好的持股），但它和一家還能用 28% 報酬率再投資十年的公司，是不同種類的持股，這兩者不該用同一套方式估值。

See's Candies, from the opening of this lesson, is the cleanest illustration of both halves of that sentence at once. Between the 1972 purchase and 2007, See's pretax profit grew from just under $5 million to about $82 million, while the capital needed to run the business rose only from about $8 million to about $40 million — roughly $32 million of cumulative reinvestment over thirty-five years, against something on the order of $1.35 billion of cumulative pretax earnings, nearly all of which was sent to the parent company to be deployed elsewhere. The moat was never in question: the business raised price ahead of inflation for three and a half decades. But it could not absorb capital — there was no way to put another few hundred million dollars into boxed chocolate at anything close to 60%. That is exactly why it was a superb thing to own and a hopeless thing to try to grow, and why the same evidence supports two very different conclusions depending on which question you were asking.

這一課開頭的喜詩糖果，正好同時示範了這句話的兩半。從 1972 年買下到 2007 年，喜詩的稅前獲利從略低於 500 萬美元成長到約 8200 萬美元，而經營這門生意所需要的資本只從約 800 萬美元增加到約 4000 萬美元——三十五年間累計再投入的資本大約只有 3200 萬美元，對照的是累計約 13.5 億美元的稅前獲利，而這些錢幾乎全部被送回母公司、拿去投到別的地方。護城河從來沒有被質疑過：這門生意連續三十五年把價格漲得比通膨快。但它吃不下資本——你沒辦法再把好幾億美元投進盒裝巧克力，還維持接近 60% 的報酬率。這正是為什麼它是一門極好的生意，卻是一門幾乎沒辦法再長大的生意；也說明了同一批證據，會因為你問的問題不同，導向兩個很不一樣的結論。

A modern, public-market version of the same pattern shows up at Moody's Corporation, the credit-rating and analytics company. In fiscal 2025, Moody's reported operating cash flow of about $2.9 billion and net income of about $2.5 billion, against capital expenditures of only about $326 million — roughly 11% of operating cash flow, according to its filings. The rest, including about $1.6 billion spent on share buybacks that year, went back out to shareholders rather than into the business, because there is no way to usefully spend several times more on the physical infrastructure a ratings and analytics franchise actually needs. The lesson is the same as See's Candies fifty years earlier, just with modern numbers: a moat this good at converting revenue into cash does not automatically need — or deserve to be valued as if it needs — a large reinvestment runway to be a good business to own.

同一種模式，在現代的公開市場上也有一個對照版本：信用評等與分析公司穆迪（Moody's Corporation）。根據其財報，2025 會計年度，穆迪的營運現金流約 29 億美元、淨利約 25 億美元，而資本支出只有約 3.26 億美元——大約只佔營運現金流的 11%。剩下的錢，包括那一年花在庫藏股買回上的約 16 億美元，都回到了股東手上，而不是投進公司本身——因為一家評等與分析業務，本來就沒有地方能有效花掉多好幾倍的實體資本支出。這個道理，和五十年前的喜詩糖果一模一樣，只是換成了現代的數字：一門把營收轉換成現金的能力這麼強的護城河生意，不代表、也不該被估值成需要一大條再投資跑道才算好生意。

The last piece of evidence connects moat back to price, which is where this lesson stops and Lesson 20 (valuation) properly begins: if a company trades at a price-to-earnings ratio more than roughly 50% above the median of its closest peers, that premium needs a specific, checkable reason — not "the market likes it" or "it has a strong brand." Run the peer-premium stress testWhen a company's P/E sits more than roughly 50% above the median of its closest peers, forcing yourself to name the specific evidence — return level, stability, pricing power, share trend — that would justify a premium of that size, before accepting it. by asking whether the ROIC level and stability, the pricing-power evidence, and the share/price trend actually support a premium of that size, or whether the premium is simply priced-in optimism with no fresh evidence behind it. This is also the plainest illustration of why a good business is not automatically a good stock: proving the moat is real answers only "does this company deserve to earn more than its cost of capital." It says nothing about whether today's price already assumes an even wider or longer-lasting moat than the evidence actually supports — which is a separate, and just as important, question.

最後一塊證據，把護城河接回價格。這一課到這裡就停，第 20 課「估值」才真正接手。規則是：如果一家公司的本益比，比最直接同業的中位數高出約 50% 以上，這個溢價就需要一個具體、可以查證的理由，不能只是「市場喜歡它」或「品牌很強」。同業溢價壓力測試當一家公司的本益比高出最直接同業中位數約 50% 以上時，強迫自己在接受這個溢價之前，先講出具體證據——報酬水準、穩定度、定價權、份額趨勢——足不足以支撐這麼大的溢價。要問的是：ROIC 的水準與穩定度、定價權的證據、份額與價格的趨勢，撐不撐得起這麼大的溢價？還是溢價只是市場先把樂觀定價進去，背後其實沒有新證據？這也是「好生意不等於好股票」最直白的示範。證明護城河是真的，只回答了一個問題：這家公司該不該賺得比資金成本多。它沒有回答另一個問題：今天的價格，是不是已經假設了一條比證據更寬、更持久的護城河？那是另一個同樣重要、但不一樣的問題。

One boundary condition worth stating plainly: this ROIC-and-margin toolkit does not work well on companies that are not yet profitable (a young high-growth company can have a real moat forming years before ROIC turns positive), on financial companies such as banks and insurers (where invested capital and leverage are defined differently and ROIC is not the right lens — a separate returns-on-equity approach applies), or on heavily regulated utilities whose allowed return is set by a regulator rather than by competitive dynamics, so stability may reflect regulation rather than a moat. In all three cases, a single year's ROIC read is close to meaningless, and a different toolkit is needed before drawing any conclusion.

有一個邊界條件值得明講：這套 ROIC 與毛利率工具，有三種公司不能直接套。第一種是還沒獲利的公司。一家年輕的高成長公司，護城河可能早在 ROIC 轉正之前好幾年就已經在成形，這時候 ROIC 看不出東西。第二種是銀行、保險這類金融公司。它們的投入資本和槓桿，定義方式和一般公司不一樣，ROIC 不是對的鏡頭，要換一套以股東權益報酬率為主的方法。第三種是受到高度監管的公用事業。它們能賺多少，是監管機關訂出來的，不是競爭決定的；所以數字很穩，反映的可能是監管，不是護城河。這三種情況，單看一年的 ROIC 幾乎沒有意義，下判斷之前要先換工具。

Think first: why would a business with a steady 19-20% ROIC every year be more trustworthy than one that swings between 5% and 31%, even if their five-year averages come out almost the same?先想一下：為什麼一家每年都穩定維持 19% 到 20% ROIC 的公司，會比一家在 5% 和 31% 之間大幅擺盪的公司更值得信任，即使兩家的五年平均算出來其實很接近？

A wide swing usually means the return is being set by something outside the company's control — a commodity price, an industry shortage, a one-off contract — that can move against it just as easily as it moved in its favour. A moat's entire purpose is to make returns predictable by removing them from that kind of outside noise. An average built from wild swings gives no confidence about which regime you'll be in next year; a steady number built the same way, year after year, is a much stronger signal that a specific, repeatable mechanism is doing the work.

大幅擺盪通常代表報酬率是被某個公司控制不了的外部因素決定的——原物料價格、產業性缺貨、一次性合約——這些因素往不利方向移動的機率，和往有利方向移動的機率一樣高。護城河的整個目的，就是把報酬率從這種外部雜訊裡抽離出來，讓它變得可預期。用大幅擺盪的數字算出來的平均值，完全不能讓你安心明年會落在哪個區間；反倒是每年都用類似方式算出來的穩定數字，更強烈地說明有一個具體、可重複的機制在發揮作用。

Think first: why is it a warning sign, not a comfort, when every major player in an industry reports high returns at the same time?先想一下：為什麼當一個產業裡所有主要玩家同時都賺到高報酬時，這反而是一個警訊，而不是一個令人安心的訊號？

A moat protects one company from the others. If everyone is earning the same abnormally high return at the same time, none of them is being protected FROM the others — they are all simply standing in the same favourable spot at the same moment, most likely a temporary one (a shortage, a capex cycle, a subsidy). That is not what "protection from competition" means; it is what "good timing" means, and good timing reverses. The useful next question is always what happens to the whole group, together, once the cycle turns.

護城河保護的是一家公司不被其他公司搶走報酬。如果所有人在同一時間都賺到同樣異常高的報酬，那沒有任何一家公司是被「保護、不被其他人搶走」——他們只是剛好同時站在同一個有利的位置上，而這個位置很可能是暫時的（缺貨、資本支出循環、補貼）。這不是「不被競爭侵蝕」的意思，而是「時機好」的意思，而時機好會反轉。真正有用的下一個問題永遠是：一旦景氣循環反轉，整組公司會一起發生什麼事。

⚠
Rising share is not automatically a rising moat. If share is being gained by cutting price, extending payment terms, or spending heavily on promotion, the company is renting customers, not earning them — and the moment the spending stops, so does the share gain.份額上升不代表護城河在變寬。如果份額是靠降價、放寬付款條件、或砸重本促銷換來的，公司其實是在用租的方式留住客戶，不是靠實力贏來的——一旦停止砸錢，份額成長也會跟著停下來。
⚠
Judging a moat from a single year. A demand spike can push almost any company's ROIC up temporarily, even one with no durable competitive advantage at all. A moat has to be visible in both the good years and the bad ones — that is the entire point of checking stability, not just level.只看一年就下結論。需求爆發幾乎能把任何一家公司的 ROIC 暫時推高，就算它根本沒有任何持續的競爭優勢也一樣。護城河必須在好年冬和壞年冬都看得見——這正是為什麼要檢查穩定度，不能只看單一年的水準。

How This Connects接回框架

This lesson depends on being able to read the three statements and compute ROIC in the first place (Lesson 2), and it assumes you already have a hypothesis about which of the five sources — cost advantage, intangible assets, switching costs, network effects, efficient scale — might explain the numbers you are seeing (Lesson 9). It also connects sideways to where the excess return actually sits in the value chain, since a moat that never shows up as captured profit is not much use to a shareholder (Lesson 5).

這一課要用到讀懂三張報表、算出 ROIC 的能力（第 2 課），也假設你已經對「五種來源裡哪一種——成本優勢、無形資產、轉換成本、網路效應、有效規模——可能解釋你看到的數字」有一個假說（第 9 課）。它也和「超額報酬到底落在價值鏈的哪個環節」這個問題橫向相連，因為一個從沒被公司實際拿到手的護城河，對股東來說用處不大（第 5 課）。

What this lesson produces — a level-and-stability read on ROIC, a pricing-power verdict, a share/price trend read — is the raw evidence the next lesson uses to answer a harder question: not just whether the moat is real today, but how many more years it is likely to keep producing returns like this, using the same evidence plus a check on how much of it can still be reinvested (Lesson 11).

這一課產出的東西——ROIC 水準與穩定度的判讀、定價權的裁定、份額與價格趨勢的解讀——正是下一課要拿去回答一個更難問題的原始證據：不只是護城河現在是不是真的，而是它還能再產出類似報酬多少年，用同一批證據，再加上檢查還有多少空間可以再投資（第 11 課）。

Proving a moat is real is only half the job — it tells you the company can earn more than its cost of capital right now, but says nothing about how many more years that can last, or how much of it the market has already paid for. The next lesson takes the same evidence one step further: how long can high returns actually last, and how do you tell a company with a decade of runway left from one whose best years are already behind it.

證明護城河是真的，只完成了一半的工作——它告訴你這家公司現在能賺得比資金成本多，但完全沒說明這個狀態還能再撐幾年，也沒說明市場已經為此付了多少錢。下一課要把同一批證據再往前推一步：高報酬到底還能維持多久，以及怎麼分辨一家還有十年跑道的公司，和一家最好的年冬已經過去的公司。
