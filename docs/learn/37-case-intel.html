The market's reaction was immediate and violent. Intel shares fell about 16% the next trading day, July 24, wiping out roughly $42 billion of market value in a single session — its worst day in years. 市場的反應又快又猛。隔一個交易日、也就是 7 月 24 日，英特爾股價重挫約 16%，一天之內市值蒸發約 420 億美元，是它多年來最慘的一天。長年的對手 AMD，同一則消息卻讓它上漲約 15%，因為 AMD 的晶片本來就是向台積電買產能，英特爾自家製程一落後，好處直接落到 AMD 身上。

That one session is the clearest single data point, five months before Gelsinger's appointment, that professional investors had started pricing Intel's manufacturing leadership as a real risk rather than a hypothetical one. Note what the market did not do: it did not mark the stock down to a distressed price. It repriced one risk, once, and then left Intel trading at a low-but-not-collapsed multiple.

這一個交易日，是 Gelsinger 上任前 5 個月最清楚的訊號：專業投資人已經開始把英特爾的製程領先地位，當成一個真實、而不是假設性的風險在定價。但也要注意市場沒做什麼：它沒有把股價打到破產價。它只是把一個風險重新定價了一次，之後就讓英特爾停在一個「低、但沒崩」的本益比上。

By the freeze date the market read Intel through two competing frames, both built on fully public facts. The bullish frame: the most profitable semiconductor company in history, still growing revenue, still generating tens of billions in free cash flow, at 12 times earnings with a real and rising dividend. If the market is wrong about anything, this frame says, it is wrong about how durable that cash machine is.

到了凍結日，市場用兩套互相競爭的框架在看英特爾，兩套用的都是完全公開的事實。看多的框架是：史上最賺錢的半導體公司，營收還在成長，還在產生數百億美元的自由現金流，本益比只有 12 倍，還發著一筆真實而且持續調高的股利。這套框架認為，如果市場真的看錯了什麼，錯的多半是低估了這台現金機器有多耐久。

The bearish frame reads the same facts differently. For three decades Intel's entire competitive position rested on having the world's best manufacturing process. Now a rival (TSMC) is unambiguously ahead on that exact measure, a direct customer (Apple) has just proven it no longer needs Intel's chips, and a hedge fund (Third Point, in a public letter dated December 29, 2020) is urging the board to consider spinning off manufacturing. The two frames share every fact; they disagree only about which one is load-bearing.

看空的框架讀的是同一批事實，得出不同結論。過去 30 年，英特爾整個競爭地位都建立在「擁有全世界最好的製程」之上。現在，恰好在這一項上，有一個對手（台積電）明顯領先，有一個大客戶（蘋果）剛證明自己不再需要英特爾的晶片，還有一支避險基金（Third Point，2020 年 12 月 29 日的公開信）正在要求董事會考慮把製造部門分拆出去。兩套框架共用每一項事實，分歧只在於哪一項才是撐住結論的那根柱子。

Demand for x86 chips comes from two engines that move differently. The PC engine is replacement-cycle demandDemand that repeats because existing units wear out or get upgraded on a roughly known schedule, rather than because a new category of buyer is showing up for the first time — see lesson 03. A second shift is easier to miss and matters as much. Alongside x86 there is a rival rulebook, ArmA competing instruction set to x86. Arm licenses its designs to anyone, so a company like Apple or Amazon can license Arm and have TSMC build a chip tuned to its own products — without buying anything from Intel., which any company can license and hand to a foundry. That turns Intel's biggest customers into potential competitors, and by the freeze date two of them had crossed the line.

還有第二個轉向，更容易被忽略，但同樣重要。x86 之外還有另一套規則叫 Arm跟 x86 打對台的另一套指令集。Arm 把設計授權給任何人，所以像蘋果或亞馬遜這種公司，可以拿 Arm 授權，再交給台積電生產一顆為自家產品量身訂做的晶片，完全不必跟英特爾買。，任何公司都可以拿授權，再交給代工廠生產。這等於把英特爾最大的客戶變成潛在競爭者，而到凍結日，已經有兩個客戶跨過了這條線。

In November 2020, Apple launched the M1, an Arm chip it designed itself and had TSMC build, and put it in its own laptops in place of the Intel processors it had bought since 2006. Amazon Web Services had done the same on the server side, with its Arm-based Graviton chips. Both are the substitution threat from lesson 07 in its most direct form: not a rival selling against you, but your own customer deciding to make the thing itself. Both were fully public before the freeze date, and both rested on the same fact — a customer could now get a better chip from TSMC than Intel could make.

2020 年 11 月，蘋果推出 M1，一顆自己設計、交給台積電生產的 Arm 晶片，裝進自家筆電，取代它從 2006 年起一路採購的英特爾處理器。亞馬遜的雲端部門 AWS 在伺服器端做了同一件事，用的是自研的 Arm 架構 Graviton 晶片。這兩件事都是第 07 課講的替代威脅最直接的版本：威脅不是對手來搶你的生意，而是你自己的客戶決定自己做。兩件事在凍結日之前都已完全公開，而且靠的是同一個事實——客戶現在可以從台積電拿到一顆比英特爾自己做得更好的晶片。

On the supply side, Intel has run a different model from almost everyone else in the industry for 50 years: IDM, or integrated device manufacturerA company that both designs its own chips and manufactures them in its own factories, as opposed to a “fabless” company (like AMD or Apple) that designs chips but pays a separate foundry to build them. IDM lets design and manufacturing improve together, but forces the company to fund its own factories no matter how the chip business is doing that year. (a company that both designs its chips and manufactures them in its own factories). AMD, by contrast, is fablessA company that designs chips but owns no factories, paying a separate foundry (most often TSMC) to manufacture them — trading manufacturing control for access to whichever fab currently has the best process. (it designs chips but owns no factories) and pays TSMC to build almost everything it sells. Owning your factories used to be a genuine edge: designers and manufacturing engineers sit in the same building tuning chip and process together, which for decades let Intel ship faster chips than anyone building on a rented process. The matching cost is the other half of the same structure — a fabless company can switch to whichever foundry is best this year, while an IDM must fund, staff and fill its own factories every year, whether or not its process is still the best available.

在供給面，英特爾 50 年來一直用一套跟業界幾乎所有人都不一樣的模式：IDM（整合元件製造商）自己設計晶片、也自己在自家工廠生產晶片的公司，相對於「無廠」公司（像 AMD 或蘋果）——只設計晶片、花錢請另一家代工廠生產。IDM 讓設計跟製造能一起優化，但也逼公司不管晶片本業那年好不好，都得自己出錢養工廠。（自己設計晶片、也自己在自家工廠生產的公司）。相對地，AMD 是無廠公司只設計晶片、不擁有工廠的公司，改花錢請另一家代工廠（最常見是台積電）生產——放棄對製造的掌控權，換來可以選當下製程最好的那一家。（只設計晶片、不擁有工廠），幾乎所有產品都花錢請台積電生產。自己蓋工廠，過去確實是真實的優勢：設計工程師跟製造工程師在同一棟樓裡一起調校晶片與製程，這讓英特爾幾十年都能比用租來的製程的對手做出更快的晶片。對稱的代價是同一個結構的另一半——無廠公司可以直接改選當年最好的代工廠；IDM 卻不管自己的製程還是不是業界最好，每年都得自己出錢、找人、把工廠填滿。

Think first: if owning your own factories forces you to pay for them even when your process falls behind, why did this model ever count as a moat?先想一下：如果自己蓋工廠，就算製程落後了也得自己付錢養，這個模式一開始為什麼算是護城河？

Because for most of Intel's history, the two costs of the model — funding your own factories and betting everything on your own process — never actually came due at the same time. As long as Intel's own process stayed ahead of every foundry's, owning the factory converted directly into a faster, cheaper chip that no fabless rival could match, no matter how much cash that rival had. The model only turns from an advantage into a trap the moment the process itself falls behind: at that point, an IDM is still paying the full fixed cost of factories that no longer produce the industry's best chip, while a fabless rival simply rents whichever factory, anywhere in the world, currently does. The moat and the trap are the same structure, viewed from before and after the process leadership is lost.

因為在英特爾大半段歷史裡，這個模式的兩項成本——自己出錢養工廠、把全部籌碼押在自己的製程上——從來沒有同時到期過。只要英特爾自己的製程還領先所有代工廠，擁有工廠就會直接轉換成一顆更快、更便宜的晶片，任何無廠對手不管手上有多少現金都追不上。這個模式從優勢變成陷阱，恰好就發生在製程本身落後的那一刻：這時候，IDM 還在付全額的固定成本，養著一批已經做不出業界最好晶片的工廠，而無廠對手只要改租全世界任何一家、當下最好的工廠就好。護城河跟陷阱，其實是同一個結構，只是從製程領先還在跟已經失去這兩個不同的時間點去看。

⚠ War story: the day Intel walked away from the business that built it 真實案例：英特爾放棄了自己起家的那門生意

Intel was founded in 1968 to make memory chips, not processors, and into the early 1980s memory was still the company's identity. Japanese manufacturers, backed by cheaper capital, spent those years undercutting Intel on price and matching it on quality, and by 1984–1985 Intel was losing money on memory every quarter. In an exchange CEO Andy Grove later made famous, he asked chairman Gordon Moore: if the board fired us and brought in a new CEO, what would that person do? Moore said: get out of memories. In 1985 they did exactly that, laid off about a quarter of the workforce, and moved all of that manufacturing capacity to microprocessors.

英特爾 1968 年創立時做的是記憶體晶片，不是處理器；到 1980 年代初，記憶體仍然是公司的身分認同。那幾年，日本製造商靠著更便宜的資本，價格上壓低英特爾、品質上也追平，到 1984 至 1985 年，英特爾每一季賣記憶體都在虧錢。執行長 Andy Grove 後來讓這段對話廣為人知：他問董事長 Gordon Moore，如果董事會把我們開除、換一個新執行長進來，那個人會怎麼做？Moore 回答：退出記憶體。1985 年他們就這麼做了，裁掉約四分之一的員工，把那些製造產能全部轉向微處理器。

The precedent this creates is narrower than it looks. Intel did not save memory by trying harder inside it; it survived by admitting the business was lost and moving capacity somewhere it could still win. So it is a real precedent for “Intel has reinvented itself before.” It is not a precedent for “Intel can out-execute a process disadvantage while staying in the same business” — the much harder task the 2021 turnaround is actually attempting.

這個先例的適用範圍，比它看起來窄。英特爾當年不是靠在記憶體裡更努力撐下去而活下來的；它是靠承認這門生意已經輸了、把產能整個搬去還打得贏的地方。所以它確實是「英特爾以前也重塑過自己」的真實先例。但它不是「英特爾可以留在同一門生意裡、硬把製程劣勢靠執行力拚回來」的先例——而後面這件事，才是 2021 年這次轉型真正要做的、難得多的任務。

⚠

Pitfall: crediting a company's past reinvention as proof it can repeat one. “This company has come back before” feels like evidence, but one successful pivot decades earlier says almost nothing about the odds of a different, harder pivot today — especially when the earlier success meant leaving a business and today's challenge means winning inside one. Before letting history reassure you, ask what the earlier turnaround actually required, and whether today needs the same thing or something structurally harder.

陷阱：把一家公司過去的成功轉型，當成它這次也做得到的證據。「這家公司以前也從逆境中回來過」聽起來像證據，但幾十年前一次成功的轉向，幾乎說明不了今天這次不同、而且更難的轉向機率有多高——尤其當年的成功是靠「離開一門生意」，現在的挑戰卻是「留在同一門生意裡打贏」。在讓歷史給你信心之前，先問：當年那次轉型實際要求什麼，今天要的是同一件事，還是結構上更難的事。

Part II — The Business: What a Good Business Looks Like Here

第二部練習：生意——好生意在這裡長什麼樣

A moatA structural advantage that lets a company keep earning above-average returns because rivals genuinely cannot copy it easily — not just a temporary lead. (a structural advantage a rival cannot easily copy) usually comes from a short list of sources: cost or process advantages, scale only the largest player can afford, customer lock-in, and brand. At the freeze date Intel had evidence for all four. Process leadership let it ship faster chips at lower cost than rivals for decades; scale let it fund tens of billions of dollars of factories a year, a bar almost no other chip designer could clear alone. Lock-in came from the x86 ecosystem — decades of business software, and the purchasing habits of PC makers like Dell, HP and Lenovo, all built around Intel chips. Brand came from “Intel Inside.”

護城河一種結構性優勢，讓公司能持續賺到高於平均的報酬，因為對手真的沒辦法輕易複製——不是暫時領先而已。（對手沒辦法輕易複製的結構性優勢）通常來自幾個固定來源：成本或製程優勢、只有最大玩家付得起的規模、客戶鎖定，還有品牌。凍結日的英特爾，這 4 項都拿得出證據。製程領先，讓它幾十年來都能用比對手更低的成本做出更快的晶片；規模，讓它每年拿得出數百億美元蓋工廠，這道門檻幾乎沒有別的晶片設計公司單獨跨得過。客戶鎖定來自 x86 生態系——幾十年累積的企業軟體，以及戴爾、惠普、聯想這些個人電腦廠商的採購習慣，全都建立在英特爾晶片上。品牌則來自「Intel Inside」。

Now the mechanism, stated plainly: these are not four independent pillars. Process leadership is the root the other three grow out of. Scale only mattered because Intel's process made factories that size worth funding — once TSMC offers the same or better process to anyone willing to pay, scale stops being exclusive. Lock-in only holds while switching to a faster rival chip costs more than it saves, so once AMD's chips are genuinely faster the maths flips; and brand follows performance with a lag, not the other way round. That is why a moat built this way does not erode one piece at a time: once the root loosens, every branch loosens with it, each on its own delay.

接下來講機制，講白一點：這 4 項不是 4 根各自獨立的柱子。製程領先是另外 3 項長出來的根。規模之所以重要，是因為英特爾的製程讓那個規模的工廠養起來划得來；一旦台積電願意把同等或更好的製程賣給任何肯付錢的人，規模就不再是英特爾獨有。客戶鎖定只在「換去對手的晶片」比「留下來」貴的時候才成立，所以 AMD 的晶片一旦真的比較快，這筆帳就翻過來；品牌則是跟著性能走、而且會落後，不是反過來領先性能。這就是為什麼這種護城河不會一片一片慢慢剝落：根一鬆，每一根枝幹都會跟著鬆，只是各有各的時間差。

So the practical question at the freeze date is whether the root is loosening, and four public numbers answer it. First, the gross-margin path already given: about 62.3% in 2017 down to 56% in 2020, three declines in a row. Second, capital spending: Intel's annual capex rose from about $9.6 billion in 2016 (roughly 16% of revenue) to about $16.2 billion in 2019 (roughly 22%), then eased to $14.3 billion in 2020. Spending a rising share of revenue on factories is normal for a manufacturer defending a lead — but doing it and still ending up a generation behind TSMC is not defense, it is a rising price for a position that is being lost anyway.

所以凍結日真正要問的是：這條根鬆了沒有？有 4 個公開數字可以回答。第一個是前面給過的毛利率路徑：2017 年約 62.3%，掉到 2020 年的 56%，連三年下滑。第二個是資本支出：英特爾每年的資本支出，從 2016 年約 96 億美元（約占營收 16%），升到 2019 年約 162 億美元（約占營收 22%），2020 年再回到 143 億美元。一家製造商為了守住領先，把越來越高比例的營收投進工廠，這很正常；但投得更多，結果還是落後台積電一個世代，那就不是防守，而是為一個反正正在失去的位置，付出越來越高的價錢。

The third number is customer behaviour, already covered: Apple gone, Amazon supplying itself. The fourth is share — AMD's share of server-CPU units, as tracked by Mercury Research, went from about 4.5% in Q4 2019 to about 7.1% in Q4 2020. Seven percent sounds small, and that is the trap: server CPUs are Intel's highest-margin product, so share leaving there costs far more profit than the unit count suggests, and what matters is the direction, not the level.

第三個數字是客戶行為，前面已經講過：蘋果走了，亞馬遜自己供應自己。第四個是市占——根據 Mercury Research 的追蹤，AMD 在伺服器處理器的出貨量市占，從 2019 年第 4 季的約 4.5%，升到 2020 年第 4 季的約 7.1%。7% 聽起來很小，而這正是陷阱：伺服器處理器是英特爾毛利率最高的產品，這裡流失的市占，扣掉的獲利遠比出貨顆數看起來嚴重；要看的是方向，不是水位。

All four are leading indicatorsEvidence that moves before the reported financial statements do — margin trend, capital intensity, customer defections, share shifts — as opposed to revenue and profit, which confirm a change only after it has happened. (evidence that moves before the income statement does). The record 2020 revenue and the 30% operating margin are the lagging ones. That gap — four indicators pointing down while the headline numbers still look excellent — is the whole reason lesson 12 insists on reading trend separately from level.

這 4 個都屬於先行指標比財報數字更早動的證據——利益率趨勢、資本支出強度、客戶出走、市占移動——相對於營收與獲利這類落後指標，後者要等事情已經發生才會確認。（比財報更早動的證據）。2020 年創新高的營收、30% 的營業利益率，則是落後指標。這個落差——4 個指標都在往下，頭條數字卻還很漂亮——正是第 12 課堅持要把趨勢跟等級分開讀的全部理由。

Worked Example (Teaching Case): How Far a Small Margin Slip Travels逐步演算範例（教學案例）：一個小小的毛利率下滑，能傳多遠
  1. 1

    Start from Intel's 2020 numbers: revenue of $77.9 billion, gross margin of about 56%, so gross profit (revenue minus the direct cost of making the chips) is roughly $43.6 billion. Operating margin was about 30%, so operating income (profit after also subtracting R&D, sales, and administrative costs) was roughly $23.4 billion. Subtracting one from the other, those R&D, sales, and administrative costs together came to roughly $20.2 billion — and, importantly, that figure moves slowly year to year, because R&D teams and sales offices are not something a company can shrink overnight.

    從英特爾 2020 年的數字開始：營收 779 億美元，毛利率約 56%，所以毛利（營收扣掉製造晶片的直接成本）約 436 億美元。營業利益率約 30%，所以營業利益（獲利再扣掉研發、銷售、管理費用後）約 234 億美元。兩者相減，研發加銷售管理費用合計約 202 億美元——而且重要的是，這個數字每年變動得很慢，因為研發團隊跟業務據點，不是公司能一夕之間縮小的東西。

  2. 2

    Now run a scenario: revenue stays flat next year, but gross margin drops 5 percentage points, from 56% to 51% — a plausible size of move given the margin had already fallen 3 points in the year just reported. New gross profit is 51% × $77.9 billion ≈ $39.7 billion, a drop of about $3.9 billion.

    現在跑一個情境：明年營收持平，但毛利率掉了 5 個百分點，從 56% 掉到 51%——考慮到剛公布的這一年，毛利率已經掉了 3 個百分點，這是一個合理可能發生的變動幅度。新的毛利＝51% × 779 億美元 ≈ 397 億美元，掉了大約 39 億美元。

  3. 3

    Because R&D, sales, and administrative costs do not shrink just because gross margin fell, that entire $3.9 billion drop passes straight through to operating income: $23.4B − $3.9B ≈ $19.5B. As a percentage, operating income fell about 16.7% — more than three times the 5-point margin move that caused it.

    因為研發跟銷售管理費用不會因為毛利率掉了就跟著縮，這 39 億美元的跌幅，會直接整個穿透到營業利益上：234 億 − 39 億 ≈ 195 億美元。換算成百分比，營業利益掉了約 16.7%——超過造成它的 5 個百分點毛利率變動的 3 倍以上。

  4. 4

    This is operating leverageThe tendency for a business with a large base of fixed costs (like R&D, factories, and sales staff that do not shrink quickly) to see profit move by a much larger percentage than revenue or margin does, in either direction. (a business with a large base of costs that do not shrink quickly sees profit swing by a much bigger percentage than revenue or margin does) working against Intel instead of for it. The same mechanism made Intel spectacularly profitable on the way up; run in reverse, the identical cost structure turns a modest margin slip into a much larger earnings decline. That is exactly what makes a “cheap” trailing P/E fragile at a manufacturer: the earnings in the denominator can fall far faster than the headline margin suggests.

    這就是營業槓桿一門有一大塊固定成本（例如研發、工廠、業務人力，不會很快縮小）的生意，獲利波動的百分比，往哪個方向都會遠大於營收或利益率本身波動的百分比。（一門有一大塊、不容易很快縮小的成本的生意，獲利波動的百分比，會遠大於營收或利益率本身波動的百分比）在往對英特爾不利的方向作用。上升期毛利率往上走時，正是同一個機制讓英特爾格外賺錢；反過來跑同一套成本結構，就會把一個不算大的毛利率下滑放大成大得多的獲利衰退。製造業這種又重又不易縮小的成本結構，正是「便宜」的近四季本益比脆弱的原因：分母裡的獲利，可能跌得比表面的毛利率數字暗示的快得多。

On archetype (lesson 16): Intel fits neither the mature scale-compounder box, where margin is supposed to hold or expand, nor the commodity cyclical box, where profit swings with a price nobody controls. On cyclical position (lesson 17), two clocks are easy to blur together. The cyclical clock: 2020's pandemic PC demand was a pull-forward, so a normal give-back over the next year or two would be an ordinary cyclical event with no structural meaning. The structural clock: a multi-year margin slide and a process delay measured in years, not quarters. Reading the coming PC slowdown as proof the structural story is fading — or dismissing the margin slide as cyclical noise that will pass — is running the two clocks together when the evidence says to read them apart.

在循環位置上（第 17 課），有兩支時鐘很容易被混在一起。循環的那支：2020 年疫情帶動的個人電腦需求是提前拉貨，接下來一兩年出現正常回吐，是尋常的循環事件，沒有結構性含意。結構的那支：連續多年的毛利率下滑，加上一個以年、不是以季計算的製程延遲。把接下來個人電腦降溫當成結構性故事也在退燒的證據，或反過來把毛利率下滑當成會過去的循環雜訊，都是把這兩支該分開讀的時鐘攪在一起。

不一定。股價這一頭下檔有限，跟獲利那一頭下檔有限，是兩件事。上面那條零成長底線，假設的是今天的獲利守得住；它沒有說如果獲利本身一直往下掉，倍數會怎麼樣。光是 8 個百分點的毛利率衝擊，就能讓每股盈餘掉約四分之一；如果市場接著又把這個下滑當成會持續、不是一次性，再把倍數往下調一點，兩個效果會疊在一起。

Pitfall: treating a below-market P/E as margin of safety on its own. A cheap multiple only protects you if the earnings under it are stable. When the leading indicators in Part I and Part II — gross margin trend, a lost marquee customer, a rival gaining share every quarter — already point toward earnings decay, a low P/E is not a cushion; it is a bet that the market is wrong about the direction those indicators are pointing. Before crediting a low multiple as safety, ask specifically what would have to be true about future earnings for today's number to actually be cheap.

A contrast worth naming directly: TSMC sits at the opposite end of the same value chain, and the opposite end of the moat spectrum. By the freeze date, TSMC was already the world's leading-edge foundry, shipping 5-nanometer chips for Apple since 2020 while Intel was still struggling to ramp 10nm and had just pushed 7nm out another year. Genuine, widening process leadership gives TSMC real pricing power that customers cannot easily source elsewhere — which is exactly why its margin structure and its multiple, at the time, looked nothing like Intel's. AMD is a different kind of contrast: a fabless company riding the same process race from the demand side, buying TSMC's best node rather than trying to build its own, and taking server share every quarter of 2020 as a direct result. All three companies sit in the same industry; at the freeze date, they were on three different sides of the same moat-trend story.

有一個對照點值得直接點名：台積電站在同一條價值鏈的另一端，也站在護城河光譜的另一端。到了凍結日，台積電已經是全球最先進的代工廠，2020 年起就已經在替蘋果量產 5 奈米晶片，而英特爾當時還在苦撐 10 奈米的量產爬坡，7 奈米又剛被延後一年。真實、還在變寬的製程領先，讓台積電握有真正的定價權，客戶不容易輕易轉單別家——這也是為什麼它的利益率結構跟本益比，跟英特爾當時看起來完全不像。AMD 是另一種對照：一家從需求端搭上同一場製程競賽的無廠公司，直接買台積電當下最好的製程來用，而不是自己硬拚，也因此 2020 年每一季，伺服器市占都在往上走。這三家公司都在同一個產業裡；在凍結日，它們卻站在同一個護城河趨勢故事的三個不同位置上。

Part IV — Judgment: Weighing the Evidence

第四部練習：判斷——把證據放上天平

Grading the evidence by the standard from lesson 21: the moat-erosion evidence is high-grade. Three consecutive years of gross-margin decline are audited, published every quarter, and verifiable against Intel's own 10-K filings. The July 2020 delay disclosure came from the company itself. Apple's move to its own chips and Amazon's Graviton servers are completed public facts, not rumours, and AMD's move from about 4.5% to about 7.1% of server units is tracked by an independent firm quarter after quarter.

Against that, the case for a successful turnaround is low-grade: a newly appointed CEO's stated intentions, no delivered results, no track record in this specific job, and no public roadmap beyond a general commitment to compete on process again. Weighted properly, the high-grade evidence supports a confident view that the moat is narrowing now; the low-grade evidence supports, at most, a provisional wait-and-see on whether new leadership can reverse it.

按第 21 課的標準替證據分級：護城河正在流失的證據，屬於高等級。連續 3 年的毛利率下滑，經過審計、每季都公告，可以直接對照英特爾自己的 10-K 年報查證。2020 年 7 月的延遲揭露，直接出自公司自己的口。蘋果轉用自家晶片、亞馬遜自研 Graviton 伺服器晶片，都是已完成的公開事實，不是傳聞；AMD 伺服器出貨市占從約 4.5% 升到約 7.1%，由獨立機構一季接一季追蹤確認。

相對之下，「轉型會成功」的證據等級很低：一位剛上任執行長講出來的企圖心，還沒有任何交出來的成果，在這個位置上也沒有往績，公開資訊裡除了「要重新在製程上競爭」這句大方向承諾外，沒有更具體的藍圖。權重放對，高等級證據支撐一個有信心的看法：護城河現在正在變窄；低等級證據頂多支撐一個暫時、需要繼續觀察的看法：新經營層能不能把它扭轉過來。

A falsifiable thesis (lesson 22), stated at the freeze date, needs kill metrics that can actually be checked later without hindsight creeping in. Three candidates: (1) if Intel's next-generation manufacturing process ships on schedule and wins at least one major external customer willing to have Intel build its chips, that would be the clearest signal the process-leadership root is being repaired, not just defended; (2) if Intel's share of the server-CPU market, as tracked by independent analyst firms, stops falling for two consecutive quarters, that would signal the customer-lock-in branch is stabilizing; (3) if gross margin holds above roughly 50% for four consecutive quarters, rather than continuing its multi-year slide, that would signal the earnings base under the “cheap” multiple is not eroding further. None of these three had been triggered at the freeze date — they are commitments about what would change the reader's mind, not predictions.

一個可證偽的論點（第 22 課），在凍結日寫下來，需要幾個之後真的能被檢驗、不會滲入後見之明的殺手指標。三個候選：(1) 如果英特爾下一代製程技術準時量產，而且贏得至少一個大型外部客戶願意把晶片交給英特爾生產，這會是最清楚的訊號，代表製程領先這條根正在被修復，不只是被硬撐著。(2) 如果英特爾在伺服器中央處理器市場的份額，按獨立分析機構的追蹤，連續兩季止跌，這代表客戶鎖定這根枝幹正在穩住。(3) 如果毛利率能連續 4 季守在約 50% 以上，不再延續這幾年的下滑趨勢，代表撐起這個「便宜」倍數的獲利基礎，沒有繼續流失。這三項在凍結日都還沒有被觸發——它們是「什麼會改變判斷」的承諾，不是預測。

Inverting the question (lesson 23) means not asking why this might work, but listing everything that would have to be true at once for Intel to be back in front. There are four such things, and they are conjunctive — all of them, not any of them. The next node has to arrive on schedule, unlike the last one. It has to be competitive with whatever TSMC is shipping by then, not with what TSMC ships today. Intel has to fund it while its own cash flow is shrinking. And outside customers, who are also Intel's competitors, have to be willing to hand their designs to it.

把問題反過來問（第 23 課），不是問「這件事為什麼可能成功」，而是把「英特爾要重回領先，必須同時成立的條件」全部列出來。這樣的條件有 4 個，而且是「而且」不是「或者」：4 個要全中，不是中一個就好。下一代製程必須準時，不能像上一代那樣延。它必須打得贏台積電到那時候會拿出來的東西，不是今天的東西。英特爾必須在自己現金流正在縮水的時候把這筆錢養出來。而且外部客戶——那些同時也是英特爾競爭對手的公司——必須願意把自家設計交給它做。

Each of those four has a public number attached to it at the freeze date, which is what turns a story into a base rate. On timing: Intel first pointed investors toward its previous node, 10nm, for roughly 2016; volume laptop chips did not arrive until 2019, and the server version had still not shipped by February 2021. That is about three years late on a technology cadence that historically ran about two years per generation — so the last attempt missed by more than a full generation.

這 4 件事，在凍結日都各自掛著一個公開數字，而這正是把一個故事變成一個基本機率的關鍵。先看時程：英特爾當年把上一代製程 10 奈米指向的時間點大約是 2016 年；量產的筆電晶片一直到 2019 年才出來，伺服器版本到 2021 年 2 月都還沒上市。這代表延了大約 3 年，而這個技術的節奏，歷史上大約是每 2 年推進一個世代——換句話說，上一次嘗試，就已經落後超過整整一個世代。

On money: on January 14, 2021, TSMC told investors it planned to spend $25–28 billion on capital projects during 2021. Intel spent $14.3 billion in 2020. Closing a gap requires outspending the leader, not matching it, and at the freeze date Intel was committing roughly half as much as the company it needed to catch. On elapsed time: a new node runs roughly four to five years from decision to volume — a rough industry magnitude, not a published figure — so a plan announced in 2021 delivers a verdict around 2025 or 2026, which is about twenty quarterly reports of waiting before anyone knows.

再看錢：2021 年 1 月 14 日，台積電對投資人表示，2021 年的資本支出計畫是 250 億到 280 億美元。英特爾 2020 年花了 143 億美元。要補上落差，必須花得比領先者更多，不是花得跟它一樣多；而在凍結日，英特爾投入的金額，大約只有那個它要追上的對手的一半。再看時間：一個新製程從決定到量產，大約要 4 到 5 年（這是產業的粗略量級，不是公告數字），所以 2021 年宣布的計畫，答案大約要到 2025 或 2026 年才會揭曉，中間大概要等 20 次季報，才有人知道結果。

And on the base rate itself: around 2001, roughly 30 companies could manufacture logic chips at the leading edge. By 2020 there were three — TSMC, Samsung, and Intel. The rest either exited or stopped at an older node, and the public record does not contain a single one that fell off the leading edge and later climbed back onto it. That is the number that matters most, and it is uncomfortable: on the evidence available in February 2021, the observed rate of successful returns to process leadership was zero out of more than twenty firms that fell off it. It is a small sample, and deserves humility — but a reader assuming Intel had a coin-flip chance was assuming something no case in the industry's history supports.

最後看基本機率本身：2001 年前後，全世界大約有 30 家公司能在最先進製程上生產邏輯晶片。到 2020 年只剩 3 家：台積電、三星、英特爾。其餘的不是退出，就是停在較舊的製程；而公開紀錄裡，找不到任何一家從最先進製程掉下來、之後又爬回去的公司。這是最重要的一個數字，而且並不好聽：以 2021 年 2 月能拿到的證據看，「重回製程領先」成功的觀察次數，是「20 幾家掉下來的公司裡，0 家」。樣本很小，值得保守看待——但一個假設英特爾有五成機會的讀者，等於在假設一件產業史上沒有任何案例支持的事。

Now the comparison with lesson 34's GE case becomes a numbers question rather than a mood. GE's new management had two levers it controlled directly: a genuinely strong core business in aviation engines that funded the rest, and assets it could sell for cash on its own schedule — levers whose results show up within quarters. Intel's lever pays off in about five years, would cost more per year than the $21.1 billion of free cash flow it generated in 2020 if it is to outspend TSMC, and only works if a rival that is also improving happens to stumble. A new CEO can fix capital allocation and organizational focus in a year. A new CEO cannot make a fab process work on schedule by force of will, and nothing about being a 30-year Intel veteran changes the physics.

到這裡，跟第 34 課 GE 案例的對照，就從一種感覺變成一道算術題。GE 的新經營層手上有兩根自己直接控制的槓桿：一門真正強健、能養活其他部門的本業（航空引擎），以及一批可以按自己時程變賣成現金的資產——這兩根槓桿的成果，用「季」就看得到。英特爾的槓桿要 5 年才有結果；要花得比台積電多，每年的支出就會超過它 2020 年賺到的 211 億美元自由現金流，而且還得剛好碰上一個同樣在進步的對手自己失足。一位新執行長，一年之內可以修好資本配置與組織的注意力方向。但一位新執行長，沒辦法靠意志力讓一座晶圓廠的製程準時做出來；在英特爾待了 30 年這件事，也改變不了物理。

Part V — Position: How Big, and What Would Change Your Mind

第五部練習：部位——多大部位，以及什麼會改變你的想法

On the evidence available in February 2021, only two of the three verdicts in lesson 24 are defensible: wait, with named triggers, or avoid. Act is the one that is hard to write down honestly, because the mechanism sentence it requires would have to be “the multiple is low” — and a multiple is not a mechanism. That is the adjudication; the rest of this section is what each verdict actually obliges you to do.

以 2021 年 2 月手上的證據來看，第 24 課的三個裁決裡，只有兩個站得住腳：帶著明確板機的「觀望」，或「迴避」。難以誠實寫下來的是「進場」，因為它要求的那句機制句，寫出來只會是「因為倍數很低」——而倍數不是機制。這就是裁決；這一節接下來要講的，是每個裁決實際上逼你去做什麼。

If the verdict leans toward avoid, the honest version is not “this company is doomed” but “the case for reversal rests on evidence too low-grade to act on today, and a statistically cheap multiple is not by itself a reason to hold a position while waiting for that evidence to improve.” What would change that mind is the same three kill metrics, moving in Intel's favour instead of against it. And if you do act anyway, the honest size is small and non-core — because the multiple offers modest compensation for a real, evidenced risk, not a discount wide enough to ignore it.

如果判斷傾向迴避，誠實的說法不是「這家公司沒救了」，而是「支持反轉的證據等級太低，不足以支持今天就動手；而一個數字上便宜的倍數，本身不構成理由，讓你邊抱著部位邊等這些證據變好」。什麼會改變這個想法：同樣那三個殺手指標，往有利於英特爾的方向動，而不是往反方向。如果你還是決定進場，誠實的規模是小型、非核心——因為這個倍數只對一個真實、有證據的風險提供了溫和補償，不是一個大到可以無視風險的折扣。

The Reveal: What Actually Happened

揭曉：後來實際發生了什麼

About five weeks after taking office, on March 23, 2021, Gelsinger unveiled a strategy the company called “IDM 2.0”: Intel would keep building its own leading-edge factories, but would also open those factories to outside customers for the first time, creating a dedicated foundry business, while using external foundries like TSMC for some of its own chips where that made sense. The market's initial reaction was positive. Intel shares, which had traded around $61 at the freeze date, rose through the spring and briefly touched roughly $68 intraday in April 2021 — a reminder, for anyone who had written “avoid” a few weeks earlier, that a correct freeze-date process does not guarantee the price moves your way immediately, or ever, in the short run.

上任大約 5 週後，2021 年 3 月 23 日，Gelsinger 公布了一套公司稱為「IDM 2.0」的策略：英特爾會繼續自己蓋最先進的工廠，但也會第一次把這些工廠開放給外部客戶，成立一個專門的代工事業；同時在合適的地方，繼續用像台積電這樣的外部代工廠，生產部分自家的晶片。市場一開始的反應是正面的。凍結日約在 61 美元的英特爾股價，經過整個春天一路走高，2021 年 4 月盤中一度短暫觸及約 68 美元——這對任何在幾週前就寫下「迴避」的讀者是個提醒：一個在凍結日正確的過程，不保證股價短期內、甚至永遠會照你的方向走。

The rally did not hold. Full-year 2021 revenue came in roughly flat around $79 billion, but the structural story kept moving in the direction the freeze-date evidence had already pointed. 2022 revenue fell to $63.1 billion, down 20% year over year. 2023 revenue fell a further 14%. Gross margin, which had already slid from about 62% in 2017 to about 56% at the freeze date, kept falling: about 42.6% for 2022, about 40.0% for 2023, and about 32.7% for 2024. In February 2023, Intel cut its quarterly dividend by roughly 65%.

這波反彈沒有撐住。2021 年全年營收大致持平，約在 790 億美元，但結構性故事，依然按著凍結日證據早就指出的方向繼續走。2022 年營收掉到 631 億美元，年減 20%。2023 年營收又進一步下滑 14%。毛利率——已經從 2017 年約 62% 掉到凍結日約 56%——繼續往下：2022 年全年約 42.6%，2023 年約 40.0%，2024 年約 32.7%。2023 年 2 月，英特爾把季度股利砍了約 65%。

In 2024, Intel began separately reporting the results of its manufacturing arm as a distinct segment for the first time. The numbers explained a great deal: in 2023, Intel Foundry posted an operating loss of about $7 billion on $18.9 billion of revenue, wider than a roughly $5.2 billion loss on $27.5 billion of revenue in 2022, while the separate Products division (chip design, selling to end customers) was solidly profitable, with about $11.3 billion of operating income on $47.7 billion of sales. The mature core was, in other words, still a real business generating real profit — it was the fabs specifically, the exact root this lesson identified in Part II, that were bleeding.

2024 年，英特爾第一次把製造部門的財務結果，單獨拆出來當一個獨立分部公告。這些數字解釋了很多事：2023 年，英特爾代工部門營運虧損約 70 億美元，營收 189 億美元；比 2022 年的約 52 億美元虧損（營收 275 億美元）虧得更多。與此同時，另外拆開的產品部門（設計晶片、賣給終端客戶）依然穩健獲利，營收 477 億美元，營業利益約 113 億美元。換句話說，成熟本業依然是一門真正在賺錢的生意——真正在流血的，具體來說是工廠，正是這一課在第二部練習裡點名的那條根。

The crisis came to a head on August 1–2, 2024. Intel reported a Q2 earnings miss and announced it would suspend its dividend entirely starting in the fourth quarter, cut more than 15% of its workforce (roughly 15,000–17,500 people), and reduce spending by more than $10 billion as part of a broader restructuring. Shares fell about 26% on August 2, closing around $21.48 — Intel's worst single trading day in roughly five decades — and the company's market capitalization, roughly $250 billion at the freeze date, fell below $100 billion. On December 2, 2024, Intel's board ousted Gelsinger as CEO, citing a loss of confidence in the pace of the turnaround, less than four years after he had taken the role.

危機在 2024 年 8 月 1 日到 2 日爆發。英特爾公布第 2 季財報未達預期，並宣布從第 4 季起，全面暫停股利，裁員逾 15%（約 1.5 萬到 1.75 萬人），並在更大規模的重整計畫下，削減超過 100 億美元支出。股價 8 月 2 日重挫約 26%，收在約 21.48 美元——是英特爾大約 50 年來單日跌幅最重的一天——公司市值，從凍結日的約 2,500 億美元，跌破 1,000 億美元。2024 年 12 月 2 日，英特爾董事會以「對轉型進度失去信心」為由，撤換 Gelsinger 的執行長職位，距離他上任還不到 4 年。

The contrast with the two companies named in Part III is stark. AMD's share of server-CPU units went from about 7% at the freeze date to above 10% by the end of 2021 and about 23% by the end of 2023, because its fabless model kept it on TSMC's leading-edge process the whole way. TSMC, never having lost its process lead, became the indispensable manufacturer not just for Apple and AMD but for the AI-chip boom that took off over the same years. Same industry, same window, completely different outcome for the company that never let its process leadership slip.

跟第三部練習點名的兩家公司對照，落差非常明顯。AMD 在伺服器處理器的出貨量市占，從凍結日的約 7%，升到 2021 年底的 10% 以上，再到 2023 年底的約 23%，因為它的無廠模式讓它全程都用得到台積電最先進的製程。台積電從來沒有丟掉製程領先，不只繼續是蘋果與 AMD 不可或缺的製造商，也在同一段時間起飛的 AI 晶片熱潮裡成為關鍵廠商。同一個產業、同一段窗口，那家始終沒讓製程領先鬆手的公司，走出完全不同的結果。

Post-Mortem: What Was Visible, What Was Not

事後覆盤：什麼當時看得見，什麼看不見

Applying lesson 25's bias framework honestly means sorting the reveal into two piles, not folding it into one smooth story after the fact. Visible at the freeze date: every piece of high-grade evidence this case built on — three straight years of gross-margin decline, capex rising to about 22% of revenue without buying back the process lead, the July 2020 delay and its $42 billion single-day market-value hit, Apple's and Amazon's completed departures, AMD's move from about 4.5% to about 7.1% of server units, the fixed-cost structure that made an IDM's earnings especially fragile to a margin slip. None of this required hindsight; all of it was in public filings and press coverage before Gelsinger's first day.

誠實套用第 25 課的偏誤框架，需要把揭曉的內容分成兩堆，不要事後把它揉成一個聽起來很順的單一故事。凍結日看得見的部分：這個案例整個建立在其上的每一項高等級證據——連續 3 年的毛利率下滑、資本支出升到約占營收 22% 卻沒買回製程領先、2020 年 7 月那次一天蒸發 420 億美元市值的延遲、蘋果與亞馬遜都已完成的離開、AMD 伺服器出貨市占從約 4.5% 到約 7.1%、讓 IDM 的獲利對毛利率下滑格外脆弱的固定成本結構。這些完全不需要後見之明；在 Gelsinger 上任第一天之前，全部都已經在公開財報跟媒體報導裡了。

Not visible at the freeze date: the speed and depth of the decline, and one specific reason for it. Nothing public in February 2021 pointed to a 20% revenue drop in 2022, a foundry segment losing $7 billion in a year, a dividend suspended entirely, or a CEO ousted inside four years. Nor did anything point to the size of what came next in the data centre — that from late 2022 onward, a large share of the world's server budget would be redirected from CPUs to AI accelerators, a category Intel barely sold into. Part I noted that GPUs were taking a growing slice of data-centre spending; the scale of that shift was not knowable.

凍結日看不見的部分：這次下滑的速度與深度，以及其中一個具體原因。2021 年 2 月的公開資訊，沒有任何一項指向 2022 年營收會掉 20%、代工部門一年虧 70 億美元、股利被全面暫停，或執行長上任不到 4 年就被換掉。也沒有任何一項，指得出接下來資料中心會發生的事有多大——2022 年底之後，全世界很大一部分的伺服器預算，會從中央處理器轉去買 AI 加速器，而這正是英特爾幾乎沒有產品的一塊。第一部練習已經提到 GPU 正在吃掉越來越多資料中心支出，但這個轉向的規模，當時無從得知。

So how does the reveal split between the two piles? Roughly: the direction was fully knowable, and most of the magnitude was knowable in kind if not in size — the operating-leverage arithmetic in Part II already showed that a margin slide of this type compounds into a much larger earnings fall. What was not knowable is the accelerant: the AI capital shift landed on top of the process problem and made an ordinary decline into a crisis. A reader who wrote “the moat looks like it is narrowing” gets credit for direction. A reader who claims to have forecast a 26%-in-one-day crash and a CEO ouster inside four years is, more likely than not, quietly rewriting how confident they actually were.

那麼揭曉的內容，在這兩堆之間怎麼分？大致是：方向完全看得見，幅度也大致看得見「會是哪一類」，只是看不見「會有多大」——第二部練習的營業槓桿算式，已經算給你看，這種毛利率下滑會滾成大得多的獲利衰退。真正看不見的是那個加速劑：AI 資本轉向壓在製程問題之上，把一場尋常的衰退變成一場危機。一個當時寫下「護城河看起來正在變窄」的讀者，在方向上值得肯定。一個宣稱自己當時就預見了單日暴跌 26%、執行長不到 4 年下台的讀者，比較可能的情況是，他正在悄悄改寫自己當年究竟有多大信心。

Think first: a reader's freeze-date verdict was “avoid,” and the stock rallied to $68 by April 2021. Was the verdict wrong?先想再看：如果讀者凍結日的判斷是「迴避」，而股價 2021 年 4 月漲到 68 美元，這個判斷算是錯的嗎？

Not automatically, because the reasoning matters more than the timing. The freeze-date reasoning was never about the next six weeks, so a rally driven by a strategy announcement, before a single dollar of results had come in, cannot confirm or refute it. That rally is a market repricing hope, which lesson 19 flags as the kind of move that needs delivered evidence to hold. The one thing a wait verdict should keep updating on is the kill metrics — and by late 2022 all three were moving the wrong way, which a good process would register long before the August 2024 crisis made it undeniable.

不會自動就是錯的，因為推理本身比時機重要。凍結日那套推理，從頭到尾談的就不是接下來這 6 週；所以一場由策略公布帶動、還沒有任何一塊錢成果進帳的反彈，既不能證實也不能推翻它。那波反彈是市場在替希望重新定價，第 19 課會提醒你，這種漲勢要靠交出來的成果才撐得住。一個觀望判斷唯一該持續更新的，是那三個殺手指標——到 2022 年底，三項全部往壞的方向動，一個好的流程早就該注意到，不必等 2024 年 8 月的危機把它變得無可否認。

⚠

Common pitfall: reading this case backward as “never buy a cheap stock with a declining moat.” That overreaches. The lesson is narrower and more useful: a below-market multiple and a real dividend do not, by themselves, tell you anything about earnings durability — you still have to check the leading indicators separately, and weigh them by evidence grade, not by how comfortable the story sounds. Some statistically cheap stocks with a narrowing moat trend do recover; the freeze-date process cannot know in advance which ones will, and that is exactly why sizing and kill metrics, not conviction, are what carry the risk.

常見誤區：把這個案例倒著讀成「毛利率在往下掉的便宜股票，永遠不要買」。這話講得過頭了。這一課真正的教訓比較窄、也比較有用：低於大盤的本益比加上一筆真實的股利，本身完全說明不了獲利耐不耐久——你還是得分開檢查先行指標，並按證據等級去衡量它們，不是按這個故事聽起來多讓人安心去衡量。有些護城河趨勢在變窄、數字上卻便宜的股票，後來確實回穩了；凍結日的過程沒辦法事先知道哪些會、哪些不會，這正是為什麼扛住風險的，該是部位規模跟殺手指標，不是信心本身。

Three Things This Archetype Teaches Us

這種原型留下的三個教訓

A cheap price is a description of what the market is currently paying, not a verdict on whether the underlying earnings will hold.

便宜的價格，描述的是市場現在願意付多少錢，不是替底層獲利守不守得住下判決。

Intel's 12–13x multiple and 2.3% yield at the freeze date were real numbers, not illusions — but they described the past year's earnings, sitting on a moat whose root was already visibly narrowing. A value trap is not a company lying about its numbers; it is a company whose numbers are honest while its denominator quietly shrinks faster than the multiple has adjusted for. Telling the two apart means checking the leading indicators separately from the multiple, every time a stock looks statistically cheap.

英特爾凍結日約 12 到 13 倍的本益比、2.3% 的殖利率，都是真實數字，不是幻覺——但它們描述的是過去一年的獲利，而底下那個護城河的根已經肉眼可見在變窄。價值陷阱不是一家公司在數字上說謊；而是數字誠實，分母卻在悄悄縮小，縮得比倍數調整的速度更快。要分辨這兩者，每次看到一檔股票數字上便宜，就得把先行指標跟倍數分開檢查。

  • Before crediting a below-market P/E as margin of safety, check whether the leading indicators (margin trend, capital intensity without a matching return, market-share trend) already point toward the earnings underneath it shrinking.在把一個低於大盤的本益比當成安全邊際之前，先檢查先行指標（利益率趨勢、資本支出強度是否有對應報酬、市占率趨勢）有沒有已經指向底下的獲利正在縮水。
  • When a company's moat rests on one root advantage feeding several branches, treat any evidence of that root weakening as evidence about the whole moat, not just one metric.當一家公司的護城河，靠一個根性優勢在餵養好幾根枝幹時，任何顯示這條根在變弱的證據，都要當成整個護城河的證據，不只是一個指標而已。
  • Run the operating-leverage arithmetic before trusting a trailing multiple — a business with a large, slow-to-shrink fixed-cost base can see earnings fall much faster than a headline margin number suggests.在相信一個近四季本益比之前，先跑一次營業槓桿的算式——一門固定成本又大、又不容易縮小的生意，獲利下滑的速度，可能遠快於表面上的利益率數字暗示的速度。
  • Before crediting a new CEO with fixing a technology gap, count how often that specific kind of catch-up has actually worked, and compare against a genuinely similar precedent rather than a superficially similar one.在把「修好技術落差」的信心記在新執行長頭上之前，先數一數這種追趕過去真的成功過幾次，並拿一個真正相似、而不是表面相似的先例來對照。
  • Write kill metrics down before the outcome is known, and revisit the verdict when they trigger — not just when the stock price moves.在結果揭曉之前先寫下殺手指標，等指標被觸發時重新檢視判斷——不是等股價變動才動作。
  • A rally after an “avoid” or a drawdown after an “act” does not by itself prove the process was wrong — check the reasoning against what was knowable at the time, over a horizon long enough for delivered evidence to appear.「迴避」後反彈、或「進場」後回檔，本身都不證明過程有錯——要拿推理對照當時真正能知道的事，而且時間要拉到夠長，長到交出來的證據有機會出現。
