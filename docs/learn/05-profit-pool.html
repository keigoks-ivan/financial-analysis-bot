Who Actually Gets Paid When You Buy a Phone

一支手機的錢，到底進了誰的口袋

A flagship phone retails for roughly $1,000. Multiply that by hundreds of millions of units sold every year, and the smartphone industry is one of the largest consumer goods businesses on earth — hundreds of billions of dollars of revenue flowing through it annually. The natural next question an investor asks is: given all that money moving through the chain, who is actually keeping it?

Independent teardown and cost estimates for a recent high-end iPhone put the physical bill of materials — the chips, the screen, the camera module, the battery, everything that goes into the box — at roughly $550 to $600 on a phone that sells for about $1,000. A bill of materials is not full cost; assembly, logistics, warranty, research and everything else still has to come out of that gap. But the room is clearly there, and it shows up at the bottom line: Apple's company-wide net margin has run in the mid-20s percent range in recent years, about 27% in its most recent fiscal year. Apple does not manufacture that hardware itself — it designs the product and contracts the physical assembly out to specialist manufacturers elsewhere in the chain. Look at what that specialist position is actually worth: Jabil (NYSE: JBL), a large U.S.-listed contract electronics manufacturer that assembles finished hardware for many companies across consumer, industrial, and healthcare electronics, posted a net margin of only about 2.2% on nearly $30 billion of revenue in its most recent fiscal year — a margin shape typical of contract assemblers across the electronics industry generally, including the ones that physically build smartphones. Two companies can sit at completely different points along essentially the same kind of manufacturing chain. One keeps roughly a quarter of every dollar it sells. The other keeps about two cents.

That gap is not an accident and it is not explained by "Apple makes a better product." It is the entire subject of this lesson: a value chain generates a pool of total dollar profit, and that pool does not distribute itself evenly, or according to who touches the most steps, or who ships the most units. It concentrates wherever one link in the chain holds real leverage over the others — and almost everywhere else in the chain earns close to nothing for its trouble. (The companies named in this lesson — Apple, Jabil, Intel, Microsoft, ASML, and others — are used purely as public, verifiable teaching examples of a mechanism; nothing here is a comment on whether any of them is a good investment today.)

一支旗艦手機的建議售價大約落在 1,000 美元上下。乘上每年賣出的數億支，智慧型手機產業因此是地球上規模最大的消費性產品產業之一——每年有數千億美元在整條鏈裡流動。投資人接下來自然會問的問題是：這麼多錢流過這條鏈，到底誰真正把它留下來了？

獨立的拆機成本估算，對一支近年的高階 iPhone 給出的物料成本（晶片、螢幕、相機模組、電池，所有裝進機殼裡的零件）大約落在 550 到 600 美元，而這支手機賣價約 1,000 美元。物料成本不等於全部成本——組裝、物流、保固、研發都還要從中間那段差額裡扣。但空間確實在那裡，而且一路撐到損益表最下面一行：蘋果近幾年的公司整體淨利率大致落在 25% 到 27% 之間，最近一個會計年度約 27%。蘋果自己不生產這些硬體，它負責設計產品，把實體組裝外包給鏈上其他專業廠商。看看這個「專業組裝」的位置實際值多少錢：捷普（Jabil，美股代號 JBL），一家美股上市的大型電子代工廠，替消費性、工業、醫療等多個領域的客戶組裝成品硬體，最近一個會計年度營收近 300 億美元，淨利率卻只有約 2.2%——這種利潤率的形狀，是整個電子代工產業普遍的樣子，包括那些實際負責組裝智慧型手機的廠商在內。兩家公司可以站在幾乎同一種製造鏈裡完全不同的位置：一家每賣一塊錢留下大約兩毛五到兩毛七，另一家只留下大約兩分錢。

這個落差不是意外，也不能只用「蘋果的產品比較好」帶過。這正是這一課要談的全部主題：一條價值鏈會產生一整池的美元利潤，而這一池利潤不會平均分配，也不看誰經手的步驟最多、誰出貨的數量最大。它會集中在鏈裡真正握有籌碼的那個環節，而鏈上幾乎其他所有環節，即使辛苦付出，拿到的利潤都趨近於零。（這一課提到的公司——蘋果、捷普、英特爾、微軟、艾司摩爾等——全部只是用來說明機制的公開、可查證教學案例；文中沒有一句話是在評論這些公司現在值不值得投資。）

How to check this yourself: finding net margin in any public 10-K自己動手查：怎麼在任何一份公開 10-K 裡找到淨利率

You do not need paid data access to reproduce the Apple/Jabil comparison above. Every U.S.-listed company's Form 10-K is free on the SEC's EDGAR full-text search system, and its consolidated income statement sits within the first few pages of the financial statements section — look for the line usually labeled "Net sales" or "Total revenue" near the top, and "Net income" near the bottom. Divide the second by the first. As a rough calibration for sanity-checking your own result: a company that designs and brands a consumer product commonly runs a net margin anywhere from the high single digits to the high 20s percent; a contract manufacturer or assembler sitting in the same physical chain commonly runs low single digits, sometimes under 2%, as the numbers above show directly. A red flag worth digging into further is a contract manufacturer whose margin has gone negative for more than one consecutive year outside of an industry-wide downturn — that usually signals it is losing pricing power to its own customers, not just having one rough quarter.

要重現上面蘋果與捷普的對照，不需要任何付費資料庫。每一家美股上市公司的 10-K 都能在美國證管會（SEC）的 EDGAR 全文檢索系統免費查到，合併損益表就在財務報表區塊的前幾頁，找上面通常標示「Net sales」或「Total revenue」（淨銷售額／總營收）的那一行，以及下面標示「Net income」（淨利）的那一行，把後者除以前者即可。給你一個粗略的校準基準：一家自己設計、自有品牌的消費性產品公司，淨利率常見範圍大約從個位數的高段到將近 30%；同一條實體鏈上的委外代工廠或組裝廠，淨利率則常見落在個位數低段，有時甚至不到 2%——上面的數字就是最直接的示範。一個值得進一步深挖的警訊，是一家委外代工廠的淨利率連續一年以上是負的，而且不是整個產業性下行造成的。這通常代表它正在對自己的客戶失去議價權，不只是單一一季表現不好而已。

What a Profit Pool Is: Revenue Location Is Not Profit Location

利潤池是什麼：營收在哪，不代表利潤在哪

Give the concept a name so it is easy to hold onto: a profit poolPlain language: the total dollar amount of profit an entire industry chain generates in a period, and how that total is split across the chain's steps — not each step's margin percentage in isolation. is the total dollar amount of operating profit an entire value chain produces over a period, distributed across each step of that chain. It is deliberately a dollar concept, not a margin-percentage concept — a step can post a spectacular margin and still control a tiny sliver of the pool, simply because its revenue base is small. The two things that matter are (1) how big is each step's slice in absolute dollars, and (2) how that slice is trending over the last 3-5 years.

A clean historical illustration is the personal-computer industry from roughly the mid-1990s through the mid-2000s — the era commonly nicknamed "Wintel," for Windows plus Intel. Dozens of PC assemblers (Compaq, IBM, Dell, and a long tail of clone-makers) fought a brutal price war for market share, competing on a product that had become largely interchangeable — a beige box running the same operating system on the same instruction set. Net margins for assemblers were routinely in the low single digits, and several well-known brands were acquired or exited the business entirely over that period. One layer up, Intel — supplying the central processor almost every one of those boxes needed — reported revenue of about $25 billion in fiscal 1997 at an operating margin of roughly 39%, and held an operating margin in the 30s or better through most of the rest of the decade. Microsoft, supplying the operating system every one of those boxes ran, did better still: on revenue of roughly $20 billion in fiscal 1999 (year ended June 30, 1999) it ran an operating margin of roughly 50%, once its Windows and Office franchises had matured. Both figures come from the companies' own annual filings and both are orders of magnitude above what the assemblers earned on each dollar. The assemblers, taken together, generated far more total industry revenue than Intel and Microsoft combined. But measured in dollars of profit kept, Intel and Microsoft dominated the pool.

How do you actually see this from public filings rather than from memory of a story? Pull each company's segment revenue and segment operating income — most annual reports break this out — for every layer of the chain you can identify, across 3-5 years. Multiply revenue by operating margin to get a dollar profit figure for each layer (not just the margin percentage), then compare those dollar figures to each other, not to each layer's own revenue. A layer whose dollar profit is growing while its revenue share of the industry is flat or shrinking is quietly gaining ground in the pool; the reverse is a warning sign.

先給這個概念一個名字，方便記住：利潤池白話：整個產業鏈在一段期間內產生的美元利潤總額，以及這個總額如何被分配到鏈上各個環節——不是各環節各自的毛利率百分比。指的是整條價值鏈在一段期間內產生的營業利潤美元總額，並且看這個總額如何被分配到鏈上每一個環節。這是刻意設計成一個「美元」概念，而不是「毛利率」概念——一個環節可以貼出漂亮到誇張的毛利率，卻只掌握整池利潤裡微不足道的一小塊，原因很單純：它的營收基期太小。真正重要的是兩件事：（一）每個環節在美元絕對值上的份額有多大；（二）這個份額在過去三到五年的走勢。

一個乾淨的歷史範例是個人電腦產業，大約從 1990 年代中期到 2000 年代中期——這段時期常被暱稱為「Wintel」，意指微軟的 Windows 加上英特爾（Intel）。數十家電腦組裝廠（康柏、IBM、戴爾，以及一長串的兼容機廠商）為了市占率打得頭破血流，賣的是一款已經高度同質化的產品——同一套作業系統、同一套指令集，裝在不同外殼裡的機箱。組裝廠的淨利率經常落在個位數的低段，那段時期有好幾個知名品牌被併購或直接退出這門生意。往上一層，替幾乎每一台機箱供應中央處理器的英特爾，1997 會計年度營收約 250 億美元，營益率約 39%，而且接下來十年大多數年份都維持在 30% 以上。替幾乎每一台機箱供應作業系統的微軟表現更好：1999 會計年度（截至 1999 年 6 月 30 日）營收約 197 億美元，營益率約 50%，尤其是在 Windows 和 Office 兩大金雞母成熟之後。這兩個數字都來自兩家公司自己的年度申報文件，而且都比組裝廠每賺一塊錢留下的比例高出好幾個數量級。把所有電腦組裝廠加總起來看，整個產業的總營收遠遠超過英特爾加微軟的總和。但如果用留下的美元利潤來衡量，英特爾和微軟才是那一池利潤真正的主人。

要怎麼不靠背故事、而是真的從公開財報看出這件事？把鏈上每一個你能辨識出來的環節，拉出過去三到五年各自的分部營收與分部營業利益——多數年報都會拆出這個層級的數字。用營收乘以營益率算出每一層的美元利潤（不是只看營益率百分比），再拿這些美元金額互相比較，而不是拿每一層自己的營收去比。如果某一層的美元利潤在成長，同時它占整個產業營收的份額卻持平甚至下滑，代表它正悄悄在利潤池裡擴大地盤；反過來則是一個警訊。

Takeaway收斂

The size of a step's revenue tells you almost nothing about how much of the industry's profit it keeps. Always convert margin percentage into a dollar figure and compare dollars, not percentages, across the chain.

一個環節的營收大小，幾乎說明不了它拿走多少產業利潤。永遠把毛利率／營益率換算成美元金額，拿美元跟美元比，而不是拿百分比跟百分比比。

Think first: why might a step with a sky-high margin still hold almost none of the profit pool?先想一下：為什麼一個毛利率高得誇張的環節，卻可能在利潤池裡幾乎分不到東西？

Because the pool is measured in dollars, and dollars are margin multiplied by revenue. A tiny, high-margin specialty supplier selling $200 million a year at an 80% margin produces $160 million of profit. A commoditized giant selling $50 billion a year at a 4% margin produces $2 billion of profit — twelve times as much, from a margin that looks worse on every printed page. Margin percentage tells you how efficiently a step converts its own revenue into profit; it tells you nothing about how big that step's slice of the whole industry's dollar profit actually is. You need both numbers, and the second one (revenue scale) is the one people forget to check.

因為利潤池是用美元衡量的，而美元等於營益率乘以營收。一家迷你、高毛利的特殊零件供應商，一年賣 2 億美元、營益率 80%，賺到 1.6 億美元的利潤。一家高度同質化的巨頭，一年賣 500 億美元、營益率只有 4%，卻賺到 20 億美元，是前者的 12 倍，儘管它的營益率在任何報表上看起來都難看得多。毛利率／營益率百分比告訴你這個環節把自己的營收轉成利潤的效率有多高；它完全沒告訴你這個環節在整個產業美元利潤總量裡占的份額有多大。這兩個數字都要看，而人們最常漏看的正是第二個——規模。

Why Profit Sticks Where It Sticks: Buyer Power, a Substitution Ceiling, a Barrier to Entry

利潤為什麼留在某個環節：買方力量、替代天花板、進入門檻

You can name three plain mechanisms that decide where a chain's profit concentrates, without ever reaching for a textbook's five-letter acronym. First: when a step's customers are few and large, those customers hold the whip hand and squeeze price down toward cost — this is buyer concentrationPlain language: when a supplier sells mostly to a handful of large customers, those customers can credibly threaten to walk, so they can push price down close to the supplier's cost. at work, and you see it wherever a supplier's top three or five customers account for the majority of its revenue — a classic case is an auto-parts maker selling almost entirely to the five largest automakers, who can and do rotate business between competing suppliers every model cycle to keep price pressure constant. Second: if a decent alternative exists nearby, price cannot rise past what that alternative costs — call this a substitution ceilingPlain language: a cheaper or good-enough alternative sitting right next to your product puts a hard lid on how high you can price, no matter how good your product is., and the cleanest textbook case is a branded drug the moment its patent expires. Pfizer's cholesterol drug Lipitor is the standard illustration: annual sales peaked at about $13 billion in 2006 and ran at up to roughly a quarter of Pfizer's total revenue for several years. Its U.S. patent expired on November 30, 2011. Chemically identical generics arrived within days at a fraction of the price, and Lipitor's sales collapsed to a small fraction of peak over the following few years. Same molecule, same efficacy, same brand — the ceiling closed the week the legal barrier did, not gradually over a decade. Third: if it takes years and enormous capital for a new competitor to replicate a step's capability, that step keeps its pricing power for a long time — this is a barrier to entryPlain language: how long and how much money it would take a brand-new competitor to copy what you do. The longer and more expensive, the longer your pricing power survives., and the single most extreme real-world example in the world right now sits in semiconductor manufacturing equipment.

One company supplies essentially all of the world's extreme ultraviolet (EUV) lithography machines — the tool required to print the smallest, most advanced transistor patterns onto a silicon wafer. Each machine has been reported to cost on the order of $150-200 million, order backlogs commonly run multiple years, and no rival has managed to bring a competing EUV tool to market despite roughly two decades of the technology being publicly known to exist. That is a barrier to entry so wide that it functions as a genuine capability monopoly: the equipment layer of the chip industry is a small fraction of the whole industry's total revenue, yet it captures a share of the whole chain's profit pool wildly out of proportion to its revenue size, purely because nobody else can build the machine. The same bottleneck logic — a handful of companies, or in some steps effectively one, controlling capacity everyone downstream needs — has repeatedly shown up in the 2023-2025 AI hardware buildout around advanced chip packaging capacity and high-bandwidth memory: relatively small revenue lines commanding outsized negotiating leverage over much larger customers, simply because there was nowhere else downstream buyers could go.

決定一條鏈的利潤會集中在哪裡，可以用三個白話機制講清楚，完全不需要動用教科書裡那套五個字母的縮寫。第一：當一個環節的客戶數量少、規模又大，客戶就握有主導權，能把價格往成本邊緣壓，這就是買方集中白話：如果供應商的營收主要來自少數幾個大客戶，客戶就有本錢威脅轉單，能把價格壓到接近成本。在起作用，凡是供應商前三到五大客戶占營收比重過半的地方都看得到：一個經典案例是零組件廠幾乎只賣給前五大車廠，這些車廠每一次改款週期都能、也真的會把訂單在幾家供應商之間輪流轉，讓價格壓力持續存在。第二：如果旁邊有一個堪用的替代方案，價格就漲不過那個替代方案的成本，這叫替代天花板白話：旁邊有一個更便宜、或堪用的替代品，會給你的訂價空間釘上一個硬天花板，不管你的產品本身有多好。，教科書等級的乾淨案例就是原廠藥專利到期的那一刻。輝瑞（Pfizer）的降膽固醇藥立普妥（Lipitor）是最標準的例子：年銷售額在 2006 年達到高峰，約 130 億美元，有好幾年占輝瑞總營收高達約四分之一。它的美國專利在 2011 年 11 月 30 日到期。幾天之內，化學成分完全相同的學名藥就以極低的價格上市，立普妥的銷售額在接下來幾年掉到只剩高峰的一小部分。同一個分子、同樣的療效、同樣的品牌——天花板是在法律屏障消失的那一週關上的，不是花 10 年慢慢關上的。第三：如果新對手需要花好幾年、投入巨額資本才能複製這個環節的能力，這個環節的訂價權就能維持很久，這叫進入門檻白話：一個全新競爭者要花多久、多少錢才能複製你在做的事。時間越長、成本越高，你的訂價權能撐得越久。，而現在全世界最極端的真實案例就在半導體製造設備裡。

全世界幾乎所有極紫外光（EUV）微影設備都由單一一家公司供應，這是把最先進、最微小的電晶體圖形印上矽晶圓所需要的設備。據報導每台機器造價落在 1.5 億到 2 億美元上下的量級，訂單排期常常長達數年，而儘管這項技術公開存在已經約 20 年，至今沒有任何一家對手成功把可競爭的 EUV 設備推上市。這就是一道寬到足以形成真正能力壟斷的進入門檻：晶片產業裡設備這一層的營收只占整個產業的一小部分，卻拿走整條鏈利潤池裡與其營收規模完全不成比例的一大塊，純粹因為沒有第二家能造出這台機器。同樣的瓶頸邏輯——只有少數幾家，甚至在某些環節實質上只有一家，掌控著下游所有人都需要的產能——在 2023 到 2025 年的 AI 硬體建設潮裡，圍繞著先進封裝產能與高頻寬記憶體反覆出現：營收規模相對不大的環節，卻對規模大得多的下游客戶握有不成比例的議價籌碼，原因很單純，下游買家沒有其他地方可以轉單。

這三個機制完全不需要靠術語解釋。只要你能回答「客戶是誰、旁邊有沒有便宜的替代方案、這個能力有多難複製」這三個問題，你就已經掌握了決定一個環節能不能守住利潤的絕大部分答案。

When you see a headline calling an industry a "tight oligopoly," what is usually actually true is that you have been handed a fact about seller headcount and invited to conclude something about pricing power. There is a specific test that separates the two, and it takes five minutes: pull the step's average selling price or the leaders' gross margin across the last ten years. If either fell by more than about a third in any single downturn, the product is commoditized and the concentration is decoration. Micron is the U.S.-listed proof: it is one of only three companies on earth making the product, and it still went to a full-year net loss under U.S. accounting rules in fiscal 2023 with free cash flow of roughly negative $6 billion. Three sellers did not save it, because a buyer can move between the three at almost no cost. A differentiated oligopoly — real switching costs, real spec gaps — shows a gross-margin line that dips in downturns instead of falling off a cliff. Run the ten-year price history before you accept the word "oligopoly" as a conclusion.

當你看到「這是一個緊密寡占產業」的標題，通常真正的情況是：別人給了你一個關於「賣家有幾家」的事實，然後請你自己推論出一個關於訂價權的結論。要分開這兩件事，有一個五分鐘就能做完的具體檢查：把這個環節過去 10 年的平均售價，或幾家龍頭的毛利率，拉出來看。只要其中任何一個在某一輪景氣下行裡跌掉超過三分之一，那這個產品就是商品化的，集中度只是裝飾。美光（Micron）就是美股上的現成證明：它是全世界僅有的三家生產者之一，卻仍然在 2023 會計年度出現公認會計原則下的全年淨虧損，自由現金流約為負 60 億美元。三家賣家沒有救得了它，因為買方在三家之間轉單幾乎零成本。一個真正有差異化的寡占——有真實轉換成本、規格真的有落差——毛利率在景氣下行時是往下凹一塊，不是直接跳崖。在你把「寡占」這兩個字當成結論之前，先把 10 年的價格史跑一次。

Think first: why does a supplier's leverage often erode before a concentrated customer has actually cut a single order?先想一下：為什麼供應商的籌碼往往在集中的客戶還沒真的砍掉一張訂單之前，就已經先弱化了？

Because the negotiation is forward-looking, not backward-looking. A customer that represents 40% of a supplier's revenue does not need to actually place a smaller order to extract a lower price — it only needs to credibly signal that it is qualifying a second source, or piloting an in-house alternative. The mere existence of a credible alternative changes every subsequent price negotiation, well before a single dollar of revenue moves. This is why watching only historical revenue and margin trends can lag reality by a year or more; the leading indicator is qualification announcements, pilot-line news, and capital-expenditure disclosures from the customer side, not the supplier's own reported numbers.

因為談判本質上是向前看的，不是向後看的。一個占供應商營收 40%的客戶，不需要真的先下一張較小的訂單才能壓價——它只需要可信地釋出訊號，說自己正在認證第二供應商，或正在試產自製替代方案。光是「有一個可信的替代方案存在」這件事，就會改變接下來每一次的價格談判，遠早於任何一塊錢的營收真正移動。這就是為什麼只盯著歷史營收和毛利趨勢，反應往往會慢現實一年以上；真正的領先指標是客戶端的認證公告、試產消息、資本支出揭露，不是供應商自己報出來的數字。

Zoom In One More Level: Who Is Who Inside a Single Step

再放大一層：同一個環節裡，誰是誰

Mapping the chain tells you which step holds the pool. It does not tell you whether the winners inside that step are all winning equally — and they almost never are. The second move is to open up the step you care about and lay its players side by side on the two things that decide who keeps the money: how much of the step's output each one actually controls, and whether what they sell is interchangeable with what the others sell. Call this a player matrixPlain language: a side-by-side table of the companies competing inside one step of the chain, comparing how much of that step each controls and how differentiated each one's product actually is — the two things that decide who inside the step keeps the profit.. A big share of a step nobody can charge for is worth nothing; a modest share of a step everyone downstream is queuing for is worth a great deal.

The memory step of the AI hardware chain during 2023-2025 is the sharpest illustration available, because the same step behaved in two completely opposite ways at once. Conventional DRAM, as the earlier prediction covered, is close to interchangeable across the three big producers and prices crash whenever supply catches up. High-bandwidth memory (HBM) — stacked memory dies sold specifically for AI accelerators — sits inside the same step, made by the same three companies, and behaved nothing like it: it has been reported to sell at several times the per-gigabyte price of conventional DRAM, supply stayed sold out for successive years, and qualification at the dominant accelerator customer was slow and technically demanding enough that one producer, SK Hynix, held roughly 54% of global HBM supply in 2024 (Samsung around 39%, Micron around 7%, per industry trackers), while its rivals spent quarters working through qualification before they could ship in volume. Same three companies, same factories, same broad step of the chain — but at the DRAM node they were interchangeable commodity sellers, and at the HBM node they were not. That is why a step-level verdict like "memory is a commodity business" is too coarse to trade on: the pool inside a step can be distributed completely differently from the step's own reputation.

The packaging step of the same chain makes the second point, about size. Advanced packaging — physically joining chip dies and memory stacks into one working module — is a small line item next to the cost of the processor it packages, yet through 2023-2024 the industry's total output of leading-edge accelerators was gated by how much of this capacity existed. The dominant supplier, TSMC, was reported to have grown capacity on its advanced-packaging platform (branded CoWoS) from roughly 13,000-16,000 wafers a month in 2023 to roughly 35,000-40,000 a month in 2024 — more than doubling in a single year — and it was still allocated rather than freely purchasable, with customers negotiating for slots. A step can be small in revenue and decisive in leverage at the same time; those two facts are not in tension, and mistaking small revenue for small importance is one of the more expensive errors available in chain analysis.

One level narrower still, inside the equipment that makes packaging possible, the same trap repeats in a form you can check on a U.S. filing. Kulicke & Soffa (Nasdaq: KLIC) is the long-standing leader in wire bonding — the mature, low-price, undifferentiated way of connecting a chip to its package — and it is also one of the companies selling the thermal-compression bonders the leading-edge stacks require. Those are two different businesses filed under one company name. Recent disclosures put its thermal-compression bonding revenue growing on the order of 70% year over year to cross roughly $100 million, off a small base, while the mature wire-bonding line moved with the ordinary semiconductor cycle. Averaged into one company-level growth rate, the interesting half disappears. This is the same lesson as DRAM versus HBM arriving one layer down: if you draw the step at the level of "packaging equipment," you get a blended number that describes neither of the two things actually happening inside it.

畫出鏈，你會知道利潤池落在哪個環節。但你還不知道，這個環節裡的贏家是不是贏得一樣多——而他們幾乎從來都不一樣。所以第二步是：把你在意的那個環節打開，把裡面的公司並排放好，只比兩件事。第一，各自實際掌握了這個環節多少產出。第二，它賣的東西，跟隔壁那家賣的能不能互換。這張表就叫玩家矩陣白話：把同一個環節裡互相競爭的公司並排列成一張表，比較各自掌握這個環節多少份額、各自的產品到底有多少差異化——這兩件事決定了環節內部的利潤歸誰。。在一個沒人收得到錢的環節裡拿到大份額，一文不值；在一個下游排隊搶著要的環節裡，就算份額不大，也可能非常值錢。

2023 到 2025 年 AI 硬體鏈的記憶體環節，是現成最銳利的例子，因為同一個環節在同一段時間裡表現出兩種完全相反的行為。傳統 DRAM 就像前面那題預測講的，在三大廠之間幾乎可以互換，供給一追上來價格就崩。高頻寬記憶體（HBM）——專門賣給 AI 加速器的堆疊記憶體——就長在同一個環節裡、由同樣三家公司製造，行為卻完全不同：據報導，它的每 GB 售價是傳統 DRAM 的好幾倍，供給連續數年處於售罄狀態，而且要通過主要加速器客戶的認證，難度高到其中一家廠商 SK 海力士，2024 年握有全球約 54%的 HBM 供給（三星約 39%，美光約 7%，據產業追蹤機構統計），對手則花了好幾季才逐步通過認證、能夠放量出貨。同樣三家公司、同樣的廠、同樣一個粗略的環節，在 DRAM 這個節點上他們是可互換的商品賣家，在 HBM 這個節點上不是。這就是為什麼「記憶體是商品生意」這種環節層級的裁決太粗，不能直接拿來做判斷：一個環節內部的利潤分配，可能和這個環節本身的名聲完全不同。

同一條鏈的封裝環節則說明第二件事：規模。先進封裝——把晶片裸晶與記憶體堆疊實體接合成一個能運作的模組——相對於它所封裝的處理器成本，只是很小的一筆，但在 2023 到 2024 年，整個產業能出貨多少最先進的加速器，卻是被這道產能卡住的。據報導，主要供應商台積電，這項先進封裝平台（品牌名 CoWoS）的產能從 2023 年約每月 1.3 萬到 1.6 萬片，擴大到 2024 年約每月 3.5 萬到 4 萬片，一年之內成長超過 1 倍——而且仍然是用配額分配、不是想買就買得到，客戶得為了取得產能位置去談判。一個環節可以同時「營收很小」和「籌碼很大」；這兩件事並不衝突，而把營收小誤讀成重要性小，是鏈級分析裡代價相當高的錯誤之一。

再往下切一層，到「做封裝的設備」這個環節裡，同樣的陷阱又出現一次，而且這次你可以直接翻美股財報查。Kulicke & Soffa（那斯達克代號 KLIC）是打線接合的老牌龍頭——那是成熟、單價低、沒什麼差異化的傳統封裝連接方式——但它同時也在賣最先進堆疊需要的熱壓接合設備。這是掛在同一家公司名下的兩門完全不同的生意。近期揭露顯示，它的熱壓接合營收年增約 70%、突破約 1 億美元，但那是從很小的基期長上來的；同時成熟的打線接合那條線，只是隨著一般半導體景氣起伏。如果把兩者平均成一個公司層級的成長率，比較有意思的那一半就消失了。這跟前面 DRAM 對 HBM 是同一件事，只是往下掉了一層：如果你把環節畫在「封裝設備」這個粗度，你算出來的會是一個混合數字，而它其實哪一件正在發生的事都沒描述到。

Profit Pool Versus Return on Capital: Do Not Stop at Revenue or Margin

利潤池 vs 資本報酬：別只看營收或毛利率就下結論

Dollar profit share tells you where an industry's absolute economic weight sits. It does not tell you how efficiently that weight was produced. A step can command a huge slice of the pool and still be a mediocre business, if it needed an enormous pile of invested capitalPlain language: the money tied up in a business to run it — factories, equipment, inventory, and receivables, net of the free financing it gets from suppliers. Profit divided by this number tells you how hard the capital is working. to get there. This is the same idea a course on reading financial statements introduces as return on invested capital, decomposed into margin times how fast that capital turns over — a chain-level profit pool map is simply that same lens applied across an entire industry's steps instead of to one company alone. A step earning a smaller dollar slice of the pool, but earning it on almost no capital, can be a far better business than a step earning a bigger dollar slice on a mountain of capital.

A real pair makes the point sharper than any illustrative table can. TSMC, the world's dominant advanced-logic foundry, posted an operating margin of about 45.7% in 2024 — a genuinely strong number — but had to spend roughly $29.8 billion that same year on capital expenditure just to keep building and upgrading the fabs that number depends on, with guidance for 2025 capex running even higher. Nvidia, a fabless designer that buys its wafers from TSMC rather than building its own fabs, has reported a non-GAAP gross margin around 75% in recent periods, earned on a comparatively tiny capital base, because it owns no fabrication capacity at all. Both companies sit at the top of the same AI-hardware chain and both are, by any measure, excellent businesses — but one had to fund a multi-decade pile of capital-intensive infrastructure to earn its margin, and the other largely did not. Margin quality alone cannot tell them apart; only dividing profit by invested capital can.

Consider a simplified four-step chain in high-end computing hardware, listed in order from upstream to downstream: a contract chip manufacturer (a foundry), a fabless chip designer that buys wafers from it, a module and board assembler that buys the finished chips, and a system integrator that sells complete machines under its own brand. Note the shape of the revenue column: each step downstream is larger than the one feeding it, because every step adds its own value on top of what it paid upstream. That ordering is a useful sanity check on any chain you map yourself — if you find an upstream step reporting more revenue than the step it sells into, you have either drawn the chain wrong or the upstream step is really selling most of its output into a completely different chain.

美元利潤份額，告訴你一個產業的經濟重量落在哪裡。但它沒告訴你，這個重量是花多少代價換來的。一個環節可以拿走利潤池裡一大塊，卻仍然只是一門平庸的生意——只要它是靠堆進去一大座投入資本白話：一門生意為了運轉而綁住的資金——廠房、設備、存貨、應收帳款，扣掉供應商給的免費融資。利潤除以這個數字，能看出資本被用得有多賣力。才換來的。想像兩個攤販：一個要先蓋一座工廠才開始賣，一個租一台推車就能賣。年底兩個人都賺 100 萬，你會說這是同一門生意嗎？財報課裡的「投入資本報酬率」問的就是這件事——你綁住多少錢，換回多少利潤。利潤池地圖只是把同一個問題，一次問遍整個產業的每一個環節，而不是只問一家公司。所以一個環節就算只分到比較小的一塊美元利潤，只要它幾乎不用綁錢就賺得到，它很可能比那個分到大塊、卻先堆了一座資本大山的環節，體質好上許多。

一組真實的對照，比任何示意表格都更能講清楚這件事。台積電是全球最領先的先進邏輯製程代工廠，2024 年營益率約 45.7%，這是一個真正亮眼的數字，但同一年它得花大約 298 億美元的資本支出，才撐得起讓這個數字成立的晶圓廠新建與升級，而且 2025 年的資本支出指引還更高。輝達（Nvidia）是一家向台積電採購晶圓、自己不蓋晶圓廠的無晶圓廠設計商，近期非公認會計原則（non-GAAP）毛利率約 75%，建立在相對極小的資本基礎上，因為它完全不擁有製造產能。兩家公司都站在同一條 AI 硬體鏈的頂端，也都是任何標準下的優秀生意——但一家得先養一座跨越數十年的資本密集基礎建設才賺得到那個利潤率，另一家幾乎不用。單看利潤率的好壞分不出兩者的差別，只有把利潤除以投入資本才分得出來。

Worked Example: Mapping a Profit Pool Across Four Chain Steps逐步範例：畫出四環節鏈的利潤池分布
  1. 1

    Start from the table above: each step's revenue and operating margin, pulled from its own segment disclosure. (The figures are illustrative teaching numbers chosen to sit at plausible industry magnitudes, not any real company's reported results — the point is the method, and the method works identically on real filings.)

    從上面的表格出發：每個環節各自的營收與營益率，來自它自己的分部揭露。（表中數字是教學用的示意值，量級刻意貼近真實產業，但不是任何一家真實公司申報的數字——重點在方法，而這套方法套到真實財報上一模一樣。）

  2. 2

    Convert margin into dollars for each step: operating profit = revenue x operating margin. Foundry: 25x40%=$10B. Designer: 60x40%=$24B. Assembler: 90x5%=$4.5B. Integrator: 150x6%=$9B.

    把每個環節的營益率換算成美元：營業利益＝營收×營益率。代工廠：25×40%＝100 億美元。設計商：60×40%＝240 億美元。組裝商：90×5%＝45 億美元。整合商：150×6%＝90 億美元。

  3. 3

    Sum the four dollar figures to get the total chain profit pool: 10 + 24 + 4.5 + 9 = $47.5B.

    把四個美元數字加總，得到整條鏈的利潤池總額：10 ＋ 24 ＋ 4.5 ＋ 9 ＝ 47.5 億美元。

  4. 4

    Compute each step's share of the pool: step profit / total pool. Foundry 10/47.5≈21%. Designer 24/47.5≈51%. Assembler 4.5/47.5≈9%. Integrator 9/47.5≈19%.

    算出每個環節在整池利潤裡的占比：環節利潤 ÷ 利潤池總額。代工廠 10/47.5≈21%。設計商 24/47.5≈51%。組裝商 4.5/47.5≈9%。整合商 9/47.5≈19%。

  5. 5

    Now divide each step's dollar profit by its invested capital to see how hard that capital is working: profit / invested capital. Foundry 10/50=20%. Designer 24/30=80%. Assembler 4.5/18=25%. Integrator 9/20=45%.

    再把每個環節的美元利潤除以投入資本，看資本被用得有多賣力：利潤 ÷ 投入資本。代工廠 10/50＝20%。設計商 24/30＝80%。組裝商 4.5/18＝25%。整合商 9/20＝45%。

  6. 6

    Line up three separate rankings. By revenue: integrator > assembler > designer > foundry. By dollar profit: designer > foundry > integrator > assembler. By profit-per-capital: designer > integrator > assembler > foundry. All three orderings are different, and every one of them is a true statement about the same four companies.

    把三種排序並排比較。按營收：整合商＞組裝商＞設計商＞代工廠。按美元利潤：設計商＞代工廠＞整合商＞組裝商。按資本效率：設計商＞整合商＞組裝商＞代工廠。三種名次完全不同，而且每一種都是對同樣這四家公司的正確描述。

  7. 7

    Read the whole picture together, and notice the single most instructive comparison in the table: the foundry and the designer post the same 40% operating margin, yet one earns 20% on its capital and the other 80%. The margin line is identical; the businesses are not remotely alike, because leading-edge fabrication has to fund a mountain of capital before it earns a dollar and the designer does not. Two more readings fall out of the same table. The integrator dominates revenue — two and a half times the designer's — and still keeps under a fifth of the pool, which is the clearest possible statement that revenue size is not a claim on profit. And the assembler, the obvious loser on margin, actually turns 25% on its capital, beating the foundry: a thin margin on a fast-turning, capital-light base is not automatically the worst business in the chain. Its real fragility is different — with only five cents of margin per dollar of sales, a modest price cut or one soft quarter wipes the profit out entirely, which is exactly the squeeze the opening case described.

    把整幅圖一起看，先注意表裡最有教育意義的一組對照：代工廠和設計商的營益率一模一樣，都是 40%，但一個的資本報酬是 20%，另一個是 80%。營益率那一行完全相同，兩門生意卻毫不相像，因為最先進的製程必須先養一座資本大山才賺得到第一塊錢，設計商不用。同一張表還能讀出另外兩件事。整合商營收稱霸，是設計商的 2.5 倍，卻只留下不到五分之一的利潤池，這是「營收規模不等於對利潤的主張權」最清楚的一句註解。而營益率看起來最難看的組裝商，資本報酬其實有 25%，還贏過代工廠：薄利搭配周轉快、資本輕的底盤，並不自動等於這條鏈上最差的生意。它真正的脆弱處在別的地方——每一塊錢營收只有五分錢的緩衝，一次小幅殺價或一個淡季就足以把利潤整個抹平，而這正是開場案例描述的那種壓榨。

  8. 8

    One condition would flip this whole ranking, and it is worth naming so you know when to distrust it: invested capital that has not finished aging into the numbers yet. A foundry mid-way through building a new fab shows that fab's cost on its balance sheet immediately, but the extra profit it will eventually produce has not shown up in the income statement yet — for a year or two, that lag makes the foundry's ROIC look artificially worse than it will settle at once the new capacity is running and earning. The reverse mistake is just as common: a designer or integrator that has been quietly under-investing in R&D or capacity can show a flattering ROIC for a while, right up until the underinvestment catches up with its competitive position. Any single-year ROIC snapshot deserves a follow-up question — is invested capital here still ramping, still depreciating, or already mature — before you treat the ranking as settled.

    有一種情況會把整個排序反過來，值得特別點名，讓你知道什麼時候該對這個排序打個問號：投入資本還沒「熟成」進數字裡。一家蓋到一半新廠的代工廠，廠房成本會立刻反映在資產負債表上，但這座廠未來會賺到的額外利潤，卻還沒出現在損益表裡——有一兩年的時間，這個時間差會讓代工廠的資本報酬率看起來比它日後產能開出、開始賺錢之後應該落在的水準還要差。反過來的錯誤同樣常見：一家在研發或產能上悄悄少投資的設計商或整合商，短期內可能顯得資本報酬率很漂亮，一直到少投資的後果反映到競爭地位上為止。任何單一年度的資本報酬率快照，都值得多問一句——這裡的投入資本現在是還在爬坡、還在攤提，還是已經成熟——再決定要不要把這個排序當成定論。

▲

When you see yourself comparing two chain steps on gross margin, what is usually actually true is that you are comparing two different accounting shapes, not two different levels of profitability. A fabless chip designer books almost nothing in cost of goods sold and carries its enormous research spending below the gross-margin line; a foundry carries factory depreciation right inside cost of goods sold. The designer's 70% gross margin and the foundry's 45% can end up at a similar operating margin once each has paid for what actually makes its business work. Compare steps on operating margin, and if you must use gross margin, check where research and depreciation sit first. A second, quieter version of the same error: do not add up the revenue of every step and call it "industry size." Each step's revenue already contains what it paid the step above it, so summing them double-counts the chain several times over. Revenue does not add up along a chain; dollar profit does.

當你發現自己正在用毛利率比較鏈上的兩個環節，通常真正的情況是：你比的是兩種不同的會計形狀，不是兩種不同的賺錢能力。一家無晶圓廠的晶片設計商，銷貨成本裡幾乎放不了什麼東西，它龐大的研發支出是掛在毛利率那條線的下面；一家晶圓代工廠則是把廠房折舊直接算進銷貨成本裡。設計商的 70% 毛利率和代工廠的 45%，等到各自把真正讓生意成立的成本都付完之後，營益率可能相當接近。比環節請用營益率；如果非用毛利率不可，先確認研發與折舊各自被放在哪一條線的上面或下面。同一個錯誤還有一個比較安靜的版本：不要把每個環節的營收加總，然後說這就是「產業規模」。每個環節的營收裡，都已經含著它付給上游那一段的錢，加總等於把整條鏈重複計算好幾次。營收在一條鏈上不能相加，能相加的是美元利潤。

Purity: Do the Division Yourself Before You Believe the Story

純度：先自己算出來，再相信那個故事

A market narrative loves to label a stock a "pure play" on whatever theme is hot — a pure AI-infrastructure name, a pure electrification name, a pure whatever-is-in-the-headlines name. The number that actually answers the question is purityPlain language: the share of a company's total revenue that actually comes from the specific industry theme you are researching, worked out from segment disclosures rather than assumed from a company's marketing pitch.: the share of a company's total revenue that is actually attributable to the theme in question, worked out as a simple division — segment revenue tied to the theme, divided by total company revenue. It sounds almost too basic to be worth stating, and that is exactly why it gets skipped. A company has every incentive to let a hot narrative attach itself as broadly as possible; nobody at the company is required to volunteer the denominator.

Here is how the mistake happens. Through the 2023-2025 advanced-packaging boom, market commentary routinely described equipment maker ASMPT (Hong Kong-listed) as a pure play on that theme. An investor who takes the label at face value has implicitly assumed something close to 100% exposure. Now do the division on the company's own reported segments. Of roughly US$1.76 billion of fiscal 2025 group revenue, the Semiconductor Solutions segment accounted for about US$0.9-1.0 billion — around 55% — and the rest came from SMT Solutions, machines that place components onto circuit boards for phones, cars and appliances, a business with nothing to do with advanced chip packaging. So the generous ceiling is roughly 55%, not 100%. And the honest number is lower still: the company separately disclosed advanced-packaging revenue of about US$532 million for the same year, roughly 30% of the group. The label said 100%; the filing says about 30%, with a defensible ceiling of about 55%. Nobody lied and no false number was published — the narrative simply never forced the denominator into the open.

The same division on a U.S.-listed name shows the pattern is not one company's quirk. Corning (NYSE: GLW) was widely re-labelled an "AI optical" name after signing multi-year fibre agreements with several large data-centre buyers. Its own first-quarter 2026 results put Optical Communications sales at about $1.85 billion out of roughly $4.35 billion of core sales — about 42%. Real, growing, and reported up about 36% year over year; also well under half the company. The other roughly 58% is display glass, solar, specialty materials and life sciences, whose demand has nothing to do with data-centre capital spending. A stock priced as if 100% of it were levered to the theme is priced against a number the filing does not support.

Two practical notes on doing the division. First: when the reported segments are coarser than the theme you care about — the segment says "semiconductor equipment," you specifically want advanced packaging — you cannot invent precision the disclosure does not contain. Use the segment you can defend as your numerator, state the answer as a ceiling ("at most about 55%, because that whole segment also contains mature wire-bonding tools"), and carry the uncertainty forward instead of rounding it away. A defensible ceiling beats a confident fiction. Second: watch which direction purity moves, and check which side moved. Purity that rises because theme revenue grew is a real change. Purity that rises because an unrelated legacy business collapsed is a shrinking denominator wearing a growth story's clothes — the company got smaller, not more thematic.

市場敘事很喜歡把一檔股票貼上「某某題材純概念股」的標籤——不管當下最熱的題材是 AI 基礎建設、電動化，還是任何當下佔據頭條的東西。真正能回答這個問題的數字是純度白話：公司總營收裡，真正來自你正在研究的產業題材的比例，要從分部揭露算出來，而不是照公司的行銷說法照單全收。：這家公司的總營收裡，真正能歸因於這個題材的比例，用一個簡單的除法算出來——與題材相關的分部營收，除以公司總營收。這聽起來基本到近乎不值得特別講，而正因為如此，它才最常被跳過。公司有十足的動機讓熱門敘事盡可能廣泛地貼在自己身上；沒有任何一家公司有義務主動把分母講清楚。

這個錯誤通常是這樣發生的。2023 到 2025 年先進封裝熱潮裡，市場評論常把在香港掛牌的設備商 ASMPT 講成這個題材的純概念股。照單全收這個標籤的投資人，等於默默假設了接近百分之百的曝險。現在把這家公司自己申報的分部拿來做除法。2025 會計年度集團營收約 17.6 億美元，其中半導體解決方案分部約 9 到 10 億美元，大約 55%；其餘來自 SMT 解決方案，也就是把零件貼到電路板上的設備，服務手機、汽車、家電，跟先進晶片封裝毫無關係。所以最寬鬆的上限是約 55%，不是 100%。而誠實的數字還更低：同一年公司另外揭露的先進封裝營收約 5.32 億美元，只占集團約 30%。標籤說 100%，財報說約 30%，站得住腳的上限是約 55%。沒有人說謊，也沒有人發布假數字——只是敘事從來沒有逼著分母現形。

把同一個除法套到一檔美股上，就知道這不是單一公司的怪癖。康寧（Corning，紐約證交所代號 GLW）在跟幾家大型資料中心買家簽下多年期光纖合約之後，被市場重新貼上「AI 光通訊」的標籤。它自己公布的 2026 年第一季財報顯示，光通訊營收約 18.5 億美元，核心營收約 43.5 億美元，占比約 42%。這門生意是真的，年增率也報約 36%，但它連公司的一半都不到。剩下約 58% 是顯示玻璃、太陽能、特殊材料與生技，這些需求跟資料中心的資本支出沒有關係。如果股價是照「這家公司百分之百押在這個題材上」在定價，那它定價的依據，是財報撐不住的數字。

實際動手做這個除法，有兩件事要注意。第一，公司揭露的分部常常比你關心的題材還粗——分部只寫「半導體設備」，你想知道的卻是先進封裝。揭露裡沒有的精度，你變不出來。這時就拿你講得出道理的分部當分子，把答案講成一個上限（「最多約 55%，因為這個分部裡還有成熟的打線接合設備」），然後把這份不確定性一路帶著走，不要四捨五入把它抹掉。一個站得住腳的上限，勝過一個講得很有信心的虛構數字。第二，看純度往哪個方向動的時候，要追問是哪一邊在動。純度上升，如果是因為題材營收成長，那是真的改變；純度上升，如果是因為不相關的舊業務崩掉了，那只是分母縮水披上成長故事的外衣——代表這家公司變小了，不是變純了。

Takeaway收斂

"Pure play" is a marketing phrase, not a financial statement line. Purity is a number you compute, not a label you accept.

「純概念股」是行銷用語，不是財報上的一行數字。純度是你自己算出來的數字，不是你照單全收的標籤。

How to check this yourself: finding segment revenue and computing purity, step by step自己動手查：一步一步找出分部營收、算出純度

Everything in this lesson runs on one disclosure that most people never open. Here is exactly where it lives and what to do with it. Step 1 — find the document. Search the company's ticker on the SEC's EDGAR system (free) and open the most recent Form 10-K; for a company listed outside the U.S., open the annual report PDF on its own investor-relations page. Step 2 — find the segment footnote. In a 10-K it is one of the last notes to the financial statements, usually titled "Segment Information," "Reportable Segments," or "Segment and Geographic Information." Accounting rules require a company to report a segment separately once it passes roughly 10% of revenue, profit, or assets, so anything material has to appear. Search the PDF for the word "segment" and jump to the table. Step 3 — pull two columns. The table gives revenue by segment and, usually, operating income by segment, for the current year and the two prior years. Copy the segment revenue line for the segment that matches your theme, and copy total company revenue from the income statement. Step 4 — divide. Segment revenue divided by total revenue is your purity. Do it for all three years shown, not just the newest.

What counts as a normal answer: a genuine single-business company usually lands above about 80%; a diversified group with a hot-theme division commonly lands somewhere between 20% and 50% — the exact zone where "pure play" language does the most damage. Below about 20%, a theme simply cannot move the whole company's earnings much, however exciting the division is. Three specific red flags are worth naming. First, the segment is defined more broadly than the theme (a "Semiconductor" segment that also contains mature, unrelated tools) — then your honest answer is a ceiling, not a point estimate, and you say so. Second, purity jumped this year because total revenue fell — check the denominator before celebrating; a shrinking company is not a purer one. Third, the company redrew its segment boundaries this year — filings disclose restatements of prior-year segments, and a boundary change conveniently timed to a hot narrative deserves a much closer read of what moved into the flattering segment and why.

這一課講的每件事，都靠一份大多數人從來不打開的揭露。它就在這裡，用法如下。第一步：找到文件。到美國證管會的 EDGAR 系統（免費）搜尋公司代號，打開最新一份 10-K；如果是非美股，就到公司投資人關係頁下載年報 PDF。第二步：找到分部附註。在 10-K 裡，它是財務報表附註靠後的其中一則，標題通常是「Segment Information」「Reportable Segments」或「Segment and Geographic Information」（分部資訊／應申報分部）。會計準則規定，一個分部只要超過營收、利益或資產的約一成，就必須單獨揭露，所以有份量的東西一定會出現。直接在 PDF 裡搜尋 segment 這個字，跳到那張表。第三步：抓兩欄數字。那張表會給你各分部的營收，通常也會給各分部的營業利益，而且含當年度與前兩個年度。把對得上你題材的那個分部營收抄下來，再從損益表抄下公司總營收。第四步：相除。分部營收除以總營收，就是純度。三個年度都算，不要只算最新那一年。

什麼算是正常的答案：真正單一業務的公司通常在約 80% 以上；一家旗下有熱門題材事業部的多角化集團，通常落在 20% 到 50% 之間——而這正是「純概念股」這種講法殺傷力最大的區間。低於約 20%，那個題材再精彩，也很難撼動整家公司的獲利。有三個警訊值得特別點名。第一，分部的定義比題材寬（一個「半導體」分部裡還塞著成熟、不相關的設備），這時你誠實的答案是一個上限，不是一個點估計，而且你要講出來。第二，今年純度跳升，是因為總營收掉了。先看分母再高興；公司變小不等於變純。第三，公司今年重畫了分部界線：財報會揭露前期分部數字的重編，而一次剛好卡在熱門敘事上的界線調整，值得你更仔細看清楚：到底是什麼被搬進了那個比較好看的分部，又為什麼。

The ASMPT purity numbers from the section above are worth walking through as a process, because the interesting part is not the answer — it is that three different defensible answers exist for the same company at the same moment, and which one you use decides how wrong you can be. Answer one, the label: 100%, a pure advanced-packaging play. Answer two, the segment ceiling: about 55%, the Semiconductor Solutions share of roughly US$1.76 billion of fiscal 2025 group revenue. Answer three, the line item that actually matches the theme: about 30%, the roughly US$532 million of advanced-packaging revenue the company disclosed separately for that year. The other roughly 45% of the group is SMT Solutions — circuit-board assembly machines whose demand comes from general electronics and car production, and which do not care whether advanced packaging is booming or not.

Now apply the "which side moved" test from the section above, because a single year's snapshot would have hidden it. Over that fiscal year the advanced-packaging line grew about 30% year over year while the SMT side edged down. So purity was rising — and rising for the right reason, the numerator growing rather than the denominator collapsing. That is a genuinely different situation from a company whose thematic percentage climbs because a legacy business is dying, even though the two look identical if you only ever compute purity once. An investor who bought the label rather than the number owned roughly a third of the exposure they believed they had, with a large slice of the rest quietly attached to the general electronics cycle. Nothing was hidden: the segment table sits in the annual report, in public, every year.

The generalizable rule is worth more than the specific company. Any time you see a percentage claim about a company's exposure to a theme, ask what the numerator is and what the denominator is. If the claim cannot be rebuilt from two numbers you can point at in a filing, treat it as unverified until you rebuild it yourself — and if you cannot rebuild it at all, that is itself the finding. It is also worth noticing when the signal was actually available: the segment table splitting the group between semiconductor equipment and circuit-board assembly was not a late discovery — it sat in the company's own annual reports throughout the period the "pure play" narrative was being repeated in market commentary. Nothing needed to change for the purity number to become knowable; what changed was simply whether someone bothered to open the filing and do the division. That said, the division itself would be misleading if applied carelessly to a company transitioning between segments mid-year — a purity score computed from a single reporting period can be distorted by a one-off divestiture, acquisition, or segment reclassification, which is exactly why the 3-5 year trend matters more than any single year's snapshot. 上一節那組 ASMPT 的純度數字，值得把過程再走一次。有趣的不是答案，而是同一家公司、同一個時點，居然有三個都講得出道理的答案，而你選哪一個，決定了你可能錯得多離譜。答案一，標籤說的：100%，先進封裝純概念股。答案二，分部的上限：約 55%，也就是半導體解決方案分部占 2025 會計年度集團約 17.6 億美元營收的比例。答案三，真正對得上題材的那一行：約 30%，公司當年另外揭露的先進封裝營收約 5.32 億美元。集團剩下的約 45% 是 SMT 解決方案——電路板組裝設備，需求來自一般電子與汽車生產，跟先進封裝景不景氣沒有關係。

接著套用上一節那個「是哪一邊在動」的檢查，因為只算一次快照會把這件事藏起來。那個會計年度裡，先進封裝那條線年增約 30%，SMT 那一側則小幅下滑。所以純度正在上升，而且是為了對的理由在上升——動的是分子在長，不是分母在垮。這跟一家公司因為舊業務快死了、題材占比才被動變高，是完全不同的處境，但如果你一輩子只算一次純度，兩者看起來一模一樣。一個買了標籤而不是買了數字的投資人，真實曝險大約只有他以為的三分之一，剩下的一大塊悄悄綁在一般電子的景氣循環上。這件事沒有任何隱瞞：那張分部表格年年都攤在年報上，公開可查。

比這家公司本身更值錢的是能推廣的規則。任何時候你看到一個關於「某公司對某題材曝險多少百分比」的說法，先問分子是什麼、分母是什麼。如果這個說法沒辦法用你能在財報上指出來的兩個數字重建，就先當它未經查證，直到你自己把它重建一次為止。而如果你根本重建不出來，這件事本身就是一個發現。也值得留意這個訊號其實什麼時候就已經看得到：那張把集團拆成半導體設備與電路板組裝的分部表格，並不是後來才被挖出來的新發現：在「純概念股」這套敘事在市場評論裡被反覆重複的那段期間，它就一直攤在公司自己的年報裡。純度這個數字本來就查得到，需要改變的從來不是資料，而是有沒有人願意打開財報、把那個除法算一次。話說回來，如果隨便套用在一家正好在分部之間轉換的公司身上，這個除法本身也可能誤導——單一報告期算出來的純度分數，很容易被一次性的分割、併購、或分部重新分類扭曲，這正是為什麼三到五年的趨勢，遠比任何單一年度的快照更重要。（此處僅為教學案例，不代表對這家公司現在的任何看法。）

Profit Pools Migrate: Three Signals, Two Reminders

利潤池會遷移：三個訊號，兩個提醒

A value chain is never a fixed photograph. Watch for three signals that a pool is starting to move. First, vertical integrationPlain language: a company starting to make in-house what it used to buy from a supplier, or starting to sell directly to a customer it used to sell through a middleman for — either way, absorbing a neighboring step's margin into its own. from the customer side — a large buyer starting to design or build in-house what it used to purchase, a credible signal well before volumes actually shift, as the earlier think-first note on buyer leverage already covers. Second, vertical integration from the supplier side — a supplier reaching downstream to sell directly to end customers, absorbing a middleman's margin along with its own. Third, standardization: a once-differentiated component becomes interchangeable across vendors as the technology matures and more competitors learn to replicate it, and the barrier to entry that protected the pool erodes — this is the same mechanism from the earlier section on why profit sticks, running in reverse.

The most concrete, fully completed example of the first signal in recent memory is Apple's transition of its Mac computer line away from Intel processors to its own in-house-designed chips: the first Apple silicon Mac chip, the M1, was announced and shipped in November 2020, and the transition ran its full course over roughly two to three years, with the Mac Pro's move to Apple silicon in June 2023 retiring the last Intel-based model in the lineup. Apple had been a customer worth billions of dollars a year to Intel's processor business. After the switch, Apple absorbed a supplier's margin into its own product, and Intel lost a customer relationship of real scale. Nothing about this was a surprise executed overnight: the plan and its timetable were announced publicly years before the last Intel Mac shipped, which is exactly the anticipation dynamic described earlier — the profit-pool consequence was knowable long before it appeared in anyone's reported revenue.

The other two signals have equally concrete forms. Supplier-side integration is what happens when a components supplier stops selling parts and starts selling the finished thing: the shift from selling accelerator chips to selling complete rack-scale systems, or a manufacturer bypassing a dealer network to sell direct, in both cases swallowing the margin of the step below it. Standardization is the slowest and the most underestimated: once buyers big enough to dictate specifications agree on a common design, the step that used to be differentiated becomes a build-to-print order. That is what happened to branded server vendors after the largest cloud buyers standardized their own rack designs and bought directly from the original design manufacturers who had been building the machines anyway — the assembly work did not move, but the brand's claim on the profit did. The tell for all three is the same: somebody who used to have no choice suddenly acquires one.

One caution belongs right next to all three signals: an announcement is not a completed migration. Companies routinely unveil an in-house chip program, a direct-to-consumer channel, or a new in-house manufacturing line with confident fanfare, and a meaningful share of those programs later ship years behind the original schedule, get quietly narrowed in scope from "full replacement" to "one product line," or are shelved outright once the wall of engineering and capital cost becomes clear. Treat a press release or an investor-day slide as a hypothesis about where the pool is heading, not as proof it has already moved. The signal worth acting on is follow-through — a design win actually shipping in volume, a supplier's revenue from that customer actually declining — not the announcement date itself.

Two reminders belong here before moving on. First, a boundary: this whole framework works best when there is genuinely a multi-party chain to map. It has little to say about a single-location, single-product local service business with no real upstream/downstream structure, and it maps poorly onto regulated utilities or financial institutions, where profit is shaped mostly by an allowed-return regulatory formula or balance-sheet mechanics rather than competitive bargaining along a chain — a different lens, covered later in this course, fits those businesses better. Second, a pricing reminder: correctly identifying who holds today's profit pool answers "is this business worth paying attention to," not "should I buy the stock today." The market can usually see who is currently winning too, and a name correctly identified as holding the pool is very often already priced with that fact assumed. The two separate follow-up questions — can this position actually be defended, and has the price already assumed too much continuation of it — are exactly what the next several lessons in this course are built to answer.

價值鏈從來不是一張固定不動的照片。留意三個利潤池開始移動的訊號。第一，來自客戶端的垂直整合白話：一家公司開始自己生產原本向供應商採購的東西，或開始跳過中間商直接賣給原本透過中間商觸及的客戶——兩種方向都是把鄰近環節的利潤吸收進自己身上。，大客戶開始自行設計或自行生產原本向外採購的東西，這個訊號的可信度早在訂單量真正轉移之前就已經成立，呼應前面那則談買方籌碼的「先想一下」。第二，來自供應端的垂直整合，供應商往下游伸手，直接賣給終端客戶，把中間商的利潤連同自己的一起吸收進來。第三，標準化：一個曾經有差異化的組件，隨著技術成熟、更多競爭者學會複製它，逐漸變得可以在不同供應商之間互換，原本保護這個利潤池的進入門檻因此被侵蝕——這正是前面談利潤為什麼留在某個環節那一段機制的反向運作。

近年記憶裡最具體、也已經完整走完全程的第一種訊號範例，是蘋果把 Mac 電腦產品線從英特爾處理器轉換成自行設計的自研晶片：第一款蘋果自研晶片 M1，於 2020 年 11 月發表並開始出貨，整個轉換過程走了大約 2 到 3 年，最後在 2023 年 6 月，Mac Pro 也換上蘋果自研晶片，退場了產品線裡最後一款採用英特爾處理器的機型。蘋果原本是英特爾處理器業務裡一年價值數十億美元的大客戶。轉換完成後，蘋果把原本付給供應商的那一段利潤吸收進自己的產品裡，英特爾則失去一段真正有規模的客戶關係。這整件事完全不是一夕之間突襲宣布：計畫和時程表早在最後一台英特爾 Mac 出貨的好幾年前就公開了，正是前面提到的預期效應在真實世界上演——利潤池會怎麼變，遠在它出現在任何人的財報營收之前就已經可知。

另外兩個訊號同樣有很具體的樣貌。供應端的垂直整合，指的是零組件供應商不再只賣零件、開始賣成品：從賣加速器晶片改成賣整櫃等級的完整系統，或是製造商跳過經銷體系直接對終端客戶銷售，兩種都是把下一個環節的利潤一口吞下。標準化則是最慢、也最被低估的一種：一旦大到足以指定規格的買方們就共同設計取得共識，原本有差異化的那個環節就退化成照圖施工的代工訂單。這正是品牌伺服器廠商後來遇到的事——最大的雲端買家把自己的機櫃設計標準化，直接向原本就在替品牌廠組裝機器的原始設計製造商下單；組裝這件事沒有移動，移動的是品牌對利潤的主張權。三個訊號的共同徵兆是同一件事：原本沒有選擇的某一方，突然有了選擇。

這三個訊號旁邊都該放一個提醒：一則公告不等於一次已經完成的遷移。公司經常信心滿滿地宣布自製晶片計畫、直接對消費者的通路、或新的自製產線，而這類計畫裡有相當一部分後來出貨時程落後原訂計畫好幾年，或是範圍從「全面取代」悄悄縮小成「單一產品線」，甚至在工程與資本的高牆真正顯現之後直接被擱置。把新聞稿或法說簡報上的一張投影片，當成利潤池可能往哪裡移動的一個假設，而不是它已經移動的證明。真正值得據以行動的訊號是後續有沒有真的兌現——一個設計標案是不是真的量產出貨、供應商來自那個客戶的營收是不是真的下滑——而不是公告發布的那個日期本身。

往下走之前，有兩個提醒。第一個是邊界：這套工具要有一條「多方參與的鏈」可以畫，才好用。一家只有一個據點、賣一種東西的在地服務業，上下游結構本來就很薄，這套框架對它幾乎沒話可說。受管制的公用事業和金融機構也不合身，因為它們賺多少，主要是監管機關的允許報酬率公式、或資產負債表的運作方式決定的，不是靠在鏈上跟人議價得來的。這類生意要用另一副眼鏡，這門課後面會講。第二個提醒跟價格有關：找出今天誰握有利潤池，回答的是「這門生意值不值得我花時間看」，不是「現在該不該買這檔股票」。市場通常也看得出來誰在贏。所以一個你正確判定握有利潤池的名字，股價往往早就把這件事算進去了。接下來還有兩個各自獨立的問題要問——這個位置守不守得住？現在的價格，是不是已經假設它會一直守下去？這正是接下來好幾課要回答的東西。

Takeaway收斂

Finding today's profit pool is the beginning of the analysis, not the end of it. It tells you where to look next, not what to do next.

找到今天的利潤池，是分析的起點，不是終點。它告訴你接下來該看哪裡，不是接下來該做什麼。

◆Real case真實案例

Back in the PC industry example from earlier in this lesson: the "beige box" assemblers were not doomed by fate — commoditization is a process, not a permanent state, and it can in principle reverse if a step manages to re-differentiate itself. But for the better part of two decades, no PC assembler found a way to break the pattern set by Intel's processor and Microsoft's operating system, both of which retained real switching costs (an entire software ecosystem built on one instruction set, and years of user habit and file compatibility built on one operating system) long after the boxes themselves became interchangeable. The lesson for a chain-mapping exercise: check not just who holds the pool today, but which step's advantage rests on genuine switching cost or capability scarcity versus which step's advantage is just a temporary lead that competitors are actively racing to close.

This same call would have been wrong applied mechanically and forever. Intel's own dominance inside the chain it once owned outright was itself challenged years later by a substitution ceiling forming in an adjacent instruction-set architecture that offered lower power consumption for mobile devices, and then increasingly for some server workloads — a reminder that "this barrier held for twenty years" is a description of the past, not a permanent guarantee about the next twenty. The practical takeaway is not that every long-held pool eventually breaks — many do not — but that a chain-mapping conclusion has an implicit expiration check built into it: re-run the same three mechanisms (buyer concentration, substitution ceiling, barrier to entry) every time you revisit the position, rather than treating a correct call from years ago as still automatically correct today.

回到這一課稍早提過的個人電腦產業範例：那些「米色機箱」組裝廠並不是命中注定要輸——商品化是一個過程，不是永久狀態，如果某個環節真的想辦法重新做出差異化，原則上是可以逆轉的。但將近 20 年的時間裡，沒有一家電腦組裝廠找到方法打破英特爾處理器和微軟作業系統設下的格局，這兩者即使在機箱本身早已變得可以互換之後，仍然長期保有真正的轉換成本（建立在單一指令集上的整個軟體生態，以及建立在單一作業系統上的多年使用習慣與檔案相容性）。這對做鏈級分析的啟示是：不只要看今天誰握有利潤池，還要看這個環節的優勢，究竟建立在真正的轉換成本或能力稀缺上，還是只是一個競爭對手正在拚命追趕的暫時領先。

如果把這個判斷當成一勞永逸、機械式套用到永遠，反而會出錯。英特爾自己曾經完全掌控的那個環節，多年後也遭到挑戰。一種在鄰近指令集架構上形成的替代天花板逐漸成形，一開始是行動裝置更看重的低耗電優勢，後來也慢慢滲透進部分伺服器工作負載。「這道門檻守了 20 年」講的是過去，不是對接下來 20 年的保證。真正該記住的不是「長期守住的利潤池最終都會破」——事實上很多不會——而是每一個鏈級判斷都內建了一個隱形的有效期限：每次重新檢視這個位置，都要把同樣的三個機制（買方集中、替代天花板、進入門檻）重新跑一次，不要把幾年前一次正確的判斷，直接當成今天依然自動正確。

Think first: why can a stock's valuation compress before a customer's in-house replacement has shipped a single unit?先想一下：為什麼客戶自製的替代方案連一顆產品都還沒出貨，供應商的股價評價就可能已經先被壓縮？

Because a stock's valuation is a bet on a future stream of profit, not just today's numbers, and the market updates that bet the moment the probability of the future stream shrinks — regardless of whether current-quarter revenue has moved at all. A credible pilot-line announcement or a design-win loss to an in-house team lowers the probability-weighted future profit pool the supplier is expected to hold, and a rational market re-rates the multiple immediately, well before any invoice changes. This is exactly why the earlier signals — qualification announcements, capital-expenditure disclosures, credible pilot-production news — are worth tracking as leading indicators, not lagging ones.

因為股價評價賭的是一段未來的利潤流，不只是今天的數字，而市場會在這段未來利潤流的機率一縮水的當下就更新這個賭注，不管本季營收有沒有真的動一分一毫。一則可信的試產線公告，或是一場設計標案輸給客戶自建團隊，都會降低供應商未來能握有那段利潤池的機率加權預期，一個理性的市場會立刻重新評價本益比，遠早於任何一張發票真正改變。這正是為什麼前面提到的那些訊號——認證公告、資本支出揭露、可信的試產消息——值得當成領先指標追蹤，而不是落後指標。

How This Connects

這一課接到哪裡

Mapping the profit pool depends on the demand and supply pictures from the previous two lessons — you cannot judge whether a bottleneck is real without knowing the demand behind it (Lesson 03) and how disciplined or fragmented the supply side is (Lesson 04). Everything this lesson produces feeds forward in three directions: whether today's pool position can survive rising capital flooding in (Lesson 06), whether it can be attacked from outside the chain entirely (Lesson 07), and whether it is durable enough to call a genuine moat (Lesson 09). Once you can name where the pool sits and why, the very last question — has the price already assumed all of this — is the subject of a lesson much later in the course (Lesson 18).

畫出利潤池，要靠前兩課建立的需求與供給圖像——如果不知道瓶頸背後的需求撐不撐得住（第 03 課），也不知道供給端是紀律嚴明還是一盤散沙（第 04 課），就沒辦法判斷一個瓶頸是真是假。這一課產出的判斷會往三個方向繼續往前接：今天的利潤池位置能不能撐過資本蜂擁而入（第 06 課）、會不會被鏈外的東西整個攻破（第 07 課）、以及它夠不夠持久，能不能真的稱得上一條護城河（第 09 課）。等你能講清楚利潤池在哪、為什麼在那裡，最後一個問題——價格有沒有已經把這一切都算進去了——是這門課後面很久才會談到的一課（第 18 課）。

Finding today's profit pool answers where the money currently sits — it says nothing about whether that position can survive the next few years of capital chasing the same returns. That question, and why "shortage plus a flood of new capital pouring in" is quietly one of the most dangerous setups in investing, is where the next lesson begins.

找到今天的利潤池，回答的是錢現在留在哪裡——它完全沒有回答這個位置能不能撐過接下來幾年，資本蜂擁而入追逐同樣報酬的過程。這個問題，以及為什麼「短缺加上熱錢大量湧入」悄悄是投資裡最危險的組合之一，正是下一課要從頭談起的地方。
