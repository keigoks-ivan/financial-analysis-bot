Substitution and Threats from Outside the Circle

替代與圈外威脅

What You'll Learn這一課學什麼
  • Tell a real substitution threat apart from an ordinary competitor by testing whether it does the same job a different, cheaper-to-switch-to way.分辨真正的替代威脅與普通競爭對手——測試它是不是用另一種、轉換成本夠低的方法完成同一件工作。
  • Classify a threat into one of four forms — cheaper technology, an architectural shift that removes the need entirely, customer in-house build, or policy — and judge each on magnitude and time horizon.把一個威脅分類到四種形態之一：更便宜的技術、讓需求直接消失的架構轉變、客戶自研垂直整合，或政策，並各自評估命中面與時間尺度。
  • Run a mechanical scan that forces you to list every adjacent technology and industry, not just the ones you already suspect.跑一次機械掃描，強迫自己列出所有相鄰技術與相鄰產業，而不只是你原本就懷疑的那幾個。
  • Explain why an analyst's own checklist shares the analyst's own blind spot, and why only an independent, blank-page pass reliably catches a silent omission.解釋為什麼分析者自己的檢查清單會跟分析者自己共享同一個盲點，以及為什麼只有獨立、不看你草稿的第二雙眼睛才抓得到「整個沒提到」這種漏洞。
  • Stress-test the load-bearing numbers behind a substitution call, and treat any fact about a customer or supplier relationship as something with a shelf life.對支撐替代判斷的關鍵數字做對抗式查證，並記得客戶或供應商關係這類事實有保質期，要定期重新查證。

In 1975, a Kodak engineer named Steven Sasson built a toaster-sized prototype that recorded a black-and-white image onto a cassette tape at 0.01 megapixels. He showed it to Kodak's management. Kodak's own film business was, at the time, one of the most profitable franchises in American industry — gross margins above 60%, a market capitalization that would peak near $30 billion in the 1990s, and a brand that was effectively synonymous with "taking a picture." Kodak did not ignore the invention. It patented it, studied it for years, and — reasonably, given the numbers on its own income statement — chose not to push it to market, because doing so would have cannibalized the film and paper business that was paying for everything else. By the time digital cameras and then smartphone cameras made film irrelevant, Kodak's core business had nowhere to retreat to. Kodak filed for bankruptcy protection in January 2012.

1975 年，柯達的工程師史蒂芬・薩森做出一台原型機，大小跟一台烤麵包機差不多，把黑白影像錄在卡帶上，解析度只有大約 1 萬像素（0.01 百萬像素）。他把機器拿給柯達的管理層看。當時柯達的底片生意是全美最會賺錢的生意之一：毛利率（賣一件東西，扣掉成本後賺到的錢占營收的比例）超過 60%，市值（公司股票的總市場價值）在 1990 年代一度接近 300 億美元，品牌幾乎等於「拍照」這兩個字。柯達沒有忽視這項發明，替它申請了專利、研究了好幾年，最後決定不推上市。從自己損益表的數字看，這個決定當時是合理的：推數位會蠶食底片與相紙的生意，而那條生意正養著整間公司。等到數位相機、後來又是手機相機讓底片不再必要，柯達的核心事業已經無處可退。2012 年 1 月，柯達申請破產保護（跟法院申請一邊還債、一邊爭取時間重整，暫時不會被逼倒閉）。

Notice what didn't happen here. Nobody at Kodak was outcompeted by a cheaper, better film. The thing that killed the business wasn't inside the industry Kodak was watching at all — it came from outside the circle of companies Kodak counted as competitors, and it didn't just take share, it removed the need for the product altogether. That is the subject of this lesson: not "who else makes what I make," but "what else could make my product unnecessary, and would I even notice it coming."

注意這裡沒有發生的事：沒有人用更便宜、更好的底片打敗柯達。殺死這門生意的東西根本不在柯達平常盯著的那個產業裡，它來自圈外，柯達從來沒把它算進競爭對手名單。而且它不是搶走市占，是讓這個產品變得不再需要。這就是這一課要談的問題：不是「還有誰做跟我一樣的東西」，而是「還有什麼東西會讓這個產品變得多餘，這家公司會不會根本沒察覺它正在靠近」。

Telling a Real Threat from a Better Competitor

分辨真威脅與更強的競爭對手

A substitution threatAn alternative that completes the same underlying job through a different mechanism, at a switching cost low enough that people actually make the jump.換一套完全不同的方法，把客戶要的同一件事做完，而且轉換成本低到人們真的會跳過去。 is not simply "a rival makes a better version of what I sell." The test that actually separates a real substitution threat from ordinary competition is this: does the alternative complete the same underlying job the customer is really paying for, through a different mechanism, at a switching cost low enough that people actually make the jump? A compact disc losing share to a streaming subscription wasn't a story about audio fidelity — CDs often sound just as good. It was a story about the job the customer was actually hiring music for: "have the song I want, whenever I want it, without carrying anything around." Streaming did that job in a completely different way, at a near-zero marginal cost, and the switching cost (cancel one subscription, sign up for another) turned out to be almost nothing.

替代威脅An alternative that completes the same underlying job through a different mechanism, at a switching cost low enough that people actually make the jump.換一套完全不同的方法，把客戶要的同一件事做完，而且轉換成本低到人們真的會跳過去。不是「有一個對手做出更好版本的同一個產品」。真正能把替代威脅跟一般競爭分開的判準只有一條：這個替代品是不是用另一種機制，完成客戶真正在付錢買的那件工作，而且轉換成本低到人們真的會跳過去？CD 輸給串流訂閱，輸的不是音質，CD 的音質往往一樣好。真正的原因是客戶買音樂想完成的工作：「任何時候都能聽到我想聽的歌，而且不用帶著東西走」。串流用完全不同的機制做到了同一件事，多服務一個人的成本接近零；轉換成本呢？取消一個訂閱、註冊另一個，幾乎等於零。

Counter-example, and this one trips people up: an electric vehicle is usually not a substitution threat to the auto industry — it's a technology change inside the industry. The underlying job ("get me from point A to point B, in a vehicle I personally own and control") hasn't changed at all; only the powertrain has. The real substitution threat sitting one layer further out is something like autonomous ride-hailing, which would change the job itself from "own a car" to "summon a ride" — that's a different, and much larger, kind of threat, and it belongs on a different part of the scan with a much longer time horizon.

這裡有一個容易搞混的反例：電動車通常不算是對整個汽車產業的替代威脅，它是產業內部的技術轉變。客戶要完成的工作沒有變（用一台自己擁有、自己開的車，從甲地到乙地），變的只有動力系統。真正的替代威脅在更外面一層：自動駕駛的叫車服務，會把工作本身從「擁有一台車」換成「叫一趟車」。那是完全不同、也大得多的威脅，該放在掃描表的另一欄，時間尺度也長得多。

The New York City taxi medallion is a clean, dated case of what happens when the switching cost turns out to be near zero. A medallion — a government-issued license required to operate a yellow cab, and capped for decades at roughly 13,600 of them — traded for roughly $1 million or more at its peak in 2013–2014, backed by that fixed supply and a locked-in customer habit of hailing a cab on the street. The underlying job was never "own a medallion," it was "get a ride right now" — and once a smartphone app could summon a private car in a few minutes at a comparable price, the job was satisfied without a medallion at all. For-hire vehicles in the city grew from roughly 63,000 in 2015 to about 100,000 by 2018, several times the capped medallion fleet; medallion prices, which had already slid to around $500,000 by late 2016, kept falling from there, with some changing hands for a few hundred thousand dollars, and many owners were left facing debt loads far above what their medallion could resell for. Nothing about a medallion's legal exclusivity mattered once the job it protected could be done a different way.

紐約市計程車牌照是一個乾淨、有日期可查的案例，示範轉換成本一旦接近零會發生什麼事。這張牌照由政府核發，要開黃色計程車就得有一張，數量幾十年來被上限鎖在大約 1.36 萬張。2013 到 2014 年的高峰，一張牌照要價 100 萬美元以上，撐住這個價格的是兩件事：供給被鎖死，以及在路邊招手攔車這個根深蒂固的習慣。但客戶要完成的工作從來不是「擁有一張牌照」，而是「現在馬上要有一趟車」。一旦一支手機 App 能在幾分鐘內、用差不多的價格叫來一台私家車，這件工作不靠牌照也做得到。紐約的叫車與租賃車輛從 2015 年大約 6.3 萬輛，長到 2018 年大約 10 萬輛，是被上限鎖死的牌照車隊的好幾倍。牌照價格在 2016 年底已經滑到大約 50 萬美元，之後繼續下探，有些成交價只剩幾十萬美元，許多持有人背的債遠高於牌照能賣掉的價錢。牌照的法定獨佔地位，在它保護的那件工作能被別的方式完成之後，就一點意義都沒有了。

A U.S.-listed version of the same pattern, visible entirely in one company's own filings: Garmin (Nasdaq: GRMN) built a large business selling standalone satellite-navigation devices for cars. Its total revenue reached roughly $3.5 billion in 2008, with the automotive and mobile segment — the dashboard navigation units — accounting for roughly two thirds of it. The job customers were paying for was "tell me where to turn next," not "own a navigation device," and once smartphones shipped with free turn-by-turn navigation, the phone already in the buyer's pocket did that job at no extra cost. By 2013 Garmin's total revenue had fallen to roughly $2.6 billion and automotive and mobile had shrunk to roughly 40 percent of it, with the company's growth by then coming from fitness, outdoor, and aviation products instead. Two things are worth taking from this. First, the segment tables in a 10-K let you watch a substitution happen line by line, years before it becomes a headline. Second, substitution kills a job, not necessarily a company — Garmin redeployed the same engineering into jobs a phone does badly, which is the outcome the scan should also be looking for. Teaching case about how a substitution shows up in segment data, not a comment on the company today.

同一個模式也有一個美股版本，全部看得到，就寫在公司自己的申報文件裡。Garmin（納斯達克代號 GRMN）靠賣車用衛星導航機建立起一門大生意。2008 年總營收大約 35 億美元，其中車用與行動裝置這個部門，也就是放在儀表板上的導航機，大約占三分之二。但客戶付錢想完成的工作是「告訴我下一個路口要往哪轉」，不是「擁有一台導航機」。等到智慧型手機內建免費的逐向導航，買家口袋裡那支手機就把這件工作做完了，而且不用多花一毛錢。到 2013 年，Garmin 總營收掉到大約 26 億美元，車用與行動裝置只剩大約四成，公司的成長改由健身、戶外與航空產品接手。這裡有兩件事值得帶走。第一，10-K（美國上市公司每年依法要交的年報）裡的部門營收表，讓人可以一行一行看著替代發生，而且比它上新聞早好幾年。第二，替代殺死的是一件工作，不一定是一家公司，Garmin 把同一批工程能力挪去做手機做不好的工作，而這正是掃描時也該一起找的結果。這是關於替代如何顯示在部門資料裡的教學案例，不是對這家公司現在的評論。

How to see it from public information: look for the verb the customer would use to describe why they buy, not the noun for what they're buying. If two very different products satisfy the same verb ("get information to my customer," "store this safely," "get from here to there"), you have a real candidate. If the alternative only changes who wins the same purchase decision, it's ordinary competition, and the right toolkit is the one for value chains and profit pools, or the industry's capital cycle, not this one. The medallion case above is a reminder that this kind of data is often sitting in a government dataset rather than a company filing — the city's own taxi and licensing regulator published the medallion transfer prices and the ride-hailing vehicle counts used above, updated on a regular public schedule, well before either number showed up in a news headline.

從公開資訊怎麼看出來：去找客戶會用來說明「為什麼要買」的那個動詞，不是「買了什麼」的那個名詞。如果兩個很不一樣的產品滿足的是同一個動詞（把資訊送到客戶手上、把東西安全存起來、從這裡到那裡），這就是真正的候選威脅。如果替代品只是換了誰贏得同一筆生意，那是一般競爭，該用的是利潤池與資本週期那一套工具，不是這一課的替代掃描。牌照那個案例還提醒一件事：這類資料常常放在政府的公開資料庫裡，不在公司的申報文件裡。上面用到的牌照成交價與車輛數，都是紐約市計程車與執照主管機關自己定期公布的，比任何一個數字上新聞標題早得多。

Takeaway一句話收斂

Ask what job the customer is really hiring the product for — if a very different product satisfies the same job at low switching cost, you've found a real substitution candidate, not just another competitor.

先問客戶真正想用這個產品完成什麼工作。如果一個完全不同的產品，能用很低的轉換成本完成同一件工作，你找到的就是真正的替代威脅，不只是另一個競爭對手。

Form One and Two: A Cheaper Technology, and an Architecture That Removes the Need

形態一與二：更便宜的技術，以及讓需求直接消失的架構轉變

The first form is the simplest to spot because it still looks like ordinary competition from a distance: a cost crossoverThe point where a new technology's cost-per-unit-of-output drops below the incumbent's, once switching cost is netted out.新技術的每單位成本，把轉換成本算進去後，開始比舊技術便宜，這個時間點一到，換過去就開始划算了。 — a different technology does the same job, through roughly the same architecture, at a steadily falling cost curve, until it crosses below the incumbent's cost and the switch pays for itself. LED lighting replacing incandescent and fluorescent bulbs is the clean case: nobody rethought what "lighting a room" means, LEDs simply got cheap enough per unit of light output, with a low enough switching cost (screw in a bulb), that the payback period collapsed from years to months. You can watch this coming from public information well before it shows up on the incumbent's income statement: track the new technology's cost-per-unit-of-output curve against the old one, and watch for the payback period on switching to shrink below whatever threshold customers actually act on (often around 12 to 24 months for a consumer-grade purchase).

第一種形態最容易從遠處看出來，因為它看起來還像是普通的競爭：出現一個成本交叉點The point where a new technology's cost-per-unit-of-output drops below the incumbent's, once switching cost is netted out.新技術的每單位成本，把轉換成本算進去後，開始比舊技術便宜，這個時間點一到，換過去就開始划算了。。另一種技術用差不多的架構完成同一件工作，成本卻一路下滑，直到跌破舊技術，換過去這件事本身開始划算。LED 照明取代白熾燈與日光燈就是最乾淨的例子。沒有人重新定義「照亮一個房間」是什麼意思，只是 LED 每發出一單位的光愈來愈便宜，換裝又只要轉一顆燈泡，回本期從好幾年壓到幾個月。這種威脅在反映到舊技術的財報之前，就從公開資訊看得出來：追蹤新技術每單位產出的成本，對照舊技術的成本，再看轉換的回本期有沒有跌破客戶真的會動手的門檻（消費級產品常常是 12 到 24 個月）。

The crossover is measurable at industry scale, not just on a single consumer purchase. U.S. government cost estimates put new utility-scale solar generation at roughly $396 per megawatt-hour in the 2010 edition of that annual estimate, against roughly $100 for a new conventional coal plant — solar was about four times more expensive for the same job of delivering a megawatt-hour to the grid. A decade later the same agency's estimate for new solar had fallen to roughly $30 to $35 per megawatt-hour, a decline of around 90 percent, while new coal capacity had become largely uneconomic to build at all. Nobody had to guess when the crossover would happen; the two cost curves were published every year by the same government agency, and the crossing point was visible years ahead of the point where utilities actually started cancelling coal projects in favor of solar.

這個交叉點在整個產業的規模上也量得到，不只發生在單一消費者的購買決定。看美國政府每年公布的發電成本估計：2010 年那一版裡，新建的大型太陽能電廠每發 1 百萬瓦時的電，成本大約 396 美元，同一版裡新建燃煤電廠大約 100 美元。同樣是把 1 百萬瓦時的電送上電網，太陽能貴了大約 4 倍。10 年後，同一個機構對新建太陽能的估計已經降到每百萬瓦時大約 30 到 35 美元，跌幅大約 90%；同一時間，新建燃煤產能已經大致蓋不划算了。交叉點什麼時候到，不用猜。這兩條成本曲線，同一個政府機構每年都公開發布，在電力公司真正開始取消燃煤計畫、改蓋太陽能之前好幾年，交叉點就已經看得到了。

The false signal to watch for on form one is a cost curve extrapolated in isolation, while the incumbent's own curve is quietly moving too. Through the 2010s, additive manufacturing — 3D printing — was widely expected to substitute for injection moulding across mass production, and the projection itself was drawn correctly: cost per printed part was falling fast. What the projection left out was the other line. Injection moulding's cost per part at volume was also falling, and it starts from a structurally different place, because the mould is a one-time cost amortised across hundreds of thousands of identical parts while a printer pays close to full cost on every single unit. Additive won decisively in prototyping, tooling, and low-volume or highly customised parts — a real win, but a bounded one — and the mass-production crossover the headlines promised has largely not arrived. The check that would have caught this in advance costs nothing: draw both curves, not one, and ask whether the challenger's advantage still holds at the volume where the incumbent actually makes its money.

形態一要留意的假訊號，是只把一條成本曲線往前外推，忘了舊技術自己的曲線也在往下走。整個 2010 年代，外界普遍預期 3D 列印會在量產領域取代射出成型。這個推論的前半段畫得沒錯：每印一件的成本確實在快速下滑。錯在漏掉另一條線。射出成型在量產規模下的單件成本同樣在下滑，而且它的起跑點本來就不一樣——模具是一次性成本，可以攤到數十萬個一模一樣的零件上，列印機卻是每做一個都要付一次接近完整的成本。結果是 3D 列印在打樣、治具、小批量與高度客製化的零件上大獲全勝，這是真的贏，但範圍有限，新聞標題保證的那個量產交叉點大致沒有出現。事前要抓到這個錯不用花一毛錢：兩條曲線都畫出來，不要只畫一條，然後問一句，在舊技術真正賺錢的那個生產規模上，挑戰者的優勢還在嗎？

The second form is the one that actually killed Kodak, and it's more dangerous precisely because it doesn't look like a cost curve at all — it's an architectural shiftA restructuring of how the underlying activity gets done that removes an entire spending category, not just makes it cheaper.不是把一件事做便宜，是把整套做法換掉，讓客戶的某個預算項目直接整條消失。 that removes the need outright. Cloud computing is a good non-Kodak example: it didn't just lower the per-unit cost of compute — it removed the entire budget line for many companies of owning servers, leasing data-center floor space, and staffing an on-premises operations team. The line item didn't shrink, it disappeared from a large share of buyers' budgets altogether. The way to catch this from the outside is different from tracking a cost curve: watch capex-to-opex mix shifts in customer-industry filings, watch whether a role category's job postings are declining even while the underlying activity — not the old way of doing it — keeps growing, and watch whether unit volumes for the old category fall even during periods when the job itself, measured a different way, is expanding.

The Encyclopaedia Britannica is a second, older instance of the same mechanism, and it comes with a full paper trail of numbers. Britannica's printed set sold roughly 120,000 copies in 1990, its best year, but had fallen to about 40,000 a year by 1996 — years before Wikipedia existed at all, showing the erosion started with cheaper digital encyclopedias like Microsoft's Encarta, not with the free, crowd-written model that later finished the job. By the time Britannica announced in March 2012 that it would stop printing entirely, only 12,000 sets of its final 2010 edition had been printed, and just 8,000 of those had actually sold. No price cut could have fixed this, because the job readers were hiring an encyclopedia for — "look something up, right now" — no longer required a 32-volume, 32,640-page set sitting on a shelf at all; it required a search box. The underlying cost of producing an encyclopedia article barely changed. What changed is that the job stopped needing the bound-book architecture to deliver it.

第二種形態才是真正殺死柯達的那種，而且更危險，原因正是它根本不長得像一條成本曲線——是一種架構轉變A restructuring of how the underlying activity gets done that removes an entire spending category, not just makes it cheaper.不是把一件事做便宜，是把整套做法換掉，讓客戶的某個預算項目直接整條消失。，直接把需求整個抹掉。雲端運算是一個不涉及柯達的好例子。它不只是讓每單位運算變便宜，而是讓很多公司把「買伺服器、租機房、養一組地端維運團隊」這整個預算項目直接刪掉。這個項目不是變小，是從一大批買方的預算裡整個消失。要從外部抓到這種威脅，看的東西跟成本曲線不一樣，有三個地方可以看。第一，客戶產業的申報文件裡，資本支出與營運支出的配比有沒有在挪動。第二，某一類工作的職缺有沒有在減少，而那件事本身其實還在成長。第三，舊產品的銷售量有沒有在掉，而換一種方式量測的那件「工作」其實還在變大。

大英百科全書是同一種機制更早的案例，而且每一步都有數字可查。印刷版最好的一年是 1990 年，大約賣出 12 萬套；到 1996 年，一年只剩大約 4 萬套。注意時間點：這比維基百科出現還早了好幾年。所以真正先動手的，是微軟 Encarta 那類便宜的數位百科。等到 2012 年 3 月大英百科宣布完全停印，最後一版（2010 年版）總共只印了大約 1.2 萬套，其中只賣掉大約 8,000 套。降價救不了它，因為讀者買百科全書要完成的工作是「現在馬上查一件事」，而這件事已經不需要一套 32 冊、3 萬多頁、擺在書架上的書，它需要的是一個搜尋框。寫一則條目的成本幾乎沒變，變的是這件工作不再需要裝訂成冊這個形式。

Takeaway一句話收斂

Form one shows up as a cost curve you can watch cross over; form two shows up as an entire line item vanishing from customer budgets even while the underlying activity keeps growing — the second is harder to see coming and does more damage.

形態一長得像一條會交叉超車的成本曲線，你看得到它逼近。形態二長得像一整個支出項目從客戶預算裡消失，即使那件事本身還在成長。後者更難提早看見，傷害也更大。

Form Three and Four: The Customer Builds It, and the Rulebook Changes

形態三與四：客戶自己動手做，以及規則被改寫

The third form doesn't involve any new technology at all — it's your own customer deciding to become your competitor, because your margin got fat enough and the customer's volume got big enough that building the capability in-house pays for itself. This is where a genuinely counter-intuitive point sits: most people assume a high-margin, highly concentrated market position is the safest place to be. It's often the opposite. A supplier's fat margin is exactly the pool of money that makes it worth a large customer's while to absorb the fixed cost of building the capability in-house — the bigger the margin being extracted, the bigger the prize for the customer that backward-integrates around the supplier. A customer that represents a small share of a supplier's revenue and threatens to "build it ourselves" is usually a negotiating bluff, because the customer's volume doesn't clear the fixed cost of building the capability. A customer that represents a large and growing share, especially one operating a scale business with enormous internal volume of its own, is a different animal entirely. What to check from the outside: is the customer actually hiring engineers in the relevant discipline (a real signal, visible in job postings), and does a rough back-of-envelope payback calculation — annual spend with the supplier divided by an estimated cost to build the capability — clear a reasonable threshold, say under three to five years.

第三種形態不涉及任何新技術。是你自己的客戶決定變成你的對手，因為你的毛利夠肥、他的用量夠大，大到自己做在財務上划算。這裡藏著一個真正反直覺的結論：多數人以為毛利高、客戶集中的位置最安全，事實往往相反。你從客戶身上賺走的那筆肥毛利，正是客戶自己蓋一條產線的獎金。你抽得愈厚，他繞過你的誘因就愈大。判斷的關鍵在客戶的體量。一個只占供應商營收一小塊的客戶說「我們要自己做」，通常是談判時的虛張聲勢，因為他的用量跨不過自建的固定成本門檻。但一個占比大、還在成長、本身又是超大規模生意的客戶，就完全是另一回事。從外部怎麼查？兩件事：一，看這個客戶是不是真的在招相關領域的工程師，職缺公告上看得到；二，粗算一次回本期（客戶每年付給供應商的錢，除以自建這項能力要花多少），看它會不會落在 3 到 5 年以內。

A live-market instance of form three, with public numbers on both sides: at its June 2020 developer conference, Apple announced it would transition the Mac line from Intel processors to its own custom-designed chips, saying the changeover would take about two years. The first Apple Silicon Macs shipped that November, and the last Intel-based model, the Mac Pro, converted in June 2023. Apple had, for years, been one of Intel's largest single customers by chip volume; once Apple's own volume, combined with the chip-design expertise it had already built up over a decade of custom mobile processors, reached a scale where in-house silicon paid for itself, the switch happened over roughly three years — not because Intel's chips got worse, but because the economics behind form three tipped.

一個活生生的形態三案例，雙邊都有公開數字可查。蘋果在 2020 年 6 月的開發者大會上宣布，Mac 系列要把英特爾處理器換成自家設計的晶片，並說轉換大約要花 2 年。第一批用蘋果自研晶片的 Mac 同年 11 月上市；最後一款還用英特爾晶片的 Mac Pro，在 2023 年 6 月完成轉換。蘋果多年來一直是英特爾用量最大的客戶之一。當它自己的用量，加上 10 年做行動處理器累積下來的晶片設計能力，一起跨過「自己做比較划算」那條線，整個轉換就在大約 3 年內走完。英特爾的晶片沒有變差，是形態三背後的那本帳翻過去了。

The fourth form moves on a completely different clock: a regulator or government changes the rulebook, and an entire business model can become illegal, heavily taxed, or starved of a subsidy it used to enjoy — sometimes within a single legislative cycle, far faster than any technology cost curve. An export restriction that cuts a supplier off from its largest customer overnight is form four, not form one or two — no better or cheaper alternative appeared, the rules of who's allowed to buy from whom simply changed. The trap here runs the other direction from the technology forms: not every regulatory headline is a real threat. A bill that hasn't passed, or a rule with a ten-year phase-in schedule and generous exemptions, can dominate the news cycle while moving very little in practice. What to check: the actual effective date and enforcement mechanism buried in the text, not the headline, and whether the policy has durable, cross-party political support or is more likely to be diluted before it ever bites.

第四種形態走的是另一個時鐘。主管機關或政府改寫規則，一整個商業模式可能突然變成違法、被課重稅，或原本的補貼被抽掉。有時候一個立法週期就走完，比任何技術的成本曲線都快。一項出口管制在一夜之間切斷供應商最大的客戶，就是第四種形態，不是第一或第二種。沒有出現更好或更便宜的東西，只是「誰能賣給誰」的規則被改了。這裡的陷阱方向跟技術威脅相反：不是每一則政策新聞都是真威脅。一項還沒通過的法案，或一條有 10 年緩衝期、豁免條款一大堆的規則，可以占滿版面，實際上動不了什麼。所以要查兩件事：條文裡真正的生效日期與執法方式（不是新聞標題），以及這項政策背後的政治支持夠不夠穩——還是在真正咬人之前就會被稀釋掉。

A concrete instance with a public magnitude: the export controls the United States announced in October 2022, and tightened repeatedly afterwards, restricted sales of the most advanced AI accelerators to customers in China. For the leading vendors, that market had been running on the order of a fifth to a quarter of data-center revenue, and it was removed by neither a competitor nor a technology — only by a rule with an effective date. Notice the two-sided lesson, which is the part most scans miss: the same rule also created a protected home market inside China for domestic designers who could not previously win on merit against the restricted products. Form-four events rarely just subtract. They redraw who is permitted to serve whom, and the substitution frequently shows up on the other side of the line, in a competitor that the rule change made viable. Teaching case on how policy substitution works, not a view on any company involved.

一個有公開量級的具體案例：美國在 2022 年 10 月宣布、後來又多次收緊的出口管制，限制最先進的 AI 加速器賣給中國客戶。對主要供應商來說，這塊市場當時大約占資料中心營收的 20% 到 25%。拿走它的不是競爭對手，也不是某項新技術，只是一條有生效日期的規則。這裡有一個雙面的教訓，也是多數掃描最常漏掉的：同一條規則，同時在中國境內圈出一塊受保護的本土市場，讓原本在產品實力上打不過的本地設計公司有了空間。第四種形態很少只是單純做減法，它重畫的是「誰被允許服務誰」，而替代往往出現在那條線的另一邊，出現在一個因為規則改變才活得下來的對手身上。這是關於政策替代如何運作的教學案例，不是對其中任何一家公司的看法。

Takeaway一句話收斂

Form three is your own success creating the incentive for your best customer to replace you; form four moves on a legislative clock, not a technology clock, so check the effective date in the text, not the headline.

形態三是你自己賺得太好，反而給了最大的客戶取代你的理由。形態四走的是立法的時鐘，不是技術的時鐘，所以要查的是條文裡的生效日期，不是新聞標題。

The Mechanical Scan: Forcing the List Out of Yourself

機械掃描：把清單從自己身上逼出來

Turn the four forms above into a fixed procedure and run it the same way every time, regardless of whether your gut says a candidate is "obviously not worth checking." List every adjacent technology and every adjacent industry you can think of — not the ones you already suspect matter, all of them, without pre-filtering. For each one, ask the same question: if this thing succeeds, what happens to my demand — does it disappear, shrink, or just move to a different vendor? Then rate each candidate on two axes: magnitudeWhether the underlying job disappears entirely, shrinks, or merely changes hands to a different vendor.底層工作是整個消失、萎縮、還是只是換手給另一個廠商。 and time horizon (within a year, three to five years, or ten-plus). The output is not a paragraph of prose, it's a short ranked list: what's high-magnitude and near-term gets an actual tracking metric this quarter; what's high-magnitude but far-off gets a single watch signal written down now, so the question doesn't have to be re-invented from scratch later; what's low on both axes gets explicitly dismissed, in writing, with a one-line reason — not silently dropped.

把上面四種形態變成一套固定程序，每次都照同一套跑，不管直覺覺得某個候選「明顯不用查」。第一步，列出所有你想得到的相鄰技術與相鄰產業。不是你原本就懷疑的那幾個，是全部，不預先篩選。第二步，對每一項問同一個問題：如果這個東西成功了，這門生意的需求會怎樣：消失、萎縮，還是只是換一家廠商拿走？第三步，用兩條軸打分：命中面Whether the underlying job disappears entirely, shrinks, or merely changes hands to a different vendor.底層工作是整個消失、萎縮、還是只是換手給另一個廠商。，以及時間尺度（一年內、三到五年、十年以上）。產出不是一段文章，是一張排序過的短清單。命中面高、時間又近的，這一季就要有一個真的在追蹤的指標。命中面高但時間遠的，現在就寫下一個監測訊號，以後才不用從零重想一次。兩軸都低的，也要寫下來排除，附一句理由——不是悄悄跳過。

How to Look It Up: Four Free Public Sources, One Afternoon動手查：四個免費的公開來源，一個下午

Start with the company's own 10-K, Item 1A ("Risk Factors"). U.S. disclosure rules require management to name what it sees as material risks, so even a boilerplate-sounding entry names a candidate management was legally required to put in writing. Search the filing text for "substitute," "alternative," and "compete" — SEC EDGAR's full-text search covers every U.S. public company and is free. Then add three more sources: trade-press coverage of each adjacent industry you have already named; patent-filing counts in the relevant technology area, searchable free through Google Patents or the USPTO; and job postings for roles tied to a capability a large customer might build in-house.

第一個來源是公司自己的 10-K 年報，第 1A 項「風險因素」。美國的揭露規定要求管理層在這裡點名它認為重大的風險，所以就算某一條讀起來像制式八股，它點到的也是管理層依法必須寫下來的候選威脅。在文件裡搜尋「substitute」（替代品）、「alternative」（另一種選擇）、「compete」（競爭）這幾個字。美國證管會的 EDGAR（SEC 維護的公開申報文件資料庫，任何人都能免費查）全文搜尋涵蓋每一家美國上市公司。再加三個來源：你已經點名的每一個相鄰產業的產業媒體報導；相關技術領域的專利申請件數，用 Google Patents 或美國專利商標局都查得到，免費；以及跟「大客戶可能自己做」那項能力有關的職缺公告。

What counts as normal, and what is a warning. In Item 1A, one or two named substitute technologies is ordinary; a new entry that did not appear in last year's filing, or wording that moves from "may" to "has begun to," is the warning — read the two years side by side rather than reading this year alone. On patents, a slowly rising count from firms already inside the industry is normal; filings appearing from companies in a different industry, roughly doubling over two or three years, is the early mechanical signal worth putting on the tracked list. On job postings, a handful of specialist roles at a large customer is normal hiring noise; a named team being built out over consecutive quarters, in the exact discipline the supplier sells, is not. None of this needs original research — it needs someone to actually run the search instead of trusting a mental list.

什麼算正常，什麼算警訊？在第 1A 項裡，點名一兩個替代技術是常態；真正的警訊是「去年那份沒有、今年多出來的一條」，或是措辭從「可能會」變成「已經開始」——所以要把兩年的版本並排看，不要只看今年這一份。專利方面，產業內公司的申請件數緩步上升是常態；如果申請件開始從別的產業的公司冒出來，2、3 年內大約翻一倍，這就是值得列入追蹤的早期訊號。職缺方面，大客戶零星開幾個專業職缺是正常的雜訊；連續好幾季在擴編一整個團隊，而且方向剛好就是供應商在賣的那項能力，就不是雜訊了。這些都不需要什麼獨門研究，需要的只是真的去查，而不是相信腦中那份清單。

Run this against the Blockbuster case told in full in the war stories below: in 2007, streaming would have appeared on a properly forced candidate list — it existed, it was public, and it satisfied the same job Blockbuster served. A rating exercise done honestly that year would likely have scored it high magnitude, medium-to-long time horizon; a streaming catalog of about a thousand titles made "next year" look implausible, but "high magnitude, further out" was defensible even then, given how directly the mechanism matched. The scale of the challenger was public too: Netflix reported roughly 6.3 million subscribers at the end of 2006 and about 7.5 million at the end of 2007 in its annual report — small against Blockbuster's store base, but growing on a cost structure that did not need stores. That's a case where doing the scan correctly would still have left genuine judgment about timing — a harder and more honest outcome than either dismissing the candidate outright or claiming false certainty about the date.

把這套方法套到百視達身上看看（完整經過在後面的真實案例）。2007 年，如果候選清單真的被逼出來，串流一定會出現在上面：它已經存在、是公開資訊，而且滿足百視達服務的同一件工作。那一年誠實評分的話，命中面大概會評為高，時間尺度中到長。當時串流片庫只有大約 1,000 部片，「明年就會發生」確實不太可信，但「命中面高、時間比較遠」在當時就站得住腳，因為機制完全對得上。挑戰者的規模也是公開的：Netflix 年報揭露 2006 年底大約 630 萬訂戶，2007 年底大約 750 萬。跟百視達的門市規模比是小的，但它成長所靠的成本結構根本不需要門市。這個案例告訴人：掃描做對了，時間點還是難判，而這比直接把候選項排除掉，或假裝知道確切日期，都誠實得多。

There is a structural point about where this scan sits in the work. It is tempting to treat "check for substitutes" as a judgment call — run it when an industry feels vulnerable, skip it when it doesn't. That is precisely the arrangement that fails, because the judgment deciding whether to run the scan is the same judgment that would have missed the threat in the first place. The cheap fix is to demote it from a judgment to a requirement: every analysis of an industry or a company reserves one slot for an outside-threat argument, stated as a claim about mechanism and timing, and the work is not finished until that slot has something in it. Filled can mean "here is the threat, here is the metric I will track." It can equally mean "scanned this category, no first-order threat at present, and the signal that would reopen the question is a competing technology's cost per unit falling below this level." What it cannot mean is blank. A requirement that costs twenty minutes on the many occasions when there is nothing there is the cheapest insurance in the whole framework, precisely because it does not depend on remembering — remembering is the faculty that just failed.

這個掃描該擺在流程的哪個位置，有一個結構性的重點。很容易把「查一下有沒有替代品」當成判斷題：覺得這個產業危險就跑一次，覺得不危險就跳過。但這正是會失效的安排。因為決定「要不要跑掃描」的那個判斷力，跟一開始就會漏掉威脅的那個判斷力，是同一個。便宜的修法是把它從判斷題降級成硬性規定：每一份對產業或公司的分析，都固定留一個位置給圈外威脅，而且要寫成一句關於機制與時間的具體主張。這個位置填滿之前，工作不算做完。填滿可以是「這是威脅，這是我要追蹤的指標」，也可以是「這一類掃過了，現在沒有第一線威脅；會讓問題重新打開的訊號，是某個競爭技術的單位成本跌破某個水準」。唯一不能填的是空白。大多數時候這裡真的什麼都沒有，只花掉二十分鐘。但它是整套框架裡最便宜的保險，因為它不依賴「記得要查」——而記憶力剛好就是前面失效的那個東西。

One more distinction the scan has to preserve: finding a real threat is not the same as finding a bad investment. A substitution risk that has already become the consensus story — every trade headline about it, the shares changing hands at a fraction of the multiple their peers command — may be more than fully reflected in the price, and the interesting question flips to whether the decline will actually be as fast as the price now assumes. The reverse case is where this scan earns its keep: a genuine, mechanically sound threat that nobody is discussing, sitting under a multiple that quietly assumes it will never arrive. Same scan, opposite conclusions, and the difference is not in the industry analysis at all — it is in what the price already contains.

還有一個區別要守住：找到一個真實的威脅，跟找到一筆糟糕的投資，不是同一件事。有些替代風險早就變成市場共識——每則產業新聞都在講，股票用同業幾分之一的倍數在成交。這種風險可能已經被價格反映得綽綽有餘，這時有意思的問題會翻過來：衰退真的會像現在的價格所假設的那麼快嗎？相反的情況才是這套掃描真正值回票價的地方：一個機制上站得住腳、卻沒人在討論的威脅，底下壓著一個默默假設它永遠不會來的倍數。同一套掃描，兩個相反的結論。差別不在產業分析裡，在價格裡已經裝了什麼。

This tool has a clear boundary. It's the wrong instrument for garden-variety price competition among near-identical rivals inside the same architecture — two chains fighting over the same customer with the same basic format is a profit-pool and supply-discipline question, not a substitution question, and running a substitution scan on it just produces noise. It's also low-yield, though not useless, for industries sitting on an extremely slow-moving physical or technological base — the underlying physics of long-distance electricity transmission, for instance, hasn't fundamentally shifted in decades, and a mechanical scan there should still be run once, but shouldn't eat the same share of analysis time as, say, the capital-cycle question, because the marginal insight per hour spent is much lower.

這個工具有清楚的不適用邊界。第一種情況：同一種架構下、幾乎一模一樣的對手在殺價。兩家連鎖店用同樣的模式搶同一群客戶，那是利潤池與供給紀律的問題，不是替代問題，硬跑替代掃描只會產生雜訊。第二種情況：底層物理或技術幾十年沒什麼變化的產業。例如長距離輸電的物理原理，幾十年來沒有根本改變。這種產業還是該跑一次掃描，但不該讓它占掉跟資本週期一樣多的時間，因為每花一小時能挖到的東西少得多。

Takeaway一句話收斂

Run the same four-question pass on every adjacent technology without pre-filtering, rank by magnitude times time horizon, and write down even the ones you dismiss — but don't run this scan against ordinary same-architecture rivals or industries whose physical base barely moves.

對每一個相鄰技術跑同一套流程，不預先篩選，用命中面乘上時間尺度排序，連被你排除的也要寫下理由。但同架構的一般對手，或物理基礎幾乎不變的產業，不必硬套這套掃描。

Worked Example: Scanning a Local Car Dealership Network逐步範例：對汽車經銷網路跑一次替代掃描
  1. 1

    Define the job, not the product. A dealership network doesn't sell "owning a car" — dig one level deeper: it lets a local customer find, finance, and get a car serviced, all without dealing directly with a distant manufacturer. Getting this right matters because it sets what counts as a threat later — anything that lets a customer do those three things without the dealer is a candidate.

    先定義工作，不要定義產品。經銷網路賣的不是「擁有一台車」，要再往下挖一層：它讓在地客戶能找到車、辦好貸款、把車送修，而且完全不用跟遠方的製造商打交道。這個定義很重要，因為它決定了後面什麼才算威脅——任何能讓客戶不透過經銷商就完成「找車、辦貸款、送修」這三件事的東西，都是候選威脅。

  2. 2

    List every adjacent technology and business model without filtering: manufacturer direct-to-consumer sales, online used-car marketplaces, electric powertrains, subscription and car-sharing services, and autonomous ride-hailing. Two of these (direct sales, online marketplaces) barely involve any new technology — they're distribution-channel threats — while the other three involve a real change in the underlying product or usage pattern.

    不篩選，把所有相鄰技術與商業模式都列出來：製造商直銷、線上二手車平台、電動動力系統、訂閱制與共享用車、自動駕駛叫車服務。其中兩項（直銷、線上平台）幾乎不涉及新技術，是通路上的威脅。另外三項則真的改變了產品本身或使用方式。

  3. 3

    Rate each one on magnitude. magnitude = disappears / shrinks / changes hands — manufacturer direct sales: high magnitude for the sales-commission part of dealer revenue, but service and parts revenue is largely untouched. Online marketplaces: medium, they disintermediate the transaction but dealers still often supply the trade-in inventory and service work. Electric powertrains: high magnitude specifically for service and parts revenue, which has historically supplied roughly half or more of a dealer's gross profit, because EVs have far fewer moving parts and need far less scheduled maintenance. Autonomous ride-hailing: potentially very high magnitude, but it changes the job itself, which is a much bigger and slower-moving claim. Where this composition number actually comes from, so it isn't taken on faith: dealer associations such as NADA publish an annual industry report breaking out gross profit by new-vehicle, used-vehicle, service-and-parts, and finance-and-insurance — the same public source lets you re-run this exact split for any given year rather than trusting the "roughly half" figure used here.

    對每一項評命中面。命中面 ＝ 消失／萎縮／換手。製造商直銷：對銷售佣金那一塊命中面高，但保養維修的營收大致不受影響。線上平台：中等，它讓交易本身少了中間人，但經銷商往往還是提供背後的中古車庫存與維修。電動動力系統：對保養維修的命中面特別高。這一塊過去常常貢獻經銷商 50% 以上的毛利，而電動車會動的零件少很多，需要的定期保養也少很多。自動駕駛叫車服務：命中面可能非常高，但它改的是工作本身，是一個大得多、也慢得多的主張。「50% 以上」這個數字不必只憑信任：像 NADA 這樣的經銷商公會每年都會發布產業報告，把毛利拆成新車、中古車、保養維修、金融保險四塊。用同一份公開資料，你自己就能重算任何一年的拆分。

  4. 4

    Rate each one on time horizon. Direct sales: already happening, though slowed in many places by dealer-franchise laws that create a multi-year, region-by-region drag. Online marketplaces: already happening, near-term and ongoing. Electric powertrains eating service revenue: gradual, tracking the EV share of the total vehicle fleet, which typically takes ten to twenty years to move from single digits toward a majority given the average vehicle's roughly 12-year life. Autonomous ride-hailing changing car ownership itself: long and genuinely uncertain, plausibly fifteen-plus years, gated by both technology maturity and regulation.

    對每一項評時間尺度。直銷：已經在發生，但很多地方有經銷特許法規擋著，變成一州一州推、拖上好幾年的阻力。線上平台：已經在發生，而且持續進行中。電動車侵蝕保養營收：慢慢來，跟著電動車占整體車隊的比例走。一台車平均用大約 12 年，所以這個比例通常要 10 到 20 年才會從個位數走到接近一半。自動駕駛叫車改變擁車這件事：時間長，而且真的不確定，合理估計 15 年以上，技術與法規兩邊都得等。

  5. 5

    Combine the two axes and rank. Electric-powertrain service erosion sits at high magnitude and a medium (not short) time horizon — that earns an actual tracked metric today: service-and-parts share of gross profit, tracked against fleet EV penetration. Direct sales and online marketplaces are near-term but capped in magnitude by regulatory friction and the enduring physical-service leg of the job — worth a watch signal, not a top-priority tracker. Autonomous ride-hailing is high-magnitude but distant and uncertain — it gets one written-down watch signal and nothing more right now, so the analysis budget isn't spent chasing a fifteen-year story at the expense of the five-year one.

    把兩軸合起來排序。電動車侵蝕保養營收：命中面高、時間中等（不是短）。這一項現在就該有一個真的在追蹤的指標——保養維修占毛利的比重，對照車隊的電動化比例一起看。直銷與線上平台：時間近，但命中面被兩件事封頂，一是法規摩擦，二是修車這件事還是得有人實體做。這兩項設一個監測訊號就好，不必列為最優先。自動駕駛叫車：命中面高，但時間遠、不確定性高，現在寫下一個監測訊號即可。別把力氣都花在一個十五年後的故事上，而排擠掉五年內就會發生的事。

Why You Can't Find Your Own Blind Spot

為什麼你找不到自己漏掉什麼

Here's a specific and instructive failure. A piece of analysis on AI inference economics covered U.S. chipmakers and U.S. large language models in real depth — margin structure, customer concentration, the works. It did not mention China's open-weight model ecosystem at all. Not "covered it and dismissed it" — never brought it up. This wasn't a depth problem. The writer hadn't done a shallow job on the topic chosen for coverage; the writer had simply never asked the question, because the mental checklist used to decide what was worth covering was drawn from the same narrow set of sources and conversations normally paid attention to. The writer's own judgment and the writer's own blind spot came from the same place, which is exactly why "try to remember everything relevant" doesn't fix this kind of gap — nobody can remember a category they never had in the first place.

這裡有一個具體又很有教育意義的失敗。一份分析 AI 推理運算經濟的報告，把美系晶片廠與美系大型語言模型寫得很深，毛利結構、客戶集中度，一應俱全。但它完全沒提到中國的開源大模型生態。不是「提了、判定不重要」，是根本沒被提起過。這不是深度不夠。寫的人在自己選定的題目上做得並不馬虎，他只是從頭到尾沒問出這個問題。因為他用來決定「什麼值得寫」的那份心中清單，本身就是從他平常在看的那一小圈來源和對話裡長出來的。他的判斷和他的盲點來自同一個地方。所以「提醒自己想周全一點」修不好這種漏洞——你想不起一件腦中根本沒有位置放它的事。

The same failure has a dated, on-the-record version in the Nokia and BlackBerry story below, where an incumbent chief executive judged the newly launched iPhone as a phone, against other phones — the exact frame a business built on phones staying phones produces by default. The dismissal was not unreasonable on its own terms: the iPhone went on sale in the United States on June 29, 2007 at $499 for the 4GB model and $599 for the 8GB one, well above the price of a mainstream handset, and it had no physical keyboard. Every one of those objections was about the phone. None of them was about the platform underneath it. A confident public dismissal of that kind is a data point worth collecting, not just colour: it states, in searchable form, which frame someone was using, and that is the frame to test against what actually happened.

同樣的失敗，在後面諾基亞與黑莓那則故事裡有一個公開留下紀錄的版本：一位在位者的執行長，用「這是不是一支好手機」去評斷剛上市的 iPhone。那正是一門靠「手機就該是手機」撐起來的生意，預設會用的框架。就他當時看到的東西而言，這個評論並不算離譜：iPhone 2007 年 6 月 29 日在美國開賣，4GB 版 499 美元、8GB 版 599 美元，遠高於主流手機，而且沒有實體鍵盤。但這些意見全都在講那支手機，沒有一句在講它底下的那個平台。這種公開又篤定的誤判值得蒐集起來，不只是案例的花絮，它等於公開寫下了「這個人當時用的是哪一個框架」，而那個框架就是你該拿去跟後來實際發生的事對照的東西。

The structural fix is not "be more careful." It's to bring in someone who hasn't read the draft — who knows only the topic, not the conclusions — and have that person independently list, from a blank page, the first-order variables a report on this subject "should" cover: competitive substitutes, geopolitical supply risk, self-supply by the largest customers, regulation, disruption from an adjacent technology stack. Then diff that list against what actually got written. Anything missing either gets researched properly, or gets written up explicitly as "considered this, excluded it, here's why" — which is itself a real, checkable answer. Your own checklist can't catch what it was never built to notice — use an independent, blank-page pass to find silent omissions, and treat a documented "scanned, found nothing" as a real answer.

Stress-Test the Load-Bearing Number, and Remember Facts Expire

承重數字要對抗式驗證，事實也有保質期

The model behind that prediction was real — an open-weight release under the name Kimi K3, at roughly 2.8 trillion parameters — and it is worth being precise about how the wrong conclusion got reached, because the failure is reusable. The claim rested on one load-bearing number, the training bill, which was not the variable driving the outcome. What drives hardware demand is the weights that must stay resident in memory for every served instance, multiplied by how many instances the world is running at once. Re-checking the training figure would never have surfaced the error, because the training figure was probably accurate. The mistake sat one level above the arithmetic, in the link between that number and the conclusion drawn from it — and that link is where the check has to be aimed. One question does the work: which number would have to change for this conclusion to reverse? Ask it, notice the answer is not the training bill, and the claim comes apart in about a minute. Teaching case about how a load-bearing number gets checked, not a view on any model or company.

那個押注題背後的模型是真的——一個以 Kimi K3 為名發表的開源模型，參數量大約 2.8 兆個。值得把「錯誤結論怎麼被推出來」講清楚，因為這個失敗模式到處都能套用。那個主張靠的是一個承重數字：訓練花費。但訓練花費不是驅動結果的變數。真正驅動硬體需求的，是每一份服務都必須常駐在記憶體裡的模型權重，再乘上全世界同時跑著多少份。把訓練花費再複查一次，永遠抓不到這個錯，因為那個數字本來大概就是對的。錯誤在算術上面一層：錯在「這個數字」跟「從它推出來的結論」之間那條連結。檢查該瞄準的就是那條連結。一個問題就能做完這件事：要讓這個結論翻轉，哪一個數字必須改變？問完就會發現答案不是訓練花費，整個主張大約一分鐘內就散掉了。這是關於「承重數字該怎麼被查」的教學案例，不是對任何模型或公司的看法。

The writer had carried forward a description that was accurate a few quarters earlier and never went back to re-check it, which costs a calendar reminder to prevent. Teaching case about how a stale fact behaves, not a comment on any of these companies today.

事實也有保質期。這一點對上面第三種形態，也就是客戶自研，特別重要，因為整個判斷就建立在「現在誰在向誰買東西」上。看一個研究圈裡常見的說法：英特爾最先進的製程，也就是它稱為 18A 的節點，實質上只服務自家產品，沒有大到值得放進模型的外部客戶。2025 年還在寫這句話的人，其實是在重複一個早就被兩則公開消息推翻的描述。2024 年 2 月，在英特爾的晶圓代工活動上，微軟公開表示會用英特爾 18A 生產一顆自己設計的晶片。2024 年 9 月，英特爾與亞馬遜雲端服務宣布一份跨越多年、金額數十億美元等級的共同投資架構，其中包含在同一個節點上生產一顆客製化的 AI 網路晶片。這兩件事都不是內幕，也不是預測，是有日期的公開新聞稿。這正是這個失敗有教育意義的地方：要避開它，根本不需要預見未來。寫的人只是沿用了一句幾季之前還準確的描述，從來沒有回頭再查一次，防住它只要一個行事曆提醒。這是關於過期事實會怎麼表現的教學案例，不是對上述任何一家公司現在的評論。

The fix is unglamorous. Facts about relationships rot; facts about physics mostly don't. A customer relationship, an order backlog, an exclusivity status, a "no external customers" claim, a market-share figure sourced from a single industry estimate — each one should carry an explicit as-of date in the note where it lives, plus a scheduled re-check. How short that shelf life should be is set by how much weight the fact carries: a background detail can sit for a year, but a fact that a whole conclusion stands on is worth re-verifying roughly every quarter, because that is the rhythm at which contracts, earnings calls, and product announcements actually make such things public. The tell that this discipline has lapsed is a note that reads with total confidence about a customer relationship and contains no date anywhere near the claim.

修法一點都不華麗：關係類的事實會壞掉，物理類的事實大多不會。客戶關係、在手訂單、獨家地位、「沒有外部客戶」這種說法、來自單一機構估計的市占數字——這些每一項在寫下來的地方都該附上查證日期，並排定下次重查的時間。保質期設多短，看這個事實扛多少重量。背景細節放一年沒關係；撐起整個結論的事實，大約每季就該重新驗一次，因為合約、法說會與產品發表差不多就是照這個節奏把消息放出來的。紀律鬆掉的跡象很好認：一份筆記把某段客戶關係寫得非常篤定，但那句話附近找不到任何日期。

Hands-on: in fiscal 2020 — the same year Apple announced it would replace Intel processors with its own chips in every Mac — Apple's 10-K reported total revenue of $274,515 million and R&D expense of $18,752 million. What share of revenue was that, and what does it tell you about the actual size of the in-house-chip commitment?動手做：2020 財年，也就是蘋果宣布要把每一台 Mac 的處理器換成自研晶片的同一年，蘋果的 10-K 年報揭露當年營收 2,745.15 億美元，研發費用（公司花在開發新產品新技術上的支出，即 R&D）187.52 億美元。這占營收多少比例？這個數字說明了自研晶片這個決定，實際上是多大規模的承諾？

Do the division: $18,752M ÷ $274,515M ≈ 6.8% of revenue. That figure is Apple's total R&D spend across every product line that year, not a separate line item for the Mac chip transition — and it sits close to the roughly 6–7% of revenue Apple's R&D line has run at in most other years, before and after. This matters for reading the in-house-build simulator above correctly: its formula treats build cost as a fresh, standalone number, but a company the size of Apple had already been funding a large internal chip-design organization for the iPhone and iPad for years before the Mac transition was even announced. The true marginal cost of extending that existing team to Macs was almost certainly far below what a naive, from-scratch build-cost estimate would produce. The practical takeaway: before plugging a real company into that slider, check its 10-K R&D line first — a company already running a large internal design organization faces a shorter, cheaper payback than the simulator's single build-cost number implies, because most of the fixed cost was sunk before the decision was ever announced.

做一次除法：187.52 億 ÷ 2,745.15 億 ≈ 營收的 6.8%。注意這是蘋果那一年所有產品線加起來的研發支出，不是 Mac 晶片轉換單獨的一筆帳，而且這個比例跟蘋果轉換前後其他多數年份差不多，都在 6% 到 7% 之間。這一點對讀懂前面那個試算器很關鍵：它的公式把自建成本當成一個全新、獨立的數字。但像蘋果這種規模的公司，早在宣布 Mac 轉換之前好幾年，就已經為 iPhone 與 iPad 養了一支龐大的晶片設計團隊。把既有團隊延伸去做 Mac 晶片，真實的增加成本幾乎肯定遠低於「從零開始蓋一支團隊」的估算。所以實務上的結論是：把一家真實公司套進那個滑桿之前，先去看它 10-K 裡的研發那一行。一家已經養著大型設計團隊的公司，回本期會比試算器算出來的更短，因為大部分固定成本在決定宣布之前就已經花掉了。

Takeaway一句話收斂

Before a number is allowed to hold up a conclusion, try to break it yourself; and treat any fact about a customer, supplier, or contract status as something with a shelf life, not a permanent truth.

在讓一個數字撐起結論之前，先自己試著把它戳破。客戶、供應商、合約狀態這類事實都有保質期，不是永久成立的真相。

⚔War Story真實案例

Nokia's market capitalization peaked near $250 billion around the year 2000, making it briefly the most valuable company in Europe. As late as 2007 — the year the iPhone launched — Nokia still held roughly 40 percent of global mobile handset shipments, more than the next two rivals combined, and dominated smartphones running its own Symbian operating system. BlackBerry told a similar story on a smaller scale: it held well over a third of the U.S. smartphone market as late as 2009, built on a physical keyboard, secure email, and a loyal enterprise customer base. Both companies had, by any conventional competitive measure, excellent hardware, strong brand loyalty, and real technical expertise in making phones. Neither of them lost to a better phone.

What beat them was form two from earlier in this lesson — an architectural shift — even though no single component got cheaper and no rival built a better handset. The underlying job shifted from "make and receive calls and email reliably" to "carry a general-purpose computing platform where anyone can build an app." Apple's App Store, launched in 2008, and the Android ecosystem that followed, turned the phone into a two-sided marketplace — developers building for a platform, users choosing a platform for its apps — a structure neither Nokia's nor BlackBerry's business model was built around at all. By 2013, Nokia sold its entire handset business to Microsoft for roughly $7 billion, a small fraction of its peak value. BlackBerry's U.S. smartphone share had fallen under one percent within about seven years of its 2009 peak, and the company exited handset manufacturing entirely in 2016. This is a teaching case about how these two companies were displaced historically, not a comment on either company's business today.

諾基亞的市值在 2000 年前後一度接近 2,500 億美元，短暫成為歐洲市值最高的公司。到了 2007 年，也就是蘋果推出 iPhone 的那一年，它在全球手機出貨量上還握有大約四成市占，比後面兩家對手加起來還多，在自家 Symbian 系統的智慧型手機上也居主導地位。黑莓是規模較小的同一齣戲：直到 2009 年，它在美國智慧型手機市場的市占仍超過三分之一，靠的是實體鍵盤、安全的企業郵件，以及忠誠的企業客戶。用任何傳統的競爭指標看，這兩家都有優秀的硬體、強大的品牌忠誠度，以及做手機的真本事。而兩家都不是輸給一支更好的手機。

打敗它們的是前面講的第二種形態：架構轉變。沒有任何一個零組件變便宜，也沒有對手做出更好的手機。變的是那件工作本身，從「可靠地打電話、收發郵件」，換成「帶著一台任何人都能在上面開發應用程式的通用電腦」。蘋果 2008 年推出 App Store，接著 Android 生態系跟上，手機因此變成一個雙邊市場：開發者為平台寫程式，使用者為了應用程式選平台。諾基亞與黑莓的商業模式，根本不是建立在這個結構上。到了 2013 年，諾基亞把整個手機部門以大約 70 億美元賣給微軟，只剩巔峰價值的一小塊。黑莓在美國智慧型手機的市占，從 2009 年的高峰起算，大約 7 年內跌到不足 1%，公司在 2016 年徹底退出手機製造。這是關於這兩家公司歷史上如何被取代的教學案例，不是對它們現在事業的評論。

One layer worth adding: the dismissal wasn't private or hedged, it was public and on the record. In a 2007 interview, Microsoft's own CEO at the time, Steve Ballmer, said of the newly launched iPhone: "There's no chance that the iPhone is going to get any significant market share. No chance." The quote is worth keeping precisely because Ballmer wasn't a fool and wasn't short on information — he ran the company that made Windows Mobile and could see the iPhone's specs and price the same day everyone else could. The miss wasn't a data problem, it was a checklist problem: judging the iPhone against "is this a good phone" rather than "does this change what a phone is for," which is the same blind spot named earlier in this lesson.

再補一層：這個誤判不是私下講講、留有餘地的話，是公開講、留下紀錄的。2007 年的一場訪談裡，時任微軟執行長史蒂夫．鮑爾默談到剛發表的 iPhone 說：「iPhone 不可能拿到任何顯著的市占率，完全不可能。」這句話值得留著，正因為鮑爾默不是笨蛋，資訊也不缺，他經營的就是做 Windows Mobile 的那家公司，iPhone 的規格與定價他跟所有人同一天看到。所以這不是資料不足，是檢查清單的問題：他問的是「這是不是一支好手機」，而不是「這會不會改變手機的定義」。這正是這一課前面點名過的同一種盲點。

⚔War Story真實案例

A cloud-computing giant spent years concentrating nearly all of its custom AI-chip design orders with a single semiconductor design partner — that partner's share of this specific order flow ran close to 95 percent for a long stretch. More recently, the cloud giant has deliberately cultivated a second supplier for the same category of chip, and industry estimates put the original partner's share drifting down toward roughly 80 percent, with further erosion toward the mid-60s expected over the following two to three years. Nothing about the underlying manufacturing process changed — both suppliers use comparable advanced chip-design methodology on similar leading-edge nodes.

This is form three from earlier in this lesson, in its purest shape: a customer, having grown large enough and paying enough margin to its supplier, deliberately builds and funds a second source specifically to take back negotiating leverage, not because the first supplier did anything wrong. From the outside, the earliest visible signal wasn't in either company's revenue — it was in the second supplier's design-win announcements and hiring activity, months before any dollar figure moved. This is a teaching example about a documented industry dynamic, not a current view on either company.

一家雲端運算巨頭多年來把幾乎所有客製化 AI 晶片的設計訂單，都交給同一家半導體設計夥伴。這家夥伴在這類訂單裡的占比，有很長一段時間接近 95%。近期這家雲端巨頭刻意扶植了第二家供應商做同一類晶片。產業估計，原本那家的占比會滑到大約 80%，未來 2、3 年可能再往 65% 附近走。底層的製造流程完全沒有變，兩家用的是相近的設計方法，做在相似的先進製程上。

這是第三種形態最純粹的樣子：客戶規模夠大、付出去的毛利夠肥，於是自己出錢養第二家供應商。目的單純是拿回議價籌碼，不是第一家做錯了什麼。從外部看，最早的訊號不在任何一家公司的營收上，而在第二家供應商的設計得標公告與招募動態裡，比金額上的變化早了好幾個月。這是關於一個有紀錄的產業動態的教學案例，不是對任何一家公司現在的看法。

One layer deeper on where that early signal actually surfaces: a customer building a second source rarely announces it as "we are replacing our main supplier." It shows up as a design-win press release from the second supplier, as new senior hires in chip-design roles on the customer's own careers page, and — a quarter or two later — as a line in the customer's earnings-call transcript about "diversifying the supply base," phrased as prudent risk management rather than as a verdict on the first supplier. None of those three sources requires anything beyond a public search; the discipline is remembering to check all three together rather than waiting for the first supplier's own revenue to move, which is the last place the change shows up.

再挖一層：那個早期訊號會從哪裡冒出來？客戶要扶植第二供應商，很少直接宣布「我們要換掉主供應商」。它會先出現在三個地方。一，第二家供應商自己發的設計得標新聞稿。二，客戶徵才頁面上新增的晶片設計職缺。三，過一兩季後，客戶法說會逐字稿裡一句「分散供應來源」，語氣通常是穩健的風險管理，不是對第一家的判決。這三個來源都是公開查得到的。真正的紀律，是記得三個一起查，而不是等第一家供應商的營收先動——營收是變化最後才反映出來的地方。

⚔War Story真實案例

A third case sits in the same family as Nokia and BlackBerry, but the substitute this time stayed invisible for years because it looked too small to matter. Blockbuster Video built the dominant chain in home movie rental: at its peak in 2004 it operated more than 9,000 stores worldwide, employed over 84,000 people, and generated roughly $6 billion in annual revenue. The job it served was never "rent a physical tape or disc" — it was "watch a specific movie tonight" — and for most of Blockbuster's history, a store with a large in-stock catalog was simply the best available way to do that.

Netflix launched a streaming option on January 16, 2007, three years after Blockbuster's peak. On the day it launched, the streaming catalog held only about 1,000 titles against roughly 70,000 titles available in its own DVD-by-mail library — a ratio that made streaming look, correctly at that exact moment, like a rounding error next to Blockbuster's in-store selection. That comparison is exactly the false signal this lesson warns about: it measures today's version of the challenger against today's incumbent, not the two improvement rates. Streaming's real advantage was never catalog size on day one — it was that adding the thousand-and-first title cost close to nothing, while Blockbuster's advantage was a store network with leases, staff, and physical inventory that could not be expanded nearly as cheaply. Blockbuster filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection on September 23, 2010, with that year's revenue down to about $3.24 billion — near half its 2004 peak — while still operating roughly 3,300 stores at the filing date. This is a teaching case about how this specific substitution unfolded historically, not a comment on any company's operations today.

第三個案例跟諾基亞、黑莓同一個家族，差別在於這次的替代品有好幾年看起來小到不像一回事。百視達建立了家庭影片租賃業最強的連鎖網路：2004 年的高峰，全球超過 9,000 家門市、員工超過 8.4 萬人、年營收大約 60 億美元。它服務的工作從來不是「租一捲錄影帶或一片光碟」，而是「今晚想看某一部電影」。在百視達歷史的大部分時間裡，一家庫存齊全的門市，就是完成這件事最好的方法。

Netflix 在 2007 年 1 月 16 日推出串流服務，比百視達的高峰晚了 3 年。上線當天，串流片庫大約只有 1,000 部片，而它自己的郵寄 DVD 服務有大約 7 萬部。這個對比在當下看起來確實像誤差範圍，遠比不上百視達店裡的選片量。這正是這一課提醒過的假訊號：拿挑戰者今天的版本，去比在位者今天的版本，而不是比兩邊變好的速度。串流真正贏的地方，是多放第 1,001 部片幾乎不用花錢。第一天片庫的大小，從來不是重點。百視達的優勢則是一整個門市網路，店租、員工、實體庫存，沒辦法用同樣便宜的方式擴張。2010 年 9 月 23 日，百視達申請破產保護，當年營收滑到大約 32.4 億美元，接近 2004 年高峰的一半，而申請當時它還在營運大約 3,300 家門市。這是關於這個替代案例歷史上如何發展的教學案例，不是對任何公司現在營運的評論。

Think first: Blockbuster actually built a DVD-by-mail service that reached 3 million subscribers by early 2007, its fastest growth quarter ever. Why didn't building the right response save the company?先想一下：百視達確實做出了一個 DVD 郵寄服務，到 2007 年初訂戶衝到 300 萬，是公司史上成長最快的一季。為什麼做出正確的回應，還是沒能救回這家公司？

Because building the right response and being allowed to keep funding it turned out to be two different problems. Blockbuster Online launched in August 2004, priced at $19.99 a month for unlimited DVDs by mail, and reached 1.5 million subscribers by the third quarter of 2006. In November 2006 the company launched "Total Access," rolled out through 2007, which let a customer rent online and exchange the disc for a new title free at any of its thousands of U.S. stores — a real advantage Netflix could not match on its own — and the online service reached roughly 3 million subscribers in the first quarter of 2007, its best subscriber quarter on record. Then the initiative was cut. Activist investor Carl Icahn opposed the program's cost, CEO John Antioco was pushed out in July 2007, and his replacement raised online prices, ended the free in-store exchange, and reportedly turned down a proposal from Netflix's Reed Hastings around the same period. Blockbuster Online's growth stopped almost immediately afterward. The lesson isn't that Blockbuster failed to see the threat — for a moment in early 2007 it was executing against it faster than anyone else. It's that a correct strategic read can still lose to a capital-allocation fight inside the company, which is a second, separate way for the same threat to win.

因為「做出正確的回應」跟「被允許一直投錢做下去」是兩件事。百視達線上服務 2004 年 8 月上線，月費 19.99 美元、DVD 郵寄無限次數，到 2006 年第 3 季已經累積 150 萬訂戶。2006 年 11 月，公司推出「Total Access」，隔年繼續擴大：客戶在線上租片，可以直接拿到全美數千家門市免費換一片新的。這是 Netflix 自己做不到的真優勢。2007 年第 1 季，線上訂戶衝到大約 300 萬，是公司史上成長最好的一季。接著這個計畫被砍掉。維權投資人卡爾．伊坎反對這個計畫的花費，執行長約翰．安蒂奧科在 2007 年 7 月被逼退，接任者調高線上租片價格、取消免費換片，據報導同一時期也回絕了 Netflix 的里德．海斯汀提出的合作提案。百視達線上服務的成長，幾乎立刻在那之後停下來。所以教訓不是百視達沒看到威脅，2007 年初有那麼一刻，它反擊的速度比任何人都快。教訓是：策略判斷對了，還是可能輸在公司內部搶錢的那場仗。同一個威脅，因此多了第二條完全不同的勝利路徑。

Think first: why would a supplier's near-monopoly share of a single large customer be a warning sign rather than a strength?先想一下：為什麼一家供應商對單一大客戶握有近乎壟斷的份額，反而可能是警訊而不是優勢？

Because the customer notices the concentration before anyone else does, and the customer is the one party with both the incentive and the scale to do something about it. A supplier collecting close to all of one giant customer's spending in a category is, from the customer's seat, a single point of failure and a pricing hostage situation at the same time. The larger and more strategic that spending category becomes, the stronger the customer's motive to fund a second source — not because the first supplier failed, but purely to restore negotiating leverage. High concentration inside one relationship is a measure of how much upside the customer has from diversifying away, which is the opposite of a moat from the supplier's side.

因為客戶最早發現這件事，而客戶剛好是唯一有動機、也有規模去處理它的人。換到客戶的位子上看：某個品類的錢幾乎全付給同一家供應商，這既是單點故障的風險，也等於在定價上被人掐住。這筆支出愈大、愈重要，客戶去養第二家供應商的動力就愈強。不是因為第一家做錯什麼，純粹是想把議價籌碼拿回來。所以，一段關係裡的高度集中，量到的其實是「客戶分散出去能賺多少好處」——對供應商來說，這正是護城河的反面。

Think first: why should "the scan found nothing" be written down and dated, rather than just quietly moving on?先想一下：為什麼「掃描完全找不到威脅」這件事本身要寫下來並標註日期，而不是默默略過就好？

Because the alternative — silence — is indistinguishable from having never checked at all. A written, dated "scanned this category, found no material threat, here is what would change my mind" is a claim that can be revisited, falsified, and updated when conditions shift. An unwritten absence carries no such trail; six months later, nobody can tell whether the topic was considered and dismissed for good reason, or simply never crossed anyone's mind. Since the entire point of the mechanical scan is to force disposition on every candidate, the negative result is not a shortcut around the work — it is the work's actual output for that candidate.

因為另一個做法是什麼都不寫，而那跟根本沒查過長得一模一樣。寫下來、附上日期的那句「查過這一類，沒找到實質威脅，以下條件出現我就會改變想法」，是一個可以回頭檢查、可以被推翻、可以隨情況更新的主張。空白則什麼都留不下。半年後沒有人分得出來：這件事是被認真想過然後排除，還是壓根沒人想到。機械掃描的重點本來就是逼你對每個候選項都給出處置，所以「沒發現威脅」不是偷懶的捷徑——它就是這個候選項該交出來的東西。

Think first: can the company that disrupted an entire industry itself later be disrupted the same way?先想一下：曾經破壞掉整個產業的公司，自己之後有沒有可能被同樣的方式破壞？

Yes, and there is no structural reason it could not — the mechanical scan does not exempt the disruptor. The same forces that let a low-cost or architecturally different challenger dislodge an incumbent (a cheaper technology crossing a cost line, an architecture that removes the need for the category, a large customer building the capability in-house, a policy shift) apply equally to whoever holds the position today, including a company that itself arrived by disruption. Running the scan only on "old" or "legacy" companies and treating a newer disruptor's position as permanent is the same blind spot in a different costume — it substitutes a story about who is on the right side of change for the actual test of mechanism, magnitude, and switching cost.

會，而且找不到任何理由說它不會。機械掃描不會放過破壞者本身。當初讓挑戰者掀翻在位者的那四股力量（更便宜的技術跨過成本線、架構轉變讓整個品類不再被需要、大客戶把能力收回自己做、政策轉向），對今天坐在這個位子上的人一樣有效，包括那家靠破壞別人走到今天的公司。只對「老公司」跑掃描，卻預設新破壞者的位子是永久的，等於同一個盲點換了件衣服：用「誰站在變化的正確一邊」這個故事，取代了機制、命中面、轉換成本這三項真正的測試。

Pitfall: dismissing an early-stage substitute because it is "not good enough yet"陷阱：因為一個替代方案「現在還不夠好」就把它打發掉

When you see an analyst or a company executive wave away a new approach because today's version is slower, more expensive, or less capable than the established one, that comment is usually true and usually irrelevant. The right comparison is never today's version against today's incumbent — it's the challenger's improvement rate against the incumbent's improvement rate, extended over the time horizon that matters for the position being evaluated. A technology that is currently ten times worse but improving three times as fast will cross over years before the entrenched player has time to adjust its business model, and the crossover date is usually knowable well in advance from public cost curves, even while the technology still looks like a toy.

分析者或公司高層常用一句話打發新方法：「現在的版本比較慢、比較貴、做得比較差。」這句話通常是對的，但通常也不重要。真正該比的，是兩邊變好的速度，還要拉到你這個部位真正在意的年限去看，不是今天的版本對今天的在位者。一個現在差十倍、但進步速度快三倍的技術，會在在位者來不及改商業模式之前就超車。而且超車那一年通常算得出來——用公開的成本曲線就能提前推，即使它現在看起來還像個玩具。

Pitfall: running the mechanical scan as a box-ticking form instead of an actual search陷阱：把機械掃描當成打勾填表，而不是真的去搜尋

The scan's value comes entirely from forcing candidates onto the list that your own judgment would have pre-filtered out as "obviously irrelevant." If the candidate list gets written after deciding the conclusion, or only includes the two or three substitutes already famous enough to be in the news, the form has been filled out but the actual work has not happened — this produces the appearance of rigor with none of the protection. A tell that the scan was done properly: at least one or two candidates on the list should look faintly embarrassing to have considered, because a real forced list always includes some low-probability entries alongside the obvious ones.

掃描的價值全在一件事上：逼你把那些你自己一看就想跳過、覺得「明顯不相關」的候選項寫進清單。如果清單是先有結論才回頭補的，或只列了兩三個已經上過新聞的替代品，那你只是把表格填完，工作並沒有發生——看起來很嚴謹，實際上零保護力。有一個跡象可以判斷掃描有沒有認真做：清單裡至少要有一兩個候選項，是你自己覺得「列出來有點不好意思」的。真正被逼出來的清單，一定會混著幾個低機率的項目，不會只有明顯的那幾個。

Pitfall: mistaking a customer's switching cost today for a permanent moat, ignoring that it is a countdown against vertical integration陷阱：把客戶現在的轉換成本當成永久護城河，忽略它其實是對抗垂直整合的倒數計時

A high switching cost genuinely protects a supplier against ordinary competitors — a rival offering a similar product at a lower price has to overcome that cost, and often can't. But switching cost does nothing to protect against a customer who chooses not to switch to a competitor at all, and instead builds the capability internally. The customer already knows the product intimately, already has the integration built, and already has the negotiating leverage of being the buyer — the same switching cost that keeps competitors out is, for the customer's own in-house effort, closer to a head start than a wall. The size of the margin the supplier earns from that customer is the fuel for the customer's in-house investment case; the bigger and more durable the margin looks from the outside, the more it should be read as building the business case for the customer to eventually stop paying it.

高轉換成本確實擋得住一般競爭對手：對手就算產品類似又更便宜，也得先幫客戶付掉這個成本，而且往往付不起。但它擋不住另一種情況——客戶根本不換供應商，而是把這件事收回自己做。客戶對這個產品早就熟到不能再熟，系統整合也做好了，身為買家本來就握有籌碼。所以，同一個把對手擋在門外的轉換成本，對客戶自研來說比較像是助跑，不是一道牆。更麻煩的是：供應商從這個客戶身上賺走的毛利，正是客戶自研的燃料。這個毛利從外面看起來愈肥、愈穩，就愈是在替客戶累積「總有一天不要再付這筆錢」的理由。

Pitfall: treating the back-of-envelope in-house-build payback number as a precise forecast instead of a coarse screen陷阱：把粗算的自研回本期，當成精確預測，而不是一個粗篩工具

The formula behind the calculator above — annual spend times supplier margin, divided by an estimated build cost — is deliberately crude, and the times it is most dangerous are exactly when it looks most precise. A payback figure of "4.2 years" invites treating the decision as settled, but every input feeding it is soft: the margin figure is usually an outside estimate of an internal number the supplier doesn't disclose at that granularity, and the build-cost figure is almost always a first guess that omits the hardest parts of actually replicating a supplier's capability — the accumulated engineering know-how, the quality and reliability track record built over years of failures nobody outside the company ever saw, and the ongoing cost of keeping the in-house effort at parity as the supplier keeps improving too. The honest use of the tool is as a screen that sorts candidates into "clearly not worth it," "clearly worth investigating hard," and a wide middle band — not as a forecast precise enough to bet a conclusion on the second decimal place.

上面試算器的公式很粗糙：年支出乘以供應商毛利率，再除以估計的自建成本。這是刻意的。而它最危險的時刻，正好是它看起來最精確的時候。一個「四點二年」的回本期，會讓人以為事情已經定案；但餵進去的每個數字都是軟的。毛利率通常是外部估的，因為供應商不會揭露到這麼細。自建成本幾乎永遠是隨手抓的第一個數字，而且漏掉最難複製的那幾樣東西：多年失敗換來、外人看不到的工程訣竅，長期累積的品質與可靠度紀錄，還有供應商自己也在進步、你得一直追加投資才不會落後。這個工具老實的用法，是把候選項粗分成三堆：明顯不划算、明顯值得認真查，以及中間一大片模糊地帶。它不是精確到能讓你押小數點後兩位的預測。

How This Connects

這一課接到哪裡

This lesson depends on two earlier ones: where the profit pool actually sits in a value chain and who has power to move it, and which phase of the capital cycle an industry is in — both determine how attractive a category looks to an outside challenger, and how much capital is chasing its way in from adjacent industries right now. A substitution threat you identify here becomes an input to the business-model lesson that follows, since the shape of a company's business model often determines which of the four forms it's most exposed to. It also feeds directly into judging whether a moat is strengthening or weakening over time, and eventually into whether a threat is already reflected in the price — a real substitution risk that the market has already priced in deeply is a very different situation from the same risk sitting unpriced.

這一課靠前面兩課撐著。一是利潤池落在價值鏈的哪一段、誰有能力把它挪走；二是這個產業現在走到資本週期的哪一階段。這兩件事決定了一個品類在外部挑戰者眼中有多誘人，也決定了現在有多少錢正從隔壁產業擠進來。你在這一課找出的替代威脅，會變成下一課商業模式的輸入：商業模式的形狀，往往決定一家公司最容易被四種形態裡的哪一種打到。它也會餵進「護城河是在變寬還是變窄」的判斷，最後餵進「這個威脅是不是已經反映在價格裡」——市場已經狠狠定價過的替代風險，跟完全沒被定價的同一個風險，是兩種處境。

See where profit pools sit and move, the capital cycle, business model shape, whether a moat is widening or narrowing, and what a price already assumes.

參見利潤池的位置與移動、資本週期、商業模式的形狀、護城河是在擴大還是縮小，以及價格已經假設了什麼。

Checklist: before you conclude a company is safe from substitution檢查清單：在你判定一家公司不會被替代之前

Once you can tell a genuine substitution threat from ordinary competition, and can trace how far a company's economics actually depend on avoiding it, the next question is what shape the business itself takes — how it makes money, who pays whom, and where the fixed and variable costs sit. That is where the next lesson picks up.

當你能分辨出真正的替代威脅、也能追出一家公司的獲利結構到底有多依賴避開這個威脅之後，下一個問題就是這門生意本身長什麼樣子——它怎麼賺錢、誰付錢給誰、固定成本與變動成本又落在哪裡。這就是下一課要接著談的問題。
