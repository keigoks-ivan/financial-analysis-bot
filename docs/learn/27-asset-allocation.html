In 1986, three researchers at a US consulting firm — Gary Brinson, Randolph Hood, and Gilbert Beebower — studied ten years of quarterly returns from 91 large US pension funds. Their finding: a fund's policy allocation — the target mix of stocks, bonds, and cash it committed to on paper — explained, on average, about 93.6% of the variance in that fund's returns from one quarter to the next. It is one of the most cited numbers in investing literature, repeated for four decades with the same gloss: "asset allocation explains 93% of investment returns." Advisors built sales pitches on that sentence; investors used it to justify skipping any thought about which fund or stock to hold.

一九八六年，Gary Brinson、Randolph Hood、Gilbert Beebower 三位研究者，檢視了九十一檔美國大型退休基金十年間的季報酬率。結論很精確：一檔基金的「政策配置」——白紙黑字承諾的股／債／現金目標配比——平均解釋了這檔基金逐季報酬變化裡約 93.6% 的變異。這是投資界被引用最多的數字之一，四十年來被反覆簡化成同一句話：「資產配置決定了 93% 的投資報酬」。理財顧問拿這句話當銷售話術；投資人拿它當理由，完全不去想該持有哪一檔基金或股票。

That gloss is wrong, and how it is wrong matters. In 2000, researchers Roger Ibbotson and Paul Kaplan went back through the same kind of data and pulled the claim apart into three questions the original number never distinguished. Allocation explains roughly 90% of a fund's return variability over time — how bumpy the ride is. It explains only about 40% of the difference in returns between funds. And it explains close to 100% of the level of return, for an unmysterious reason: most funds, most of the time, end up close to the broad market, because most of a fund's assets simply are the market. Three questions, three answers, collapsed by decades of shorthand into one misleading number.

但這句簡化過的話是錯的，錯的方式值得細看。二〇〇〇年，研究者 Roger Ibbotson 與 Paul Kaplan 回頭檢視同一批資料，把原本沒分清楚的三個問題拆開來看。配置解釋了一檔基金隨時間波動的大約 90%——逐季顛簸的程度；只解釋了基金之間報酬差異的大約 40%；卻解釋了將近 100% 的報酬水準本身，原因不神秘：多數基金多數時候結果都跟大盤差不多，因為基金資產本質上大部分就是市場本身。三個問題、三個答案，被四十年的口耳相傳壓成一句誤導人的單一數字。

What Allocation Decides, What It Doesn't

配置決定了什麼、沒決定什麼

Put the Ibbotson–Kaplan finding in plain terms: policy allocationThe target mix of stocks, bonds, cash and real assets an investor commits to on paper, before any individual security is chosen.投資人白紙黑字承諾的股債現金實質資產目標配比，在挑選任何個別證券之前就定下來。 decides which bus you are on and how much it rattles. It decides whether a typical year swings plus or minus 20% or plus or minus 7%, and most of why your account felt calm or terrifying this year versus last. What it does not decide is whether you end up ahead of or behind someone who picked a similar bus with different passengers — that gap comes from selection, fees, timing and discipline — allocation explains only a modest slice of it. Reframed: allocation is not a search for history's highest-returning combination — it is a search for a combination whose bumpiness a specific person can actually sit through without abandoning it at the worst moment.

把 Ibbotson–Kaplan 的拆解講成白話：政策配置The target mix of stocks, bonds, cash and real assets an investor commits to on paper, before any individual security is chosen.投資人白紙黑字承諾的股債現金實質資產目標配比，在挑選任何個別證券之前就定下來。決定的是你坐上哪一班車、這班車有多顛。它決定了典型一年是上下震盪 20% 還是 7%，也是你帳戶今年跟去年相比感覺平靜還是驚魂未定的主因。它沒決定的，是你最後有沒有贏過坐了類似班車、但選了不同乘客的人——那個差距來自選股、費用、時機、紀律——配置只解釋了其中一小塊。換句話說：資產配置不是在史上所有組合裡找報酬最高的那一個，而是找一個你真的坐得住、不會在最糟那一刻棄船的組合。

Two more pieces of cost-side evidence, both arithmetic, not opinion. First, fees compound the same way returns do, in reverse. A portfolio earning 7% nominal a year for 30 years grows to roughly 1.07^30 ≈ 7.61 times its starting value; the same portfolio earning 6%, because 1 percentage point a year went to fees, grows to roughly 1.06^30 ≈ 5.74 times — a final balance about 24.6% smaller, purely from one percentage point a year of compounding (the arithmetic behind John Bogle's "cost matters hypothesis"). Second, less obvious: even a genuinely good allocation, held by a real investor, tends to earn less than the fund's own reported return, because real investors buy and sell at the wrong moments. Morningstar's Mind the Gap series tracked this: over 2014-2023, the average US fund investor earned roughly 6.3% a year dollar-weighted — weighted by when money actually moved in and out — versus the funds' own time-weighted return of roughly 7.3%, a gap of about 1.1 percentage points a year. The same research found this behavior gapThe gap between a fund's reported (time-weighted) return and what its investors actually earned (dollar-weighted), caused by buying and selling at the wrong times.基金公布的（時間加權）報酬率，跟投資人實際賺到的（金額加權）報酬率之間的落差，成因是買賣時機不對。 was smallest, at roughly 0.4 percentage points, for balanced or allocation-style funds, and largest for volatile, narrative-driven, single-theme funds — evidence that a portfolio's actual behavioral cost depends heavily on how easy it is to hold.

成本這一面還有兩件證據，都是算術，不是意見。第一，費用跟報酬用一樣的方式複利，只是方向相反。一個組合名目年報酬 7%，跑三十年會成長到約 7.61 倍起始金額；同一個組合每年被費用吃掉 1 個百分點、變成 6%，三十年只長到約 5.74 倍——最終餘額大約少了 24.6%，純粹來自每年 1 個百分點的複利（這就是 John Bogle「成本重要假說」背後的算術）。第二個較不明顯：即使配置本身不錯，真實投資人拿到的往往比基金自己公布的報酬率少，因為人常在錯的時機買賣。晨星《Mind the Gap》系列追蹤了這件事：二〇一四到二〇二三年間，美國基金投資人以金額加權（依資金實際進出時間加權）算出的年化報酬約 6.3%，同一批基金自己的時間加權報酬約 7.3%，差了約 1.1 個百分點。這個行為缺口The gap between a fund's reported (time-weighted) return and what its investors actually earned (dollar-weighted), caused by buying and selling at the wrong times.基金公布的（時間加權）報酬率，跟投資人實際賺到的（金額加權）報酬率之間的落差，成因是買賣時機不對。在平衡型／配置型基金上最小，只約 0.4 個百分點，在波動大、單一主題的基金上最大——證明組合真正的行為成本，很大程度取決於它有多容易被拿住。

The boundary worth stating: none of this makes the "best" theoretical allocation useless, only incomplete. A portfolio with the highest paper return its owner cannot sit through delivers a real-world result closer to the behavior-gap number than to the paper number — and that gap is a question about the investor at least as much as the portfolio.

這裡有個邊界要講清楚：這不代表理論上「最好」的配置沒用，只代表它不是完整答案。一個紙上報酬最高、但持有者坐不住的組合，真實結果會更接近行為缺口那個數字——這道落差，至少一半是投資人自己的問題，不只是組合的問題。

Takeaway一句話收斂

Allocation mostly decides how bumpy the ride is, not how you compare to someone else — and fees plus behavior gap both compound the same way returns do, quietly eating a return you never see subtracted anywhere on a statement.配置主要決定這趟旅程有多顛，不是你跟別人比起來怎樣——費用跟行為缺口，都用跟報酬一樣的方式複利，安靜吃掉一塊對帳單上永遠不會顯示被扣除的報酬。

The Long-Run Menu: Returns, Risk, and Why "the US" Is Not "the World"

長期菜單：報酬、風險，以及為什麼「美國」不等於「世界」

Before comparing any combination of assets, it helps to know the raw ingredients each is made of. The most exhaustive public source is the Global Investment Returns Yearbook, compiled by Elroy Dimson, Paul Marsh, and Mike Staunton, tracking returns across 21 countries back to 1900. Its 2024 edition, covering 1900–2023, gives approximate real (inflation-adjusted) annualized figures: global equities (USD, including dividends) around 5.0%; global bonds around 1.7%; cash (treasury bills) around 0.4%; US equities around 6.5%, one of the sample's best-performing markets; US bonds around 1.7%. The gap between equities and cash — the equity risk premium — runs roughly 4 to 5 percentage points a year over that span, but it is an average earned across more than a century, with entire decades where equities lost money relative to cash.

比較任何資產組合之前，先弄清楚各個原料本身長什麼樣會有幫助。目前最詳盡的公開來源，是 Elroy Dimson、Paul Marsh、Mike Staunton 三人編的《全球投資報酬年鑑》，追蹤 21 個國家回溯到一九〇〇年的報酬紀錄。它的二〇二四年版，涵蓋一九〇〇到二〇二三年，約略的實質（已扣通膨）年化數字是：全球股票（美元計、含股利）約 5.0%；全球債券約 1.7%；現金（國庫券）約 0.4%；美國股票約 6.5%，是樣本裡表現最好的市場之一；美國債券約 1.7%。股票相對現金的落差——股票風險溢酬——整段期間平均約每年 4 到 5 個百分點，但中間有好幾個完整十年，股票是輸給現金的。

The counterintuitive part is not the average — it is the survivors. An investor standing in 1900 had no way of knowing the United States would become the best-performing large market of the next century. Over that same window, stock markets in Austria, Germany, Japan, Russia, and China each fell more than 90% in real terms, in some cases a total loss, driven by hyperinflation, war, or nationalization. A backtest built only on US market history — the default in almost every public tool and textbook — is quietly built on a sample that happened to survive intact. This is survivorship biasDrawing conclusions from a sample that excludes cases that failed or disappeared — using US-only history silently assumes the US keeps being a winner, as it always was in the sample.從排除了失敗或消失案例的樣本下結論——只用美國歷史，等於默默假設美國會一直是贏家，就像樣本裡一直是那樣。, and the next lesson covers it in more depth; here, the takeaway is narrower: any number quoted from a US-only dataset needs a silent asterisk — this is what happened in a market that, in hindsight, turned out to be one of the century's winners.

這份資料真正反直覺的地方，不是平均值，而是誰活下來了。站在一九〇〇年的投資人，不可能知道美國會成為下一個世紀表現最好的大型市場。同一段期間，奧地利、德國、日本、俄羅斯、中國的股市，都曾出現超過九成的實質跌幅，有些甚至完全歸零，原因是惡性通膨、戰爭、或乾脆被國有化。一份只建立在美國歷史上的回測——這是幾乎每個公開工具、教科書的預設做法——其實悄悄建立在一個剛好完整存活下來的樣本之上。這就是存活者偏誤Drawing conclusions from a sample that excludes cases that failed or disappeared — using US-only history silently assumes the US keeps being a winner, as it always was in the sample.從排除了失敗或消失案例的樣本下結論——只用美國歷史，等於默默假設美國會一直是贏家，就像樣本裡一直是那樣。，下一課會深入處理；這裡的教訓比較窄：任何只從美國資料算出的數字，都該默默加上一個註腳——這是本世紀贏家之一的市場發生過的事。

The magnitude of risk each asset carries: volatility (annualized, dollar terms, past 50-100 years) is what makes any diversification calculation possible — US equities roughly 17–20%; intermediate-term US government bonds roughly 5–7%; long-term government bonds roughly 10–12%; treasury bills roughly 1%; gold roughly 15–20%; broad commodities 20% or more. Gold and commodities carry stock-like volatility on their own — their appeal has nothing to do with being individually calm, everything to do with how they move relative to everything else.

每個資產帶著多大風險：波動率（過去 50 到 100 年、美元計、年化）正是任何分散投資計算成立的前提——美國股票約 17–20%；中期美國公債約 5–7%；長天期公債約 10–12%；國庫券約 1%；黃金約 15–20%；廣泛大宗商品 20% 以上。黃金跟大宗商品單獨來看波動率都跟股票差不多——它們的吸引力跟「自己波動小」無關，跟「它相對其他一切怎麼走」有關。

Takeaway一句話收斂

The century-long equity premium is real but was earned by markets that happened to survive; any number built only on US history carries a silent survivorship asterisk.跨越一個世紀的股票溢酬是真的，但它是由剛好存活下來的市場賺到的；任何只建立在美國歷史上的數字，都帶著一個沒說出口的存活者偏誤註腳。

Think first: if survivorship bias is a known problem, why do almost all public backtesting tools still default to US-only data?先想一下：既然存活者偏誤是個已知的問題，為什麼幾乎所有公開的回測工具，預設還是只用美國資料？

Mostly reasons unrelated to the bias being unimportant: US data is the longest, cleanest, most standardized series in the world, and building an equivalent series for a country whose market was nationalized or hyperinflated away is genuinely hard — much of that data is fragmentary or does not exist in comparable form. Convenience is not evidence the bias does not apply; it just explains why it stays unexamined.

大多是跟「偏誤不重要」無關的理由：美國資料是全世界最長、最乾淨、最標準化的序列；替一個曾被國有化或惡性通膨吃掉的國家建出等效乾淨資料，難度很高——很多資料殘缺，或根本不存在可比較形式。資料好拿不代表偏誤不成立，只解釋了它為什麼這麼容易被放著不查。

The Arithmetic of Diversification — and Why Correlation Is Not a Constant

分散投資的算術——以及為什麼相關性不是一個常數

Diversification is often described qualitatively — "don't put all your eggs in one basket" — but it has an exact formula, and the formula reveals why the same two assets can diversify each other well in one decade and barely at all in another. For a two-asset portfolio, portfolio volatility follows σ_p² = w₁²σ₁² + w₂²σ₂² + 2·w₁·w₂·ρ·σ₁·σ₂, where w is each asset's weight, σ is its own volatility, and ρ (rho) is the correlation coefficientA number from -1 to +1 measuring how closely two assets move together — +1 always in lockstep, -1 always opposite, 0 no relationship.一個介於 -1 到 +1 之間的數字，衡量兩個資產走勢有多同步——+1 永遠同向，-1 永遠反向，0 沒有關係。 between them — a single number describing how closely the two move together. Concrete example, using figures from the previous section: equities at roughly 18% volatility and bonds at roughly 6%, in a 60/40 split. At a correlation of exactly 0 (the two move independently), the combined portfolio's volatility works out to about 11.1%. Push the same two assets into a correlation of -0.3 (bonds tend to rise when stocks fall, a genuine hedge), and the combined volatility drops to roughly 10.3%. Push the correlation instead to +0.7 (bonds and stocks tend to fall together, as they did in 2022), and the combined volatility climbs to roughly 12.6% — pushing toward the +0.9-1.0 range seen in the worst crisis windows climbs it further still, toward 13%.

「不要把雞蛋放同一個籃子」——分散投資常被講成一句口號，但它有精確公式，解釋了為什麼同樣兩個資產，某個十年分散得好，換一個十年卻幾乎沒用。雙資產組合波動率公式是 σ_p² = w₁²σ₁² + w₂²σ₂² + 2·w₁·w₂·ρ·σ₁·σ₂：w 是各資產權重，σ 是各自波動率，ρ 是兩者之間的相關係數A number from -1 to +1 measuring how closely two assets move together — +1 always in lockstep, -1 always opposite, 0 no relationship.一個介於 -1 到 +1 之間的數字，衡量兩個資產走勢有多同步——+1 永遠同向，-1 永遠反向，0 沒有關係。。具體例子：股票波動率約 18%、債券約 6%，60/40 配置。相關係數是 0 時，合成波動率約 11.1%。推到 -0.3（債券在股票跌時傾向上漲，真正的避險），合成波動率降到約 10.3%。推到 +0.7（債券跟股票一起跌，像二〇二二年），合成波動率升到約 12.6%；若逼近危機窗口常見的 +0.9 到 +1.0，還會再升到約 13%。

The reverse-intuitive conclusion sits right there: the weights never moved. Every scenario above is the same 60% stocks, 40% bonds. The entire double-digit-percentage swing in risk came from one input most investors never check, and it moves on its own. Per long-run correlation series compiled by researchers such as Antti Ilmanen at AQR, US stock-bond correlation was mostly positive through the 1970s-1990s (roughly +0.2 to +0.5, an inflation-dominated era), turned mostly negative from roughly 2000-2020 (roughly -0.3, a growth-scare era where bonds rallied on equity panics), and swung sharply positive again in 2022. The feeling that "60/40 always cushions the fall" is not a property of 60/40 — it is a property of a specific correlation regime that, historically, comes and goes.

60/40 in Practice: What Its Worst Years Actually Looked Like

60/40 的實戰紀錄：它最慘的幾年到底長什麼樣

The classic 60% stocks / 40% bonds split is not a recommendation this lesson is making — it is the most heavily studied allocation in the public record, the best available case study for how a "balanced" mix behaves under stress. Since 1926, a US 60/40 mix has compounded at roughly 8–9% a year nominal, with annualized volatility around 11–12% — tame numbers on their own. The main drawdowns tell a more useful story. In 1973–74, 60/40 fell roughly 28% nominal and roughly 40% real, because inflation was running near 12% a year and eroding both legs at once. From 2000 to 2002, it fell roughly 20%. Peak to trough of the 2007–09 financial crisis, roughly 30–32%. And in 2022, the full calendar year alone produced a loss of roughly 16–17% — equities down about 18%, a broad bond index down about 13% — one of the worst years for a combined stock-and-bond portfolio since 1937, and this lesson's fourth core historical case study.

經典的 60% 股票／40% 債券配置，不是這一課在推薦的答案——它只是公開紀錄裡被研究得最透徹的配置，正好拿來看「平衡型」配置在壓力下實際怎麼表現。自一九二六年起，美國 60/40 名目年化複利大約 8 到 9%，年化波動率大約 11 到 12%——單看這兩個數字很溫和，但幾次回撤說出更有用的故事。一九七三到七四年，60/40 名目跌幅約 28%，實質跌幅約 40%，因為當時通膨接近每年 12%，同時侵蝕股債兩腿的價值。二〇〇〇到二〇〇二年跌了約 20%。二〇〇七到二〇〇九年金融危機從高點到低點跌了約 30 到 32%。二〇二二年，光是這一整年就虧損約 16 到 17%——股票跌約 18%、一檔綜合債券指數跌約 13%——是一九三七年以來股債合成組合表現最差的年份之一，也是這一課四個核心歷史案例裡的第四個。

2022 broke a rule a generation of investors had come to treat as close to permanent: that bonds cushion a stock market decline. Equities fell as the fastest rate-hiking cycle in decades repriced growth expectations; bonds, rather than rallying as a safe haven the way they had in 2000–02 and 2008, fell alongside them — the same rate hikes devaluing existing lower-rate bonds were simultaneously compressing stock valuations via higher discount rates. A widely circulated headline that year read "60/40 is dead," and some advisors began recommending clients abandon the mix entirely. By 2023–24, the same combination had rebounded sharply, and total ten-year returns through the full episode remained solidly positive.

二〇二二年打破了一整個世代投資人幾乎當成鐵律的規則：債券能墊住股市下跌。史上數一數二快速的升息循環重新定價成長股預期，股票因此下跌；債券沒有像二〇〇〇到〇二年、或二〇〇八年那樣扮演避風港反向上漲，反而跟著一起跌——同一波升息，一邊讓既有低票息債券機械性貶值，另一邊透過較高折現率同時壓縮股票估值。當年財經媒體流傳一句被廣泛引用的標題：「60/40 已死」，甚至有理財顧問開始建議客戶徹底放棄這個配置。到了二〇二三到二四年，同樣的組合強力反彈，十年期總報酬依然穩穩為正。

The pain was real; the "death" headlines were not wrong about that — they got the diagnosis wrong. The mix hit its worst nominal year in nearly a century inside exactly the environment — sharp, synchronized, inflation-driven rate hikes — that both the correlation-regime evidence above and 1973–74 already identified as its weak spot. The mistake was never owning that weak environment; it was not knowing it in advance.

那個痛是真的；「60/40 已死」的標題沒說錯這一點——說錯的是診斷。這個配置遇到近一個世紀以來最慘的名目年份，剛好落在上面相關性環境的證據、以及一九七三到七四年那段插曲早就標示出來的弱項環境裡——急遽、同步、通膨驅動的升息。真正的錯誤從來不是持有這個弱項環境，而是事前不知道。

Diversification's Limits: International Markets, and When Everything Falls Together

分散投資的極限：跨國市場，以及當一切一起下跌的時候

Diversifying between stocks and bonds is one axis. A second, independent axis is geography, and the evidence there is at least as sobering. Japan's Nikkei 225 index hit an all-time high near 38,916 in December 1989; it did not close above that level again until February 2024 — a round trip of roughly 34 years. The US S&P 500, over its own "lost decade" from 2000 to 2009, produced a total nominal return, including dividends, of roughly -1% a year, while emerging markets over that same decade returned roughly +10% a year. Winners rotate across roughly decade-long stretches no one can reliably predict: non-US markets outperformed through the 1970s-80s, the US led through the 1990s, non-US led through the 2000s, and the US led again through the 2010s into the 2020s. Looking only backward in any of those decades would have given the wrong answer for the decade that followed.

股債之間分散是一個軸，地理位置是另一個獨立的軸，證據一樣發人深省。日本日經 225 指數一九八九年十二月觸及約 38,916 點高點，直到二〇二四年二月才再收在那之上——來回花了約 34 年。美國 S&P 500 在「失落的十年」（二〇〇〇到二〇〇九年），含股利年化報酬約 -1%，同一個十年新興市場約 +10%。贏家大約每十年輪替一次，沒有人能事先準確預測：一九七〇、八〇年代非美市場勝出，九〇年代美國領先，二〇〇〇年代非美再度勝出，二〇一〇年代到二〇二〇年代初又是美國領先。只回頭看歷史，結論常跟下一個十年的正確答案相反。

Taiwan's own broad market index tells a starker single-market story than either Japan or the US. The Taiwan Weighted Index (TAIEX) peaked at approximately 12,682 points in February 1990, at the top of an extraordinary domestic bull run driven heavily by margin-financed speculation and real estate leverage. By October of the same year — eight months later — it had collapsed to roughly 2,485, a decline of about 80%. The index did not durably reclaim its 1990 high until around 2020, a round trip of roughly three decades. The mechanism behind both rise and fall was concentrated domestic leverage: retail margin trading dominated daily volume, real estate and equity speculation fed each other, and when tightening policy and a real estate correction hit simultaneously, the same leverage that accelerated the climb accelerated the collapse.

台灣自己的廣泛型市場指數，講的是一個比日本或美國都更極端的單一市場故事。台灣加權指數一九九〇年二月觸及約 12,682 點高點，那是融資投機與房地產槓桿重度驅動的驚人多頭頂點。到同一年十月——短短八個月後——指數崩跌到約 2,485 點，跌幅約 80%。指數一直要到約二〇二〇年才真正穩固收復高點，來回花了約三十年。暴漲暴跌背後的機制是集中的本土槓桿：散戶融資交易一度占每日成交量絕大部分，房地產跟股票投機互相餵養，緊縮政策跟房地產修正同時打下來時，加速上漲的槓桿同樣加速了崩跌。

The lesson is not that Taiwan's market is uniquely dangerous. It is that any single domestic market — including one that has matured considerably since — can produce a multi-decade round trip on its own, — an investor whose equity exposure lives entirely inside one market owns that leverage cycle with nothing else behaving differently when it matters.

教訓不是「台灣市場特別危險」，而是任何單一本土市場——包括後來已成熟許多的市場——都可能自己走出一趟橫跨數十年的來回。股票曝險全活在單一市場的投資人，等於整包承接那個市場的槓桿週期——最要緊的那一刻，組合裡沒有任何一塊會表現得不一樣。

The deepest limit of diversification shows up not across decades but inside a single crisis window, and 2008 is the cleanest example. That year, US equities fell roughly 37%; developed non-US markets fell roughly 43%; emerging markets fell roughly 53%; REITs fell roughly 37%; broad commodities fell roughly 36%; high-yield corporate bonds fell roughly 26%. Nearly every category typically sold as a diversifier — different countries, sectors, asset structures — fell together, hard, at the same time. What actually held up was a narrow list: long-term US government bonds (up roughly 20% or more) and cash.

分散投資最深的極限，不是出現在跨越數十年的比較裡，而是出現在單一一次危機的窗口之內，二〇〇八年是最乾淨的例子。那一年，美國股票跌約 37%；已開發非美市場跌約 43%；新興市場跌約 53%；REITs 跌約 37%；廣泛大宗商品跌約 36%；高收益公司債跌約 26%。幾乎每個通常被當成分散工具賣的類別——不同國家、產業、資產結構——全在同一段時間一起大跌。真正撐住的只有一份短清單：美國長天期公債（漲約 20% 以上）跟現金。

The mechanism is worth naming: in a systemic liquidity crisis, correlation between risk assets of almost any stripe converges toward 1, since the driving force is not any one asset's own fundamentals but a single shared cause — a scramble for cash and safety hitting every risk asset through the same channel at once. Diversification built only across labels, not underlying risk sources, fails exactly when needed most — nearly everything with equity-like risk is, functionally, one position.

機制值得點名：系統性流動性危機裡，幾乎任何風險資產之間的相關性都會趨近 1，因為驅動的力量不是任一資產自己的基本面，而是一個共用原因——搶現金與安全性透過同一個管道同時打中每個風險資產。分散若只建立在標籤不同、不是底層風險來源不同，就會剛好在最需要它的那一刻失效——那一刻幾乎每個帶股票般風險的東西，功能上都是同一個部位。

Winners rotate across decades in a way no one can predict in advance, which is the case against home bias — and in a systemic crisis, risk-asset correlations converge toward 1 regardless of label, which is the case for diversifying across risk sources, not just names.贏家跨數十年輪替，沒有人能事先預測——這正是反對家鄉偏誤的理由；系統性危機裡，不管標籤是什麼，風險資產相關性都會趨近於 1——這正是「要跨風險來源分散、不只是跨名字分散」的理由。

Think first: in 2008, why did long-term government bonds rise while corporate high-yield bonds — also "bonds" — fell by roughly a quarter?先想一下：二〇〇八年，為什麼長天期政府公債上漲，而同樣掛著「債券」名字的高收益公司債，卻跌了大約四分之一？

Because "bond" is a label, not a risk source. A long-term government bond carries mostly interest-rate risk and benefits directly in a flight to safety. A high-yield corporate bond carries mostly credit risk — the risk the issuer itself cannot pay — and in a systemic crisis, credit risk behaves like equity risk, not government-bond risk, because the same fear of corporate failure crushes both stock prices and the value of that company's repayment promise. Sorting by the word "bond" hides this; sorting by the actual risk reveals it.

因為「債券」是一個標籤，不是風險來源。長天期政府公債主要帶著利率風險，資金逃向安全資產時直接受益。高收益公司債主要帶著信用風險——發行公司自己可能還不出錢——系統性危機裡，信用風險表現更像股票風險，不像政府公債風險，因為同一種對企業倒閉的恐懼，一邊打垮股價，另一邊打垮「這家公司會還錢」這個承諾的價值。用「債券」分類會藏起這件事；用實際承擔的風險分類才會揭露它。

Commodities deserve the same honesty. As a broad basket, their long-run real return has historically been close to zero — a volatility source, not a compounding engine. They earned their place in some mixes by doing something else: in 2022, when almost nothing worked, broad commodities rose roughly 16%, one of the very few positive lines that year. They also fell by roughly half across 2008-2020. That is the whole case for and against holding them — the same shape as the case for gold below.

🪙War Story戰爭故事

Gold is often sold as the diversifier that never fails, and its 1970s record — up roughly 24x from 1971 to 1980 as the US left the gold standard and inflation surged — is the chart most often shown. Shown far less often: what happened next. Gold peaked at roughly $850 an ounce in January 1980, and did not durably reclaim that level in nominal terms until around 2008 — a 28-year round trip. In real, inflation-adjusted terms, an investor who bought at the 1980 peak was still underwater decades later.

黃金常被賣成永遠不會失效的分散工具，它一九七〇年代的紀錄——一九七一到一九八〇年，隨美國脫離金本位、通膨飆升，大漲約 24 倍——是最常被拿出來佐證的那張圖。比較少被講的是接下來發生了什麼。黃金一九八〇年一月觸及約每盎司 850 美元高點，以名目美元計算一直要到約二〇〇八年才重新站上那個高點——來回一趟花了 28 年。以剔除通膨的實質計算，一九八〇年高點買進的投資人，好幾十年後仍是虧損的。

The mechanism behind that 28-year drought has a name: gold pays no yield. Its opportunity cost is set directly by the real interest rate — the rate after subtracting inflation — because holding gold means giving up whatever a bond or deposit would have paid instead. From 1980 onward, Fed chair Paul Volcker pushed real interest rates sharply positive to break 1970s inflation, and kept policy tight for years after. As long as real rates stayed meaningfully positive, the opportunity cost of a zero-yield asset stayed high, and gold's price reflected that drag for nearly three decades, even as inflation itself moderated.

這 28 年乾旱背後的機制有名字：黃金不配息。它的機會成本直接由實質利率決定——名目利率減掉通膨——因為持有黃金等於放棄放進債券或銀行存款原本能拿到的利息。從一九八〇年起，聯準會主席 Paul Volcker 大幅把實質利率推升為正值以打斷一九七〇年代通膨，之後好幾年都維持緊縮。只要實質利率持續明顯為正，持有零配息資產的機會成本就持續偏高，黃金價格反映這股拖力長達將近三十年，即便通膨本身早已趨緩。

The lesson is not that gold is a bad asset — in specific crisis windows it has done exactly what it was bought to do. It is that "diversifier" does not mean "cannot have a multi-decade bad stretch of its own." Even assets bought as insurance have a known weak environment; gold's is a sustained period of high, positive real interest rates.

這裡的教訓不是「黃金是爛資產」——在特定危機窗口裡，它確實做到當初被買進的理由。教訓是：「分散工具」不代表不會有長達數十年的低潮期。連特意當保險買進的資產都有已知的弱項環境，黃金的弱項環境就是一段持續、正值的高實質利率時期。

Rebalancing: What the Evidence Actually Supports

再平衡：證據真正支持的是什麼

Rebalancing means periodically selling down whatever has grown oversized and topping up whatever has shrunk, back toward original target weights. Vanguard's research on the question — first published in 2010, revisited in 2022 — tested this across a wide range of rules using US 60/40 data back to 1926: calendar-based (monthly, quarterly, annually), threshold-based (rebalance whenever a holding drifts more than some percentage from target), and combinations of both. The counterintuitive finding: the exact frequency or threshold barely matters. Annual, quarterly, and a 5-percentage-point threshold rule produced close enough return and risk outcomes over multi-decade periods that no single rule stood out — annualized returns clustered around 8.5-8.9% with volatility of roughly 11.8-12.6%. What mattered far more was whether the investor rebalanced consistently at all: a 60/40 mix left alone drifted past 80/20 as equities compounded faster, arriving at a materially higher-risk portfolio nobody ever chose.

再平衡的意思，是定期賣掉成長到占比過大的資產、補足占比萎縮的資產，把配置拉回接近原本的目標權重。Vanguard 針對這個問題的研究——二〇一〇年首度發表、二〇二二年再更新——用一九二六年以來的美國 60/40 資料，測試了一大批再平衡規則：以日曆為準（每月、每季、每年）、以門檻為準（某項持股偏離目標超過某個百分點就再平衡），以及兩者的組合。反直覺的發現：確切選哪一種頻率或門檻幾乎不影響結果。每年、每季、5 個百分點的門檻規則，在跨越數十年的期間裡，報酬與風險結果彼此相近，沒有一條明顯優於其他——年化報酬全都擠在約 8.5 到 8.9%，波動率約 11.8 到 12.6%。真正重要的，是投資人到底有沒有持續執行：放著不管的 60/40 配置，會因股票複利得比較快，一路漂過 80/20，變成一個沒有任何人選過、但風險明顯更高的組合。

A concrete case makes this less abstract. Through the market's low point on March 23, 2020, global equities (MSCI ACWI) had fallen roughly 32-34% from their February 19 peak while the broad US bond index (Bloomberg US Aggregate) was down only roughly 0-1% — a gap wide enough that an investor who started 2020 at 60/40 would have drifted to roughly 55/45 by that low. An investor who rebalanced back to 60/40 at month-end (March 31, 2020) sold bonds and bought stocks near the bottom, then rode the rebound — global equities rose roughly 20% and bonds gained a further percent or two from April through June 2020 — capturing more of that recovery than one who never rebalanced. This is not a claim rebalancing timed the bottom on purpose; the point is structural — a fixed target forces a specific action (sell what held up, buy what fell) at the moment prices are most stressed, without requiring a view on where the market goes next.

拿一個具體案例來看，會比較不抽象。到二〇二〇年三月二十三日市場低點為止，全球股票（MSCI ACWI）從二月十九日高點跌了約 32 到 34%，同期廣泛美國債券指數（Bloomberg US Aggregate）只小跌約 0 到 1%——差距大到讓一個原本 60/40 出發的投資人，跌到低點時比例已漂到大約 55/45（股／債）。一個在月底（二〇二〇年三月三十一日）把配置拉回 60/40 的投資人，等於在崩盤接近谷底時賣債券、買股票，接著搭上反彈——那年四到六月，全球股票再漲約 20%，債券也小漲一兩個百分點——比一路沒再平衡的投資人接住了更多那段反彈。這不是說再平衡故意抓到了底部——重點是結構性的：固定目標比例會在價格壓力最大的那一刻，強迫做出具體動作（賣撐住的、買跌掉的），不需要對接下來市場怎麼走有看法。

What rebalancing is not, according to the same evidence, is a return booster. The measured "rebalancing bonus" comes out at roughly 0.1 to 0.5 percentage points a year in most published studies, sometimes closer to zero or even slightly negative, since selling an asset that then keeps outperforming has a real opportunity cost. Rebalancing's actual, well-supported job is risk control: keeping the portfolio's risk profile close to what was originally chosen, rather than letting a strong bull market in one asset quietly turn 60/40 into 80/20 with nobody deciding that on purpose. One recurring rule of thumb, associated with researcher Larry Swedroe, is the "5/25" rule: rebalance when an asset class drifts more than 5 absolute percentage points, or more than 25% of its own original weight, whichever triggers first.

根據同一批證據，再平衡不是報酬加速器。多數已發表研究測得的「再平衡紅利」大約落在每年 0.1 到 0.5 個百分點，某些期間更接近零，甚至略為負值，因為賣掉之後持續表現更好的資產確實有真實的機會成本。再平衡真正、有充分證據支持的工作，是風險控制：讓組合的風險特性維持接近當初選定的樣子，不讓某個資產的強勢多頭悄悄把 60/40 配置變成 80/20。實務文獻裡有個經驗法則，跟研究者 Larry Swedroe 有關，叫「5/25」法則：一個資產類別偏離超過絕對的 5 個百分點，或超過自己原始權重的 25%，哪個先觸發就照哪個。

One misreading of the evidence is worth heading off, since it is why many people quietly stop rebalancing. Over a stretch when one asset dominates — US equities against almost everything else through the 2010s — rebalancing repeatedly takes money from the thing that keeps winning and hands it to the thing that keeps losing, and by decade's end the rebalanced portfolio is visibly behind the one left alone. That is not a malfunction; it is the design working as intended. What that forgone gain buys is a guarantee the portfolio never quietly became a concentrated bet on the winner right before leadership rotated — and the international evidence above is precisely a record of that rotation happening, repeatedly, without warning.

有一個對這批證據的誤讀值得直接擋掉，因為它正是很多人默默停止再平衡的原因。某個資產壓倒性領先的期間——例如二〇一〇年代美股對上幾乎所有其他市場——再平衡會一次又一次把錢從一直贏的那邊搬給一直輸的那邊，到那個十年結束時，有再平衡的組合明顯落後於放著不管的組合。這不是故障，是設計本來就會這樣運作。用那塊被放棄的漲幅換到的，是一個保證：組合不會在領先權輪替前一刻，悄悄變成押在贏家身上的集中賭注——上面國際分散一節的證據，正是「領先權會反覆輪替、不會事先通知」的紀錄。

One practical refinement matters more than the choice of rule, especially in a taxable account: rebalancing does not have to be done by selling. Directing new contributions into whichever asset has fallen below target — or, in a withdrawal phase, taking withdrawals from whichever asset has risen above target — moves weights back toward target using flows that were going to happen anyway, without realizing a capital gain or paying a spread. This handles most ordinary drift, leaving actual sell-and-buy rebalancing for moves flows alone cannot absorb.

有一個實務細節，重要性勝過選哪一條規則，在有稅負的帳戶裡尤其如此：再平衡不一定要靠賣出完成。把新投入的資金導向已跌到目標之下的資產——或在提領階段，從已漲到目標之上的資產提領——就能用「反正本來也會發生」的現金流把權重推回目標，不用實現資本利得或付買賣價差。這樣就能吸收掉大部分日常漂移，把真正要賣出再買進的再平衡，留給現金流吸收不了的大幅偏離。

Takeaway一句話收斂

Rebalancing is a risk-control habit, not a return engine — the specific frequency or threshold barely matters, but doing it consistently at all is what keeps a portfolio's actual risk close to the risk that was originally chosen.再平衡是風險控制的習慣，不是報酬引擎——確切選哪種頻率或門檻幾乎不影響結果，但有沒有持續執行，才是讓組合實際承擔的風險貼近當初選定風險的關鍵。

Beyond Fixed Weights: Rules That Change Exposure (an Introduction, Not a Recommendation)

固定比例之外：幾種會「改變曝險」的規則（只是介紹，不是推薦）

Everything above holds the weights fixed and rebalances back to them. There is a second family of approaches that lets exposure move with a signal. Four are common enough, and well enough documented in public research, that you should at least know what they are: trend following, volatility targeting, risk parity, and — as the baseline they are all measured against — fixed weights with rebalancing. None of them is "the answer". Each has a mechanism, a body of public evidence, and a known weak environment. What follows is a map, not a recommendation.

前面講的方法有一個共同點：比例固定，漂掉了就拉回來。另外還有一族方法，讓曝險跟著某個訊號走。常見的有四種，公開研究也夠多，至少該知道它們是什麼：趨勢跟蹤、波動率目標、風險平價，以及當作對照基準的「固定比例＋再平衡」。沒有哪一種是標準答案。每一種都有機制、公開證據，也有做得很差的環境。下面是一張地圖，不是推薦。

Trend following (time-series momentum) holds an asset while its price is above a long moving average and cuts back — to cash or a lower weight — below it. The record is long: Faber's much-replicated study, updated through 2012, applies a ten-month moving average to US stocks from 1901 and finds a compound return roughly on par with buy-and-hold (both about 9–10% a year), volatility about a third lower, and max drawdown cut from over 80% to roughly half; Moskowitz, Ooi and Pedersen (2012) document the same effect across 58 futures markets. Its weak spot is a sideways, choppy market that sells after a dip and buys back after a recovery, paying a small loss each time (2011 and 2015–16). A month-end-only rule is also slow in a crash as fast as March 2020's: the S&P 500 fell from its February 19 peak to its March 23 low — roughly 34% in about 23 trading days — and by the March 31 read, the index was already down roughly 20% for the quarter; the market then rallied roughly 20% in Q2 alone, so a rule waiting for confirmation gives back a real share of the fastest recovery. It also trades, so taxes and costs are part of the price.

趨勢跟蹤（也叫時間序列動能）的規則很簡單：價格在長期均線之上就持有，跌破就減碼——換成現金或降低比重。公開紀錄很長：Faber 更新到 2012 年的研究，把十個月均線套在 1901 年起的美股上，年化報酬跟買進持有差不多（都約 9–10%），波動少約三分之一，最大回撤從八成多降到大約一半；Moskowitz、Ooi 與 Pedersen（2012）在 58 個期貨市場也看到同樣效果。弱項是「橫盤洗盤」：跌一下就賣、漲回來又買，每次都賠一小筆（2011、2015–16 年）。只在月底讀訊號的規則，遇到像二〇二〇年三月那麼快的崩盤也會反應慢：S&P 500 從二月十九日高點跌到三月二十三日低點，約 23 個交易日跌了約 34%，月線第一次讀到的訊號是三月三十一日收盤，那時整季已跌約 20%；接著市場光第二季就反彈約 20%，讓出了反彈裡最快的那一段。它也會交易，稅和成本都是代價的一部分。

Volatility targeting sizes exposure inversely to recent volatility: hold more when calm, less when turbulent, aiming to keep the portfolio's own volatility near a chosen level. Moreira and Muir (2017) show scaling the US market and several factor portfolios by the inverse of the previous month's realized variance raised risk-adjusted returns over 1926–2015 and shortened the left tail; Harvey and co-authors (2018) find the benefit real for equities and credit but close to zero for bonds, commodities and currencies. Its weak spots are equally public: adding exposure in calm markets usually requires leverage, bringing volatility drag and financing cost, and a sudden spike forces selling into a falling market — the cleanest example is February 5, 2018, when the VIX closed at roughly 17.3 the Friday before and roughly 37.3 that Monday, more than doubling in a session. Funds sizing positions off the VIX or realized variance were forced to cut equity exposure sharply, and short-volatility products such as the XIV note lost roughly 96% of value and were wound down within days. The target itself is a parameter too, and can be overfitted like any other.

波動率目標是「近期波動小就多拿一點、波動大就少拿一點」，讓組合波動維持在選定水準附近。Moreira 與 Muir（2017）發現，用上個月已實現變異數的倒數調整美股大盤和幾個因子組合曝險，1926–2015 年風險調整後報酬提高、左尾變短；Harvey 等人（2018）發現這個好處在股票和信用債上是真的，在債券、商品、貨幣上接近零。弱點也一樣公開：平靜時要多拿曝險通常得借錢，帶來波動拖累和資金成本，波動一旦突然飆高就逼你在下跌中賣出——最乾淨的案例是二〇一八年二月五日，VIX 從前一個星期五收盤約 17.3，跳到那個星期一收盤約 37.3，一個交易日翻了一倍多。用 VIX 或已實現變異數決定部位大小的基金被迫大幅減碼股票，做空波動率的 XIV 市值蒸發約 96%，幾天內就被清算下市。「目標波動」本身也是一個參數，跟任何參數一樣可以被過度擬合。

Risk parity was covered above: equalise each asset's risk contribution rather than dollar weight, then lever the low-volatility mix up to an equity-like target. 2022 is the cleanest read on that leverage cutting both ways: Bridgewater's flagship All Weather fell roughly 22% for the year — worse than its roughly 20% decline in 2008 — and the HFR Risk Parity 10% Volatility Target Institutional Index fell roughly 19.5%, both because the long-duration bonds core to the approach fell alongside stocks in the same rate-hike environment that hurt 60/40. Fixed weights with rebalancing is itself a rule that changes exposure the opposite direction: rebalancing sells what rose and buys what fell, contrarian by construction, while trend following is pro-cyclical — the two profit in different environments, why some run both.

風險平價上一節講過：讓每個資產貢獻的「風險」一樣多，不是「金額」一樣多，再把低波動組合用槓桿拉到接近股票的水準。二〇二二年是槓桿「雙面刃」最乾淨的案例：Bridgewater 旗艦策略 All Weather 那年跌了約 22%——比二〇〇八年約 20% 的跌幅還深；HFR Risk Parity 10% Volatility Target Institutional Index 跌了約 19.5%，都因核心持有的長天期債券，在同一波打擊 60/40 的升息環境裡跟股票一起下跌。固定比例＋再平衡其實也是會改變曝險的規則，只是方向相反：再平衡賣漲買跌，天生逆勢；趨勢跟蹤則順勢——兩者在不同環境裡賺錢，所以有人兩種都用。

How do you choose? Three questions do most of the work. First: how much trading, tax and cost can you tolerate? Second: when the rule tells you to cut exposure mid-decline, or add after a long rise, will you actually do it — a rule you override is worse than no rule, since you pay the costs without the discipline. Third: do you know the rule's weak environment in advance, and can you sit through it? Sideways for trend, sudden spikes for volatility targeting, rising rates for risk parity, long bull markets for rebalancing. A backtest can only exclude rules whose weak environment you cannot survive — it cannot prove the one you kept will keep working, the subject of lesson 28.

那要怎麼選？三個問題能決定大半。第一：你能忍受多少交易、稅和成本？第二：規則在下跌途中叫你減碼、或漲很久後叫你加碼，你真的做得到嗎？會被自己推翻的規則比沒有規則更糟——成本照付，紀律沒有。第三：你事先知不知道它的弱項環境是什麼，撐不撐得過去？趨勢跟蹤怕橫盤、波動率目標怕突然飆高、風險平價怕升息、再平衡怕一路單邊大多頭。回測只能把撐不過的規則排除掉，證明不了留下來那條會一直有效——這是第 28 課的主題。

Pitfall陷阱

Copying someone else's parameters — a moving-average length, a volatility target, a cap — as if they were laws. They are choices fit to that person's costs and history; the public evidence supports the family of rules, not any single setting. Withdrawal Timing: Sequence-of-Returns Risk

提領的時機：報酬順序風險

Everything above concerns a portfolio that only ever adds money. A different problem shows up once a portfolio starts having money taken out regularly — in retirement, or any long planned drawdown period — and the evidence here contains a genuine, still-unsettled academic disagreement worth presenting honestly.

以上談的都是只有錢流入的組合。組合開始定期被提領資金出來的那一刻起——退休期間，或任何長期規劃好的提領期間——會出現不一樣的問題。這裡有一個真實、至今未解決的學界分歧，值得誠實呈現。

Start with what time alone does, since it is the premise the withdrawal question rests on. Using rolling nominal US equity returns from 1926 to 2023, the probability of ending a holding period with less money than you started with falls steeply with horizon: roughly 26% of one-year periods finished negative, roughly 12% of five-year periods, roughly 5% of ten-year periods, and essentially none of the twenty-year periods. That last figure is the most quoted line in favor of simply holding on — and the one most needing its asterisk: it is a US number. Japan's market delivered negative real returns over holding periods longer than twenty years starting from the 1990 peak; Italy and several other developed markets have produced their own twenty-year real losses. "Equities always win over twenty years" describes one country's twentieth century, not a theorem.

先看「時間本身」做了什麼，因為提領這個問題就建立在這個前提上。用一九二六到二〇二三年美國股票的名目滾動報酬來看，持有期結束時拿回的錢比投入時少的機率，隨持有時間拉長急遽下降：約 26% 的一年期是負的、約 12% 的五年期、約 5% 的十年期，二十年期幾乎沒有負的。最後這個數字最常被引用來支持「就抱著別動」——但也最需要那個註腳：它是一個美國數字。日本市場從一九九〇年高點起算，超過二十年的持有期交出過實質負報酬；義大利與其他已開發市場也各自出現過二十年期的實質虧損。「股票抱二十年一定贏」是對某一個國家二十世紀的描述，不是定理。

The core mechanism is called sequence-of-returns riskTwo portfolios with identical average returns can end up with wildly different final balances, depending only on the order good and bad years occurred — withdrawals taken during a down year lock in losses that never recover.兩個平均報酬相同的組合，最後餘額可能天差地遠，差別只在好年壞年出現的順序——下跌年提領資金會把虧損鎖死，永遠沒機會回本。: two portfolios can have the exact same average annual return over 30 years and end up with wildly different final balances, purely because of the order the good and bad years arrived in. A retiree who withdraws a fixed amount yearly and hits two or three bad market years right at the start is forced to sell a larger share of a shrinking portfolio, permanently reducing the base that has to recover later — the same bad years landing near the end instead cause almost no damage, since most money is already withdrawn by then. Illustration: a retiree starting in 1966 ran into roughly a decade and a half of poor, inflation-eroded US returns almost immediately, against one starting in 1982, at the start of one of the strongest multi-decade bull markets in US history — same rule, same long-run average, opposite outcomes.

核心機制叫報酬順序風險Two portfolios with identical average returns can end up with wildly different final balances, depending only on the order good and bad years occurred — withdrawals taken during a down year lock in losses that never recover.兩個平均報酬相同的組合，最後餘額可能天差地遠，差別只在好年壞年出現的順序——下跌年提領資金會把虧損鎖死，永遠沒機會回本。：兩個組合可能三十年間平均年報酬完全一樣，卻因好年壞年出現的先後順序不同，最後餘額天差地遠。每年提領固定金額的退休族，若退休一開始就遇上兩三個壞年頭，會被迫從萎縮的組合裡賣出更大比例資產——永久縮小之後要靠它回本的本金基礎；同樣平均報酬的退休族，若壞年頭落在三十年尾端，幾乎不受傷害，因為那時大部分的錢早就領走了。示範：一九六六年開始提領的退休族，幾乎立刻撞上將近十五年、被通膨侵蝕的美股疲弱報酬；一九八二年開始提領的退休族，則站在美國史上數一數二強勁的多年多頭起點——同樣的規則、同樣的長期平均報酬，結果卻天差地遠。

The most widely cited rule of thumb here is financial planner William Bengen's "4% rule," published in 1994: withdraw 4% of the starting portfolio value in year one, then adjust that dollar amount for inflation every year after — based on the worst historical US sequence in his dataset (including the 1966 start), the portfolio should last at least 30 years. It remains widely taught, and extensively challenged. Researcher Wade Pfau examined the same withdrawal-rate analysis across 17 developed countries, not just the US, and found a 4% starting rate would have failed — run the portfolio to zero before 30 years — in a majority of those countries, since the US had an unusually favorable 20th-century market history relative to most of the world. More recently, a 2023 paper by Aizhan Anarkulova, Scott Cederburg, and Michael O'Doherty, using a broader international dataset and bootstrap-resampling, argued the safe withdrawal rate for a globally diversified stock/bond portfolio may be meaningfully below 4%, while other researchers have pushed back on its methodology and country sample. This is a live, unsettled disagreement, not a resolved fact — 4% is a widely used rule of thumb built substantially on one country's 20th-century experience, not a mathematical law, and any single point estimate deserves real humility.

最廣為引用的經驗法則，是理財規劃師 William Bengen 一九九四年發表的「4% 法則」：第一年提領起始組合價值的 4%，之後每年依通膨調整——根據他資料集裡最糟的美國歷史序列（含一九六六年那組），組合至少能撐過 30 年。這條法則至今仍被廣泛教授，也被大量挑戰。Wade Pfau 檢視 17 個已開發國家自己的市場歷史，發現若一開始就用 4% 提領率，多數國家的組合會提早破產——不到 30 年就歸零——因為美國剛好在二十世紀相對世界多數地方有一段異常有利的市場歷史。更近期，二〇二三年 Aizhan Anarkulova、Scott Cederburg 與 Michael O'Doherty 合著的論文，用更廣泛的國際資料集跟拔靴重抽樣方法，主張全球分散股債組合的安全提領率可能明顯低於 4%，其他研究者則對其方法論與國家樣本提出反駁。這裡呈現為一個活著、尚未解決的分歧，不是已定案的事實——4% 是廣泛使用、但很大程度建立在單一國家二十世紀經驗上的經驗法則，不是數學定律，任何單一點估計都值得謙遜對待。

Takeaway一句話收斂

Two portfolios with the same average return can have wildly different real-world outcomes depending purely on when the bad years land relative to withdrawals — and the "4% rule" built to manage that risk rests on one country's 20th-century history, with genuine, still-open academic disagreement about whether it generalizes.兩個平均報酬相同的組合，可能因壞年頭相對提領時機的先後不同，實際結果天差地遠——用來管理這個風險的「4% 法則」，很大程度建立在單一國家的二十世紀歷史上，學界對它能不能推廣到別處，至今仍有真實、尚未解決的分歧。

Think first: two retirees have the exact same 30-year average annual return. One's portfolio survives comfortably; the other runs out of money in year 22. If the average return is identical, what single variable most likely explains the gap?先想一下：兩個退休族，三十年的平均年報酬完全一樣。一個的組合撐得很輕鬆；另一個在第 22 年就把錢花光了。如果平均報酬完全相同，最可能解釋這個差距的，是哪一個單一變數？

The order good and bad years arrived, relative to when withdrawals were taken — sequence-of-returns risk. A retiree who hit bad years early, while still withdrawing a fixed amount, was forced to sell a larger share of a shrinking portfolio, permanently shrinking the base that has to compound back. The same bad years landing late would do far less damage. The 30-year average hides this completely; only the year-by-year path reveals it.

好年跟壞年出現的順序，相對於提領的時間點——報酬順序風險。一個早期就撞上壞年頭、又持續提領固定金額的退休族，被迫從萎縮中的組合賣出更大比例資產，永久縮小之後回本複利的基礎。同樣的壞年頭如果晚一點出現，傷害會小得多。三十年的平均值完全藏起這件事；只有逐年路徑才會揭露它。

Six Traps in How This Evidence Gets Misused

六個誤用這些證據的常見陷阱

⚠

Treating US market history as if it were world history. Nearly every widely quoted long-run number in finance — the 4% withdrawal rule, the roughly-10%-a-year stock return, most textbook drawdown charts — is built on US data, because the US has the longest, most complete, most convenient public record. But Pfau's 17-country study above found the same 4% rule failed in most other countries, and Japan's multi-decade round trip shows a fully developed market can behave nothing like the US pattern for a generation. Any number sourced from US-only data should be labeled as such.

把美國市場歷史當成全世界的市場歷史。幾乎每一個金融界廣為引用的長期數字——4% 提領法則、大約每年 10% 的股票報酬、多數教科書回撤圖的形狀——都建立在美國資料上，因為美國擁有最長、最方便取用的公開紀錄。但 Pfau 的 17 國研究發現，同一條 4% 法則在多數其他國家會失敗；日本橫跨數十年的來回，證明一個完全成熟的市場，整個世代都可能長得跟美國模式完全不一樣。任何只從美國資料算出來的數字都該明確標註來源。

⚠

Backtesting a long list of allocations and picking whichever produced the highest historical return. A mix that looks best over 1980–2020 was, to some real degree, positioned for what happened in those specific 40 years; rerun over 1930–1970 and a different "winner" routinely appears. Picking the top backtested performer quietly assumes the next 40 years will rhyme with the last 40.

回測一長串配置，挑歷史報酬最高的那一個。1980 到 2020 年間看起來最好的配置，很大程度是剛好在那特定 40 年裡佔到位置；換 1930 到 1970 年重跑，通常會跑出不同的「贏家」。挑回測表現最好的當標準答案，等於默默假設接下來 40 年會跟過去 40 年押韻。

⚠

Reading "smaller historical drawdown" as simply "better." The Permanent Portfolio's roughly -13 to -16% max drawdown looks safer on paper than 100% stocks' roughly -51%, but that smaller number came largely from holding heavy weights in cash and gold across decades that did not happen to punish either one — and both assets have their own well-documented multi-decade weak stretches (see this lesson's gold war-story). A smaller historical drawdown is a fact about which decades were sampled, not a law about what a mix can never do again.

把「歷史回撤比較小」直接讀成「比較好」。永久組合約 -13 到 -16% 的最大回撤，紙面上比 100% 股票約 -51% 安全，但這個較小的數字很大程度來自數十年間重壓現金跟黃金，剛好那幾十年沒懲罰到這兩者——這兩種資產都有自己有紀錄的多年低潮期（見這一課的黃金戰爭故事）。歷史回撤小，是「剛好抽樣到哪幾個十年」的事實，不是這個配置以後不會發生同樣事的定律。

⚠

Expecting rebalancing to be a source of extra return. As shown above, the measured "rebalancing bonus" is small — roughly 0.1 to 0.5 percentage points a year in most studies — and can turn negative in strong, sustained trends. Rebalancing's real job is keeping actual risk close to what was originally chosen; treating it as a return-generating strategy sets an expectation the evidence does not support.

期待再平衡能成為額外報酬的來源。就像上面證據顯示的，測得的「再平衡紅利」很小——多數研究裡大約每年 0.1 到 0.5 個百分點——強勁持續的趨勢裡甚至可能變負值。再平衡真正的工作是讓實際風險貼近當初選定的風險；把它當成能創造報酬的策略是不支持的期待。

⚠

Home bias dressed up as a rational choice. "I understand this market best" is real and human, but not evidence that market will keep outperforming — the two get confused constantly. The tell: the reason offered is about the investor's own familiarity, while the claim being made is about future returns.

把家鄉偏誤包裝成理性的選擇。「我最懂這個市場」是真實人性的理由，但不是這個市場會繼續勝出的證據——這兩件事卻一直被混為一談。破綻在於：理由講的是投資人自己的熟悉度，主張講的卻是未來的報酬。

⚠

Treating Bengen's 4% rule as a proven constant rather than a historical rule of thumb. Using 4% as a starting assumption is reasonable; treating it as settled science closes off a debate still genuinely open.

Worked Example: Backing Out an Allocation From a Drawdown You Can Actually Tolerate

逐步範例：從能真正承受的回撤，倒推一個配置

A teaching-only, illustrative example — not a recommendation for any specific reader. The method matters far more than the numbers below, which are illustrative for one hypothetical investor, not a suggestion for anyone reading this.

這是一個純教學、示範用的例子——不是給任何特定讀者的建議。真正重要的是方法，不是底下的數字，那只是說明一個假設情境。

Step by step逐步拆解
  1. Start from a stated, specific pain tolerance, not a return target. Suppose a hypothetical investor states plainly: "I could not tolerate watching my portfolio fall more than about 25% from its peak without making a panicked, badly timed decision." That number — a maximum tolerable drawdown, not a desired return — is the actual starting constraint.從一個明確、具體的痛苦承受度開始，不是從報酬目標開始。假設一位投資人說：「如果我看著組合從高點跌超過大約 25%，一定會做出恐慌、時機很差的決定。」這個數字——能承受的最大回撤——才是真正的起始限制，不是想要的報酬。
  2. Screen the six-portfolio table above against that 25% ceiling. 100% stocks (~-51%) and 60/40 (~-30 to -32%) both exceed it. The three-fund mix (~-30 to -40%, depending on international weight) exceeds it too, unless weighted much more conservatively than the illustrative figures shown. The Permanent Portfolio (~-13 to -16%), All Weather-style (~-20%), and simple unlevered risk-parity-style mixes (~-15 to -20%) all sit inside the stated ceiling.用這個 25% 天花板，篩選上面六種配置的表格。100% 股票（約 -51%）跟 60/40（約 -30 到 -32%）都超過。三基金配置（約 -30 到 -40%，依國際比重而定）如果沒配置得比示範數字保守很多，也會超過。永久組合（約 -13 到 -16%）、全天候風格（約 -20%）、不加槓桿的簡化風險平價風格（約 -15 到 -20%），都落在天花板之內。
  3. Then re-screen episode by episode, since a single max-drawdown number hides which stress produced it. In 1973–74, 60/40 lost roughly 28% nominal but roughly 40% real — for a purchasing-power ceiling, that alone is disqualifying, and 100% stocks was worse. In 2008, every equity-heavy candidate broke the ceiling while gold-and-cash-heavy mixes did not. In 2022 the ranking inverted: the mixes that looked safest carried the long-bond and gold weight that fell hardest, and the Permanent Portfolio and All Weather-style rows produced their own worst or near-worst years. No candidate stays inside the ceiling in all three episodes — the honest output of the exercise, not a failure of it.接著逐個事件重篩一次，因為單一最大回撤數字會把「這個回撤是被哪種壓力打出來的」藏起來。一九七三到七四年，60/40 名目大約虧 28%，實質大約虧 40%——對購買力天花板來說，光這一段就已出局，100% 股票更慘。二〇〇八年，每一個股票偏重的候選都撞破天花板，黃金與現金偏重的配置沒有。二〇二二年排名反轉：先前看起來最安全的那幾個，正好重壓在跌得最凶的長債與黃金上，永久組合與全天候風格都交出自己最差或接近最差的一年。沒有任何候選在三個事件都待在天花板之內——這才是這個練習誠實的產出，不是失敗。
  4. Now decide the international weight, which the drawdown screen alone never touches. The rotation evidence above says leadership between home and non-US markets has changed hands roughly every decade with no advance warning — an argument for holding both, sized so being wrong about which leads next does not decide the outcome. A reader whose home market is roughly 2% of global equity value makes a much larger active bet by holding only it than one whose home market is 60%+.接著決定國際比重，這是回撤篩選完全碰不到的一件事。上面輪替的證據說，本國市場與非美市場之間的領先權，大約每十年換手一次，且不會事先通知——這是「兩邊都持有」的理由，配到「猜錯下一個十年誰領先」不會決定最終結果的比例。本國市場只占全球股票市值約 2% 的讀者，只持有本國市場所下的主動賭注，遠比本國市場占 60% 以上的讀者大得多。
  5. The 25% ceiling eliminated two candidates and flagged a third as needing a more conservative weighting, but did not identify which survivor is "best" — screening out is a question a backtest can answer; ranking is not.25% 天花板淘汰了兩個候選、標記第三個需要更保守的配置，但沒有指出存活下來的哪一個「最好」——「篩掉」是回測答得出來的問題，「排名」不是。
  6. Next, pick a rebalancing rule and stick to it — the specific choice barely matters, so an annual calendar rule or the 5/25 threshold rule are both defensible; applying it consistently matters more than which one.接著，選一條再平衡規則並堅持下去——具體選哪一條幾乎不影響結果，每年日曆規則或 5/25 門檻規則都站得住腳；持續執行比選哪一條重要。
  7. Finally, check two frictions no backtest chart contains: currency exposure and tax treatment, covered next — they belong inside the decision, not after it.最後，檢查回測圖表不會包含的兩個摩擦：匯率曝險與稅務處理，下一節詳談——它們屬於決策的一部分，不是決策之後才補的註腳。

What Backtesting Is For Here: Excluding Fragile Mixes, Not Picking a Winner

回測在這裡的用法：排除脆弱的配置，不是挑出一個贏家

Every backtest referenced in this lesson — the six-portfolio table, the 60/40 drawdown history, the rebalancing studies — reports on exactly one path history happened to take. That is a reason to be precise about the job they can do, not to ignore them. A backtest is well suited to screening out configurations that failed a specific, well-documented stress the investor has already said they cannot tolerate, which is what the worked example did. It is poorly suited to identifying a single best performer, since the ranking itself is a fact about the decades sampled.

這一課引用的每一份回測——六種配置的表格、60/40 的回撤歷史、再平衡研究——報告的都只是歷史剛好走過的那一條路徑。這不是忽視回測的理由，而是精確界定它能做什麼工作的理由。回測適合拿來排除那些沒能撐過某個特定、有充分紀錄、投資人已表明無法承受的壓力的配置，這正是上面範例做的事；它不適合拿來找出單一表現最好的配置，因為「最好」這個排名本身就是「剛好抽樣到哪幾個十年」的事實。

This is the same discipline the next lesson, Backtest Honesty and the Risk Budget, develops in far more depth for strategy backtesting generally — a backtest's honest job is disqualifying what has demonstrably failed under specific conditions, not certifying what will win. If a configuration or strategy has never been tested against a genuine stress case in its own asset class's history, that absence is itself a finding worth caution, not license to assume it would have survived.

這正是下一課回測的誠實與風險預算會針對策略回測整體用更深的篇幅發展的同一套紀律——回測誠實的工作是淘汰在特定條件下已證明會失敗的東西，不是認證誰會贏。如果一個配置或策略從來沒在自己那個資產類別的歷史裡被真正的壓力情境測試過，這個「缺乏證據」的事實本身就值得謹慎，不是可以隨意假設它撐得過去。
