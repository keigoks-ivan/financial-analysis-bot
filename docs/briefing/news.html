Analysis
This is analysis, not settled fact: the combination of sub-10-day memory inventory and fully booked CoWoS capacity through 2026 suggests the current AI-chip supply constraint may be structural rather than purely cyclical, which would support elevated pricing power for TSMC, SK Hynix and Nvidia's supply chain into 2027. The reported AMD GPU price hike is an early test of whether that pricing power is being passed through to AI infrastructure buyers.
Structural signal
Supply-demand gaps in DRAM (4.9%), NAND (4.2%) and HBM (5.1%) are reported at their highest levels since 2011, an unusually tight setup this deep into an AI capex cycle.
Bull case ▲
Sold-out capacity through 2026-2027 with reported HBM price gains of 20-40% year-over-year points to sustained margin expansion for memory and packaging suppliers.
Bear case ▼
A supply chain this tight has almost no inventory buffer, so any deceleration in AI capex demand could trigger an outsized correction.
Implication
The reported supply constraint reinforces today's SOX-led rally, but it also means the semis complex has little cushion against any AI-demand disappointment.
