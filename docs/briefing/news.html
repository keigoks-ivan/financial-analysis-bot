Analysis
Analysis: the shift of the bottleneck from wafer fabrication to advanced packaging and memory suggests AI hardware capex will keep concentrating around a narrow set of suppliers (TSMC, SK Hynix, Micron, Samsung) regardless of near-term demand wobbles, reinforcing their pricing power. This is consistent with hyperscaler 2026 capex guidance of roughly $635-670 billion, but it also means any slowdown in AI spending would likely show up first as inventory buildup at the packaging and memory tier rather than at the fab level.
Structural signal
Supply constraints have moved from leading-edge wafer capacity to advanced packaging and HBM memory, concentrating pricing power in TSMC, SK Hynix, Samsung and Micron.
Bull case ▲
Sustained HBM and CoWoS scarcity through 2027 supports pricing power and margins for the concentrated set of suppliers serving AI infrastructure demand.
Bear case ▼
Memory prices up 90-95% year-to-date and HBM4 pricing set to roughly triple raise input costs for AI system builders, risking margin compression or demand pushback further up the stack.
Implication
The AI infrastructure trade increasingly depends on a small number of packaging and memory suppliers, making their capacity additions (TSMC targeting 130,000 CoWoS WPM by year-end) a key variable to track.
