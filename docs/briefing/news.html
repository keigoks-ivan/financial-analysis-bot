Key data

Analysis
This is analysis, not settled fact: the combination of negative free cash flow at Meta and the emergence of a dedicated late-fee insurance market suggests the AI capex cycle is reaching a point where financing terms and construction execution, not just end demand, are becoming binding constraints. If hyperscaler free cash flow keeps deteriorating through this week's earnings, credit markets may start pricing AI infrastructure debt differently from generic tech credit.
Structural signal
AI-related capex stress is shifting from a demand question to a financing and execution question, visible in credit (negative FCF) and insurance (late-fee coverage) markets simultaneously.
Bull case ▲
Hyperscaler capex is largely front-loaded onto long-duration contracted revenue, so near-term negative free cash flow does not necessarily signal weakening demand.
Bear case ▼
If free cash flow keeps deteriorating across this week's megacap earnings, credit investors could demand wider spreads on AI-linked debt, tightening financing for the entire infrastructure buildout.
Implication
This week's hyperscaler earnings and guidance are the next test of whether AI capex credit stress is contained or spreading, directly relevant to the single-stock sleeve's AI infrastructure and semiconductor exposure.
