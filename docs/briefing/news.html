Analysis
The gap between still-expanding AI capex commitments (CoWoS capacity growth, sold-out HBM supply) and sharply higher financing costs (SoftBank at 9.75%, Treasury yields near multi-decade highs) suggests the AI buildout's economics are becoming more sensitive to the rate path than to order books; this is an interpretation, not a confirmed credit event.
Structural signal
Credit markets are pricing meaningfully higher risk premia into AI-linked debt even as the equity complex (SOX +1.41%) keeps rallying, a divergence between debt and equity pricing of the same AI infrastructure exposure.
Bull case ▲
If Treasury yields stabilize, sold-out HBM/DRAM capacity and expanding CoWoS supply still support AI capex growth into 2027.
Bear case ▼
If 10Y and 30Y yields keep climbing toward the 5.60%+ falsifier zone, refinancing costs for debt-funded AI datacenter operators could force capex delays or wider spreads in private credit.
Implication
The interest-rate path, not chip demand, is currently the swing factor for AI infrastructure financing conditions.
