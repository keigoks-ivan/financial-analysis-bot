Nvidia leadership reversal and semiconductor selloff Huang reinstatement rally reverses as Nvidia falls 5.86%, SOX drops 7.88%
2026-11-24 · CNBC／Reuters
Situation
Nvidia's board reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO on Nov 23 after his earlier departure amid a personal and espionage-adjacent scandal, sparking a reported ~31.5% Monday surge in the stock. That gain reversed to roughly -5.86% on Tuesday per CNBC, and the broader chip complex fell further today with SOX down 7.88%. Micron fell about 11% and Analog Devices fell 17-18% despite a fiscal Q4 beat, while Congress (Schumer, Warren, three House committee chairs) criticized the reinstatement and the US blacklisted Megaspeed, a Nvidia customer tied to the scandal.
Key data
Nvidia (NVDA)-5.86%Tue close, reversing Monday's reported +31.5%Shows Monday's reinstatement rally was largely a short squeeze
SOX index8,832.50-7.88% todayBroad semiconductor reversal beyond just Nvidia
Analog Devices (ADI)-17% to -18%Despite a fiscal Q4 beatEarnings beats are not preventing sector-wide selling
Micron (MU)-11%Tue sessionMemory names caught in the same reversal despite a strong HBM demand backdrop
Analysis
Analysis: the speed and size of the reversal, Monday's roughly 31% Nvidia surge largely round-tripped by Tuesday's -5.86% plus today's 7.88% SOX drop, suggests the initial move was a short-covering and relief bounce rather than a repricing of fundamentals. Ongoing DOJ and SEC probes, congressional hostility, and the Megaspeed blacklisting keep a governance overhang on the stock and its supply chain that one day of reinstatement news could not resolve.
Structural signal
Chip stocks are reversing broadly (SOX -7.88%) while Taiwan's TAIEX, which closed before the US reversal, still reflects Monday's +8.99% spillover, a timing gap likely to close in the next session.
Bull case ▲
Huang's reinstatement removes near-term leadership uncertainty and Asian suppliers gave the move a cautious welcome, per Reuters.
Bear case ▼
Congressional investigations, the Commerce Department's Megaspeed blacklisting, and a reported admission by Huang of a code-of-conduct violation keep legal and governance risk alive for Nvidia and its supply chain.
Implication
The chip sector's reversal shows Monday's rally priced out governance risk too quickly, and that risk is now being repriced back in.
