2026-10-01 · CNBC／DIGITIMES／Barron's
Key data
Micron FQ4 revenue$54.23B+31% QoQ, +379% YoYConfirms memory demand is accelerating faster than supply can be added.
FQ1 2027 guidance$61.5B revenue / $38.15 EPSAbove consensus of $57.02B / $35.40Guidance implies the shortage is intensifying into 2027, not stabilizing.
DRAM revenue$39.8B+343% YoY, 73% of total salesDRAM, not NAND, is the primary driver of the current cycle.
Analysis
Analysis: the combination of record guidance and a stock move that still fell on the news suggests much of the memory upcycle was already priced in, with the market now parsing whether the current margin profile is durable once new capacity lands. The DIGITIMES observation that Micron's best margins come from PC/phone markets rather than AI data centers is worth tracking against HBM pricing data (HBM3E near $300/stack, HBM4 near $550/stack) that points to separate AI-specific pricing power.
Structural signal
SK Hynix's entire 2026 capacity already booked and over 75% of Micron's fiscal 2027 production committed signal a supply-constrained cycle rather than a purely demand-driven one.
Bull case ▲
If HBM and server DRAM pricing power holds through 2028 as memory executives expect, current earnings estimates across Micron, Samsung and SK Hynix likely still understate the cycle.
Bear case ▼
If PC and phone demand, Micron's actual margin driver per DIGITIMES, rolls over before AI data-center DRAM and HBM demand fully offsets it, the premium on memory stocks could compress quickly.
Implication
The memory supercycle read-through connects directly to the single-stock AI infrastructure sleeve via SOX's 1.59% gain and TAIEX's record close, both led by chip-supply-chain names.
