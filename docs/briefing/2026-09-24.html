MARKET PULSE cross-indicator signals
Dominant theme: Rate-shock dollar strength squeezing risk assets outside AI chips
Gold and Bitcoin selling off together
Gold fell 1.23% to $4,322.70 and Bitcoin fell 2.03% to $84,422.80 on the same day DXY rose 0.66% to 101.09 and the 10Y yield rose 15bps to 5.11%. Both traditional and crypto hedges declined together rather than diverging, which is inconsistent with a classic flight-to-safety pattern.
If the dollar and yields keep climbing, expect continued pressure on both gold and Bitcoin rather than a rotation into either as a hedge.
Chips diverge from broad market breadth
SOX rose 0.81% to 12,534.27 and NYFANG rose 0.44% to 83.55 even as IWM/SPY fell 1.13% to 0.3672 and the S&P 500 fell 0.76%. Capital is concentrating in AI/semiconductor mega-caps while smaller, more rate-sensitive names absorb the yield shock.
Watch whether SOX and NYFANG strength persists as the 10Y climbs, since higher discount rates eventually catch up to long-duration AI capex stories.
Yield spike outruns equity vol
The 10Y yield jumped 15bps to 5.11%, the highest since 2007, while VIX rose only 2.08% to 15.18. MOVE at 80.64 versus realized rate vol of 74.00 shows the bond term structure already tilting toward stress that equity vol has not caught up to.
A further leg up in yields could force a delayed VIX catch-up move.
Hidden risk
The simultaneous decline in gold (-1.23%) and Bitcoin (-2.03%) suggests liquidity-driven de-risking rather than a benign sector rotation, a pattern that has historically preceded broader equity vol spikes. A further leg up in the 10Y past 5.25% could force position unwinds in the same AI-capex names (NYFANG, SOX) that have absorbed most of the market's recent gains.
Hidden opportunity
TAIEX's 1.14% gain to 47,718.84 and SOX's 0.81% move show the physical AI-infrastructure supply chain pricing through the rate shock, unlike US small caps. If the Fed's likely October hike proves closer to a peak than the start of a sustained cycle, the chip complex is positioned to lead any subsequent relief rally.
