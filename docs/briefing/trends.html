CERN transports 92 antiprotons 7.5 km in cryogenic trap
Physics / novel instruments
BASE Collaboration, CERNlab result
The BASE Collaboration successfully transported 92 antiprotons 7.5 kilometers using a cryogenic, open Penning-trap system called BASE-STEP, published in Nature on September 24. The approach enables up to 100-fold improved precision in CPT symmetry tests by moving antimatter to low-noise offline laboratories.
Why it matters ▸ Precision antimatter transport removes a key barrier to testing fundamental physics symmetries outside noisy accelerator environments.

New boron allotrope shows conductivity a million times higher
Materials science
Research team, Nature Chemistrylab result
A new boron allotrope called Imma-B60 exhibits electrical conductivity of about 900 S/m, more than a million times higher than conventional boron, with 23% plastic deformability before fracturing and a narrow bandgap under 0.2 eV, published in Nature Chemistry in September. Commercialization requires scaling the high-pressure Na4B60 precursor synthesis beyond lab conditions.
Why it matters ▸ A conductive, deformable boron material could open new applications in flexible electronics and energy storage if synthesis costs come down.

Human cortical organoids reach 86.2% graft success in mice
Synthetic biology
Research team, Naturelab result
Human stem-cell-derived cortical organoids transplanted into mice with depleted neocortex achieved an 86.2% graft success rate across 29 mice, growing 4.7-fold between months 2 and 3 to make up 91.9% of combined cortical tissue by month 3, per a Nature study published in September. The platform was demonstrated testing antisense oligonucleotide therapeutics for Timothy syndrome.
Why it matters ▸ A working xenograft platform could improve preclinical prediction of human drug toxicity and efficacy ahead of clinical trials.

Brain-computer interfaces
Borui Kang Medical Technologylimited commercial
Chinese regulators approved the first-ever clinical brain-computer interface device from Borui Kang Medical Technology for patients with severe paralysis including quadriplegia and cervical spinal cord injury. Why it matters ▸ First regulatory clearance anywhere for a clinical BCI device sets a precedent other regulators, including the FDA, will likely be pressed to follow.
