What You'll Learn這一課學什麼
  • Tell a real substitution threat apart from an ordinary competitor by testing whether it does the same job a different, cheaper-to-switch-to way.分辨真正的替代威脅與普通競爭對手——測試它是不是用另一種、轉換成本夠低的方法完成同一件工作。
  • Classify a threat into one of four forms — cheaper technology, an architectural shift that removes the need entirely, customer in-house build, or policy — and judge each on magnitude and time horizon.把一個威脅分類到四種形態之一——更便宜的技術、讓需求直接消失的架構轉變、客戶自研垂直整合、或政策——並各自評估命中面與時間尺度。
  • Run a mechanical scan that forces you to list every adjacent technology and industry, not just the ones you already suspect.跑一次機械掃描，強迫自己列出所有相鄰技術與相鄰產業，而不只是你原本就懷疑的那幾個。
  • Explain why an analyst's own checklist shares the analyst's own blind spot, and why only an independent, blank-page pass reliably catches a silent omission.解釋為什麼分析者自己的檢查清單會跟分析者自己共享同一個盲點，以及為什麼只有獨立、不看你草稿的第二雙眼睛才抓得到「整個沒提到」這種漏洞。
  • Stress-test the load-bearing numbers behind a substitution call, and treat any fact about a customer or supplier relationship as something with a shelf life.對支撐替代判斷的關鍵數字做對抗式查證，並記得客戶或供應商關係這類事實有保質期，要定期重新查證。

In 1975, a Kodak engineer named Steven Sasson built a toaster-sized prototype that recorded a black-and-white image onto a cassette tape at 0.01 megapixels. He showed it to Kodak's management. Kodak's own film business was, at the time, one of the most profitable franchises in American industry — gross margins above 60%, a market capitalization that would peak near $30 billion in the mid-1990s, and a brand that was effectively synonymous with "taking a picture." Kodak did not ignore the invention. It patented it, studied it for years, and — reasonably, given the numbers on its own income statement — chose not to push it to market, because doing so would have cannibalized the film and paper business that was paying for everything else. By the time digital cameras and then smartphone cameras made film irrelevant, Kodak's core business had nowhere to retreat to. Kodak filed for bankruptcy protection in January 2012.

一九七五年，柯達一位名叫史蒂芬・薩森的工程師做出一台烤麵包機大小的原型機，把黑白影像錄在卡帶上，解析度只有百萬分之一的零點零一。他把這台機器拿給柯達的管理層看。當時柯達自己的底片事業，是美國企業界獲利最好的生意之一——毛利率超過六成，市值在一九九零年代中期一度接近三百億美元，品牌幾乎就等於「拍照」這件事本身。柯達沒有忽視這項發明，還申請了專利，研究了好幾年——而且從自己損益表上的數字看，不推向市場其實是合理的決定，因為那會蠶食掉正在養活整間公司的底片與相紙生意。等到數位相機、後來又是智慧型手機的相機讓底片變得不再必要時，柯達的核心事業已經無處可退。二零一二年一月，柯達申請破產保護。

Notice what didn't happen here. Nobody at Kodak was outcompeted by a cheaper, better film. The thing that killed the business wasn't inside the industry Kodak was watching at all — it came from outside the circle of companies Kodak counted as competitors, and it didn't just take share, it removed the need for the product altogether. That is the subject of this lesson: not "who else makes what I make," but "what else could make my product unnecessary, and would I even notice it coming."

注意這裡沒有發生什麼事：沒有任何人用更便宜、更好的底片打敗柯達。真正殺死這門生意的東西，根本不在柯達平常盯著的那個產業裡——它來自柯達從來沒有算進「競爭對手」名單裡的圈外，而且它不只是搶走市占，是直接讓這個產品變得不再需要。這正是這一課要談的：不是「還有誰跟這門生意做一樣的東西」，而是「還有什麼東西可以讓這個產品變得不再必要，而分析者會不會根本沒察覺到它正在靠近」。

Telling a Real Threat from a Better Competitor

分辨真威脅與更強的競爭對手

A substitution threatAn alternative that completes the same underlying job through a different mechanism, at a switching cost low enough that people actually make the jump.用另一種機制完成同一件底層工作、轉換成本低到人們真的會跳過去的替代品。 is not simply "a rival makes a better version of what I sell." The test that actually separates a real substitution threat from ordinary competition is this: does the alternative complete the same underlying job the customer is really paying for, through a different mechanism, at a switching cost low enough that people actually make the jump? A compact disc losing share to a streaming subscription wasn't a story about audio fidelity — CDs often sound just as good. It was a story about the job the customer was actually hiring music for: "have the song I want, whenever I want it, without carrying anything around." Streaming did that job in a completely different way, at a near-zero marginal cost, and the switching cost (cancel one subscription, sign up for another) turned out to be almost nothing.

替代威脅An alternative that completes the same underlying job through a different mechanism, at a switching cost low enough that people actually make the jump.用另一種機制完成同一件底層工作、轉換成本低到人們真的會跳過去的替代品。並不是單純「有一個對手做出更好版本的同一個產品」。真正能把替代威脅跟一般競爭區分開來的判準是：這個替代品是不是用另一種機制，完成客戶真正在付錢買的那件「工作」，而且轉換成本低到人們真的會跳過去？CD 輸給串流訂閱，靠的不是音質——CD 的音質往往一樣好。真正的原因是客戶買音樂時真正想完成的那件工作：「任何時候都能聽到我想聽的歌，而且不用帶著東西走」。串流用完全不同的機制完成了同一件工作，邊際成本接近零，轉換成本（取消一個訂閱、註冊另一個）幾乎等於零。

Counter-example, and this one trips people up: an electric vehicle is usually not a substitution threat to the auto industry — it's a technology change inside the industry. The underlying job ("get me from point A to point B, in a vehicle I personally own and control") hasn't changed at all; only the powertrain has. The real substitution threat sitting one layer further out is something like autonomous ride-hailing, which would change the job itself from "own a car" to "summon a ride" — that's a different, and much larger, kind of threat, and it belongs on a different part of the scan with a much longer time horizon.

這裡有一個容易搞混的反例：電動車通常不算是對整個汽車產業的替代威脅，它是產業內部的技術轉變。客戶真正在買的那件工作（「用我自己擁有、自己控制的一台車，從甲地到乙地」）完全沒有改變，只有動力系統換了。真正在更外一層的替代威脅，比較像是自動駕駛的共享叫車服務——那會把工作本身從「擁有一台車」換成「召喚一趟車程」，是完全不同、而且大得多的威脅類型，應該放進掃描表裡不同的欄位，而且時間尺度也長得多。

How to see it from public information: look for the verb the customer would use to describe why they buy, not the noun for what they're buying. If two very different products satisfy the same verb ("get information to my customer," "store this safely," "get from here to there"), you have a real candidate. If the alternative only changes who wins the same purchase decision, it's ordinary competition, and the right toolkit is the one for value chains and profit pools, or the industry's capital cycle, not this one.

從公開資訊怎麼看出來：去找客戶會用來描述「為什麼要買」的那個動詞，而不是「買了什麼」的那個名詞。如果兩個非常不一樣的產品滿足的是同一個動詞（「把資訊送到客戶手上」「把東西安全存起來」「從這裡到那裡」），就有真正的候選威脅；如果替代品只是換了誰贏得同一個購買決策，那是一般競爭，該用的分析工具是價值鏈利潤池的位置與產業所在的資本週期階段，不是這一課要教的替代掃描。

Takeaway一句話收斂

Ask what job the customer is really hiring the product for — if a very different product satisfies the same job at low switching cost, you've found a real substitution candidate, not just another competitor.

問客戶真正在用產品完成的是什麼工作——如果一個完全不同的產品能用低轉換成本滿足同一件工作，你找到的是真正的替代候選，不只是另一個競爭對手。

Form One and Two: A Cheaper Technology, and an Architecture That Removes the Need

形態一與二：更便宜的技術，以及讓需求直接消失的架構轉變

The first form is the simplest to spot because it still looks like ordinary competition from a distance: a cost crossoverThe point where a new technology's cost-per-unit-of-output drops below the incumbent's, once switching cost is netted out.新技術每單位產出的成本，扣掉轉換成本後跌破舊技術的那個點。 — a different technology does the same job, through roughly the same architecture, at a steadily falling cost curve, until it crosses below the incumbent's cost and the switch pays for itself. LED lighting replacing incandescent and fluorescent bulbs is the clean case: nobody rethought what "lighting a room" means, LEDs simply got cheap enough per unit of light output, with a low enough switching cost (screw in a bulb), that the payback period collapsed from years to months. You can watch this coming from public information well before it shows up on the incumbent's income statement: track the new technology's cost-per-unit-of-output curve against the old one, and watch for the payback period on switching to shrink below whatever threshold customers actually act on (often around 12 to 24 months for a consumer-grade purchase).

第一種形態最容易從遠處看出來，因為它看起來還像是普通的競爭：一個成本交叉點The point where a new technology's cost-per-unit-of-output drops below the incumbent's, once switching cost is netted out.新技術每單位產出的成本，扣掉轉換成本後跌破舊技術的那個點。——另一種技術用大致相同的架構完成同一件工作，但成本曲線持續下滑，直到跌破舊技術的成本，轉換這件事本身開始划算為止。LED 照明取代白熾燈與日光燈是很乾淨的案例：沒有人重新定義「照亮一個房間」是什麼意思，LED 只是每單位光通量的成本夠便宜了，轉換成本也夠低（換一顆燈泡而已），回本期從好幾年壓縮到幾個月。你可以在這反映到舊技術的損益表之前，就從公開資訊看出它正在逼近：追蹤新技術每單位產出的成本曲線相對於舊技術的走勢，並留意轉換的回本期是不是正在跌破客戶真的會付諸行動的門檻（消費級產品常常落在十二到二十四個月左右）。

The second form is the one that actually killed Kodak, and it's more dangerous precisely because it doesn't look like a cost curve at all — it's an architectural shiftA restructuring of how the underlying activity gets done that removes an entire spending category, not just makes it cheaper.重新安排底層活動怎麼被完成，直接讓一整個支出項目消失，而不只是變便宜。 that removes the need outright. Cloud computing is a good non-Kodak example: it didn't just lower the per-unit cost of compute — it removed the entire budget line for many companies of owning servers, leasing data-center floor space, and staffing an on-premises operations team. The line item didn't shrink, it disappeared from a large share of buyers' budgets altogether. The way to catch this from the outside is different from tracking a cost curve: watch capex-to-opex mix shifts in customer-industry filings, watch whether a role category's job postings are declining even while the underlying activity — not the old way of doing it — keeps growing, and watch whether unit volumes for the old category fall even during periods when the job itself, measured a different way, is expanding.

第二種形態才是真正殺死柯達的那種，而且更危險，原因正是它根本不長得像一條成本曲線——是一種架構轉變A restructuring of how the underlying activity gets done that removes an entire spending category, not just makes it cheaper.重新安排底層活動怎麼被完成，直接讓一整個支出項目消失，而不只是變便宜。，直接把需求整個抹掉。雲端運算是一個不涉及柯達的好例子：它不只是降低了每單位運算的成本——它讓很多公司把自己買伺服器、租機房空間、養一組地端維運團隊這整個預算項目直接刪掉了。這個項目不是變小，是從一大批買方的預算裡整個消失。從外部要抓到這種威脅，方法跟追蹤成本曲線不一樣：去看客戶產業申報文件裡資本支出與營運支出的配比是不是在位移，去看某一類職缺的招聘是不是在減少、即使底層活動（不是舊的做法）還在成長，以及舊類別的銷售量是不是在下滑，即使用另一種方式量測的「那件工作」其實還在擴大。

Takeaway一句話收斂

Form one shows up as a cost curve you can watch cross over; form two shows up as an entire line item vanishing from customer budgets even while the underlying activity keeps growing — the second is harder to see coming and does more damage.

形態一會表現成一條你可以觀察它交叉超車的成本曲線；形態二則會表現成一整個支出項目從客戶預算裡消失，即使底層活動本身還在成長——後者更難被提早看見，傷害也更大。

Form Three and Four: The Customer Builds It, and the Rulebook Changes

形態三與四：客戶自己動手做，以及規則被改寫

The third form doesn't involve any new technology at all — it's your own customer deciding to become your competitor, because your margin got fat enough and the customer's volume got big enough that building the capability in-house pays for itself. This is where a genuinely counter-intuitive point sits: most people assume a high-margin, highly concentrated market position is the safest place to be. It's often the opposite. A supplier's fat margin is exactly the pool of money that makes it worth a large customer's while to absorb the fixed cost of building the capability in-house — the bigger the margin being extracted, the bigger the prize for the customer that backward-integrates around the supplier. A customer that represents a small share of a supplier's revenue and threatens to "build it ourselves" is usually a negotiating bluff, because the customer's volume doesn't clear the fixed cost of building the capability. A customer that represents a large and growing share, especially one operating a scale business with enormous internal volume of its own, is a different animal entirely. What to check from the outside: is the customer actually hiring engineers in the relevant discipline (a real signal, visible in job postings), and does a rough back-of-envelope payback calculation — annual spend with the supplier divided by an estimated cost to build the capability — clear a reasonable threshold, say under three to five years.

第三種形態根本不涉及任何新技術——是你自己的客戶決定變成你的競爭對手，因為你的毛利夠肥、他的用量夠大，大到自己動手做這件事在財務上划算。這裡正好放得下一個真正反直覺的結論：多數人以為毛利高、市占集中的位置最安全，事實往往相反。供應商肥厚的毛利，正是讓大客戶願意吸收自建產能固定成本的那筆獎金——你從客戶身上抽走的毛利愈厚，客戶反向整合繞過你的誘因就愈大。一個只占供應商營收一小部分的客戶說「我們要自己做」，通常只是談判時的虛張聲勢，因為他的用量根本跨不過自建的固定成本門檻；但一個占比大、還在成長，而且本身就是超大規模生意的客戶，就完全是另一回事。從外部怎麼查：看這個客戶是不是真的在招募相關領域的工程師（這是看得到的訊號，職缺公告會顯示出來），以及粗算一次回本期——客戶每年付給供應商的金額，除以自建這項能力的估計成本——是不是能落在一個合理的門檻內，例如三到五年以內。

The fourth form moves on a completely different clock: a regulator or government changes the rulebook, and an entire business model can become illegal, heavily taxed, or starved of a subsidy it used to enjoy — sometimes within a single legislative cycle, far faster than any technology cost curve. An export restriction that cuts a supplier off from its largest customer overnight is form four, not form one or two — no better or cheaper alternative appeared, the rules of who's allowed to buy from whom simply changed. The trap here runs the other direction from the technology forms: not every regulatory headline is a real threat. A bill that hasn't passed, or a rule with a ten-year phase-in schedule and generous exemptions, can dominate the news cycle while moving very little in practice. What to check: the actual effective date and enforcement mechanism buried in the text, not the headline, and whether the policy has durable, cross-party political support or is more likely to be diluted before it ever bites.

第四種形態走的是完全不同的時鐘：主管機關或政府改寫規則，一整個商業模式可能一夜之間變成違法、被重稅、或原本享有的補貼被抽掉——有時候只要一個立法週期就能發生，遠比任何技術成本曲線快得多。一項出口管制在一夜之間切斷供應商最大的客戶，屬於第四種形態，不是第一或第二種——沒有出現更好或更便宜的替代品，只是誰能向誰買東西的規則被改了。這裡的陷阱方向跟技術威脅相反：不是每一則政策新聞都是真威脅。一項還沒通過的法案，或一條有十年緩衝期、附帶大量豁免條款的規則，可以主導新聞版面，實際上動的東西卻很少。該查的是：條文裡真正的生效日期與執法機制，而不是標題，以及這項政策是不是有跨黨派、持久的政治支持，還是更可能在真正生效前就被稀釋掉。

Takeaway一句話收斂

Form three is your own success creating the incentive for your best customer to replace you; form four moves on a legislative clock, not a technology clock, so check the effective date in the text, not the headline.

形態三是你自己的成功，替你最大的客戶製造了取代你的誘因；形態四走的是立法時鐘，不是技術時鐘，該查的是條文裡的生效日期，不是新聞標題。

The Mechanical Scan: Forcing the List Out of Yourself

機械掃描：把清單從自己身上逼出來

Turn the four forms above into a fixed procedure and run it the same way every time, regardless of whether your gut says a candidate is "obviously not worth checking." List every adjacent technology and every adjacent industry you can think of — not the ones you already suspect matter, all of them, without pre-filtering. For each one, ask the same question: if this thing succeeds, what happens to my demand — does it disappear, shrink, or just move to a different vendor? Then rate each candidate on two axes: magnitudeWhether the underlying job disappears entirely, shrinks, or merely changes hands to a different vendor.底層工作是整個消失、萎縮、還是只是換手給另一個廠商。 and time horizon (within a year, three to five years, or ten-plus). The output is not a paragraph of prose, it's a short ranked list: what's high-magnitude and near-term gets an actual tracking metric this quarter; what's high-magnitude but far-off gets a single watch signal written down now, so the question doesn't have to be re-invented from scratch later; what's low on both axes gets explicitly dismissed, in writing, with a one-line reason — not silently dropped.

把上面四種形態變成一套固定程序，每次都用同一套方式跑一遍，不管你的直覺覺得某個候選「明顯不用查」。列出所有你想得到的相鄰技術與相鄰產業——不是你原本就懷疑重要的那幾個，是全部，不預先篩選。對每一項都問同一個問題：如果這個東西成功了，這門生意原本服務的需求會怎樣——消失、萎縮，還是只是換了個廠商拿走？然後在兩條軸上評分：命中面Whether the underlying job disappears entirely, shrinks, or merely changes hands to a different vendor.底層工作是整個消失、萎縮、還是只是換手給另一個廠商。與時間尺度（一年內、三到五年、還是十年以上）。輸出不是一段散文，是一張排序過的短清單：命中面高、時間又近的，這一季就要有一個真正在追蹤的指標；命中面高但時間遠的，現在就寫下一個監測訊號，免得以後要從零重新想這個問題；兩軸都低的，要明確寫下來排除，附一句理由——不是悄悄跳過不寫。

This tool has a clear boundary. It's the wrong instrument for garden-variety price competition among near-identical rivals inside the same architecture — two chains fighting over the same customer with the same basic format is a profit-pool and supply-discipline question, not a substitution question, and running a substitution scan on it just produces noise. It's also low-yield, though not useless, for industries sitting on an extremely slow-moving physical or technological base — the underlying physics of long-distance electricity transmission, for instance, hasn't fundamentally shifted in decades, and a mechanical scan there should still be run once, but shouldn't eat the same share of analysis time as, say, the capital-cycle question, because the marginal insight per hour spent is much lower.

這個工具有清楚的不適用邊界。用在同一種架構下、幾乎完全相同的對手之間的一般價格競爭上，是用錯了工具——兩家連鎖店用同樣的基本形式搶同一群客戶，那是利潤池與供給紀律要處理的問題，不是替代問題，對它跑替代掃描只會產生雜訊。對於底層物理或技術基礎變化極慢的產業，這個工具也不是沒用，但收穫有限——例如長距離輸電的底層物理原理，幾十年來沒有根本性改變，對這種產業還是該跑一次機械掃描，但不應該讓它占掉跟資本週期問題一樣多的分析時間，因為每花一小時能得到的邊際洞察小得多。

Takeaway一句話收斂

Run the same four-question pass on every adjacent technology without pre-filtering, rank by magnitude times time horizon, and write down even the ones you dismiss — but don't run this scan against ordinary same-architecture rivals or industries whose physical base barely moves.

對每一個相鄰技術都跑同一套四問流程，不預先篩選，依命中面乘以時間尺度排序，連你排除掉的也要寫下理由——但不要對同架構的一般對手，或物理基礎幾乎不變的產業硬套這套掃描。

Worked Example: Scanning a Local Car Dealership Network逐步範例：對汽車經銷網路跑一次替代掃描
  1. 1

    Define the job, not the product. A dealership network doesn't sell "owning a car" — dig one level deeper: it lets a local customer find, finance, and get a car serviced, all without dealing directly with a distant manufacturer. Getting this right matters because it sets what counts as a threat later — anything that lets a customer do those three things without the dealer is a candidate.

    先定義工作，不是定義產品。經銷網路賣的不是「擁有一台車」這件事本身——要再往下挖一層：它讓在地客戶能找到車、辦好融資、把車送修，而完全不用直接跟遠方的製造商打交道。把這個定義弄對很重要，因為它決定了後面什麼才算威脅——任何能讓客戶不透過經銷商就完成「找車、辦融資、送修」這三件事的東西，都是候選威脅。

  2. 2

    List every adjacent technology and business model without filtering: manufacturer direct-to-consumer sales, online used-car marketplaces, electric powertrains, subscription and car-sharing services, and autonomous ride-hailing. Two of these (direct sales, online marketplaces) barely involve any new technology — they're distribution-channel threats — while the other three involve a real change in the underlying product or usage pattern.

    不篩選地列出所有相鄰技術與商業模式：製造商直銷、線上二手車平台、電動動力系統、訂閱制與共享用車服務、自動駕駛叫車服務。其中兩項（直銷、線上平台）幾乎不涉及新技術——是通路威脅；另外三項則涉及產品或使用型態本身真正的改變。

  3. 3

    Rate each one on magnitude. magnitude = disappears / shrinks / changes hands — manufacturer direct sales: high magnitude for the sales-commission part of dealer revenue, but service and parts revenue is largely untouched. Online marketplaces: medium, they disintermediate the transaction but dealers still often supply the trade-in inventory and service work. Electric powertrains: high magnitude specifically for service and parts revenue, which has historically supplied roughly half or more of a dealer's gross profit, because EVs have far fewer moving parts and need far less scheduled maintenance. Autonomous ride-hailing: potentially very high magnitude, but it changes the job itself, which is a much bigger and slower-moving claim.

    對每一項評命中面。命中面 ＝ 消失／萎縮／換手——製造商直銷：對經銷商的銷售佣金部分命中面高，但保養維修零件的營收大致不受影響。線上平台：中等——它讓交易本身去中介化，但經銷商往往還是提供背後的中古車庫存與維修服務。電動動力系統：對保養維修營收的命中面特別高——這部分過去往往貢獻經銷商毛利的一半以上，而電動車活動零件少得多，排程保養需求也少得多。自動駕駛叫車服務：命中面可能非常高，但它改動的是工作本身，是一個大得多、也慢得多的主張。

  4. 4

    Rate each one on time horizon. Direct sales: already happening, though slowed in many places by dealer-franchise laws that create a multi-year, region-by-region drag. Online marketplaces: already happening, near-term and ongoing. Electric powertrains eating service revenue: gradual, tracking the EV share of the total vehicle fleet, which typically takes ten to twenty years to move from single digits toward a majority given the average vehicle's roughly 12-year life. Autonomous ride-hailing changing car ownership itself: long and genuinely uncertain, plausibly fifteen-plus years, gated by both technology maturity and regulation.

    對每一項評時間尺度。直銷：已經在發生，但在很多地方被經銷特許法規拖慢，形成長達數年、逐區推進的阻力。線上平台：已經在發生，近期且持續進行中。電動動力系統侵蝕保養營收：漸進式，跟著電動車占整體車隊比例走，考量一台車平均約十二年的使用壽命，通常要花十到二十年才能從個位數走到接近過半。自動駕駛叫車服務改變擁車本身：時間長，而且真的不確定，合理估計十五年以上，同時受技術成熟度與法規進度雙重制約。

  5. 5

    Combine the two axes and rank. Electric-powertrain service erosion sits at high magnitude and a medium (not short) time horizon — that earns an actual tracked metric today: service-and-parts share of gross profit, tracked against fleet EV penetration. Direct sales and online marketplaces are near-term but capped in magnitude by regulatory friction and the enduring physical-service leg of the job — worth a watch signal, not a top-priority tracker. Autonomous ride-hailing is high-magnitude but distant and uncertain — it gets one written-down watch signal and nothing more right now, so the analysis budget isn't spent chasing a fifteen-year story at the expense of the five-year one.

    把兩軸合起來排序。電動動力系統侵蝕保養營收落在命中面高、時間尺度中等（不是短）的位置——這個現在就該有一個真正在追蹤的指標：保養維修零件占毛利的比重，對照車隊電動化滲透率一起追蹤。直銷與線上平台時間近，但命中面被法規摩擦與服務這條物理性業務長期存在給封頂——值得設一個監測訊號，不必列為最優先追蹤項目。自動駕駛叫車服務命中面高但時間遠、不確定性也高——現在只寫下一個監測訊號就好，不必現在就投入太多分析預算去追一個十五年後的故事，而排擠掉五年內就會發生的事。

Why You Can't Find Your Own Blind Spot

為什麼你找不到自己漏掉什麼

Here's a specific and instructive failure. A piece of analysis on AI inference economics covered U.S. chipmakers and U.S. large language models in real depth — margin structure, customer concentration, the works. It did not mention China's open-weight model ecosystem at all. Not "covered it and dismissed it" — never brought it up. This wasn't a depth problem. The writer hadn't done a shallow job on the topic chosen for coverage; the writer had simply never asked the question, because the mental checklist used to decide what was worth covering was drawn from the same narrow set of sources and conversations normally paid attention to. The writer's own judgment and the writer's own blind spot came from the same place, which is exactly why "try to remember everything relevant" doesn't fix this kind of gap — nobody can remember a category they never had in the first place.

這裡有一個具體、而且很有教育意義的失敗案例。一份分析 AI 推理運算經濟的報告，把美系晶片廠與美系大型語言模型分析得很深入——毛利結構、客戶集中度，一應俱全。但這份報告完全沒有提到中國的開源大模型生態。不是「提了但判定不重要」，是根本沒被提出來過。這不是深度不夠的問題——寫這份報告的人在自己選定要談的主題上並沒有做得馬虎，而是他根本沒問出這個問題，因為他用來決定「什麼值得寫」的那份心中檢查清單，本身就是從他平常關注的那一小圈資訊來源和對話裡長出來的。他自己的判斷跟他自己的盲點來自同一個地方，這正是為什麼「提醒自己想周全一點」沒辦法修好這種漏洞——你沒辦法想起一件你腦中根本沒有分類放它的事。

The structural fix is not "be more careful." It's to bring in someone who hasn't read the draft — who knows only the topic, not the conclusions — and have that person independently list, from a blank page, the first-order variables a report on this subject "should" cover: competitive substitutes, geopolitical supply risk, self-supply by the largest customers, regulation, disruption from an adjacent technology stack. Then diff that list against what actually got written. Anything missing either gets researched properly, or gets written up explicitly as "considered this, excluded it, here's why" — which is itself a real, checkable answer. The rule worth remembering: a scan that finds nothingA documented negative result — the scan ran, no material threat surfaced, and a specific trigger signal is named that would reopen the question.有紀錄的否定結果——掃描確實跑過、沒有找到實質威脅，並且點名一個會讓這個問題重新打開的具體訊號。 is still a valid outcome. Skipping the scan and writing nothing is not the same thing, even though both produce a report with no mention of the topic — one is a documented decision that can be revisited, the other is a hole nobody knows is there.

結構性的修法不是「再小心一點」。而是找一個沒讀過草稿的人——只知道主題，不知道結論——讓他從一張白紙開始，獨立列出這個主題「應該」涵蓋的第一層變數：競爭替代品、地緣政治供給風險、最大客戶的自研動向、法規、來自相鄰技術堆疊的破壞。然後把這張清單拿去跟實際寫出來的東西對照。少掉的項目，要不就是真的補去查證，要不就明白寫出來「考慮過、排除了、理由是什麼」——這本身就是一個真實、可以被檢查的答案。值得記住的規則是：掃描完全找不到威脅A documented negative result — the scan ran, no material threat surfaced, and a specific trigger signal is named that would reopen the question.有紀錄的否定結果——掃描確實跑過、沒有找到實質威脅，並且點名一個會讓這個問題重新打開的具體訊號。，本身就是一個有效的結論。跳過掃描、什麼都不寫，不是同一回事——即使兩者最後都是一份完全沒提到這個主題的報告，一個是留下紀錄、可以被回頭檢查的決定，另一個是連自己都不知道存在的坑。

Takeaway一句話收斂

Your own checklist can't catch what it was never built to notice — use an independent, blank-page pass to find silent omissions, and treat a documented "scanned, found nothing" as a real answer.

你自己的檢查清單抓不到它本來就沒被設計去注意的東西——用一雙沒讀過你草稿的獨立眼睛從白紙開始查，把有記錄的「掃過、沒找到威脅」當成一個真正的答案。

Stress-Test the Load-Bearing Number, and Remember Facts Expire

承重數字要對抗式驗證，事實也有保質期

The open-weight model example above is a real pattern, not a hypothetical. A widely discussed model released with a training cost far below its closed-source rivals led to an immediate, intuitive conclusion — cheap training means less hardware demand — that fell apart on the very first serious look. The model's total parameter count sat in the trillions, which set a hardware floor for actually running it at any meaningful scale that had nothing to do with how cheaply it had been trained. Training cost and inference hardware requirement are simply different numbers, driven by different things, and the intuitive leap from one to the other was wrong from the first glance.

上面提到的開源模型案例，是真實發生過的模式，不是假設。一個廣受討論的模型發表時，訓練成本遠低於封閉模型對手，立刻引出一個直覺結論——訓練便宜就代表硬體需求會變少——但這個結論在第一次認真檢視時就站不住腳。這個模型的總參數量落在數兆等級，光是要在有意義的規模下跑起來，就設下了一個跟訓練成本高低完全無關的硬體地板。訓練成本跟推理階段的硬體需求根本是兩個不同的數字，由不同的東西驅動，從一個直接跳到另一個的直覺推論，從第一眼看就是錯的。

The general discipline this points to: before a load-bearing numberA figure that a conclusion or debate directly rests on — if it's wrong, the conclusion doesn't just weaken, it collapses.一個結論或判斷直接靠著它站立的數字——如果它錯了，結論不只是變弱，是整個垮掉。 is allowed to carry a conclusion, someone should actively try to break it, not just double-check the arithmetic. List out the handful of numbers a call actually depends on, and for each one, ask what would have to be true for it to be misleading — wrong scope, wrong time period, self-reported by an interested party, or simply extrapolated from too small a sample. This is adversarial verificationActively trying to disprove the numbers a conclusion depends on, rather than only confirming them.主動嘗試推翻一個結論所依賴的數字，而不是只做確認。, and it's cheap insurance relative to what it costs to be wrong about a conclusion that a large decision rests on.

這裡指向一個通用的紀律：在讓一個承重數字A figure that a conclusion or debate directly rests on — if it's wrong, the conclusion doesn't just weaken, it collapses.一個結論或判斷直接靠著它站立的數字——如果它錯了，結論不只是變弱，是整個垮掉。撐起一個結論之前，應該有人主動去嘗試把它戳破，而不是只做算術上的複查。把一個判斷真正依賴的那幾個數字列出來，對每一個都問：如果這個數字有誤導性，需要什麼條件成立——範圍算錯了、時間點算錯了、是利害關係方自己說的、或只是從太小的樣本外推。這就是對抗式驗證Actively trying to disprove the numbers a conclusion depends on, rather than only confirming them.主動嘗試推翻一個結論所依賴的數字，而不是只做確認。，相對於判斷錯誤時要付出的代價，這是很便宜的保險。

Facts also have an expiration date, and this matters most for the third form above — customer in-house build. A judgment written at one point in time that "this manufacturer's most advanced technology has essentially no outside customers, it's mostly captive" can be falsified within a single quarter by events that are entirely public: a major cloud provider confirms it's outsourcing custom chip production to that manufacturer, another large technology company signs a foundry contract, a leading chip designer announces a manufacturing partnership. None of that requires inside information — it just requires re-checking a fact that was true when written and treating it as perishable, not permanent. A customer relationship, an order backlog, or a supplier's exclusivity status should carry an implicit "as of" date, and the more load-bearing the fact is to a conclusion, the shorter that shelf life should be treated as.

事實也有保質期，這一點對上面第三種形態的威脅——客戶自研——尤其重要。一個在某個時間點寫下的判斷，說「這家製造商最先進的技術幾乎沒有外部客戶，主要是服務自己」，可能在短短一季內就被完全公開的事件推翻：一家雲端巨頭確認把自研晶片委外給這家製造商代工、另一家大型科技公司簽下代工合約、一家知名晶片設計公司宣布代工夥伴關係。這些都不需要任何內部消息——只需要重新查證一個寫下時是對的事實，並把它當成會過期的東西，而不是永久成立的東西。客戶關係、訂單能見度、或供應商的獨家地位，都應該帶著一個隱含的「查證日期」，而且這個事實對結論愈是關鍵，保質期就應該被設得愈短。

Takeaway一句話收斂

Before a number is allowed to hold up a conclusion, try to break it yourself; and treat any fact about a customer, supplier, or contract status as something with a shelf life, not a permanent truth.

在讓一個數字撐起結論之前，先自己試著把它戳破；客戶、供應商或合約狀態這類事實，都要當成有保質期的東西，不是永久成立的真相。

⚔War Story戰爭故事

Nokia's market capitalization peaked near $250 billion around the year 2000, making it briefly the most valuable company in Europe. As late as 2007 — the year the iPhone launched — Nokia still held roughly half of the global mobile phone market and dominated smartphones running its own Symbian operating system. BlackBerry told a similar story on a smaller scale: it held well over a third of the U.S. smartphone market as late as 2009, built on a physical keyboard, secure email, and a loyal enterprise customer base. Both companies had, by any conventional competitive measure, excellent hardware, strong brand loyalty, and real technical expertise in making phones. Neither of them lost to a better phone.

What beat them was a business-model substitution, not a technology one: the underlying job shifted from "make and receive calls and email reliably" to "carry a general-purpose computing platform where anyone can build an app." Apple's App Store, launched in 2008, and the Android ecosystem that followed, turned the phone into a two-sided marketplace — developers building for a platform, users choosing a platform for its apps — a structure neither Nokia's nor BlackBerry's business model was built around at all. By 2013, Nokia sold its entire handset business to Microsoft for roughly $7 billion, a small fraction of its peak value. BlackBerry's U.S. smartphone share had fallen under one percent within about seven years of its 2009 peak, and the company exited handset manufacturing entirely in 2016. This is a teaching case about how these two companies were displaced historically, not a comment on either company's business today.

諾基亞的市值在二零零零年前後一度接近兩千五百億美元，曾短暫成為歐洲市值最高的公司。就算到了二零零七年——蘋果推出 iPhone 的那一年——諾基亞在全球手機市場仍握有大約一半的市占率，而且在自家 Symbian 作業系統的智慧型手機上占有主導地位。黑莓走的是規模較小的相同劇本：直到二零零九年，它在美國智慧型手機市場的市占率仍超過三分之一，靠的是實體鍵盤、安全的企業電子郵件系統，以及忠誠的企業客戶群。就任何傳統競爭指標來看，這兩家公司都擁有優秀的硬體、強大的品牌忠誠度，以及做手機的真正技術實力。兩家都不是輸給了一支更好的手機。

打敗它們的是商業模式替代，不是技術替代：底層的工作從「可靠地打電話、收發郵件」，換成了「攜帶一個任何人都能在上面開發應用程式的通用運算平台」。蘋果二零零八年推出的 App Store，以及隨後崛起的 Android 生態系，把手機變成了一個雙邊市場——開發者為一個平台開發，使用者因為應用程式選擇平台——這是諾基亞與黑莓的商業模式當初完全沒有建立在上面的結構。到了二零一三年，諾基亞把整個手機部門以大約七十億美元的價格賣給微軟，只是巔峰價值的一小部分。黑莓在美國智慧型手機的市占率，在二零零九年的高峰後大約七年內就跌到不到百分之一，公司在二零一六年徹底退出手機製造。這是關於這兩家公司歷史上如何被取代的教學案例，不是對這兩家公司現在事業的評論。

⚔War Story戰爭故事

A cloud-computing giant spent years concentrating nearly all of its custom AI-chip design orders with a single semiconductor design partner — that partner's share of this specific order flow ran close to 95 percent for a long stretch. More recently, the cloud giant has deliberately cultivated a second supplier for the same category of chip, and industry estimates put the original partner's share drifting down toward roughly 80 percent, with further erosion toward the mid-60s expected over the following two to three years. Nothing about the underlying manufacturing process changed — both suppliers use comparable advanced chip-design methodology on similar leading-edge nodes.

This is form three from earlier in this lesson, in its purest shape: a customer, having grown large enough and paying enough margin to its supplier, deliberately builds and funds a second source specifically to take back negotiating leverage, not because the first supplier did anything wrong. From the outside, the earliest visible signal wasn't in either company's revenue — it was in the second supplier's design-win announcements and hiring activity, months before any dollar figure moved. This is a teaching example about a documented industry dynamic, not a current view on either company.

一家雲端運算巨頭多年來把幾乎全部的客製化 AI 晶片設計訂單，集中交給同一家半導體設計夥伴——這家夥伴在這一類訂單裡的市占率，有很長一段時間接近百分之九十五。近期這家雲端巨頭刻意培養了同一類晶片的第二家供應商，產業估計原本那家夥伴的市占會滑落到大約百分之八十左右，接下來兩三年內可能進一步侵蝕到六十五附近。底層的製造流程完全沒有改變——兩家供應商用的是相近的先進晶片設計方法論，在相似的先進製程節點上。

這正是這一課前面第三種形態最純粹的樣子：一個客戶，因為規模已經夠大、付給供應商的毛利也夠肥，主動出資扶植第二供應商，單純是為了拿回議價籌碼，不是因為第一家供應商做錯了什麼。從外部看，最早看得到的訊號都不在任何一家公司的營收上——而是在第二家供應商的設計得標公告與招募動態裡，比任何一個金額數字變動早了好幾個月。這是關於一個有紀錄的產業動態的教學案例，不是對任何一家公司現在的看法。

Think first: why would a supplier's near-monopoly share of a single large customer be a warning sign rather than a strength?先想一下：為什麼一家供應商對單一大客戶握有近乎壟斷的份額，反而可能是警訊而不是優勢？

Because the customer notices the concentration before anyone else does, and the customer is the one party with both the incentive and the scale to do something about it. A supplier collecting close to all of one giant customer's spending in a category is, from the customer's seat, a single point of failure and a pricing hostage situation at the same time. The larger and more strategic that spending category becomes, the stronger the customer's motive to fund a second source — not because the first supplier failed, but purely to restore negotiating leverage. High concentration inside one relationship is a measure of how much upside the customer has from diversifying away, which is the opposite of a moat from the supplier's side.

因為客戶比任何人都更早注意到這個集中度，而客戶正是唯一同時擁有動機與規模去採取行動的一方。一家供應商幾乎吃下某個巨型客戶在某個品類的全部支出，從客戶的角度看，這同時是單點故障風險，也是被人拿捏定價的處境。這個支出品類愈大、愈具策略性，客戶扶植第二供應商的動機就愈強——不是因為第一家供應商做錯了什麼，純粹是為了拿回議價籌碼。一段關係裡的高度集中，其實衡量的是客戶分散出去能拿到多少上檔空間，這對供應商來說剛好是護城河的反面。

Think first: why should "the scan found nothing" be written down and dated, rather than just quietly moving on?先想一下：為什麼「掃描完全找不到威脅」這件事本身要寫下來並標註日期，而不是默默略過就好？

Because the alternative — silence — is indistinguishable from having never checked at all. A written, dated "scanned this category, found no material threat, here is what would change my mind" is a claim that can be revisited, falsified, and updated when conditions shift. An unwritten absence carries no such trail; six months later, nobody can tell whether the topic was considered and dismissed for good reason, or simply never crossed anyone's mind. Since the entire point of the mechanical scan is to force disposition on every candidate, the negative result is not a shortcut around the work — it is the work's actual output for that candidate.

因為另一個選項——什麼都不寫——跟根本沒查過是分不出來的。一句寫下來、標註日期的「查過這個類別，沒找到實質威脅，以下是會讓我改變想法的條件」，是一個可以被回頭檢查、被證偽、隨情況更新的主張。沒寫下來的空白不會留下任何軌跡；半年後，沒有人分得出這個主題是被認真考慮過、有充分理由排除，還是根本沒人想到過。既然機械掃描整件事的重點就是強迫對每個候選項都做出處置，否定結果不是省略工作的捷徑——它就是這個候選項該有的實際產出。

Think first: can the company that disrupted an entire industry itself later be disrupted the same way?先想一下：曾經破壞掉整個產業的公司，自己之後有沒有可能被同樣的方式破壞？

Yes, and there is no structural reason it could not — the mechanical scan does not exempt the disruptor. The same forces that let a low-cost or architecturally different challenger dislodge an incumbent (a cheaper technology crossing a cost line, an architecture that removes the need for the category, a large customer building the capability in-house, a policy shift) apply equally to whoever holds the position today, including a company that itself arrived by disruption. Running the scan only on "old" or "legacy" companies and treating a newer disruptor's position as permanent is the same blind spot in a different costume — it substitutes a story about who is on the right side of change for the actual test of mechanism, magnitude, and switching cost.

會，而且沒有任何結構性的理由說它不會——機械掃描不會豁免破壞者本身。讓一個成本更低或架構不同的挑戰者能夠取代在位者的那些力量（更便宜的技術跨過成本線、一個讓整個品類的需求消失的架構轉變、大客戶把能力收回自己手上、政策轉向），同樣適用於今天站在這個位置上的任何一方，包括這家公司自己也是靠破壞別人才走到今天的。只對「老公司」或「舊產業」跑掃描，卻把新一代破壞者的位置當成永久的，是同一個盲點換了一套服裝——它用一個「誰站在變化正確的一邊」的故事，取代了真正該做的機制、命中面、轉換成本測試。

Pitfall: dismissing an early-stage substitute because it is "not good enough yet"陷阱：因為一個替代方案「現在還不夠好」就把它打發掉

When you see an analyst or a company executive wave away a new approach because today's version is slower, more expensive, or less capable than the established one, that comment is usually true and usually irrelevant. The right comparison is never today's version against today's incumbent — it's the challenger's improvement rate against the incumbent's improvement rate, extended over the time horizon that matters for the position being evaluated. A technology that is currently ten times worse but improving three times as fast will cross over years before the entrenched player has time to adjust its business model, and the crossover date is usually knowable well in advance from public cost curves, even while the technology still looks like a toy.

當你看到分析者或公司高層用「現在的版本比較慢、比較貴、能力比較差」來打發一個新方法時，這句話通常是對的，但通常也不重要。正確的比較從來不是今天的版本對今天的在位者，而是挑戰者的改善速度對在位者的改善速度，拉長到你正在評估的這個部位真正在意的時間長度。一個目前差十倍、但改善速度快三倍的技術，會在在位者還來不及調整商業模式之前就完成交叉超車，而這個交叉的時間點，通常可以從公開的成本曲線提前推算出來——即使這個技術現在看起來還像個玩具。

Pitfall: running the mechanical scan as a box-ticking form instead of an actual search陷阱：把機械掃描當成打勾填表，而不是真的去搜尋

The scan's value comes entirely from forcing candidates onto the list that your own judgment would have pre-filtered out as "obviously irrelevant." If the candidate list gets written after deciding the conclusion, or only includes the two or three substitutes already famous enough to be in the news, the form has been filled out but the actual work has not happened — this produces the appearance of rigor with none of the protection. A tell that the scan was done properly: at least one or two candidates on the list should look faintly embarrassing to have considered, because a real forced list always includes some low-probability entries alongside the obvious ones.

這個掃描的價值，完全來自於強迫把那些你自己的判斷早就會先過濾掉、覺得「明顯不相關」的候選項放進清單裡。如果候選清單是先有結論之後才回頭補的，或者只列了兩三個已經上過新聞、大家都知道的替代品，那表格是填完了，但真正的工作根本沒發生——這樣做出來的東西看起來很嚴謹，卻完全沒有保護力。一個掃描確實有認真做的跡象：清單裡至少該有一兩個候選項，是你自己覺得「列出來有點不好意思」的，因為一份真正被逼出來的清單，一定會混雜著一些低機率的項目，不會只有那些明顯的。

Pitfall: mistaking a customer's switching cost today for a permanent moat, ignoring that it is a countdown against vertical integration陷阱：把客戶現在的轉換成本當成永久護城河，忽略它其實是對抗垂直整合的倒數計時

A high switching cost genuinely protects a supplier against ordinary competitors — a rival offering a similar product at a lower price has to overcome that cost, and often can't. But switching cost does nothing to protect against a customer who chooses not to switch to a competitor at all, and instead builds the capability internally. The customer already knows the product intimately, already has the integration built, and already has the negotiating leverage of being the buyer — the same switching cost that keeps competitors out is, for the customer's own in-house effort, closer to a head start than a wall. The size of the margin the supplier earns from that customer is the fuel for the customer's in-house investment case; the bigger and more durable the margin looks from the outside, the more it should be read as building the business case for the customer to eventually stop paying it.

高轉換成本確實能保護供應商不受一般競爭對手侵擾——一個提供類似產品、價格更低的對手，得先克服這個成本，而且往往做不到。但轉換成本完全無法保護供應商不受一種情況侵擾：客戶根本不打算轉去用競爭對手，而是選擇把這個能力收回自己手上做。客戶已經對這個產品瞭若指掌，整合早就做好了，而且身為買家本來就握有議價籌碼——同樣一個把競爭對手擋在外面的轉換成本，對客戶自己想做的自研來說，比較接近助跑優勢而不是一道牆。供應商從這個客戶身上賺到的毛利有多大，本身就是客戶自研商業案的燃料；這個毛利從外部看起來愈大、愈穩固，愈應該被解讀成正在替客戶累積「總有一天不再付這筆錢」的說服力。

How This Connects

這一課接到哪裡

This lesson depends on two earlier ones: where the profit pool actually sits in a value chain and who has power to move it, and which phase of the capital cycle an industry is in — both determine how attractive a category looks to an outside challenger, and how much capital is chasing its way in from adjacent industries right now. A substitution threat you identify here becomes an input to the business-model lesson that follows, since the shape of a company's business model often determines which of the four forms it's most exposed to. It also feeds directly into judging whether a moat is strengthening or weakening over time, and eventually into whether a threat is already reflected in the price — a real substitution risk that the market has already priced in deeply is a very different situation from the same risk sitting unpriced.

這一課依賴前面兩課：利潤池實際上落在價值鏈的哪個位置、誰有能力把它挪走，以及一個產業目前處在資本週期的哪個階段——這兩者都決定了一個品類對外部挑戰者看起來有多誘人，以及現在有多少資本正從相鄰產業湧進來搶。你在這一課找出的替代威脅，會成為接下來商業模式那一課的輸入，因為一家公司商業模式的形狀，往往決定了它最容易暴露在四種形態裡的哪一種之下。它也會直接餵進判斷護城河隨時間是在增強還是在減弱，最終再餵進判斷一個威脅是不是已經反映在價格裡——一個市場已經深度定價過的真實替代風險，跟同一個風險完全沒被定價，是兩種完全不同的處境。

See where profit pools sit and move, the capital cycle, business model shape, whether a moat is widening or narrowing, and what a price already assumes.

參見利潤池的位置與移動、資本週期、商業模式的形狀、護城河是在擴大還是縮小，以及價格已經假設了什麼。

Checklist: before you conclude a company is safe from substitution檢查清單：在你判定一家公司不會被替代之前

Once you can tell a genuine substitution threat from ordinary competition, and can trace how far a company's economics actually depend on avoiding it, the next question is what shape the business itself takes — how it makes money, who pays whom, and where the fixed and variable costs sit. That is where the next lesson picks up.

當你能分辨出真正的替代威脅、也能追出一家公司的獲利結構到底有多依賴避開這個威脅之後，下一個問題就是這門生意本身長什麼樣子——它怎麼賺錢、誰付錢給誰、固定成本與變動成本又落在哪裡。這就是下一課要接著談的問題。
