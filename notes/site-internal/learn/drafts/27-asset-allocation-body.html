In 1986, three researchers at a US consulting firm — Gary Brinson, Randolph Hood, and Gilbert Beebower — published a study examining ten years of quarterly returns from 91 large US pension funds. Their finding, stated precisely, was that a fund's policy allocation — the target mix of stocks, bonds, and cash it had committed to on paper — explained, on average, about 93.6% of the variance in that fund's returns from one quarter to the next. It is one of the most cited numbers in the entire investing literature, and it has been repeated for four decades in almost every flavor of financial media, seminar, and glossy brochure, nearly always with the same one-sentence gloss: "asset allocation explains 93% of investment returns." Advisors have built entire sales pitches on that sentence. Investors have used it to justify skipping any thought about which specific fund or stock to hold, on the theory that the number proves it barely matters.

一九八六年，一家美國顧問公司的三位研究者——Gary Brinson、Randolph Hood、Gilbert Beebower——發表了一份研究，檢視九十一檔美國大型退休基金十年間的季報酬率。他們精確的結論是：一檔基金的「政策配置」——它白紙黑字承諾要維持的股／債／現金目標配比——平均解釋了這檔基金逐季報酬率變化裡大約 93.6% 的變異。這是整個投資文獻裡被引用最多的數字之一，四十年來在幾乎每一種財經媒體、講座、精美文宣裡被重複，而且幾乎每次都被簡化成同一句話：「資產配置決定了 93% 的投資報酬」。理財顧問拿這句話蓋起整套銷售話術；投資人拿它當理由，完全不去想該持有哪一檔特定基金或股票，理由是這個數字已經證明反正也沒差多少。

That one-sentence gloss is wrong, and the way it is wrong matters. Fourteen years later, in 2000, researchers Roger Ibbotson and Paul Kaplan went back through the same kind of data and pulled the claim apart into three separate questions the original number never distinguished. Asset allocation explains roughly 90% of a single fund's return variability over time — how bumpy the ride is, quarter to quarter. It explains only about 40% of the difference in returns between funds — the gap between a fund that did well and one that did poorly. And it explains close to 100% of the level of return a typical fund achieves, for a reason that has nothing mysterious about it: most funds, most of the time, end up close to whatever the broad market did, because most of a fund's assets simply are the market. Three different questions, three different answers, collapsed by decades of shorthand into a single, misleading number.

這句被簡化過的話，是錯的，而且錯的方式本身就值得細看。十四年後的二〇〇〇年，研究者 Roger Ibbotson 與 Paul Kaplan 回頭檢視同一類資料，把原本那個數字沒有區分開的三個不同問題拆開來看。資產配置解釋了一檔基金隨時間波動的大約 90%——也就是它逐季顛簸的程度；它只解釋了基金與基金之間報酬差異的大約 40%——也就是一檔表現好的基金跟一檔表現差的基金之間，差距是怎麼來的；而它解釋了將近 100% 的報酬水準本身，這個原因一點都不神秘：大多數基金在大多數時候，最後的結果都會跟大盤整體表現相去不遠，因為一檔基金的資產組成，本質上大部分就是市場本身。三個不同的問題、三個不同的答案，卻被四十年的口耳相傳，壓縮成一句以訛傳訛的單一數字。

What Allocation Decides, What It Doesn't

Put the Ibbotson–Kaplan breakdown in plain terms: policy allocationThe target long-run mix of asset classes an investor commits to on paper — stocks, bonds, cash, real assets — before any individual security is chosen.投資人白紙黑字承諾要維持的長期資產類別目標配比——股票、債券、現金、實質資產——在挑選任何個別證券之前就已經定下來。 decides which bus you are on and how much it rattles. It decides whether a typical year swings plus or minus 20% or plus or minus 7%, and it decides most of why your own account, this year versus last year, felt calm or terrifying. What it does not decide, on its own, is whether you personally end up ahead of or behind someone else who picked a similar bus with different passengers — that gap is a mix of security selection, fees, timing, and discipline, and the allocation choice explains only a modest slice of it. This reframes the entire question this lesson exists to answer: allocation is not a search for the highest-returning combination in history. It is a search for a combination whose bumpiness a specific person, with a specific time horizon and a specific temperament, can actually sit through without abandoning it at the worst possible moment.

把 Ibbotson–Kaplan 的拆解講成白話：政策配置The target long-run mix of asset classes an investor commits to on paper — stocks, bonds, cash, real assets — before any individual security is chosen.投資人白紙黑字承諾要維持的長期資產類別目標配比——股票、債券、現金、實質資產——在挑選任何個別證券之前就已經定下來。決定的是你坐上哪一班車、這班車會多顛簸。它決定了典型的一年是上下震盪 20% 還是 7%，也決定了你自己的帳戶今年跟去年比起來，感覺是平靜還是驚魂未定的大部分原因。它自己單獨沒有決定的，是你最後有沒有贏過另一個坐上類似班車、但選了不同乘客的人——那個差距，是選股、費用、時機、紀律混在一起造成的，配置這個選擇只解釋了其中一小塊。這重新定義了這一課要回答的問題：資產配置不是在史上所有組合裡找出報酬最高的那一個，而是在找一個組合，它的顛簸程度，是一個具體的人、帶著具體的時間軸跟具體的性情，真的能坐得住、不會在最糟的那一刻棄船的那一個。

Two further pieces of evidence show up on the cost side of this same picture, and both are arithmetic, not opinion. First, fees compound the same way returns do, in the opposite direction. A portfolio earning 7% nominal a year for 30 years grows to roughly 1.07^30 ≈ 7.61 times its starting value; the same portfolio earning 6% because 1 percentage point a year went to fees grows to roughly 1.06^30 ≈ 5.74 times — a final balance that is about 24.6% smaller, purely from one percentage point a year, purely from compounding (this is the arithmetic behind John Bogle's "cost matters hypothesis"). Second, and less obvious: even a genuinely good allocation, held by a real investor, tends to earn less than the fund's own reported return, because real investors buy and sell at the wrong moments. Morningstar's Mind the Gap series has tracked this directly: over the ten years from 2014 to 2023, the average US fund investor earned roughly 6.3% a year on a dollar-weighted basis — meaning weighted by when money actually moved in and out — versus the funds' own time-weighted return of roughly 7.3%, a gap of about 1.1 percentage points a year. 這幅圖畫的成本那一面，還有兩件證據，而且都是算術，不是意見。第一，費用跟報酬複利的方向完全一樣，只是反方向作用。一個組合，名目年報酬 7%，跑三十年，會成長到大約 1.07 的 30 次方，也就是約 7.61 倍起始金額；同一個組合，因為每年 1 個百分點被費用吃掉、變成 6%，跑三十年只會成長到大約 1.06 的 30 次方，也就是約 5.74 倍——最終餘額大約少了 24.6%，純粹來自每年 1 個百分點的差距，純粹是複利造成的（這就是 John Bogle「成本重要假說」背後的算術）。第二，比較不明顯：即使一個配置本身真的不錯，一個真實的投資人實際拿到的，往往比這檔基金自己公布的報酬率更少，因為真人投資人常常在錯的時機買進賣出。晨星的《Mind the Gap》系列直接追蹤了這件事：二〇一四到二〇二三這十年間，美國基金投資人平均以金額加權（也就是依照資金實際進出的時間點加權）算出來的年化報酬率，大約只有 6.3%，而同一批基金自己以時間加權算出的報酬率大約是 7.3%，落差大約 1.1 個百分點。同一份研究也發現，這個行為缺口The difference between a fund's own reported (time-weighted) return and what its actual investors earned (dollar-weighted), caused by buying and selling at the wrong times rather than by the fund itself.一檔基金自己公布的（時間加權）報酬率，跟它的實際投資人真正賺到的（金額加權）報酬率之間的落差，成因是投資人買賣的時機不對，不是基金本身的問題。在平衡型／配置型基金上最小，大約只有 0.4 個百分點，而在波動大、敘事驅動、單一主題的基金上最大——這是直接的證據，證明一個組合真正的行為成本，很大程度上取決於它有多容易被拿住。

The boundary worth stating directly: none of this means the "best" theoretical allocation is a useless concept, only that it is not the whole answer. A portfolio that would have earned the highest historical return on paper, but that a specific investor cannot actually sit through during its worst stretch, produces a real-world result closer to the behavior-gap number than to the paper number — the gap between "the return this earned" and "the return I earned" is where a large fraction of practical outcomes are actually decided, and it is a question about the investor at least as much as it is a question about the portfolio.

這裡有一個邊界值得直接講清楚：這一切都不代表理論上「最好」的配置是個沒用的概念，只是它不是完整的答案。一個紙上歷史報酬最高的組合，如果一個具體的投資人在它最難熬的那段時間根本坐不住，真實世界的結果會更接近行為缺口那個數字，而不是紙上那個數字——「這個配置賺了多少」跟「我自己實際賺了多少」之間的落差，才是大多數實際結果真正被決定的地方，而這至少一半是關於投資人本身的問題，不只是關於組合的問題。

The Long-Run Menu: Returns, Risk, and Why "the US" Is Not "the World"

Before comparing any specific combination of assets, it helps to know the raw ingredients each one is made of. The most exhaustive public source for this is the Global Investment Returns Yearbook, compiled by Elroy Dimson, Paul Marsh, and Mike Staunton, which tracks returns across 21 countries back to 1900. In its 2024 edition, covering 1900–2023, the approximate real (inflation-adjusted) annualized figures are: global equities (in US dollars, including dividends) around 5.0%; global bonds around 1.7%; cash (treasury bills) around 0.4%; US equities specifically around 6.5%, one of the best-performing markets in the sample; US bonds around 1.7%. The gap between equities and cash — the equity risk premium — runs roughly 4 to 5 percentage points a year over that entire span, but it is an average earned across more than a century, with entire decades inside it where equities lost money relative to cash.

在比較任何具體的資產組合之前，先弄清楚各個原料本身長什麼樣會有幫助。目前最詳盡的公開資料來源，是 Elroy Dimson、Paul Marsh、Mike Staunton 三人編纂的《全球投資報酬年鑑》，追蹤了 21 個國家回溯到一九〇〇年的報酬紀錄。在它的二〇二四年版本裡，涵蓋一九〇〇到二〇二三年，約略的實質（已扣除通膨）年化數字是：全球股票（以美元計、含股利）約 5.0%；全球債券約 1.7%；現金（國庫券）約 0.4%；美國股票單獨來看約 6.5%，是樣本裡表現最好的幾個市場之一；美國債券約 1.7%。股票相對現金的落差——也就是股票風險溢酬——在這整段期間平均大約是每年 4 到 5 個百分點，但這是橫跨一個多世紀才換來的平均值，中間有好幾個完整的十年，股票相對現金是虧錢的。

The counterintuitive part of this dataset is not the average — it is the survivors. An investor standing in 1900 had no way of knowing the United States would become the best-performing large market of the following century. Over that same window, stock markets in Austria, Germany, Japan, Russia, and China each experienced a real (inflation-adjusted) decline of more than 90%, and in some cases a total loss, driven by hyperinflation, war, or outright nationalization. A backtest built only on US market history — which is the default in almost every public tool, calculator, and textbook example, including most of the ones referenced later in this lesson — is quietly built on a sample that happened to survive intact. Mostly for reasons that have nothing to do with the bias being unimportant: US market data is the longest, cleanest, most continuously available, most standardized series in the world, and building an equivalent clean series for a country whose market was nationalized or hyperinflated away is genuinely difficult — much of that data is fragmentary or simply does not exist in a comparable form. The Arithmetic of Diversification — and Why Correlation Is Not a Constant

分散投資的算術——以及為什麼相關性不是一個常數

Diversification is often described qualitatively — "don't put all your eggs in one basket" — but it has an exact formula, and the formula is what reveals why the same two assets can diversify each other well in one decade and barely at all in another. For a two-asset portfolio, portfolio volatility follows σ_p² = w₁²σ₁² + w₂²σ₂² + 2·w₁·w₂·ρ·σ₁·σ₂, where w is each asset's weight, σ is each asset's own volatility, and ρ (rho) is the correlation coefficientA number from -1 to +1 measuring how closely two assets move together — +1 means they always move in lockstep, -1 means they always move in exact opposite directions, 0 means no relationship.一個介於 -1 到 +1 之間的數字，衡量兩個資產走勢有多同步——+1 代表兩者永遠同向、同步移動，-1 代表兩者永遠完全反向，0 代表沒有關係。 between them — a single number describing how closely the two move together. Take a concrete example using two of the volatility figures from the previous section: equities at roughly 18% volatility and bonds at roughly 6%, held in a 60/40 split. At a correlation of exactly 0 (the two move independently), the combined portfolio's volatility works out to about 11.1%. Push the same two assets into a correlation of -0.3 (bonds tend to rise when stocks fall, a genuine hedge), and the combined volatility drops to roughly 10.3%. Push the correlation instead to +0.7 (bonds and stocks tend to fall together, as they did in 2022), and the combined volatility climbs to roughly 12.6% — and if the correlation pushes toward the +0.9 to +1.0 range that shows up in the worst crisis windows, it climbs further still, toward 13%.

分散投資常常被用定性的方式描述——「不要把雞蛋放在同一個籃子裡」——但它其實有一個精確的公式，而這個公式，正是能解釋為什麼同樣兩個資產，在某個十年互相分散得很好、換到另一個十年卻幾乎沒用的原因。對一個雙資產組合來說，組合波動率遵循 σ_p² = w₁²σ₁² + w₂²σ₂² + 2·w₁·w₂·ρ·σ₁·σ₂ 這個公式，其中 w 是各資產的權重、σ 是各資產自己的波動率，而 ρ（相關係數）則是兩者之間的相關係數A number from -1 to +1 measuring how closely two assets move together — +1 means they always move in lockstep, -1 means they always move in exact opposite directions, 0 means no relationship.一個介於 -1 到 +1 之間的數字，衡量兩個資產走勢有多同步——+1 代表兩者永遠同向、同步移動，-1 代表兩者永遠完全反向，0 代表沒有關係。——一個單一數字，描述兩者走勢有多同步。用上一節的波動率數字舉一個具體例子：股票波動率約 18%、債券約 6%，以 60/40 配置。當相關係數恰好是 0（兩者各走各的）時，合成後的組合波動率算出來大約是 11.1%。把同樣這兩個資產的相關係數推到 -0.3（債券在股票跌的時候傾向上漲，是真正的避險），合成後的波動率會降到大約 10.3%。反過來把相關係數推到 +0.7（債券跟股票傾向一起跌，就像二〇二二年那樣），合成後的波動率會升到大約 12.6%——如果相關係數在最惡劣的危機時刻推到 +0.9 到 +1.0 那個區間，波動率還會再往上，逼近 13%。

The reverse-intuitive conclusion sits right there in those three numbers: the weights never moved. Every one of those scenarios is the same 60% stocks, 40% bonds. The entire swing in risk — a meaningful, double-digit-percentage change in how much the portfolio actually bounces around — came from one input that most investors never look at, and that changes on its own, for reasons that have nothing to do with the investor's chosen weights. This is why, according to long-run correlation series compiled by researchers such as Antti Ilmanen at AQR, US stock-bond correlation was mostly positive through the 1970s to 1990s (roughly +0.2 to +0.5, an inflation-dominated era in which both stocks and bonds suffered together when prices rose too fast), turned mostly negative from roughly 2000 to 2020 (roughly -0.3, a growth-scare-dominated era in which bonds rallied whenever equity markets panicked), and swung back sharply positive in 2022. The protective feeling that "60/40 always cushions the fall" is not a property of the number 60/40 — it is a property of a specific correlation regime that has, historically, come and gone.

反直覺的結論就寫在這三個數字裡：權重從頭到尾都沒有動過。每一個情境都是同一組 60% 股票、40% 債券。整段風險的變化——組合實際彈動幅度出現的、有意義的兩位數百分比變化——來自一個大多數投資人從來不會去看的輸入變數，而且這個變數會自己改變，跟投資人選了哪組權重完全無關。這正是為什麼，依照 AQR 的 Antti Ilmanen 等研究者整理的長期相關性序列，美國股債相關性在一九七〇到一九九〇年代大多是正的（大約 +0.2 到 +0.5，那是一個通膨主導的年代，物價漲太快時股債會一起受傷）；到了大約二〇〇〇到二〇二〇年，轉為大多是負的（大約 -0.3，那是一個成長恐慌主導的年代，每次股市恐慌，債券就會上漲）；到了二〇二二年，又劇烈翻回正相關。「60/40 永遠能墊住下跌」這種保護感，並不是 60/40 這個數字本身的屬性——它是某一個特定相關性環境的屬性，而這個環境，歷史上是會來來去去的。

The boundary worth naming directly: this formula requires an input — the correlation — that cannot be observed in real time with any precision; it can only be estimated from a trailing window of historical data, and that estimate is itself unstable, especially over short windows. A diversification calculation built on a correlation measured over the calm years just before a regime shift will understate the portfolio's true risk the moment the regime actually shifts — which is exactly the moment the estimate is needed most and trusted least.

這裡有一個邊界值得直接點名：這個公式需要一個輸入——相關係數——而這個輸入，在真實時間裡沒辦法被精確觀測到，只能用一段回顧性的歷史資料窗口去估計，而這個估計本身就不穩定，尤其是窗口很短的時候。一份用「環境剛要轉變之前那段平靜歲月」量出來的相關係數所建構的分散投資計算，會在環境真正轉變的那一刻低估組合的真實風險——而那一刻，恰恰是這個估計值最被需要、卻也最不值得信任的時候。

Takeaway一句話收斂

The same weights can carry meaningfully different risk depending on a correlation input most investors never check — and stock-bond correlation has historically flipped sign across multi-decade regimes, most recently in 2022.同一組權重，可能帶著明顯不同的風險，差別在於一個大多數投資人從來不會去檢查的相關性輸入——而股債相關性歷史上曾經在跨越數十年的不同環境裡翻過正負號，最近一次就是二〇二二年。

60/40 in Practice: What Its Worst Years Actually Looked Like

60/40 的實戰紀錄：它最慘的幾年到底長什麼樣

The classic 60% stocks / 40% bonds split is not a recommendation this lesson is making — it is the most heavily studied allocation in the public record, which makes it the best available case study for how a "balanced" mix actually behaves under stress. Since 1926, a US 60/40 mix has compounded at roughly 8–9% a year nominal, with annualized volatility around 11–12% — numbers that, on their own, look tame. The main drawdowns tell a more useful story. In 1973–74, 60/40 fell roughly 28% in nominal terms and roughly 40% in real terms, because inflation was running near 12% a year and eroding the value of both legs at once. From 2000 to 2002, it fell roughly 20%. From the peak to the trough of the 2007–09 financial crisis, roughly 30–32%. And in 2022, the full calendar year alone produced a loss of roughly 16–17% — equities down about 18%, a broad bond index down about 13% — one of the worst years for a combined stock-and-bond portfolio since 1937, and worth naming as the fourth of this lesson's four core historical case studies.

經典的 60% 股票／40% 債券配置，不是這一課在提出的建議——它是公開紀錄裡被研究得最透徹的一種配置，這正好讓它成為觀察「平衡型」配置在壓力下實際會怎麼表現的最佳案例。自一九二六年起，美國的 60/40 配置名目年化複利大約 8 到 9%，年化波動率大約 11 到 12%——單看這兩個數字，顯得很溫和。它主要的幾次回撤，說出了更有用的故事。一九七三到七四年，60/40 名目跌幅大約 28%，實質跌幅大約 40%，因為當時通膨接近每年 12%，同時侵蝕著股債兩腿的價值。二〇〇〇到二〇〇二年，跌了大約 20%。二〇〇七到二〇〇九年金融危機從高點到低點，大約跌了 30 到 32%。而二〇二二年，光是這整整一個日曆年，就虧損了大約 16 到 17%——股票跌了大約 18%，一檔綜合債券指數跌了大約 13%——是一九三七年以來，股債合成組合表現最差的年份之一，也是這一課四個核心歷史案例裡的第四個，值得單獨點名。

📉War Story戰爭故事

2022 broke a rule that a generation of investors had come to treat as close to permanent: that bonds cushion a stock market decline. Equities fell as the fastest interest-rate hiking cycle in decades repriced growth expectations; bonds, rather than rallying as a safe haven the way they had in 2000–02 and 2008, fell alongside them, because the same rate hikes that were hurting stock valuations were directly crushing bond prices from the other direction — when a central bank raises rates to fight inflation, existing bonds paying yesterday's lower rate become mechanically less valuable, the same day that higher discount rates are compressing stock multiples. A widely circulated headline in financial media that year read "60/40 is dead," and the phrase spread fast enough that some advisors began recommending clients abandon the mix entirely. By 2023–24, the same combination had rebounded sharply, and total ten-year returns through the full episode remained solidly positive.

二〇二二年打破了一條被一整個世代的投資人幾乎當成永恆定律的規則：債券能墊住股市下跌。當史上數一數二快速的升息循環重新定價成長股的預期時，股票下跌了；而債券並沒有像二〇〇〇到〇二年、或二〇〇八年那樣，扮演避風港角色反向上漲，反而跟著一起跌——因為同一波升息，一邊在打擊股票的估值，另一邊直接從反方向壓垮了債券價格：當央行為了對抗通膨升息，昨天發行、票息較低的既有債券，會在機械上立刻變得比較不值錢，而這正好發生在較高的折現率同時壓縮股票倍數的同一天。當年財經媒體流傳一句被廣泛引用的標題：「60/40 已死」，這句話傳得夠快，甚至有理財顧問開始建議客戶徹底放棄這個配置。到了二〇二三到二四年，同樣的組合強力反彈，整段插曲貫穿下來，十年期總報酬依然穩穩為正。

The teaching point is not that the "death" headlines were wrong to note the pain — the pain was real and large. It is that a 60/40 mix hitting its worst nominal year in nearly a century, during exactly the environment (sharp, synchronized inflation-driven rate hikes) that both the correlation-regime evidence above and the 1973–74 episode would have predicted as its weak spot, is not evidence the mix is broken. It is evidence the mix has a known weak environment, and the mistake is not owning that environment — the mistake is not knowing in advance what that environment was.

這裡的教學重點，不是說「60/40 已死」這種標題不該點出當時的痛——那個痛是真的，而且很大。重點是：60/40 配置遇到近一個世紀以來最慘的名目年份，而且剛好發生在上面相關性環境的證據、以及一九七三到七四年那段插曲都已經預言過的那個弱項環境（急遽、同步、通膨驅動的升息）裡，這件事本身並不是這個配置壞掉的證據。它是「這個配置有一個已知的弱項環境」的證據，而真正的錯誤不是持有這個弱項環境，錯誤是事前不知道那個弱項環境是什麼。

Takeaway一句話收斂

A configuration's worst historical year almost always arrives inside its own known weak environment — 60/40's is a sharp, synchronized, inflation-driven rate-hiking regime — and that is a fact to know in advance, not a verdict to render after the fact.一個配置歷史上最慘的一年，幾乎總是發生在它自己已知的弱項環境裡——60/40 的弱項環境，就是急遽、同步、通膨驅動的升息——這是一件該事先知道的事實，不是事後才拿來下判決的理由。

Diversification's Limits: International Markets, and When Everything Falls Together

分散投資的極限：跨國市場，以及當一切一起下跌的時候

Diversifying between stocks and bonds is one axis. A second, independent axis is geography, and the evidence there is at least as sobering. Japan's Nikkei 225 index hit an all-time high near 38,916 in December 1989; it did not close above that level again until February 2024 — a round trip of roughly 34 years. The US S&P 500, over its own "lost decade" from 2000 to 2009, produced a total nominal return, including dividends, of roughly -1% a year over the full ten years, while emerging markets over that same decade returned roughly +10% a year. Winners rotate across roughly decade-long stretches in a way no one can reliably predict in advance: non-US markets outperformed through the 1970s and 1980s, the US led through the 1990s, non-US led through the 2000s, and the US led again through the 2010s and into the 2020s. An investor sitting in any one of those decades, looking only backward, would have drawn the opposite conclusion from the one that turned out to be correct for the decade that followed.

在股票跟債券之間分散，是一個軸。另一個獨立的軸，是地理位置，而這方面的證據，至少一樣發人深省。日本日經 225 指數在一九八九年十二月，觸及約 38,916 點的歷史高點；一直到二〇二四年二月，它才再次收在那個高點之上——來回一趟，大約花了 34 年。美國 S&P 500 指數，在它自己「失落的十年」，也就是二〇〇〇到二〇〇九年，含股利的十年總名目報酬大約是每年 -1%，而同一個十年裡，新興市場大約是每年 +10%。贏家的輪替，是以大約每十年一個週期在發生的，而且沒有人能夠可靠地事先預測誰會贏：一九七〇跟八〇年代非美市場勝出，一九九〇年代美國領先，二〇〇〇年代非美市場再度勝出，二〇一〇年代到二〇二〇年代初又是美國領先。一個站在其中任何一個十年、只回頭看歷史的投資人，得出的結論，都會跟接下來那個十年真正正確的答案剛好相反。

This pattern of "no one can reliably predict which market wins next" runs directly against a common, understandable behavior: home biasThe well-documented tendency for investors worldwide to hold a much larger share of their own domestic market than that market's actual weight in the global economy would justify.全世界投資人都有的一個有充分紀錄的傾向：持有本國市場的比重，遠遠超過該市場在全球經濟裡實際占的份量所能證成的比例。, the well-documented tendency for investors everywhere to hold 70–90% of their equity portfolio in their own domestic market, regardless of what share that market actually represents of the world. The United States currently makes up somewhere north of 60% of global equity market capitalization, which makes a heavy US home bias less costly than most; Taiwan's entire market makes up roughly 2% of global market capitalization, and its flagship broad index is itself heavily concentrated in a single industry — semiconductors. A Taiwanese investor who holds equity exposure entirely through one domestic broad-market instrument is not just skipping international diversification; they are concentrating the vast majority of their equity risk inside a market that represents about one-fiftieth of the world's investable equity, and inside an index that is itself dominated by a single sector's fortunes.

Taiwan's own broad market index tells a starker single-market story than either Japan or the US. The Taiwan Weighted Index (TAIEX) peaked at approximately 12,682 points in February 1990, at the top of an extraordinary domestic bull run driven heavily by margin-financed speculation and real estate leverage. By October of the same year — eight months later — it had collapsed to roughly 2,485, a decline of about 80%. The index did not durably reclaim its 1990 high until around 2020, a round trip of roughly three decades. The mechanism behind both the rise and the fall was concentrated domestic leverage: retail margin trading had become a dominant share of daily volume, real estate and equity speculation fed each other, and when tightening monetary policy and a real estate correction hit simultaneously, the same leverage that had accelerated the climb accelerated the collapse.

台灣自己的廣泛型市場指數，講的是一個比日本或美國都更極端的單一市場故事。台灣加權指數在一九九〇年二月，觸及約 12,682 點的高點，那是一段由融資投機與房地產槓桿重度驅動的驚人本土多頭行情的頂點。到了同一年十月——短短八個月後——指數崩跌到大約 2,485 點，跌幅大約 80%。指數一直要到大約二〇二〇年，才真正穩固地收復一九九〇年的高點，來回一趟，大約花了三十年。這一段暴漲暴跌背後的機制，是集中的本土槓桿：散戶融資交易一度占了每日成交量的絕大部分，房地產跟股票投機互相餵養，當緊縮的貨幣政策跟房地產修正同時打下來，正是同一批加速上漲的槓桿，加速了崩跌。

The lesson here is not "Taiwan's market is uniquely dangerous." It is that any single domestic market — including one that has since matured considerably — can produce a multi-decade round trip on its own, and an investor whose equity exposure lives entirely inside one market is fully exposed to that market's own leverage cycle, valuation extremes, and concentration risk, with nothing else in the portfolio behaving differently at the moment it matters most.

這裡的教訓不是「台灣市場特別危險」。而是任何單一的本土市場——包括一個後來已經成熟許多的市場——都可能靠自己走出一趟橫跨數十年的來回，而一個股票曝險完全活在單一市場裡的投資人，會完全暴露在那個市場自己的槓桿週期、估值極端、集中度風險之下，而在最重要的那一刻，組合裡沒有任何一塊會表現得不一樣。

The deepest limit of diversification shows up not across decades but inside a single crisis window, and 2008 is the cleanest public example. Over that year, US equities fell roughly 37%; developed non-US markets fell roughly 43%; emerging markets fell roughly 53%; publicly traded real estate (REITs) fell roughly 37%; broad commodities fell roughly 36%; high-yield corporate bonds fell roughly 26%. Nearly every category typically sold as a diversifier — different countries, different sectors, different asset structures — fell together, and fell hard, at the same time. What actually held up that year was a narrow list: long-term US government bonds (up roughly 20% or more) and cash.

分散投資最深的極限，不是出現在跨越數十年的比較裡，而是出現在單一一次危機的窗口之內，而二〇〇八年是最乾淨的公開例子。那一年，美國股票跌了大約 37%；已開發非美市場跌了大約 43%；新興市場跌了大約 53%；公開交易的不動產投資信託（REITs）跌了大約 37%；廣泛大宗商品跌了大約 36%；高收益公司債跌了大約 26%。幾乎每一個通常被拿來當分散工具賣的類別——不同國家、不同產業、不同資產結構——全都一起下跌，而且跌得很凶，就在同一段時間。那一年真正撐住的，是一份很短的清單：美國長天期公債（漲了大約 20% 以上）跟現金。

The mechanism is a specific one worth naming precisely: in a systemic liquidity crisis, the correlation between risk assets of almost any stripe converges toward 1, because the driving force is not any one asset's own fundamentals but a single, shared cause — a scramble for cash and safety that hits every risk asset through the same channel at once. Diversification that is built only across labels (different countries, different sectors) rather than across underlying risk sources (equity-market risk broadly, versus interest-rate risk, versus genuine safe-haven demand) fails exactly when it is needed most, because in that moment nearly everything with equity-like risk is, functionally, one position.

這裡的機制值得精確點名：在一場系統性的流動性危機裡，幾乎任何種類的風險資產之間，相關性都會趨近於 1，因為驅動的力量，不是任何一個資產自己的基本面，而是一個單一、共用的原因——一場對現金與安全性的搶奪，透過同一個管道，同時打中每一個風險資產。如果分散投資只建立在「標籤不同」（不同國家、不同產業）之上，而不是建立在「底層風險來源不同」之上（廣泛的股權市場風險，對比利率風險，對比真正的避險需求），它會剛好在最需要它的那一刻失效，因為在那一刻，幾乎每一個帶著股票般風險的東西，功能上都是同一個部位。

Takeaway一句話收斂

Winners rotate across decades in a way no one can predict in advance, which is the case against home bias — and in a systemic crisis, risk-asset correlations converge toward 1 regardless of label, which is the case for diversifying across risk sources, not just names.贏家跨數十年輪替，而且沒有人能事先預測——這正是反對家鄉偏誤的理由；而在系統性危機裡，不管標籤是什麼，風險資產的相關性都會趨近於 1——這正是「要跨風險來源分散、不只是跨名字分散」的理由。

Think first: in 2008, why did long-term government bonds rise while corporate high-yield bonds — also "bonds" — fell by roughly a quarter?先想一下：二〇〇八年，為什麼長天期政府公債上漲，而同樣掛著「債券」名字的高收益公司債，卻跌了大約四分之一？

Because "bond" is a label, not a risk source. A long-term government bond carries mostly interest-rate risk and, in a flight to safety, benefits directly from falling rates and a rush of demand for the safest available asset. A high-yield corporate bond carries mostly credit risk — the risk that the issuing company itself cannot pay — and in a systemic crisis, credit risk behaves far more like equity risk than like government-bond risk, because the same fear of corporate failure that crushes stock prices crushes the value of a promise to be repaid by that same company. Sorting by the word "bond" hides this; sorting by the actual risk being carried reveals it.

因為「債券」是一個標籤，不是一個風險來源。長天期政府公債主要帶著利率風險，在資金逃向安全資產的時候，會直接受益於利率下降跟對最安全資產的搶購需求。高收益公司債主要帶著信用風險——也就是發行公司自己可能還不出錢的風險——在一場系統性危機裡，信用風險的表現，遠比較像股票風險，而不像政府公債風險，因為同一種對企業倒閉的恐懼，一邊打垮股價，另一邊也打垮了「這家公司會還錢」這個承諾的價值。用「債券」這個詞去分類，會把這件事藏起來；用實際承擔的風險去分類，才會把它揭露出來。

Gold is often sold as the diversifier that never fails, and its 1970s record — up roughly 24x from 1971 to 1980 as the US left the gold standard and inflation surged — is the chart most often shown to make that case. What is shown far less often is what happened next. Gold peaked at roughly $850 an ounce in January 1980. In nominal dollar terms, it did not durably reclaim that level until around 2008 — a 28-year round trip. In real, inflation-adjusted terms, an investor who bought at the 1980 peak was still underwater decades later.

黃金常常被賣成一個永遠不會失效的分散工具，而它一九七〇年代的紀錄——從一九七一到一九八〇年，隨著美國脫離金本位、通膨飆升，大漲了大約 24 倍——是最常被拿出來佐證這個說法的那張圖。比較少被拿出來講的，是接下來發生了什麼事。黃金在一九八〇年一月，觸及大約每盎司 850 美元的高點。以名目美元計算，它一直要到大約二〇〇八年，才穩固地重新站上那個高點——來回一趟，花了 28 年。以剔除通膨的實質計算，一個在一九八〇年高點買進的投資人，好幾十年後，仍然是虧損的。

The mechanism behind that 28-year drought has a name: gold pays no yield. Its opportunity cost is set directly by the real interest rate — the interest rate after subtracting inflation — because holding gold means giving up whatever a bond or bank deposit would have paid instead. From 1980 onward, Federal Reserve chair Paul Volcker pushed real interest rates sharply positive to break the inflation of the 1970s, and kept policy tight for years afterward. Every year that real rates stayed meaningfully positive, the opportunity cost of holding a zero-yield asset stayed high, and gold's price reflected that drag for nearly three decades, even as inflation itself moderated.

這 28 年乾旱背後的機制，是有名字的：黃金不配息。它的機會成本，直接由實質利率決定——也就是名目利率減掉通膨之後的利率——因為持有黃金，代表放棄了把同一筆錢放進債券或銀行存款原本可以拿到的利息。從一九八〇年起，聯準會主席 Paul Volcker 大幅把實質利率推升為正值，藉此打斷一九七〇年代的通膨，並在之後好幾年維持緊縮的政策。只要實質利率持續明顯為正，持有一個零配息資產的機會成本就持續偏高，而黃金的價格，反映了這股拖力，長達將近三十年，即便通膨本身早已趨緩。

The lesson is not "gold is a bad asset" — over the very long run and across specific crisis windows, it has done exactly what it was bought to do. The lesson is that "diversifier" does not mean "cannot have a multi-decade bad stretch of its own." Every asset in a portfolio, including the ones bought specifically as insurance, has its own known weak environment, and gold's is a sustained period of high, positive real interest rates.

這裡的教訓不是「黃金是一個爛資產」——拉長到非常長的時間，以及在特定的危機窗口裡，它確實做到了當初被買進的理由。這裡的教訓是：「分散工具」不代表「自己不會有長達數十年的低潮期」。組合裡的每一個資產，包括那些特意當作保險買進的資產，都有自己已知的弱項環境，而黃金的弱項環境，就是一段持續、且為正值的高實質利率時期。

Rebalancing: What the Evidence Actually Supports

再平衡：證據真正支持的是什麼

Rebalancing means periodically selling down whatever has grown to be an oversized share of the portfolio and topping up whatever has shrunk, to bring the mix back toward its original target weights. Two separate research efforts — one from Vanguard published in 2010, updated again in 2022 — tested this across a wide range of rebalancing rules: calendar-based (monthly, quarterly, annually), threshold-based (rebalance whenever a holding drifts more than some number of percentage points from target), and combinations of both. The counterintuitive finding, worth naming explicitly as this lesson's reverse-intuitive conclusion: the exact frequency or threshold chosen barely matters. Annual rebalancing, quarterly rebalancing, and a 5-percentage-point threshold rule produced return and risk outcomes close enough to each other, over multi-decade periods, that no single rule stood out as clearly superior. What mattered far more than the specific rule was whether the investor rebalanced consistently at all, versus letting weights drift unchecked for years.

再平衡的意思，是定期把成長到占比過大的資產賣掉一部分，並補足占比萎縮的資產，把配置拉回接近原本的目標權重。兩份獨立的研究——一份是 Vanguard 二〇一〇年發表、二〇二二年再更新的研究——測試了一大批不同的再平衡規則：以日曆為準（每月、每季、每年）、以門檻為準（只要某項持股偏離目標超過某個百分點就再平衡），以及兩者的組合。這裡有一個反直覺的發現，值得明講，作為這一課的反直覺結論：確切選了哪一種頻率或門檻，幾乎不影響結果。每年再平衡、每季再平衡、5 個百分點的門檻規則，在跨越數十年的期間裡，產生的報酬與風險結果彼此相近到，沒有任何一條規則明顯優於其他規則。真正重要的，遠遠不是選了哪一條具體規則，而是投資人到底有沒有持續地執行再平衡，還是任由權重好幾年不受控地漂移。

What rebalancing is not, according to the same evidence, is a return booster. The measured "rebalancing bonus" — the extra annualized return attributable to the act of rebalancing itself, isolated from everything else — comes out at roughly 0.1 to 0.5 percentage points a year in most published studies, and in some periods and asset combinations it is closer to zero or even slightly negative, because selling an asset that then keeps outperforming does have a real opportunity cost. Rebalancing's actual, well-supported job is risk control: it keeps the portfolio's risk profile close to what was originally chosen, rather than letting a strong bull market in one asset quietly turn a 60/40 mix into an 80/20 mix with nobody deciding that on purpose. One rule of thumb that shows up repeatedly in the practitioner literature, associated with researcher Larry Swedroe, is the "5/25" rule: rebalance when an asset class drifts more than 5 absolute percentage points, or more than 25% of its own original weight, whichever triggers first — a simple threshold designed to catch large drifts in both large and small target allocations.

根據同一批證據，再平衡不是什麼呢？它不是一個報酬加速器。多數已發表研究測得的「再平衡紅利」——也就是把再平衡這個動作本身、獨立於其他一切因素之外，額外貢獻的年化報酬——大約落在每年 0.1 到 0.5 個百分點，而在某些期間跟資產組合裡，這個數字更接近零，甚至略為負值，因為賣掉一個之後持續表現更好的資產，確實有真實的機會成本。再平衡真正、而且有充分證據支持的工作，是風險控制：它讓組合的風險特性，維持接近當初選定的樣子，而不是任由某個資產的一波強勢多頭，在沒有人刻意決定的情況下，悄悄把一個 60/40 配置變成 80/20 配置。實務文獻裡反覆出現的一個經驗法則，跟研究者 Larry Swedroe 有關，叫做「5/25」法則：當一個資產類別偏離超過絕對的 5 個百分點，或超過它自己原始權重的 25%，兩個條件哪個先觸發就照哪個——這是一個簡單的門檻，設計來同時抓住大權重與小權重配置裡的大幅漂移。

Takeaway一句話收斂

Rebalancing is a risk-control habit, not a return engine — the specific frequency or threshold barely matters, but doing it consistently at all is what keeps a portfolio's actual risk close to the risk that was originally chosen.再平衡是一種風險控制的習慣，不是報酬引擎——確切選哪種頻率或門檻幾乎不影響結果，但有沒有持續執行，才是讓組合實際承擔的風險，貼近當初選定風險的關鍵。

Withdrawal Timing: Sequence-of-Returns Risk

提領的時機：報酬順序風險

Everything above concerns a portfolio that is only ever adding money. A different problem shows up the moment a portfolio starts having money taken out of it on a regular basis — in retirement, or any long, planned drawdown period — and the evidence here contains a genuine, still-unsettled academic disagreement worth presenting honestly rather than papering over.

以上談的一切，都是一個只有錢流入的組合。當一個組合開始定期被提領資金出來的那一刻起——退休期間，或任何長期、規劃好的提領期間——會出現一個不一樣的問題，而這裡的證據，包含一個真實、至今仍未解決的學界分歧，值得誠實呈現，而不是含糊帶過。

The core mechanism is called sequence-of-returns riskThe finding that two portfolios with identical average returns over a period can end up with wildly different final balances, depending only on the order in which the good and bad years occurred — because withdrawals taken during a down year lock in losses that never get the chance to recover.同一段期間內，兩個平均報酬完全相同的組合，最後的餘額可能天差地遠，差別只在於好年跟壞年出現的先後順序——因為在下跌的年份提領資金，會把虧損鎖死，讓那部分永遠沒有機會回本。: two portfolios can have the exact same average annual return over 30 years and end up with wildly different final balances, purely because of the order the good and bad years arrived in. A retiree who withdraws a fixed amount every year, and who happens to hit two or three bad market years right at the start of retirement, is forced to sell a larger share of a shrinking portfolio to cover the same withdrawal — permanently reducing the base that has to recover later. A retiree with the identical average return but whose bad years land near the end of the 30-year period suffers almost no damage, because most of the money has already been withdrawn by the time the bad years hit. The classic illustration compares a retiree starting withdrawals in 1966 — who ran into roughly a decade and a half of poor, inflation-eroded US market returns almost immediately — against a retiree starting in 1982, at the beginning of one of the strongest multi-decade bull markets in US history. Same withdrawal rule, same average long-run market return over the full multi-decade horizon, dramatically different outcomes, driven entirely by when the bad years landed.

核心的機制，叫做報酬順序風險The finding that two portfolios with identical average returns over a period can end up with wildly different final balances, depending only on the order in which the good and bad years occurred — because withdrawals taken during a down year lock in losses that never get the chance to recover.同一段期間內，兩個平均報酬完全相同的組合，最後的餘額可能天差地遠，差別只在於好年跟壞年出現的先後順序——因為在下跌的年份提領資金，會把虧損鎖死，讓那部分永遠沒有機會回本。：兩個組合，可能在三十年間有完全一樣的平均年報酬，卻因為好年跟壞年出現的先後順序不同，最後的餘額天差地遠。一個每年提領固定金額的退休族，如果剛好在退休一開始就遇上兩三個壞年頭，會被迫從一個正在萎縮的組合裡賣出更大比例的資產，去支應同樣金額的提領——這會永久性地縮小之後要靠它回本的本金基礎。另一個平均報酬完全相同的退休族，如果壞年頭剛好落在這三十年的尾端，幾乎不會受到什麼傷害，因為壞年頭來的時候，大部分的錢早就已經被領走了。經典的示範，是拿一個一九六六年開始提領的退休族——幾乎立刻就撞上將近十五年、被通膨侵蝕的美股疲弱報酬——跟一個一九八二年開始提領的退休族相比，後者剛好站在美國史上數一數二強勁的多年多頭行情起點。同樣的提領規則，同樣拉長到數十年的平均市場報酬，結果卻天差地遠，差別完全來自壞年頭落在什麼時候。

The most widely cited rule of thumb attempting to solve this problem is financial planner William Bengen's "4% rule," published in 1994: withdraw 4% of the starting portfolio value in year one, then adjust that dollar amount for inflation every year after, and — based on the worst historical US sequence in his dataset, including the 1966 start — the portfolio should have lasted at least 30 years. The rule has been enormously influential and is still widely taught. It has also been extensively challenged. Researcher Wade Pfau examined the same style of withdrawal-rate analysis across 17 developed countries' own market histories, not just the US, and found that a 4% starting withdrawal rate would have failed — run the portfolio to zero before 30 years were up — in a majority of those countries, because the US happens to have had an unusually favorable 20th-century market history relative to most of the rest of the world. More recently, a 2023 academic paper by Aizhan Anarkulova, Scott Cederburg, and Michael O'Doherty, using a broader international dataset and a bootstrap-resampling methodology, argued the safe withdrawal rate for a globally diversified stock/bond portfolio may be meaningfully below 4%, while other researchers have pushed back on aspects of that paper's methodology and country sample. This is presented here as a live, unsettled disagreement, not a resolved fact — the honest takeaway for a course like this one is that 4% is a widely used historical rule of thumb built substantially on one country's 20th-century experience, not a mathematical law, and any single point estimate deserves to be treated with real humility.

試圖解決這個問題、最廣為引用的一條經驗法則，是理財規劃師 William Bengen 一九九四年發表的「4% 法則」：第一年提領起始組合價值的 4%，之後每年再依通膨調整這個提領的金額——根據他資料集裡最糟的美國歷史序列（包含一九六六年那組），組合至少能撐過 30 年。這條法則影響力極大，至今仍被廣泛教授。它也被大量挑戰過。研究者 Wade Pfau 用同樣風格的提領率分析，檢視了 17 個已開發國家自己的市場歷史，而不只是美國，結果發現如果一開始就用 4% 的提領率，在這些國家裡的多數，組合會提早破產——不到 30 年就歸零——因為美國剛好在二十世紀，相對世界上多數其他地方，有著一段異常有利的市場歷史。更近期，二〇二三年一篇由 Aizhan Anarkulova、Scott Cederburg 與 Michael O'Doherty 合著的學術論文，用更廣泛的國際資料集跟拔靴重抽樣的方法，主張一個全球分散的股債組合，安全提領率可能明顯低於 4%，而其他研究者則對那篇論文的方法論與國家樣本提出了反駁。這裡把它呈現為一個活著、尚未解決的分歧，而不是一個已經定案的事實——對這門課而言，誠實的結論是：4% 是一條被廣泛使用、但很大程度建立在單一國家二十世紀經驗之上的經驗法則，不是一條數學定律，任何單一的點估計，都值得用真正的謙遜去對待。

Takeaway一句話收斂

Two portfolios with the same average return can have wildly different real-world outcomes depending purely on when the bad years land relative to withdrawals — and the "4% rule" built to manage that risk rests on one country's 20th-century history, with genuine, still-open academic disagreement about whether it generalizes.兩個平均報酬相同的組合，可能因為壞年頭相對提領時機的先後不同，實際結果天差地遠——而用來管理這個風險的「4% 法則」，很大程度建立在單一國家的二十世紀歷史上，學界對它能不能推廣到別處，至今仍有真實、尚未解決的分歧。

Think first: two retirees have the exact same 30-year average annual return. One's portfolio survives comfortably; the other runs out of money in year 22. If the average return is identical, what single variable most likely explains the gap?先想一下：兩個退休族，三十年的平均年報酬完全一樣。一個的組合撐得很輕鬆；另一個在第 22 年就把錢花光了。如果平均報酬完全相同，最可能解釋這個差距的，是哪一個單一變數？

The order in which good and bad years arrived, relative to when withdrawals were being taken — sequence-of-returns risk. A retiree who hit bad years early, while still withdrawing a fixed dollar amount, was forced to sell a larger fraction of a shrinking portfolio, permanently shrinking the base available to compound back. The same bad years landing late, after most withdrawals were already made, would have done far less damage. The average across 30 years hides this completely; only the year-by-year path reveals it.

好年跟壞年出現的順序，相對於提領發生的時間點——也就是報酬順序風險。一個在早期就撞上壞年頭、同時還在提領固定金額的退休族，被迫從一個正在萎縮的組合裡賣出更大比例的資產，永久縮小了之後用來回本複利的基礎。同樣的壞年頭如果晚一點才出現，等大部分提領都已經完成之後，造成的傷害會小得多。三十年的平均值完全把這件事藏起來了；只有逐年的路徑，才會把它揭露出來。

Six Traps in How This Evidence Gets Misused

六個誤用這些證據的常見陷阱

⚠

Treating US market history as if it were world history. Nearly every widely quoted long-run number in finance — the 4% withdrawal rule, the roughly-10%-a-year stock return, the shape of most textbook drawdown charts — is built on US data, because the US has the longest, most complete, most convenient public record. But Pfau's 17-country study above found the same 4% rule failed in most other countries, and Japan's multi-decade round trip shows a fully developed market can behave nothing like the US pattern for an entire generation. Any number sourced from US-only data should be labeled as such, explicitly, every time it is used.

把美國市場歷史，當成全世界的市場歷史。幾乎每一個金融界廣為引用的長期數字——4% 提領法則、大約每年 10% 的股票報酬、多數教科書回撤圖的形狀——都是建立在美國資料上，因為美國擁有最長、最完整、最方便取用的公開紀錄。但上面 Pfau 的 17 國研究發現，同一條 4% 法則在多數其他國家會失敗，而日本橫跨數十年的來回，證明一個完全成熟的市場，整整一個世代都可能長得跟美國模式完全不一樣。任何只從美國資料算出來的數字，每次被使用時，都應該明確標註來源是美國資料。

⚠

Backtesting a long list of allocations and picking whichever one produced the highest historical return. Every backtest is a report on exactly one path history happened to take; a mix that looks best over 1980–2020 was, to some real degree, the mix that happened to be positioned for what actually occurred during those specific 40 years. Running the comparison again over a different 40-year window — 1930–1970, say — routinely produces a different "winner." Picking the best backtested performer and calling it the answer quietly assumes the next 40 years will rhyme with the last 40, which is exactly the assumption this lesson's "backtesting's real job" section (below) argues against.

回測一長串配置，然後挑出歷史報酬最高的那一個。每一份回測，報告的都只是歷史剛好走過的那一條路徑；一個在 1980 到 2020 年間看起來最好的配置，在某種真實的程度上，就是剛好在那特定 40 年裡發生的事情面前，佔到位置的那一個配置。把同樣的比較，換一個不同的 40 年窗口重跑一次——比方說 1930 到 1970 年——通常會跑出一個不一樣的「贏家」。挑選回測表現最好的那個、然後把它當成標準答案，等於默默假設接下來的 40 年會跟過去的 40 年押韻，而這正是這一課下面「回測真正的用法」那一節所反對的假設。

⚠

Reading "smaller historical drawdown" as simply "better." The Permanent Portfolio's roughly -12 to -14% max drawdown looks safer on paper than 100% stocks' roughly -51%, but that smaller number came largely from holding heavy weights in cash and gold across decades that did not happen to punish either one — and both of those assets have their own well-documented multi-decade weak stretches, as this lesson's gold war-story shows directly. A smaller historical drawdown is a fact about which specific decades were sampled, not a law about what any given mix can never do again.

把「歷史回撤比較小」直接讀成「比較好」。永久組合大約 -12 到 -14% 的最大回撤，看在紙面上比 100% 股票大約 -51% 安全，但這個較小的數字，很大程度來自於數十年間重壓現金跟黃金，而剛好那幾十年沒有懲罰到這兩者——而這兩個資產各自都有充分紀錄、長達數十年的弱勢期，這一課的黃金戰爭故事就是直接的證明。歷史回撤比較小，是一個「剛好抽樣到哪幾個十年」的事實，不是一條「這個配置以後絕對不會發生同樣的事」的定律。

⚠

Expecting rebalancing to be a source of extra return. As the rebalancing evidence above shows, the measured "rebalancing bonus" is small — roughly 0.1 to 0.5 percentage points a year in most studies — and can turn negative in strong, sustained trends. Rebalancing's real, well-documented job is keeping the portfolio's actual risk close to the risk originally chosen; treating it as a return-generating strategy in its own right sets an expectation the evidence does not support.

期待再平衡能成為額外報酬的來源。就像上面再平衡證據顯示的，測得的「再平衡紅利」很小——多數研究裡大約每年 0.1 到 0.5 個百分點——而且在強勁、持續的趨勢裡，可能會變成負值。再平衡真正、有充分紀錄的工作，是讓組合實際承擔的風險，貼近當初選定的風險；把它當成一個能自己創造報酬的策略，設下的期待，是證據不支持的。

⚠

Home bias dressed up as a rational choice. Holding 70–90% of an equity portfolio in one's own domestic market is the global norm, not a Taiwan-specific problem — but the case above showed decade-scale leadership rotating unpredictably between the US and the rest of the world, and Taiwan's own market represents roughly 2% of global equity value, concentrated heavily in one industry. "I understand this market best" is a real, human reason to prefer it; it is not evidence that the market will keep outperforming, and the two should not be confused with each other.

把家鄉偏誤，包裝成一個理性的選擇。把股票組合的 70 到 90% 放在本國市場，是全世界的常態，不是台灣特有的問題——但上面的案例顯示，領先的市場，會以十年為單位、在美國與世界其他地方之間，不可預測地輪替，而台灣自己的市場，大約只占全球股票市值的 2%，還高度集中在單一產業。「我最懂這個市場」是一個真實、人性的偏好理由；它不是這個市場會繼續勝出的證據，兩者不該被混為一談。

⚠

Treating Bengen's 4% rule as a proven mathematical constant rather than a historical rule of thumb. It was derived from one country's historical record, tested against the worst US sequence available at the time, and later research — Pfau's cross-country study and the 2023 Anarkulova/Cederburg/O'Doherty paper among them — has directly challenged how far it generalizes. Using 4% as a starting planning assumption is reasonable; treating it as settled science closes off a genuine, still-active debate.

把 Bengen 的 4% 法則，當成一個已被證明的數學常數，而不是一條歷史經驗法則。它是從單一國家的歷史紀錄推導出來的，測試對象是當時可取得、最糟的美國序列，而後續研究——包括上面 Pfau 的跨國研究，以及二〇二三年 Anarkulova／Cederburg／O'Doherty 那篇論文——都直接挑戰了它能推廣到多遠。把 4% 當成一個起始的規劃假設，是合理的；把它當成已經定案的科學，等於關上了一場真實、至今仍在進行的辯論。

What Backtesting Is For Here: Excluding Fragile Mixes, Not Picking a Winner

回測在這裡的用法：排除脆弱的配置，不是挑出一個贏家

Every backtest referenced in this lesson — the six-portfolio table, the 60/40 drawdown history, the rebalancing studies — is a report on exactly one path history happened to take. That is not a reason to ignore backtests; it is a reason to be precise about what job they can and cannot do. A backtest is well suited to screening out configurations that failed to survive some specific, well-documented stress the investor has stated they cannot tolerate — the worked example above used a stated 25% ceiling to eliminate 100% stocks and the base 60/40 mix, which is a legitimate, evidence-grounded use. A backtest is poorly suited to identifying the single best-performing configuration and treating that as a forecast, because the specific ranking of "best" is itself a fact about the specific decades sampled, and there is no guarantee the next several decades resemble the ones that produced that ranking.

這一課引用的每一份回測——六種配置的表格、60/40 的回撤歷史、再平衡研究——報告的都只是歷史剛好走過的那一條路徑。這不是應該忽視回測的理由；而是應該精確界定回測能做跟不能做什麼工作的理由。回測很適合拿來排除那些沒能撐過某個特定、有充分紀錄、投資人已經表明自己無法承受的壓力測試的配置——上面的範例，用一個明訂的 25% 天花板，淘汰了 100% 股票跟基本款 60/40，這是一個合理、有證據支撐的用法。回測不適合拿來找出單一表現最好的配置、然後把它當成一個預測，因為「最好」這個具體排名，本身就是一個關於「剛好抽樣到哪幾個特定十年」的事實，沒有任何保證接下來的幾十年，會長得跟產生出那個排名的那幾十年一樣。

This is the same discipline the next lesson, Backtest Honesty and the Risk Budget, develops in far more depth for strategy backtesting generally — the core idea that a backtest's honest job is disqualifying what has demonstrably failed under specific conditions, not certifying what will win, carries over directly from allocation-level evidence to strategy-level evidence. If a configuration or a strategy has never been tested against a genuine stress case in its own asset class's history, that absence of evidence is itself a finding worth treating with caution, not a blank check to assume it would have survived.

這正是下一課回測的誠實與風險預算，會針對策略回測整體，用更深的篇幅發展的同一套紀律——一份回測誠實的工作，是取消資格：淘汰在特定條件下已經證明會失敗的東西，而不是認證誰會贏，這個核心概念，會直接從配置層級的證據，延續到策略層級的證據。如果一個配置或一個策略，從來沒有在它自己那個資產類別的歷史裡，被真正的壓力情境測試過，這個「缺乏證據」的事實本身，就值得用謹慎對待，而不是一張可以隨意假設它撐得過去的空白支票。
