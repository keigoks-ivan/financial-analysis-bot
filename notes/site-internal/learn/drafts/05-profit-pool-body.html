Who Actually Gets Paid When You Buy a Phone

一支手機的錢，到底進了誰的口袋

A flagship phone retails for roughly $1,000. Multiply that by hundreds of millions of units sold every year, and the smartphone industry is one of the largest consumer goods businesses on earth — hundreds of billions of dollars of revenue flowing through it annually. The natural next question an investor asks is: given all that money moving through the chain, who is actually keeping it?

Independent teardown and cost estimates for a recent high-end iPhone put the physical bill of materials — the chips, the screen, the camera module, the battery, everything that goes into the box — at roughly $550 to $600 on a phone that sells for about $1,000. That already tells you Apple's hardware gross margin is comfortably above 30-40%. But look one layer deeper: who actually assembles that pile of components into a working phone? Mostly Hon Hai (better known globally as Foxconn) and, to a smaller degree, Pegatron — both Taiwanese contract manufacturers whose publicly reported net margins have sat in the low single digits, commonly cited around 2-3%, for years. Two companies touch the exact same physical object. One keeps roughly a third of the sale price. The other keeps two or three cents on the dollar.

That gap is not an accident and it is not explained by "Apple makes a better product." It is the entire subject of this lesson: a value chain generates a pool of total dollar profit, and that pool does not distribute itself evenly, or according to who touches the most steps, or who ships the most units. It concentrates wherever one link in the chain holds real leverage over the others — and almost everywhere else in the chain earns close to nothing for its trouble. (The companies named in this lesson — Apple, Foxconn, Intel, Microsoft, ASML, and others — are used purely as public, verifiable teaching examples of a mechanism; nothing here is a comment on whether any of them is a good investment today.)

一支旗艦手機的建議售價大約落在一千美元上下。乘上每年賣出的數億支，智慧型手機產業因此是地球上規模最大的消費性產品產業之一——每年有數千億美元在整條鏈裡流動。投資人接下來自然會問的問題是：這麼多錢流過這條鏈，到底誰真正把它留下來了？

獨立的拆機成本估算，對一支近年的高階iPhone給出的物料成本（晶片、螢幕、相機模組、電池，所有裝進機殼裡的零件）大約落在五百五十到六百美元，而這支手機賣價約一千美元。光是這樣就能看出蘋果的硬體毛利率舒服地站在30－40%以上。但再往下看一層：這堆零件是誰把它組裝成一支能開機的手機？主要是鴻海（國際上更常被稱為Foxconn）,以及規模較小的和碩，兩家都是台灣的委外代工廠，長年公開財報上的淨利率都落在個位數的低段，常被引用的數字約在2－3%。兩家公司碰的是同一支實體手機，一家留下大約三分之一的售價，另一家只留下每一塊錢裡的兩三分。

這個落差不是意外，也不能只用「蘋果的產品比較好」帶過。這正是這一課要談的全部主題：一條價值鏈會產生一整池的美元利潤，而這一池利潤不會平均分配，也不看誰經手的步驟最多、誰出貨的數量最大。它會集中在鏈裡真正握有籌碼的那個環節，而鏈上幾乎其他所有環節，即使辛苦付出，拿到的利潤都趨近於零。（這一課提到的公司——蘋果、鴻海、英特爾、微軟、艾司摩爾等——全部只是用來說明機制的公開、可查證教學案例；文中沒有一句話是在評論這些公司現在值不值得投資。）

What a Profit Pool Is: Revenue Location Is Not Profit Location

利潤池是什麼：營收在哪，不代表利潤在哪

Give the concept a name so it is easy to hold onto: a profit poolPlain language: the total dollar amount of profit an entire industry chain generates in a period, and how that total is split across the chain's steps — not each step's margin percentage in isolation. is the total dollar amount of operating profit an entire value chain produces over a period, distributed across each step of that chain. It is deliberately a dollar concept, not a margin-percentage concept — a step can post a spectacular margin and still control a tiny sliver of the pool, simply because its revenue base is small. The two things that matter are (1) how big is each step's slice in absolute dollars, and (2) how that slice is trending over the last 3-5 years.

A clean historical illustration is the personal-computer industry from roughly the mid-1990s through the mid-2000s — the era commonly nicknamed "Wintel," for Windows plus Intel. Dozens of PC assemblers (Compaq, IBM, Dell, and a long tail of clone-makers) fought a brutal price war for market share, competing on a product that had become largely interchangeable — a beige box running the same operating system on the same instruction set. Net margins for assemblers were routinely in the low single digits, and several well-known brands were acquired or exited the business entirely over that period. One layer up, Intel — supplying the central processor almost every one of those boxes needed — posted gross margins commonly in the 55-65% range and operating margins often above 30%, year after year. Microsoft, supplying the operating system every one of those boxes ran, posted margins even higher, particularly once its Windows and Office franchises matured. The PC assemblers, taken together, generated far more total industry revenue than Intel and Microsoft combined. But measured in dollars of profit kept, Intel and Microsoft dominated the pool.

How do you actually see this from public filings rather than from memory of a story? Pull each company's segment revenue and segment operating income — most annual reports break this out — for every layer of the chain you can identify, across 3-5 years. Multiply revenue by operating margin to get a dollar profit figure for each layer (not just the margin percentage), then compare those dollar figures to each other, not to each layer's own revenue. A layer whose dollar profit is growing while its revenue share of the industry is flat or shrinking is quietly gaining ground in the pool; the reverse is a warning sign.

Takeaway收斂

The size of a step's revenue tells you almost nothing about how much of the industry's profit it keeps. Always convert margin percentage into a dollar figure and compare dollars, not percentages, across the chain.

一個環節的營收大小，幾乎說明不了它拿走多少產業利潤。永遠把毛利率／營益率換算成美元金額，拿美元跟美元比，而不是拿百分比跟百分比比。

先給這個概念一個名字，方便記住：利潤池白話：整個產業鏈在一段期間內產生的美元利潤總額，以及這個總額如何被分配到鏈上各個環節——不是各環節各自的毛利率百分比。指的是整條價值鏈在一段期間內產生的營業利潤美元總額，並且看這個總額如何被分配到鏈上每一個環節。這是刻意設計成一個「美元」概念，而不是「毛利率」概念——一個環節可以貼出漂亮到誇張的毛利率，卻只掌握整池利潤裡微不足道的一小塊，原因很單純：它的營收基期太小。真正重要的是兩件事：（一）每個環節在美元絕對值上的份額有多大；（二）這個份額在過去三到五年的走勢。

一個乾淨的歷史範例是個人電腦產業，大約從1990年代中期到2000年代中期——這段時期常被暱稱為「Wintel」,意指微軟的Windows加上英特爾。數十家電腦組裝廠（康柏、IBM、戴爾，以及一長串的兼容機廠商）為了市占率打得頭破血流，賣的是一款已經高度同質化的產品——同一套作業系統、同一套指令集，裝在不同外殼裡的機箱。組裝廠的淨利率經常落在個位數的低段，那段時期有好幾個知名品牌被併購或直接退出這門生意。往上一層，替幾乎每一台機箱供應中央處理器的英特爾，毛利率常年落在55－65%的區間，營益率經常站上30%以上。替幾乎每一台機箱供應作業系統的微軟，毛利率甚至更高，尤其是在Windows和Office兩大金雞母成熟之後。把所有電腦組裝廠加總起來看，整個產業的總營收遠遠超過英特爾加微軟的總和。但如果用留下的美元利潤來衡量，英特爾和微軟才是那一池利潤真正的主人。

要怎麼不靠背故事、而是真的從公開財報看出這件事？把鏈上每一個你能辨識出來的環節，拉出過去三到五年各自的分部營收與分部營業利益——多數年報都會拆出這個層級的數字。用營收乘以營益率算出每一層的美元利潤（不是只看營益率百分比），再拿這些美元金額互相比較，而不是拿每一層自己的營收去比。如果某一層的美元利潤在成長，同時它占整個產業營收的份額卻持平甚至下滑，代表它正悄悄在利潤池裡擴大地盤；反過來則是一個警訊。

Think first: why might a step with a sky-high margin still hold almost none of the profit pool?先想一下：為什麼一個毛利率高得誇張的環節，卻可能在利潤池裡幾乎分不到東西？

Because the pool is measured in dollars, and dollars are margin multiplied by revenue. A tiny, high-margin specialty supplier selling $200 million a year at an 80% margin produces $160 million of profit. A commoditized giant selling $50 billion a year at a 4% margin produces $2 billion of profit — twelve times as much, from a margin that looks worse on every printed page. Margin percentage tells you how efficiently a step converts its own revenue into profit; it tells you nothing about how big that step's slice of the whole industry's dollar profit actually is. You need both numbers, and the second one (revenue scale) is the one people forget to check.

因為利潤池是用美元衡量的，而美元等於營益率乘以營收。一家迷你、高毛利的特殊零件供應商，一年賣兩億美元、營益率80%，賺到一億六千萬美元的利潤。一家高度同質化的巨頭，一年賣五百億美元、營益率只有4%，卻賺到二十億美元——是前者的十二倍，儘管它的營益率在任何報表上看起來都難看得多。毛利率／營益率百分比告訴你這個環節把自己的營收轉成利潤的效率有多高；它完全沒告訴你這個環節在整個產業美元利潤總量裡占的份額有多大。這兩個數字都要看，而人們最常漏看的正是第二個——規模。

Why Profit Sticks Where It Sticks: Buyer Power, a Substitution Ceiling, a Barrier to Entry

利潤為什麼留在某個環節：買方力量、替代天花板、進入門檻

You can name three plain mechanisms that decide where a chain's profit concentrates, without ever reaching for a textbook's five-letter acronym. First: when a step's customers are few and large, those customers hold the whip hand and squeeze price down toward cost — this is buyer concentrationPlain language: when a supplier sells mostly to a handful of large customers, those customers can credibly threaten to walk, so they can push price down close to the supplier's cost. at work, and you see it wherever a supplier's top three or five customers account for the majority of its revenue — a classic case is an auto-parts maker selling almost entirely to the five largest automakers, who can and do rotate business between competing suppliers every model cycle to keep price pressure constant. Second: if a decent alternative exists nearby, price cannot rise past what that alternative costs — call this a substitution ceilingPlain language: a cheaper or good-enough alternative sitting right next to your product puts a hard lid on how high you can price, no matter how good your product is., and the cleanest textbook case is a branded drug the moment its patent expires: a chemically identical generic appears at a fraction of the price, and the branded product's pricing power evaporates within months, not years. Third: if it takes years and enormous capital for a new competitor to replicate a step's capability, that step keeps its pricing power for a long time — this is a barrier to entryPlain language: how long and how much money it would take a brand-new competitor to copy what you do. The longer and more expensive, the longer your pricing power survives., and the single most extreme real-world example in the world right now sits in semiconductor manufacturing equipment.

One company supplies essentially all of the world's extreme ultraviolet (EUV) lithography machines — the tool required to print the smallest, most advanced transistor patterns onto a silicon wafer. Each machine has been reported to cost on the order of $150-200 million, order backlogs commonly run multiple years, and no rival has managed to bring a competing EUV tool to market despite roughly two decades of the technology being publicly known to exist. That is a barrier to entry so wide that it functions as a genuine capability monopoly: the equipment layer of the chip industry is a small fraction of the whole industry's total revenue, yet it captures a share of the whole chain's profit pool wildly out of proportion to its revenue size, purely because nobody else can build the machine. The same bottleneck logic — a handful of companies, or in some steps effectively one, controlling capacity everyone downstream needs — has repeatedly shown up in the 2023-2025 AI hardware buildout around advanced chip packaging capacity and high-bandwidth memory: relatively small revenue lines commanding outsized negotiating leverage over much larger customers, simply because there was nowhere else downstream buyers could go.

Takeaway收斂

None of these three mechanisms require jargon to explain. If you can answer "who are the customers, is there a cheap alternative next door, and how hard is this to copy" for a step in a chain, you already know most of what determines whether it keeps the profit or gives it away.

這三個機制完全不需要靠術語解釋。只要你能回答「客戶是誰、旁邊有沒有便宜的替代方案、這個能力有多難複製」這三個問題，你就已經掌握了決定一個環節能不能守住利潤的絕大部分答案。

決定一條鏈的利潤會集中在哪裡，可以用三個白話機制講清楚，完全不需要動用教科書裡那套五個字母的縮寫。第一：當一個環節的客戶數量少、規模又大，客戶就握有主導權，能把價格往成本邊緣壓——這就是買方集中白話：如果供應商的營收主要來自少數幾個大客戶，客戶就有本錢威脅轉單，能把價格壓到接近成本。在起作用，凡是供應商前三到五大客戶占營收比重過半的地方都看得到——一個經典案例是零組件廠幾乎只賣給前五大車廠，這些車廠每一次改款週期都能、也真的會把訂單在幾家供應商之間輪流轉，讓價格壓力持續存在。第二：如果旁邊有一個堪用的替代方案，價格就漲不過那個替代方案的成本——這叫替代天花板白話：旁邊有一個更便宜、或堪用的替代品，會給你的訂價空間釘上一個硬天花板，不管你的產品本身有多好。，教科書等級的乾淨案例就是原廠藥專利到期的那一刻：一個化學成分完全相同的學名藥立刻出現，價格只要原本的一小部分，原廠的訂價權在幾個月內、而不是幾年內就會蒸發。第三：如果新對手需要花好幾年、投入巨額資本才能複製這個環節的能力，這個環節的訂價權就能維持很久——這叫進入門檻白話：一個全新競爭者要花多久、多少錢才能複製你在做的事。時間越長、成本越高，你的訂價權能撐得越久。，而現在全世界最極端的真實案例就在半導體製造設備裡。

全世界幾乎所有極紫外光（EUV）微影設備都由單一一家公司供應——這是把最先進、最微小的電晶體圖形印上矽晶圓所需要的設備。據報導每台機器造價落在一億五千萬到兩億美元上下的量級，訂單排期常常長達數年，而儘管這項技術公開存在已經約二十年，至今沒有任何一家對手成功把可競爭的EUV設備推上市。這就是一道寬到足以形成真正能力壟斷的進入門檻：晶片產業裡設備這一層的營收只占整個產業的一小部分，卻拿走整條鏈利潤池裡與其營收規模完全不成比例的一大塊，純粹因為沒有第二家能造出這台機器。同樣的瓶頸邏輯——只有少數幾家，甚至在某些環節實質上只有一家，掌控著下游所有人都需要的產能——在2023到2025年的AI硬體建設潮裡，圍繞著先進封裝產能與高頻寬記憶體反覆出現：營收規模相對不大的環節，卻對規模大得多的下游客戶握有不成比例的議價籌碼，原因很單純，下游買家沒有其他地方可以轉單。

▲

When you see a headline calling an industry a "tight oligopoly" and assume that alone protects margin, what is usually actually true is that concentration only sets the stage — check separately whether the product itself is commoditized. A commoditized oligopoly (identical specs, easy buyer switching, cyclical demand) still crashes on price in every downturn; a differentiated oligopoly (real switching costs, real spec differences) is the one that actually holds pricing power.

當你看到「這是一個緊密寡占產業」的標題就直接假設利潤有保障，通常真正的情況是集中度只搭好了舞台，還要另外檢查產品本身有沒有商品化。一個商品化的寡占（規格相同、買方轉單容易、需求循環性強）在每一輪景氣下行照樣會殺價見骨；真正握有訂價權的，是那種有真實轉換成本、規格真的有差異的寡占。

Think first: why does a supplier's leverage often erode before a concentrated customer has actually cut a single order?先想一下：為什麼供應商的籌碼往往在集中的客戶還沒真的砍掉一張訂單之前，就已經先弱化了？

Because the negotiation is forward-looking, not backward-looking. A customer that represents 40% of a supplier's revenue does not need to actually place a smaller order to extract a lower price — it only needs to credibly signal that it is qualifying a second source, or piloting an in-house alternative. The mere existence of a credible alternative changes every subsequent price negotiation, well before a single dollar of revenue moves. This is why watching only historical revenue and margin trends can lag reality by a year or more; the leading indicator is qualification announcements, pilot-line news, and capital-expenditure disclosures from the customer side, not the supplier's own reported numbers.

因為談判本質上是向前看的，不是向後看的。一個占供應商營收40%的客戶，不需要真的先下一張較小的訂單才能壓價——它只需要可信地釋出訊號，說自己正在認證第二供應商，或正在試產自製替代方案。光是「有一個可信的替代方案存在」這件事，就會改變接下來每一次的價格談判，遠早於任何一塊錢的營收真正移動。這就是為什麼只盯著歷史營收和毛利趨勢，反應往往會慢現實一年以上；真正的領先指標是客戶端的認證公告、試產消息、資本支出揭露，不是供應商自己報出來的數字。

Profit Pool Versus Return on Capital: Do Not Stop at Revenue or Margin

利潤池 vs 資本報酬：別只看營收或毛利率就下結論

Dollar profit share tells you where an industry's absolute economic weight sits. It does not tell you how efficiently that weight was produced. A step can command a huge slice of the pool and still be a mediocre business, if it needed an enormous pile of invested capitalPlain language: the money tied up in a business to run it — factories, equipment, inventory, and receivables, net of the free financing it gets from suppliers. Profit divided by this number tells you how hard the capital is working. to get there. This is the same idea a course on reading financial statements introduces as return on invested capital, decomposed into margin times how fast that capital turns over — a chain-level profit pool map is simply that same lens applied across an entire industry's steps instead of to one company alone. A step earning a smaller dollar slice of the pool, but earning it on almost no capital, can be a far better business than a step earning a bigger dollar slice on a mountain of capital.

Consider a simplified but realistic four-step chain in high-end computing hardware: a fabless chip designer, a contract chip manufacturer (a foundry), a module and board assembler, and a system integrator that sells finished machines under its own brand. Takeaway收斂

Rank the same four steps by revenue, by dollar profit, and by profit-per-capital, and you get three different orderings. All three are true at once — they just answer different questions.

把同樣四個環節分別用營收、美元利潤、每一塊資本賺回的利潤來排序，會得到三種不同的名次。三種排序同時成立——它們只是在回答不同的問題。

美元利潤份額告訴你一個產業的絕對經濟重量落在哪裡，但沒有告訴你這個重量是靠多少效率換來的。一個環節可以拿走利潤池裡一大塊，但如果它是靠堆進去一大座投入資本白話：一門生意為了運轉而綁住的資金——廠房、設備、存貨、應收帳款，扣掉供應商給的免費融資。利潤除以這個數字，能看出資本被用得有多賣力。才換來的，這個環節仍然可能只是一門平庸的生意。這正是財報課裡介紹「投入資本報酬率」——拆成營益率乘以資本周轉速度——的同一套邏輯；一張鏈級的利潤池地圖，只是把同一副眼鏡套用到整個產業的每一個環節，而不是只套用在單一一家公司身上。一個環節即使只拿到利潤池裡較小的美元份額，但如果幾乎不需要什麼資本就能賺到，很可能是一門比另一個拿到較大美元份額、卻堆了一座資本大山的環節，體質好上許多的生意。

來看一條簡化但貼近真實的高階運算硬體四環節鏈：一家無晶圓廠的晶片設計商、一家晶圓代工廠、一家模組／板卡組裝商、以及一家以自有品牌販售整機的系統整合商。下表列出示意的營收、營益率、換算出來的美元營業利益，以及每個環節的投入資本估計值——如果你真的要做這件事，這正是你會從每家公司自己的分部揭露和資產負債表裡拉出來的那種資料。

Worked Example: Mapping a Profit Pool Across Four Chain Steps逐步範例：畫出四環節鏈的利潤池分布
  1. 1

    Start from the table above: each step's revenue and operating margin, pulled from its own segment disclosure.

    從上面的表格出發：每個環節各自的營收與營益率，來自它自己的分部揭露。

  2. 2

    Convert margin into dollars for each step: operating profit = revenue x operating margin. Designer: 20x40%=$8B. Foundry: 70x40%=$28B. Assembler: 40x5%=$2B. Integrator: 150x6%=$9B.

    把每個環節的營益率換算成美元：營業利益＝營收×營益率。設計商：20×40%＝80億美元。代工廠：70×40%＝280億美元。組裝商：40×5%＝20億美元。整合商：150×6%＝90億美元。

  3. 3

    Sum the four dollar figures to get the total chain profit pool: 8 + 28 + 2 + 9 = $47B.

    把四個美元數字加總，得到整條鏈的利潤池總額：8 + 28 + 2 + 9 ＝ 470億美元。

  4. 4

    Compute each step's share of the pool: step profit / total pool. Designer 8/47≈17%. Foundry 28/47≈60%. Assembler 2/47≈4%. Integrator 9/47≈19%.

    算出每個環節在整池利潤裡的占比：環節利潤 ÷ 利潤池總額。設計商8/47≈17%。代工廠28/47≈60%。組裝商2/47≈4%。整合商9/47≈19%。

  5. 5

    Now divide each step's dollar profit by its invested capital to see how hard that capital is working: profit / invested capital. Designer 8/15≈53%. Foundry 28/140=20%. Assembler 2/8=25%. Integrator 9/20=45%.

    再把每個環節的美元利潤除以投入資本，看資本被用得有多賣力：利潤 ÷ 投入資本。設計商8/15≈53%。代工廠28/140＝20%。組裝商2/8＝25%。整合商9/20＝45%。

  6. 6

    Line up three separate rankings. By revenue: integrator > foundry > assembler > designer. By dollar profit: foundry > integrator > designer > assembler. By profit-per-capital: designer > integrator > assembler > foundry. All three are different.

    把三種排序並排比較。按營收：整合商＞代工廠＞組裝商＞設計商。按美元利潤：代工廠＞整合商＞設計商＞組裝商。按資本效率：設計商＞整合商＞組裝商＞代工廠。三種排序完全不同。

  7. 7

    Read the whole picture together: the foundry holds the biggest absolute claim on the pool (~60%) but only middling capital efficiency, because leading-edge fabrication is punishingly capital-intensive. The designer holds a smaller dollar claim but the best capital efficiency by far — a genuinely enviable position. The integrator dominates revenue but keeps under a fifth of the pool. The assembler is the vulnerable step on every dimension at once: thin dollar profit, weak capital efficiency, and — matching the opening case — the position most exposed to being squeezed the hardest when volumes soften.

    把整幅圖一起看：代工廠握有整個利潤池裡最大的絕對份額（約60%），但資本效率只是中段班，因為最先進的製程本來就是資本極度密集的生意。設計商拿到的美元份額較小，卻是資本效率最好的一個——確實是令人羨慕的位置。整合商營收稱霸，卻只留下不到五分之一的利潤池。組裝商則是每個面向同時都最脆弱的一個環節：美元利潤薄、資本效率差，而且——呼應開場案例——是需求一旦轉弱時最容易被壓榨的位置。

When you see a company touted purely on its headline gross margin percentage, what is usually actually true is that margin percentage and profit-pool share are two different questions being quietly conflated. Always ask the follow-up: on how much revenue, and how does that dollar figure compare to the rest of the chain?

當你看到一家公司只靠毛利率百分比的頭條數字被捧上天，通常真正的情況是毛利率百分比和利潤池份額是兩個不同的問題，卻被悄悄混為一談。永遠要多問一句：這個毛利率是建立在多少營收上，換成美元之後跟整條鏈的其他環節比起來又是什麼名次？

Purity: Do the Division Yourself Before You Believe the Story

純度：先自己算出來，再相信那個故事

A market narrative loves to label a stock a "pure play" on whatever theme is hot — a pure AI-infrastructure name, a pure electrification name, a pure whatever-is-in-the-headlines name. The number that actually answers the question is purityPlain language: the share of a company's total revenue that actually comes from the specific industry theme you are researching, worked out from segment disclosures rather than assumed from a company's marketing pitch.: the share of a company's total revenue that is actually attributable to the theme in question, worked out as a simple division — segment revenue tied to the theme, divided by total company revenue. It sounds almost too basic to be worth stating, and that is exactly why it gets skipped. A company has every incentive to let a hot narrative attach itself as broadly as possible; nobody at the company is required to volunteer the denominator.

Here is a realistic version of how the mistake happens. An equipment maker is widely described in market commentary as a pure play on advanced semiconductor back-end packaging — a genuinely hot theme. An investor who takes that framing at face value and buys the stock as a concentrated bet on packaging demand has, without checking, implicitly assumed something close to 100% exposure. Pull the company's own segment revenue breakdown from its actual filings, though, and a very different picture can emerge: for some real equipment makers in this space, a substantial share of revenue — commonly close to half in cases that have been examined this way — comes from a broader surface-mount technology (SMT) business serving general electronics assembly, not from semiconductor back-end packaging at all. The company was not lying; nobody at the company published a false number. The narrative simply never forced the denominator into the open, and the investor's actual thematic exposure was roughly half of what the story implied.

Takeaway收斂

"Pure play" is a marketing phrase, not a financial statement line. Purity is a number you compute, not a label you accept.

「純概念股」是行銷用語，不是財報上的一行數字。純度是你自己算出來的數字，不是你照單全收的標籤。

市場敘事很喜歡把一檔股票貼上「XX純概念股」的標籤——不管當下最熱的題材是AI基礎建設、電動化，還是任何當下佔據頭條的東西。真正能回答這個問題的數字是純度白話：公司總營收裡，真正來自你正在研究的產業題材的比例，要從分部揭露算出來，而不是照公司的行銷說法照單全收。：這家公司的總營收裡，真正能歸因於這個題材的比例，用一個簡單的除法算出來——與題材相關的分部營收，除以公司總營收。這聽起來基本到近乎不值得特別講，而正因為如此，它才最常被跳過。公司有十足的動機讓熱門敘事盡可能廣泛地貼在自己身上；沒有任何一家公司有義務主動把分母講清楚。

以下是這個錯誤常見的發生方式。某家設備商在市場評論裡被廣泛描述成先進半導體後段封裝的純概念股——這確實是個熱門題材。一個直接照單全收這套敘事、把這檔股票當成封裝需求集中賭注買進的投資人，在沒有查證的情況下，等於默默假設了接近百分之百的曝險。但如果真的把這家公司自己申報的分部營收拆解拉出來看，往往會看到完全不同的畫面：在已經被這樣檢視過的一些真實設備商案例裡，相當大一塊——常常接近一半——的營收其實來自服務一般電子組裝的表面黏著技術（SMT）業務，根本不是半導體後段封裝。公司沒有說謊；也沒有任何人發布一個假數字。只是敘事從來沒有逼著分母現形，而投資人真正對這個題材的曝險，大約只有故事所暗示的一半。

◆Real case真實案例

This pattern has repeated closely enough across real semiconductor equipment names that it produced a standing lesson in professional coverage of the space: before ever quoting a "purity" percentage for a company's exposure to a specific chip-industry theme, an analyst is now expected to show the actual one-line derivation — segment revenue tied to the theme, divided by total revenue — rather than accepting a plausible-sounding round number. The reason the rule exists at all is that an early published estimate for one well-known equipment maker's semiconductor-packaging purity turned out to be inflated: the true figure, once the segment math was actually shown, was closer to half of what had been assumed, because a large share of the company's real business sits in a broader surface-mount equipment category that gets grouped alongside packaging in casual descriptions but is not the same end market.

The fix was not "try to remember to check next time." It was structural: no purity number gets published or relied on anymore without the division shown alongside it. The lesson generalizes cleanly beyond one industry — any time a percentage headline number cannot be reconstructed from its own stated numerator and denominator, treat it as unverified until you rebuild it yourself.

這個模式在真實的半導體設備公司之間反覆出現到一定程度，以至於在這個領域的專業覆蓋裡形成了一條常設規則：現在任何人要引用某家公司對特定晶片產業題材的「純度」百分比之前，都被要求先秀出實際的一行推導——與題材相關的分部營收，除以總營收——而不是照單全收一個聽起來合理的整數。這條規則之所以存在，是因為某家知名設備商早期發布的一份半導體封裝純度估計值後來被證實高估了：一旦真的把分部算式攤開，真實數字比原本假設的低了將近一半，因為這家公司相當大一塊真實業務其實屬於更廣義的表面黏著設備類別，在隨口描述裡常被和封裝混為一談，實際上卻不是同一個終端市場。

解法不是「下次記得檢查一下」，而是結構性的：從此以後，任何純度數字如果沒有把推導過程一起附上，就不再被發布或引用。這個教訓能乾淨地推廣到任何一個產業之外：只要一個百分比頭條數字沒辦法從自己聲稱的分子和分母重建出來，就先當它未經查證，直到你自己動手把它重建一次為止。

▲

When you see a company or an earnings-call slide state its own "exposure percentage" to a hot theme, what is usually actually true is that the number has an incentive to be as generous as possible, and you have no way to know whether it is until you rebuild the derivation from segment revenue yourself. Companies do not have to lie to mislead — a fuzzy or broad definition of the theme does the work for them.

當你看到一家公司或法說簡報自己講出對某個熱門題材的「曝險百分比」，通常真正的情況是這個數字有動機盡量講得寬鬆，而在你自己從分部營收重建推導過程之前，你根本無從得知它有多寬鬆。公司不需要說謊就能誤導——一個模糊或寬泛的題材定義就足以達到同樣的效果。

Profit Pools Migrate: Three Signals, Two Reminders

利潤池會遷移：三個訊號，兩個提醒

A value chain is never a fixed photograph. Watch for three signals that a pool is starting to move. First, vertical integrationPlain language: a company starting to make in-house what it used to buy from a supplier, or starting to sell directly to a customer it used to sell through a middleman for — either way, absorbing a neighboring step's margin into its own. from the customer side — a large buyer starting to design or build in-house what it used to purchase, a credible signal well before volumes actually shift, as the earlier think-first note on buyer leverage already covers. Second, vertical integration from the supplier side — a supplier reaching downstream to sell directly to end customers, absorbing a middleman's margin along with its own. Third, standardization: a once-differentiated component becomes interchangeable across vendors as the technology matures and more competitors learn to replicate it, and the barrier to entry that protected the pool erodes — this is the same mechanism from the earlier section on why profit sticks, running in reverse.

The most concrete, fully completed example of the first signal in recent memory is Apple's transition of its Mac computer line away from Intel processors to its own in-house-designed chips, announced in 2020 and substantially completed within roughly two years. Apple had been a large, multi-billion-dollar-a-year customer for Intel's processors. After the switch, analyst estimates commonly cited a meaningful improvement in Mac gross margin — frequently described in the mid-to-high single-digit percentage-point range — while Intel lost a customer relationship of real scale from its personal-computer chip business. Nothing about this was a surprise announcement executed overnight: the credible threat and multi-year transition plan were public well before the last unit shipped, exactly the anticipation dynamic described earlier.

Two reminders belong here before moving on. First, a boundary: this whole framework works best when there is genuinely a multi-party chain to map. It has little to say about a single-location, single-product local service business with no real upstream/downstream structure, and it maps poorly onto regulated utilities or financial institutions, where profit is shaped mostly by an allowed-return regulatory formula or balance-sheet mechanics rather than competitive bargaining along a chain — a different lens, covered later in this course, fits those businesses better. Second, a pricing reminder: correctly identifying who holds today's profit pool answers "is this business worth paying attention to," not "should I buy the stock today." The market can usually see who is currently winning too, and a name correctly identified as holding the pool is very often already priced with that fact assumed. The two separate follow-up questions — can this position actually be defended, and has the price already assumed too much continuation of it — are exactly what the next several lessons in this course are built to answer.

Takeaway收斂

Finding today's profit pool is the beginning of the analysis, not the end of it. It tells you where to look next, not what to do next.

找到今天的利潤池，是分析的起點，不是終點。它告訴你接下來該看哪裡，不是接下來該做什麼。

價值鏈從來不是一張固定不動的照片。留意三個利潤池開始移動的訊號。第一，來自客戶端的垂直整合白話：一家公司開始自己生產原本向供應商採購的東西，或開始跳過中間商直接賣給原本透過中間商觸及的客戶——兩種方向都是把鄰近環節的利潤吸收進自己身上。——大客戶開始自行設計或自行生產原本向外採購的東西，這個訊號的可信度早在訂單量真正轉移之前就已經成立，呼應前面談買方籌碼的那段先想一下。第二，來自供應端的垂直整合——供應商往下游伸手，直接賣給終端客戶，把中間商的利潤連同自己的一起吸收進來。第三，標準化：一個曾經有差異化的組件，隨著技術成熟、更多競爭者學會複製它，逐漸變得可以在不同供應商之間互換，原本保護這個利潤池的進入門檻因此被侵蝕——這正是前面談利潤為什麼留在某個環節那一段機制的反向運作。

近年記憶裡最具體、也已經完整走完全程的第一種訊號範例，是蘋果把Mac電腦產品線從英特爾處理器轉換成自行設計的自研晶片——2020年宣布，大約兩年內基本完成轉換。蘋果原本是英特爾處理器業務裡一個規模達數十億美元等級的大客戶。轉換完成後，分析師估計常引用Mac毛利率出現有意義的改善——經常被形容落在百分點的中高個位數區間——而英特爾則從個人電腦晶片業務裡失去一段真正有規模的客戶關係。這整件事完全不是一夕之間突襲宣布：這個可信的威脅與長達數年的轉換計畫，早在最後一台機器出貨之前就已經公開，正是前面提到的預期效應在真實世界上演。

在往下走之前，這裡有兩個提醒。第一，是邊界：整套框架在真的存在多方參與的鏈可以畫的時候效果最好。對於一個沒有真正上下游結構的單一據點、單一產品在地服務業，這套框架幾乎沒什麼話可說；套用在受管制的公用事業或金融機構身上也常常不合身，因為它們的利潤主要是由允許報酬率的監管公式或資產負債表機制決定，而不是靠鏈上的競爭議價——這門課後面會介紹更適合這類生意的另一副眼鏡。第二，是定價提醒：正確找出今天誰握有利潤池，回答的是「這門生意值不值得留意」，不是「現在該不該買這檔股票」。市場通常也看得出來現在誰在贏，一個被正確判定握有利潤池的名字，往往股價已經把這個事實算了進去。接下來兩個各自獨立的問題——這個位置守不守得住、以及現在的價格有沒有已經算進太多它會持續下去的樂觀——正是這門課接下來好幾課要回答的問題。

◆Real case真實案例

Back in the PC industry example from earlier in this lesson: the "beige box" assemblers were not doomed by fate — commoditization is a process, not a permanent state, and it can in principle reverse if a step manages to re-differentiate itself. But for the better part of two decades, no PC assembler found a way to break the pattern set by Intel's processor and Microsoft's operating system, both of which retained real switching costs (an entire software ecosystem built on one instruction set, and years of user habit and file compatibility built on one operating system) long after the boxes themselves became interchangeable. The lesson for a chain-mapping exercise: check not just who holds the pool today, but which step's advantage rests on genuine switching cost or capability scarcity versus which step's advantage is just a temporary lead that competitors are actively racing to close.

回到這一課稍早提過的個人電腦產業範例：那些「白色機箱」組裝廠並不是命中注定要輸——商品化是一個過程，不是永久狀態，如果某個環節真的想辦法重新做出差異化，原則上是可以逆轉的。但將近二十年的時間裡，沒有一家電腦組裝廠找到方法打破英特爾處理器和微軟作業系統設下的格局，這兩者即使在機箱本身早已變得可以互換之後，仍然長期保有真正的轉換成本（建立在單一指令集上的整個軟體生態，以及建立在單一作業系統上的多年使用習慣與檔案相容性）。這對做鏈級分析的啟示是：不只要看今天誰握有利潤池，還要看這個環節的優勢，究竟建立在真正的轉換成本或能力稀缺上，還是只是一個競爭對手正在拚命追趕的暫時領先。

Think first: why can a stock's valuation compress before a customer's in-house replacement has shipped a single unit?先想一下：為什麼客戶自製的替代方案連一顆產品都還沒出貨，供應商的股價評價就可能已經先被壓縮？

Because a stock's valuation is a bet on a future stream of profit, not just today's numbers, and the market updates that bet the moment the probability of the future stream shrinks — regardless of whether current-quarter revenue has moved at all. A credible pilot-line announcement or a design-win loss to an in-house team lowers the probability-weighted future profit pool the supplier is expected to hold, and a rational market re-rates the multiple immediately, well before any invoice changes. This is exactly why the earlier signals — qualification announcements, capital-expenditure disclosures, credible pilot-production news — are worth tracking as leading indicators, not lagging ones.

因為股價評價賭的是一段未來的利潤流，不只是今天的數字，而市場會在這段未來利潤流的機率一縮水的當下就更新這個賭注——不管本季營收有沒有真的動一分一毫。一則可信的試產線公告，或是一場設計標案輸給客戶自建團隊，都會降低供應商未來能握有那段利潤池的機率加權預期，一個理性的市場會立刻重新評價本益比，遠早於任何一張發票真正改變。這正是為什麼前面提到的那些訊號——認證公告、資本支出揭露、可信的試產消息——值得當成領先指標追蹤，而不是落後指標。

How This Connects

這一課接到哪裡

Mapping the profit pool depends on the demand and supply pictures from the previous two lessons — you cannot judge whether a bottleneck is real without knowing the demand behind it (Lesson 03) and how disciplined or fragmented the supply side is (Lesson 04). Everything this lesson produces feeds forward in three directions: whether today's pool position can survive rising capital flooding in (Lesson 06), whether it can be attacked from outside the chain entirely (Lesson 07), and whether it is durable enough to call a genuine moat (Lesson 09). Once you can name where the pool sits and why, the very last question — has the price already assumed all of this — is the subject of a lesson much later in the course (Lesson 18).

畫出利潤池，要靠前兩課建立的需求與供給圖像——如果不知道瓶頸背後的需求撐不撐得住（第03課），也不知道供給端是紀律嚴明還是一盤散沙（第04課），就沒辦法判斷一個瓶頸是真是假。這一課產出的判斷會往三個方向繼續往前接：今天的利潤池位置能不能撐過資本蜂擁而入（第06課）、會不會被鏈外的東西整個攻破（第07課）、以及它夠不夠持久，能不能真的稱得上一條護城河（第09課）。等你能講清楚利潤池在哪、為什麼在那裡，最後一個問題——價格有沒有已經把這一切都算進去了——是這門課後面很久才會談到的一課（第18課）。

Finding today's profit pool answers where the money currently sits — it says nothing about whether that position can survive the next few years of capital chasing the same returns. That question, and why "shortage plus a flood of new capital pouring in" is quietly one of the most dangerous setups in investing, is where the next lesson begins.

找到今天的利潤池，回答的是錢現在留在哪裡——它完全沒有回答這個位置能不能撐過接下來幾年，資本蜂擁而入追逐同樣報酬的過程。這個問題，以及為什麼「短缺加上熱錢大量湧入」悄悄是投資裡最危險的組合之一，正是下一課要從頭談起的地方。
