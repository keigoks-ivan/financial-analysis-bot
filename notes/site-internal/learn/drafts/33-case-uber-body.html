June 2022: A Company That Loses Billions and a Stock That Has Already Been Cut in Half

2022 年 6 月：一家虧損數十億美元、股價卻已經腰斬的公司

It is June 2022. Uber Technologies shares are trading somewhere between roughly $20 and $26 over the course of the month, putting the company's market value at somewhere around $40–50 billion — against an initial public offering price of $45 a share back in May 2019. A share bought at the IPO is worth roughly half of that today. Three weeks earlier the Federal Reserve raised interest rates by 0.75 percentage points in a single move, its largest increase since 1994, on top of a 0.50-point hike in May and a 0.25-point hike in March — three consecutive, accelerating increases that had pushed the target rate from near zero to 1.50–1.75% in about three months. Every company whose valuation depends on cash flows far in the future is being repriced, hard, and unprofitable growth stocks are being repriced hardest of all: the home-fitness company Peloton, to pick one visible casualty, has fallen from a peak valuation of roughly $50 billion in January 2021 to around $8 billion by April 2022, a decline of roughly 84% in fifteen months. (Every company named in this lesson is a teaching case, used to make a mechanism visible — none of it is a view on how that company looks today.)

現在是 2022 年 6 月。Uber 的股價這個月大約在 20 到 26 美元一帶波動，市值大約落在 400 到 500 億美元之間——對照 2019 年 5 月掛牌時每股 45 美元的發行價，等於當時用發行價買進的股票，現在只剩不到一半的價值。三週前，聯準會才把利率一口氣升了 0.75 個百分點，是 1994 年以來最大單次升息，加上五月才升的 0.50 個百分點、三月的 0.25 個百分點——三次連續且愈升愈快的升息，把利率目標區間在大約三個月內，從接近零推到 1.50% 到 1.75%。任何估值仰賴遙遠未來現金流的公司，都在被重新定價，而未獲利的成長股被砍得最重：舉一個明顯的例子，居家健身公司 Peloton，市值已經從 2021 年 1 月大約 500 億美元的高點，一路跌到 2022 年 4 月大約 80 億美元，十五個月內跌掉約 84%。（這一課提到的每一家公司都是教學案例，用來把某個機制講清楚，不是對那家公司現在的看法。）

Uber's own numbers, released on May 4, 2022 for the first quarter, do nothing to calm the mood at first glance. Revenue came in at $6.85 billion, up sharply from a year earlier — but the company reported a GAAP net loss of $5.93 billion for the quarter alone, one of the largest quarterly losses in its history. Read only as a headline, that number looks like confirmation that the growth-stock carnage is deserved. Read one layer deeper, in the numbers the company actually disclosed alongside it, the loss is overwhelmingly a non-cash, non-operating item: a mark-to-market write-down on Uber's minority equity stakes in other companies — among them the self-driving startup Aurora (which had taken over Uber's own self-driving unit in a December 2020 deal), and stakes in Didi and Grab left over from Uber selling its China and Southeast Asia ride-hailing operations to local rivals years earlier. None of that write-down is cash leaving the building, and none of it says anything about whether a ride booked on the app in Chicago or a meal delivered in Taipei was profitable to fulfill. Underneath the headline, the operating trend was moving the other way: Uber's full-year operating margin had gone from roughly −22% in 2021 to roughly −5.75% in 2022, and free cash flow for full-year 2021 had been a $743 million outflow. Two numbers, same earnings release, pointing in opposite directions — and the entire question of whether Uber in June 2022 is a falling knife or a coiled spring runs straight into which of those two numbers is the signal.

Uber 自己在 2022 年 5 月 4 日公布的第一季數字，乍看之下並沒有讓氣氛好轉。營收 68.5 億美元，比一年前大幅成長——但公司單季認列 GAAP 淨虧損 59.3 億美元，是公司史上數一數二大的單季虧損。如果只看這個頭條數字，看起來完全印證了「這波成長股屠殺是活該」。但只要往下多看一層、看公司自己揭露的附註，就會發現這筆虧損絕大部分是非現金、非本業的項目：Uber 持有的幾筆少數股權投資（包括自駕新創 Aurora——2020 年 12 月接手了 Uber 自己的自駕部門——以及當年把中國、東南亞叫車業務賣給當地對手後留下的滴滴與 Grab 股權）被按市值重新估價，帳面產生的減損。這筆錢一分錢都沒有真的流出公司，也完全沒有回答芝加哥一趟叫車、台北一份外送到底賺不賺錢。頭條數字底下，營運趨勢其實在往反方向走：Uber 全年營益率從 2021 年約 −22%，改善到 2022 年約 −5.75%；而 2021 年全年自由現金流是流出 7.43 億美元。同一份財報裡的兩個數字，方向完全相反——2022 年 6 月的 Uber 到底是正在墜落的刀子，還是壓縮到底準備彈起的彈簧，整個問題就卡在這兩個數字裡哪一個才是真訊號。

Uber holds minority equity stakes in a handful of other companies — the self-driving startup Aurora (which took over Uber's own self-driving unit in a December 2020 deal), and stakes in Didi and Grab left over from Uber selling its China and Southeast Asia ride-hailing operations to local rivals years earlier. Accounting rules require these stakes to be marked to market each quarter, meaning their value on Uber's books moves up or down with those companies' own share prices, and that swing runs straight through Uber's reported net income even though not a single dollar of cash changes hands. When markets broadly sold off growth and tech names in the first months of 2022, those stakes lost a large chunk of paper value, and that non-cash, non-operating loss is what drove most of the $5.93 billion headline number. It says nothing about whether a ride booked in Chicago or a meal delivered in Taipei was profitable to fulfill that quarter — which is a separate question, answered by the operating-margin and free-cash-flow lines, not the net-income line.

Part One, the Industry: What Is Pulling the Ride-Hailing and Delivery Engine

第一部練習：產業——是什麼在拉動叫車與外送這台引擎

Start with demand, the way lesson 03 teaches it. By June 2022, ride demand is recovering toward pre-pandemic levels as offices, travel, and nightlife reopen; food-delivery demand, pulled forward hard by 2020–2021 lockdowns, is decelerating from its pandemic-peak growth rate but has not given back its gains — a large share of people who tried delivery for the first time in 2020 are still ordering in 2022. That is two different demand curves bundled inside one reported revenue number, which is exactly why a single blended growth figure is hard to trust without separating Mobility from Delivery first.

先照第 03 課教的方式看需求。到 2022 年 6 月，隨著辦公室、旅遊、夜生活重新開放，叫車需求正朝疫情前水準回升；外送需求則相反——被 2020 到 2021 年封城硬拉出來的爆量正從高峰成長率降溫，但沒有把疫情期間拉到的用戶還回去——很多 2020 年第一次用外送的人，2022 年還在訂。這是兩條不同的需求曲線，被包在同一個公布的營收數字裡，這正是為什麼不先把叫車跟外送拆開，一個合併的成長率很難直接信任的原因。

Supply, per lesson 04, looks unusual here because the supply side is not a factory with a multi-year build lead time — it is individual drivers and couriers deciding, day to day, whether the pay and flexibility are worth their time. Through 2021 and into 2022, driver supply had been unusually tight coming out of the pandemic (many drivers who left the platform in 2020–2021 had not fully returned), and Uber's own commentary through this period pointed to gradually improving supply as a factor behind shorter wait times and lower surge pricing heading into 2022. Whether that improvement continues or stalls is exactly the kind of supply-side variable this course teaches you to keep watching quarter to quarter, not to assume from one good data point.

照第 04 課教的方式看供給，這裡會有點不一樣，因為供給端不是要蓋好幾年才能量產的工廠，而是每個司機、每個外送員每天自己決定：這份薪水跟彈性，值不值得花這個時間。整個 2021 年到 2022 年初，走出疫情之後的司機供給一直偏緊（2020 到 2021 年離開平台的司機，並沒有完全回流），Uber 自己這段期間的說法，也把等待時間縮短、加成倍數下降，部分歸因於供給正逐步改善，一路走進 2022 年。這個改善會不會持續、還是會停滯，正是這門課教你要一季一季持續觀察、而不是靠一個好數字就假設下去的那種供給端變數。

The substitution threat, per lesson 07, sits one layer further out than a competing ride-hailing app: fully autonomous vehicles. By June 2022, this is no longer a distant hypothetical. Waymo has been operating a driverless robotaxi service (no human safety driver in the vehicle) to the public in the Phoenix, Arizona area since late 2020, and in June 2022 California's Public Utilities Commission approved Cruise to begin charging fares for a driverless robotaxi service in parts of San Francisco. Neither company is a scale threat to Uber's roughly $112 billion of 2021 gross bookings yet — both remain limited to small geographic footprints — but the direction is no longer purely speculative, and it sits on the same balance sheet issue as the freeze-date loss: Uber does not build autonomous vehicles itself, it holds an equity stake in one developer (Aurora) instead, a bet that could pay off as a demand-aggregation layer or could leave Uber disintermediated if a robotaxi operator eventually decides it does not need Uber's app to fill its cars.

照第 07 課教的方式看替代威脅，這裡的威脅比「另一個叫車 App 來搶市占」更深一層：完全自動駕駛的車輛。到 2022 年 6 月，這已經不是遙遠的假設。Waymo 從 2020 年底起，已經在美國亞利桑那州鳳凰城地區，對一般大眾提供沒有安全員坐在車上的全自駕叫車服務；2022 年 6 月，加州公用事業委員會核准 Cruise 開始在舊金山部分地區，對全自駕叫車服務收費載客。這兩家公司目前都還構不成對 Uber 2021 年全年大約 1,120 億美元總預訂金額的規模性威脅——都還侷限在很小的地理範圍——但這個方向已經不再純粹是假設，而且它跟凍結日這筆虧損，站在同一個資產負債表問題上：Uber 自己不造自駕車，而是持有一家自駕開發商（Aurora）的股權，這場賭注可能讓 Uber 以「需求聚合層」的角色分到好處，也可能讓 Uber 被繞過去，如果哪天一家自駕營運商決定不再需要 Uber 的 App 來幫忙載客。

Takeaway收斂

The industry read as of June 2022: ride demand is normalizing up, delivery demand is normalizing down from a high base but not collapsing, driver supply is tight but plausibly recovering, and a real (if still small-scale) autonomous-vehicle substitution threat is already operating commercially in two U.S. cities. None of this alone answers whether Uber is a good business — it sets the stage for Part Two.

2022 年 6 月的產業判讀：叫車需求正在向上正常化，外送需求正從高基期向下正常化但沒有崩跌，司機供給偏緊但比較像是可望回補，而一個真實（雖然規模還很小）的自駕替代威脅，已經在美國兩座城市商業化運轉。單靠這些還無法回答 Uber 是不是一門好生意——這只是為第二部練習搭好舞台。

Part Two, the Business: What a Good Business Looks Like Here

第二部練習：生意——好生意在這裡長什麼樣子

Start with the business model, the way lesson 08 teaches it. Uber does not own cars, employ drivers, or hold food inventory — it is an asset-light marketplace that takes a cut of a transaction it facilitates but does not produce. The revenue formula is almost embarrassingly simple: gross bookings times take rate. In Q1 2022, that arithmetic reads $26.4 billion of gross bookings and $6.85 billion of revenue, a take rate of roughly 26%. What is genuinely hard is everything the simple formula hides: insurance costs that scale with trips and vary wildly by regulatory jurisdiction, incentive payments to drivers and riders that show up as a reduction to that take rate in bad competitive periods, and a cost structure for Delivery that includes far more logistics complexity per dollar of gross bookings than Mobility does. An asset-light model does not mean a simple one — it means the complexity moved from the balance sheet into the income statement's footnotes.

先照第 08 課教的方式看商業模式。Uber 不擁有車輛、不雇用司機、也不持有食物庫存——它是一個資產輕的市集，對它促成、但自己不生產的交易抽一手。營收公式簡單到近乎樸素：總預訂金額 × 抽成率。用 2022 年第一季的數字代進去，264 億美元的總預訂金額，換來 68.5 億美元的營收，抽成率大約 26%。真正難的地方，全部藏在這個簡單公式沒寫出來的地方：隨趟次規模擴大、又依監管管轄區差異極大的保險成本；在競爭激烈的時期會直接侵蝕抽成率的司機與乘客補貼；還有外送業務裡，每一塊錢總預訂金額背後所牽涉的物流複雜度，遠高於叫車業務。資產輕的模式，不代表這門生意簡單——它代表複雜度從資產負債表，搬進了損益表的附註裡。

Now the moat, the way lesson 09 teaches it — and here the honest analysis has to start with a counter-argument, not a confirmation. Lesson 09's own treatment of network effects singles out ride-hailing as the standing example of a network effect that looks textbook on paper but is structurally weak in practice, because of multi-homingA user being active on two or more competing platforms at once at little or no cost, which prevents any single network from becoming exclusive or gaining real pricing power over that user.一個使用者同時活躍在兩個以上的競爭平台上、幾乎沒有代價，這會讓任何單一網路都無法變得排他，也拿不到對這個使用者真正的定價權。. Drivers routinely run Uber and Lyft simultaneously and take whichever ride pings first; riders routinely check both apps' prices before booking. That is exactly the pattern lesson 09 warns turns a two-sided network into a decade-long two-player market with thin margins instead of a moat — and it is worth naming plainly here rather than glossing over it, because a lazy version of this case would simply assert “network effects” and move on.

再照第 09 課教的方式看護城河——而這裡誠實的分析必須先從一個反方論點開始，不是先確認結論。第 09 課本身在談網路效應的時候，就特別點名叫車服務是一個「看起來像教科書式網路效應、實際上結構很脆弱」的標準案例，原因正是多平台使用One user active on two or more rival platforms at once, at little or no cost — a pattern that keeps any single network from becoming exclusive.一位使用者同時活躍在兩個以上的對手平台，幾乎沒有代價——這個模式讓任何單一網路都無法變得排他。：司機經常同時開著 Uber 跟 Lyft 兩個 app，哪一單先跳出來就接哪一單；乘客也習慣訂車前兩個 app 的價格都查一下。這正是第 09 課警告過的模式——它會把一個雙邊網路，變成一個維持十年、利潤薄弱的雙人市場，而不是一條護城河。這裡值得直接講清楚，而不是輕輕帶過，因為一個偷懶版本的案例分析，很容易就直接斷言「有網路效應」然後就跳過去了。

So where, if anywhere, is Uber's edge? Three places worth checking, in order of how directly they show up in the numbers, following lesson 10's discipline of demanding numerical evidence rather than a plausible story. First, density economicsA local, not global, network effect: liquidity (driver availability, wait times, price stability) is won or lost city by city, so scale in one market does not automatically transfer to another, but deep density within a market does lower cost per trip.一種局部而非全球性的網路效應：流動性（司機可得性、等待時間、價格穩定度）是一個城市一個城市分別打的，所以在一個市場的規模，不會自動搬到另一個市場，但在同一個市場裡的高密度，確實會壓低每趟的成本。 is real even though it is local rather than global — a market where Uber has enough simultaneous supply and demand runs shorter pickup times and smoother pricing than a thinner market, and that shows up as a genuine cost and reliability advantage within that city, even if it does nothing to help Uber in a city it has not yet won. Second, cross-platform bundling: the Uber One membership, which pairs Mobility and Delivery discounts under one subscription, is the one mechanism that pulls the relationship from a per-trip decision (where multi-homing thrives) to an annual one (where switching has real friction) — but at the freeze date this program is still young and covers a modest share of the user base, so it is evidence of a strategy, not yet proof that the strategy has worked. Third, scale advantages in insurance underwriting, payments infrastructure, and driver background-check systems that are expensive to build once and cheap to run at volume — genuine, but the kind of edge that shows up as a slowly widening cost gap rather than a wall competitors cannot climb at all.

那麼 Uber 的優勢究竟在哪裡，如果真的存在的話？照第 10 課「要求數字證據、不要接受一個聽起來合理的故事」的紀律，依照它在數字上出現的直接程度，有三個地方值得檢查。第一，密度經濟A local rather than global network effect — liquidity is won city by city, so density within one market lowers cost per trip even though it does not transfer automatically to another city.一種局部而非全球性的網路效應——流動性是一個城市一個城市分別打下來的，所以在一個市場內的密度會壓低每趟成本，但不會自動搬到另一個城市。是真的，只是它是局部而非全球性的——一個 Uber 同時擁有足夠供給與需求的市場，接送等待時間更短、定價更穩，這在那個城市裡是真實的成本與可靠度優勢，即使這對 Uber 還沒打下來的城市完全沒有幫助。第二，跨產品綁定：Uber One 會員把叫車與外送的折扣包進同一個訂閱方案，是唯一一個能把使用者關係，從「單趟決定」（多平台使用最容易發生的地方）拉到「年度決定」（換平台真的有摩擦成本）的機制——但在凍結的這個當下，這個方案還很年輕，覆蓋的用戶比例也還不高，所以它是一個策略存在的證據，還不是這個策略已經奏效的證明。第三，在保險核保、金流基礎設施、司機背景查核系統上的規模優勢——建置一次很貴、量大之後邊際成本很低，這是真的優勢，但屬於那種會慢慢拉開成本差距、而不是對手完全爬不過去的一道牆。

Uber's CEO memo, May 9, 2022. Six days after the Q1 2022 earnings call, CEO Dara Khosrowshahi sent an internal memo to Uber's roughly 30,000 employees, later reported by multiple outlets, describing “a seismic shift” in capital markets and announcing that hiring would be treated “as a privilege” going forward, with the company becoming “even more hardcore about costs across the board.” The memo explicitly named the mechanism this lesson keeps returning to: with financing suddenly expensive and public-market investors no longer willing to fund years of cash burn on faith, the company's internal priority was shifting from headline growth to free cash flow. This is lesson 15's capital-allocation discipline made visible in real time — not a slide in an investor deck, but a same-week reaction to a financing environment that had just changed underneath the company, arriving in the same month gross bookings were still growing 35% year over year. The two things — strong top-line growth and a management team tightening spending — are not contradictory; a management team that waits for growth to actually slow before tightening spending has usually waited too long.

Uber 執行長內部信，2022 年 5 月 9 日。第一季法說會結束六天後，執行長 Dara Khosrowshahi 發了一封內部信給公司大約三萬名員工——後來被多家媒體報導——信裡形容資本市場出現「一次地震式的轉變」，並宣布往後招募將被當成「一種特權」，公司整體要「對成本更加硬派」。這封信明講出這一課一直在回頭談的那個機制：融資突然變貴，公開市場的投資人不再願意單憑信念資助好幾年的燒錢，公司內部的優先順序，正從頭條成長率，轉向自由現金流。這正是第 15 課談的資本配置紀律，在真實時間裡被具體看見——不是投資人簡報裡的一張投影片，而是融資環境剛在公司腳下轉變之後，同一週內的即時反應；而且這個反應，跟總預訂金額還在以 35% 年增率成長的同一個月同時發生。這兩件事——營收頭部還在強勁成長、經營層卻同時在收緊支出——並不矛盾；一個等到成長真的放緩才開始收緊支出的經營團隊，通常已經等得太晚了。

Worked Example: Reading the Take-Rate Arithmetic Yourself實作範例：自己動手算抽成率

Uber's Q1 2022 shareholder letter disclosed roughly $26.4 billion of gross bookings and $6.85 billion of revenue for the quarter. Here is the whole calculation lesson 08 asks you to run on any marketplace business before trusting its growth headline.

Uber 2022 年第一季的股東信揭露，該季總預訂金額大約 264 億美元、營收 68.5 億美元。以下是第 08 課教的、在相信任何市集型公司的成長頭條之前，你自己該跑一遍的完整計算。

  1. 1
    Take rate = revenue ÷ gross bookings.抽成率 ＝ 營收 ÷ 總預訂金額。
    $6.85B ÷ $26.4B ≈ 25.9%
  2. 2
    Compare that to the prior year's take rate (roughly 20–21% in early 2021, when incentive spending to win back drivers coming out of the pandemic was heavier). A rising take rate over that stretch is a sign the company is retaining more of each dollar transacted — not by raising prices alone, but partly by spending less on incentives per trip as driver supply recovered.跟前一年同期的抽成率比較（2021 年初大約 20% 到 21%，那時為了把疫情期間流失的司機找回來，補貼支出比較重）。這段期間抽成率上升，代表公司從每一塊錢的交易金額裡留下更多——不完全是靠漲價，有一部分是司機供給恢復之後，每趟需要花的補貼變少了。
  3. 3
    Now ask the question the number alone cannot answer: is a rising take rate coming from Uber having genuine pricing power, or simply from a temporary lull in driver-incentive competition with Lyft that could reverse the moment competition heats up again? At the freeze date, the honest answer is “cannot fully separate the two from public disclosure alone” — which is itself a useful, humble conclusion, not a failure of the exercise.接著問這個數字本身回答不了的問題：抽成率上升，是因為 Uber 真的有定價權，還是只是跟 Lyft 之間的司機補貼競爭暫時降溫，一旦競爭再升溫就可能反轉？在凍結的這個時間點，誠實的答案是「光靠公開揭露沒辦法完全分開這兩個原因」——這本身就是一個有用、謙遜的結論，不是這個練習失敗了。
Think first: if you only had one number to check every quarter to know whether Uber's unit economics were really turning, which would it be — take rate, gross bookings growth, or free cash flow — and why?先想一下：如果你每季只能檢查一個數字，來判斷 Uber 的單位經濟到底有沒有真的在轉正，你會選抽成率、總預訂金額成長率，還是自由現金流？為什麼？

Free cash flow is the hardest to fake among the three. Gross bookings growth can rise even while the company is losing more money per trip, if it is buying growth with incentives. Take rate can rise for reasons unrelated to durable pricing power, as the worked example above just showed. Free cash flow is what is left after all of it — incentives, insurance, driver payouts, corporate overhead, capital spending — nets out against cash coming in, so a company cannot dress it up by simply spending more to grow faster. It is not a perfect number either (it says nothing about SBC dilution, as the previous section noted), but among the three, it is the one that is hardest to manufacture a good quarter for artificially.

三個數字裡，自由現金流最難造假。總預訂金額的成長率，即使公司每趟虧得更多，也可能靠補貼買出來、持續上升。抽成率上升的原因，可能跟前面實作範例show的一樣，跟持久的定價權完全無關。自由現金流則是把補貼、保險、付給司機的錢、公司營運費用、資本支出全部扣掉之後，實際留下來的現金——公司沒辦法單純靠花更多錢衝更快的成長，來把這個數字打扮得好看。它也不是完美指標（就像前一段提過的，它完全沒反映股權薪酬造成的稀釋），但這三個數字裡，它是最難靠人為操作生出一個漂亮單季數字的一個。

Part Three, the Market: Archetype, Price, and What Expectations Already Assume

第三部練習：市場——原型、價格，以及股價已經假設了什麼

Uber in June 2022 fits lesson 16's “unprofitable high growth” archetype almost exactly — a company still burning cash in aggregate, whose entire investment case depends on believing the burn will end within a visible time horizon rather than continuing indefinitely. That archetype comes with two named tools. Rule of 40A rough health check for unprofitable growth companies: revenue growth rate plus profit margin (or free-cash-flow margin) should add up to roughly 40 or more. Comfortably clearing 40 suggests the growth is being bought at a reasonable cost; falling well short suggests growth is being bought expensively.給未獲利成長公司用的粗略健康檢查：營收成長率加上利潤率（或自由現金流利潤率），大約要加起來到 40 以上。輕鬆超過 40，代表這個成長買得還算划算；遠遠不到，代表這個成長買得很貴。 is one: with Q1 2022 gross bookings growth of roughly 35% year over year and a free-cash-flow margin still negative (roughly −1 to −4% depending on which recent quarter is used, since the trend was moving toward breakeven but had not yet crossed it at the freeze date), the sum lands somewhere in the low-to-mid 30s — close to the 40 line but, on the public numbers available in June 2022, not yet clearing it. That is a genuinely close call, not a comfortable pass.

2022 年 6 月的 Uber，幾乎完全符合第 16 課「未獲利高成長」這個原型——一家整體上還在燒錢的公司，整個投資論點都建立在「相信這個燒錢會在可預見的時間內結束、而不是無限期燒下去」這個信念上。這個原型配有兩個工具。40 法則A rough health check: revenue growth rate plus profit margin should add up to roughly 40 or more.一個粗略的健康檢查：營收成長率加利潤率，大約要加到 40 以上。是其中一個：以 2022 年第一季總預訂金額約 35% 的年增率，加上仍是負值的自由現金流利潤率（大約 −1% 到 −4% 之間，依採用哪一季而定，因為趨勢正在往損益兩平走、但在凍結的當下還沒真正跨過去），加總落在 30 出頭到 30 中段之間——接近 40 這條線，但以 2022 年 6 月當時公開能拿到的數字來看，還沒真的跨過去。這是一個真正接近的判斷，不是輕鬆過關。

The second tool, from lesson 18, matters more here than a traditional P/E ratio ever could, because Uber had no GAAP earnings to put in the denominator of a P/E in the first place. The question lesson 18 forces is not “is this cheap or expensive” in the abstract, but “what does the current price already assume has to go right, and how likely is that?” At a roughly $45–50 billion market cap in June 2022 against 2021 gross bookings of about $112 billion, the market was pricing Uber at under half a times gross bookings — a level that, relative to Uber's own 2019 IPO pricing and relative to where high-growth marketplace businesses had traded in 2021, already looked like it was assuming a difficult multi-year stretch, not a rosy one. That is the single most important sentence in this whole valuation discussion: a beaten-down price does not mean a stock is safe, but it does mean the bar for what has to happen next is already lower than it was a year earlier.

第二個工具來自第 18 課，在這裡比傳統本益比更有用，因為 Uber 當時根本沒有 GAAP 盈餘可以放進本益比的分母。第 18 課逼你問的問題，不是抽象地問「這檔便宜還是貴」，而是問「現在這個股價，已經假設了接下來要發生哪些好事，而這些好事發生的機率有多高？」以 2022 年 6 月大約 450 到 500 億美元的市值，對照 2021 年全年大約 1,120 億美元的總預訂金額，市場給 Uber 的定價，不到總預訂金額的半倍——這個水準，不論是相對 Uber 自己 2019 年掛牌時的定價，還是相對高成長市集型公司在 2021 年的交易水準，看起來已經是在假設接下來會是一段艱難的路，而不是樂觀的路。這是這整段估值討論裡最重要的一句話：股價被砍得很低，不代表這檔股票安全，但確實代表接下來要發生的事，門檻已經比一年前低了。

One boundary worth naming explicitly, per this course's recurring habit of stating a tool's limits rather than only its uses: neither Rule of 40 nor a gross-bookings multiple tells you anything about competitive durability. A company can clear both comfortably while its moat is eroding underneath it, and a company can fail both while quietly building a durable position. These are screening tools for this specific archetype, not a substitute for the moat and management analysis already done in Part Two.

有一個邊界值得明講出來，照這門課一貫的習慣——不只講一個工具能做什麼，也講它做不到什麼：不管是 40 法則還是總預訂金額倍數，都完全沒有告訴你任何跟競爭持續力有關的事。一家公司可以兩個都輕鬆過關，但護城河同時正在底下被侵蝕；也可能兩個都沒過，卻在安靜地建立一個持久的位置。這兩個是這個特定原型的篩選工具，不能取代第二部練習已經做過的護城河與經營層分析。

Part Four, the Judgment: Weighing the Evidence and Writing a Falsifiable Thesis

第四部練習：判斷——權衡證據、寫下一個可被證偽的論點

Following lesson 21's evidence-tier discipline: the strongest evidence at the freeze date is Uber's own audited Q1 2022 operating-margin and gross-bookings figures (primary, company-disclosed) and the Fed's own published rate decisions (primary, government). Weaker, still useful evidence includes analyst commentary on driver-supply recovery and competitive intensity with Lyft (verified secondary, cross-checkable against multiple outlets but reliant on estimates). The weakest tier — worth naming and then setting aside rather than leaning on — is anything resembling a specific price target or a confident call on where interest rates go next; nobody in June 2022, whatever their title, actually knew that.

照第 21 課教的證據分級紀律：凍結當下最強的證據，是 Uber 自己揭露、經過審計的 2022 年第一季營益率與總預訂金額數字（一手、公司自己揭露），以及聯準會自己公布的升息決議（一手、政府）。較弱、但仍有用的證據，包括分析師對司機供給恢復狀況、以及跟 Lyft 之間競爭強度的評論（經查證的二手，可以跨多家媒體交叉核對，但終究仰賴估計值）。最弱的一級——值得點名出來、然後放到一邊、不要靠它——是任何形式的具體目標價，或是對利率接下來會怎麼走的自信斷言；2022 年 6 月，不管頭銜多響亮，其實沒有人真的知道答案。

Per lesson 22, a real thesis has to be a sentence that could turn out to be wrong, not a mood. Here is one candidate: “Uber's operating losses are narrowing because of durable structural improvements — take-rate discipline, Uber One bundling, and driver-supply normalization — not because of temporary, reversible cost-cutting; therefore free cash flow should turn sustainably positive within roughly four to six quarters, and the current gross-bookings multiple already prices in a difficult path, making the risk/reward asymmetric to the upside for a patient holder.” Notice everything this sentence commits to that could later be checked against reality: a time window, a mechanism, and an explicit statement of what would make it wrong.

照第 22 課教的，一個真正的論點必須是一句「有可能被證明是錯的」的句子，不是一種情緒。這裡提出一個候選版本：「Uber 的營運虧損正在收窄，是因為結構性、持久的改善——抽成率紀律、Uber One 的產品綁定、司機供給正常化——而不是暫時、可逆的成本削減；因此自由現金流應該會在大約四到六季內，穩定轉正；而目前的總預訂金額倍數，已經price in 了一段艱難的路，這讓風險報酬對願意耐心持有的人來說偏向有利的一邊。」注意這句話承諾了哪些之後可以拿現實去核對的東西：一個時間窗、一個機制、以及一句明講「什麼情況會讓它錯」的話。

Per lesson 23, the useful next move is inversion — not asking why this thesis might be right, but assuming it fails and working backward to find the most likely reason why. Run a pre-mortem: it is, say, eighteen months later and the thesis has failed. What is the single most likely killer assumption that broke? Three candidates surface immediately. First, that driver-incentive competition with Lyft stays muted — if either company decides to spend aggressively to win share, take-rate gains reverse fast. Second, that a recession does not sharply cut discretionary ride and delivery spending — a company whose growth depends partly on consumers eating out and taking rides for convenience is exposed to a spending pullback in a way a true necessity is not. Third, and least visible at the freeze date, that regulatory reclassification of drivers as employees rather than contractors does not spread from the handful of jurisdictions already fighting over it (California's Proposition 22, and ongoing European Union discussions) to Uber's largest markets — a reclassification would restructure the cost base in a way no amount of operating-leverage math could offset.

照第 23 課教的，接下來有用的一步是反過來想——不是問「這個論點為什麼可能是對的」，而是先假設它失敗了，再倒推最可能的原因。跑一次事前驗屍：假設十八個月後，這個論點失敗了。最可能被打破的殺手假設是哪一個？立刻浮現三個候選。第一，跟 Lyft 之間的司機補貼競爭維持低溫——只要其中一家決定砸錢搶市占，抽成率的改善就會很快反轉。第二，衰退不會大砍消費者在叫車與外送上的非必要支出——一家成長有一部分靠消費者為了方便而外食、叫車的公司，暴露在支出緊縮風險下的程度，跟一個真正的民生必需品不一樣。第三，也是在凍結當下最不明顯的一個：司機被重新歸類為員工而非承攬者的監管風險，不會從目前已經在打的少數轄區（加州的 Proposition 22、以及歐盟持續進行中的討論）擴散到 Uber 最大的幾個市場——如果真的擴散，會直接重組整個成本結構，任何營運槓桿的算術都補不回來。

Per lesson 24's three-way framework, this is not a binary buy-or-don't decision. Weighing the evidence above — a genuinely improving operating trend against a genuinely fragile competitive moat, a price that already looks cautious against three specific, trackable killer assumptions — the case leans toward act rather than avoid, but only as a modest position sized for the uncertainty, not a conviction-scale bet; the honest alternative reasonable readers could reach is wait, if they weight the regulatory and recessionary risks more heavily than the improving operating numbers. Before reading further, write down your own verdict.

照第 24 課教的三分框架，這不是一個買或不買的二選一。把上面的證據放在天平兩端——一個真正在改善的營運趨勢，對上一個真正脆弱的競爭護城河；一個看起來已經偏保守的股價，對上三個具體、可追蹤的殺手假設——這個案子偏向進場，但只是一個因應不確定性而縮小規模的部位，不是一個信念全押的重倉；另一個誠實、合理的讀者也可能得出的答案是觀望，如果他們把監管與衰退風險，看得比營運數字的改善更重。在往下讀之前，先寫下你自己的裁決。

What would change the size, up or down, follows directly from the three killer assumptions named above: two consecutive quarters of confirmed positive free cash flow (not just a narrowing loss) would be evidence the margin-inflection thesis is real rather than a temporary cost-cutting exercise, arguing for adding within the satellite-structural ceiling; a reignition of driver-incentive spending against Lyft, a sharp pullback in ride/delivery frequency during a downturn, or a regulatory reclassification ruling against Uber in a major market would each independently argue for cutting the position, not waiting to see if the others also happen.

Pitfall: mistaking “the stock already fell a lot” for “the stock is now safe.” A share price cut in half from its IPO tells you the market has already priced in a great deal of bad news — it does not tell you whether the remaining bad news is fully priced in, over-priced in, or under-priced in. Plenty of stocks that had already fallen 50% in June 2022 went on to fall another 50% from there. The correct use of a beaten-down price is as one input into the expectations question from Part Three (what does this price already assume?), never as a standalone reason to buy.

陷阱：把「股價已經跌了很多」誤當成「股價現在安全了」。股價從 IPO 價腰斬，告訴你的是市場已經price in 了不少壞消息——它沒有告訴你，剩下的壞消息，到底是完全反映了、反映過頭了、還是根本沒反映夠。2022 年 6 月時已經跌了 50% 的股票，很多後來又再跌了 50%。一個被砍得很低的股價，正確的用法，是當成第三部練習「這個價格已經假設了什麼」這個問題的其中一項輸入，而不是單獨拿來當作買進的理由。

⚠

Pitfall: confusing a management team's cost-cutting announcement with proof that unit economics are structurally better. The Khosrowshahi memo above is real and meaningful, but a memo is a stated intention, not a result. Cutting hiring and discretionary spending genuinely improves near-term cash flow — that part is mechanical and close to certain — but it does not by itself prove that the underlying per-trip economics (take rate net of incentives, insurance cost per mile, driver retention cost) have durably improved. The only evidence that actually answers that question is several consecutive quarters of margin and free-cash-flow trend, which is exactly why lesson 24's framework leans toward a small position and a wait-for-confirmation trigger rather than a large one taken purely on management's stated intent.

陷阱：把經營層宣布削減成本，誤當成單位經濟結構性變好的證明。前面那封 Khosrowshahi 的內部信是真實且有意義的，但一封信是表達出來的意圖，不是結果。減少招募跟非必要支出，確實會改善短期現金流——這部分是機械式的、幾乎確定會發生——但它本身不能證明每趟交易背後真正的經濟結構（扣掉補貼後的抽成率、每英里保險成本、司機留任成本）已經持久變好。真正能回答這個問題的證據，只有連續好幾季的利潤率與自由現金流趨勢——這正是為什麼第 24 課的框架，會傾向小部位加上「等確認訊號出現」的觸發條件，而不是單純因為經營層說了什麼就重壓。

Reveal: how the margin-inflection thesis actually played out, 2022–2024揭曉：利潤率轉折這個論點，2022 到 2024 年實際上是怎麼演變的

Q2 2022, reported August 2, 2022 — exactly the quarter right after the freeze date — was Uber's first quarter of positive free cash flow, at roughly $382 million. That was the confirmation the wait-verdict reader was holding out for. It did not stop there: full-year 2022 free cash flow came in at roughly $390 million, full-year 2023 free cash flow rose to roughly $3.4 billion, and full-year 2023 also delivered Uber's first full year of GAAP net income, at roughly $1.9 billion (helped by a one-time deferred tax benefit, a detail worth noting rather than skipping past, since it means the “first profitable year” headline is not purely an operating story). Full-year 2024 free cash flow reached roughly $6.9 billion on revenue of roughly $44.0 billion. Uber was added to the S&P 500 index in December 2023, a milestone that specifically requires demonstrated GAAP profitability, not just narrative momentum. Stock-based compensation, the item flagged in Part Two, stayed roughly flat in absolute dollars (in the high-single-digit-percent range as a share of revenue in 2021, falling to roughly the mid-single digits by 2024) — meaning dilution slowed as a percentage even though the operating turnaround was not free of it. The shares, roughly $20–26 at the freeze date, traded in the roughly $70–80 range through late 2024 and into 2025 — a multi-year gain of somewhere around three-fold, though anyone using this fact alone as evidence the June 2022 “act” verdict was obviously correct is committing exactly the outcome-bias error the next section addresses.

The Post-Mortem: What Was Foreseeable, and What Genuinely Was Not

事後檢討：什麼是當時就看得出來的，什麼真的不是

Per lesson 25's core distinction, the right question after any outcome is known is never “was the outcome good?” but “was the process that produced the decision sound, given what was knowable at the time?” A three-fold stock gain does not retroactively make the June 2022 thesis a good one if it was reached by luck, and a stock that had instead fallen further would not retroactively make it a bad one if the process was sound and simply lost to a low-probability outcome. Judge the process, not the scoreboard.

照第 25 課教的核心區分，知道結果之後，正確的問題永遠不是「結果好不好」，而是「照當時能知道的資訊，做出這個決定的過程本身站不站得住腳」。就算股價漲了三倍，如果 2022 年 6 月的論點是靠運氣矇對的，也不會因此變成一個好論點；反過來，如果股價後來反而繼續下跌，只要過程本身合理、只是輸給了一個低機率結果，也不會因此變成一個爛論點。判斷過程，不是看計分板。

Score the three killer assumptions from Part Four against what actually happened. The driver-incentive competition risk with Lyft: this one turned out favorably for the thesis — Lyft's own growth and market share stayed well behind Uber's through 2022–2024, so the feared reignition of a subsidy war never fully materialized at the scale that would have broken the take-rate trend. The recession-cuts-discretionary-spending risk: the U.S. avoided the sharp downturn many feared in mid-2022, so this risk simply did not fire — which matters, because it means part of the good outcome came from a macro environment that was genuinely uncertain at the freeze date turning out favorably, not purely from company-specific execution. The regulatory-reclassification risk: this one stayed a live, unresolved issue in specific jurisdictions rather than either fully firing or fully disappearing — a reminder that not every named risk resolves cleanly one way or the other within the review window, and that is a legitimate outcome for a risk factor, not a flaw in the pre-mortem.

把第四部練習點名的三個殺手假設，拿去跟實際發生的事對帳。跟 Lyft 之間的司機補貼競爭風險：這一個對論點來說是有利的——Lyft 自己的成長跟市占，在 2022 到 2024 年間一路遠遠落後 Uber，原本擔心會重新升溫的補貼戰，並沒有燒到足以打斷抽成率趨勢的規模。衰退侵蝕非必要支出的風險：美國躲過了 2022 年年中許多人擔心的急遽衰退，所以這個風險根本沒有真的發生——這一點很重要，因為它代表好結果裡，有一部分來自凍結當下真的不確定、後來剛好走向有利的總體環境，不是純粹靠公司自己執行出來的。監管重新歸類的風險：這一個在特定轄區維持懸而未決，沒有完全爆發，也沒有完全消失——提醒我們，不是每一個點名出來的風險，都會在複審的時間窗內乾脆俐落地朝一個方向解決，這對一個風險項目來說是合理的結果，不代表事前驗屍做得不好。

📜War Story戰爭故事

WeWork's bankruptcy, November 2023. WeWork is the sharpest available contrast to Uber's outcome, and both were routinely mentioned in the same breath as “unprofitable growth darlings” of the 2019–2021 era. WeWork filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in November 2023 — roughly the same window in which Uber was being added to the S&P 500. Both companies shared the archetype label; they did not share a moat. WeWork's business — leasing office space long-term and subletting it short-term — had no density economics, no bundling mechanism, and no scale-driven cost advantage of the kind Part Two identified as Uber's (thin as they were); it was, in effect, a leveraged real-estate bet dressed in growth-company language. The lesson is not “unprofitable growth companies eventually fail” or “unprofitable growth companies eventually succeed” — both halves of that sentence are wrong as a rule. The lesson is that within the same archetype and the same macro window, the moat-and-unit-economics analysis from Part Two is what actually separated the two outcomes, which is exactly why that analysis cannot be skipped in favor of just checking the archetype box.

WeWork 破產，2023 年 11 月。WeWork 是跟 Uber 這個結果對照最鮮明的案例，而且兩家公司在 2019 到 2021 年那個年代，經常被同一口氣提到，都被叫做「未獲利成長寵兒」。WeWork 在 2023 年 11 月聲請美國破產法第 11 章重整——大約就在 Uber 被納入標普 500 的同一個時間窗附近。兩家公司share 同一個原型標籤，但沒有share 同一種護城河。WeWork 的生意——長期承租辦公空間、短期轉租出去——完全沒有第二部練習找出來 Uber 有的那種密度經濟、產品綁定機制、或規模驅動的成本優勢（即使 Uber 的這些優勢也不算厚）；它本質上是一場包裝成成長股語言的槓桿房地產賭注。這裡的教訓不是「未獲利成長公司最後都會失敗」，也不是「未獲利成長公司最後都會成功」——這兩句話當成一條規則都是錯的。真正的教訓是：在同一個原型標籤、同一段總經環境下，真正把兩個結果分開的，是第二部練習做的護城河與單位經濟分析——這正是為什麼那個分析不能被省略、不能只靠打勾「這是未獲利高成長」這個原型標籤就跳過去。

Three Things to Carry Forward帶走的三件事

One, a headline net loss and an improving operating trend can both be true in the same earnings release — always separate the non-cash, non-operating items before judging a growth stock's trajectory. Two, the archetype label (“unprofitable high growth”) tells you which tools to reach for, not what verdict to reach; Uber and WeWork wore the same label and had opposite outcomes because the underlying moat and unit economics were different, not because one was labeled correctly and the other was not. Three, a good outcome does not retroactively validate a lucky process, and a bad outcome does not retroactively condemn a sound one — the only fair grade is whether the pre-mortem named the real risks and whether the position size matched the genuine uncertainty at the time.

第一，頭條淨損跟一個正在改善的營運趨勢，可以同時出現在同一份財報裡——判斷一檔成長股的走向之前，永遠先把非現金、非本業的項目拆開來看。第二，原型標籤（「未獲利高成長」）告訴你該拿哪些工具出來用，不是告訴你該下什麼裁決；Uber 跟 WeWork 戴著同一個標籤，結果卻完全相反，因為底層的護城河與單位經濟不一樣，不是因為一個貼對標籤、一個貼錯標籤。第三，好結果不會反過來讓一個靠運氣矇對的過程變成好過程，壞結果也不會反過來讓一個紮實的過程變成壞過程——唯一公平的評分標準，是事前驗屍有沒有點名出真正的風險，以及部位大小有沒有配上當時真實存在的不確定性。
