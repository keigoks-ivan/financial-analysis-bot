In 2016, the memory-chip industry was a place investors avoided. DRAM prices had fallen for two straight years, and by the end of 2016 some commodity DRAM was selling close to cash cost. Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron — the three firms that together made almost all of the world's DRAM — were reporting semiconductor returns on capital barely above their cost of capital, and in some quarters below it. New fab announcements had gone quiet. Capital spending, adjusted for depreciation, was flat to declining. Nobody wanted to own memory stocks.

二〇一六年，記憶體晶片產業是投資人避之唯恐不及的地方。DRAM 價格已經連跌兩年，到二〇一六年底，部分大宗規格的 DRAM 報價逼近現金成本。三星、SK 海力士、美光——三家公司合計生產全球絕大多數的 DRAM——半導體業務的資本報酬率僅略高於資金成本，某些季度甚至低於資金成本。新晶圓廠的宣布幾乎停了。經折舊調整後的資本支出持平甚至下滑。沒有人想碰記憶體股。

Eighteen months later the same industry looked unrecognizable. Smartphone makers and, increasingly, data-center operators were buying more memory per device than anyone had modeled. Prices roughly doubled, then kept climbing. By late 2018, DRAM industry returns on capital had swung from barely-above-water to somewhere north of 40% — a level normally associated with software franchises, not commodity chip manufacturing. And the three producers, who eighteen months earlier had frozen expansion plans, were now spending at a pace that pushed combined annual capital spending toward roughly double its 2016 level. Fewer than eighteen months after that, prices were falling again — this time by more than 40% in a single year — and industry returns on capital were sliding back toward the cost of capital.

十八個月後，同一個產業已經面目全非。手機廠商，還有愈來愈多的資料中心業者，買進的記憶體用量都超出多數人原本的模型假設。價格大約翻了一倍，然後繼續往上衝。到二〇一八年底，DRAM 產業的資本報酬率從勉強打平，一路衝到 40% 以上——這種水準平常是屬於軟體業的，不是商用晶片製造業。十八個月前才凍結擴產計畫的三家廠商，這時候的資本支出步調已經把合計年度資本支出推到接近二〇一六年的兩倍。再過不到十八個月，價格又開始下跌——這次是單一年度跌幅超過四成——產業資本報酬率也重新滑向資金成本邊緣。

Nothing about DRAM's underlying technology changed across those three years. What changed was the supply of capital chasing an increasingly attractive return — and the delay between the decision to spend and the new supply actually arriving. That gap between a return signal and the capacity it triggers, and between the capacity decision and the capacity's arrival, is the entire subject of this lesson. The Loop That Corrects Itself

會自我修正的循環

The mechanism is short enough to say in one sentence: high returns attract capital, capital eventually becomes capacity, capacity pushes returns back down, and low returns eventually drive capital — and capacity — back out. Each half of that loop is a real, observable, causal chain, not a vague pattern. When an industry earns returns well above its cost of capital, three things happen roughly in sequence: incumbents expand because the project math works; new entrants show up because the same math works for them too; and lenders and equity investors get more willing to fund growth in that industry specifically, because recent returns look good. None of that requires demand to change at all — it is a pure response to the return, and it takes time, because plants, mines, ships, and fabs are not built overnight.

這個機制可以濃縮成一句話：高報酬吸引資本流入，資本最終變成產能，產能又把報酬打下去，而低報酬最終又把資本——連同產能——逼出這個產業。這個循環的每一半都是真實、可觀察、有因果關係的鏈條，不是模糊的型態。當一個產業的報酬明顯高過資金成本，大致會依序發生三件事：既有廠商擴產，因為專案的財務數字划算；新進者出現，因為同樣的數字對他們也划算；放款方與股權投資人也更願意資助這個特定產業的成長，因為近期的報酬看起來很好。這一切完全不需要需求發生任何變化——純粹是對報酬的反應，而且需要時間，因為工廠、礦場、船隻、晶圓廠都不是一夜之間蓋好的。

The correction half of the loop works the same way in reverse, and it is just as mechanical. Once new capacity starts arriving — often years after the capital was committed — supply grows faster than demand for a stretch, prices soften, and returns compress. Weaker projects get shelved, marginal producers cut output or exit, and financing dries up for anyone still proposing new capacity in that industry. Eventually supply growth slows enough for demand to catch back up, returns recover, and — if nobody has learned anything — the whole loop starts again. This is why capital cycle thinking is sometimes summarized as "a good business is not the same as a good industry to add capacity to right now": the two questions have different answers depending entirely on where the industry sits in this loop.

循環修正的那一半，運作方式完全一樣，只是反過來，而且同樣機械化。一旦新產能開始到位——通常是資本投入後好幾年的事——供給在一段時間內成長得比需求快，價格軟化，報酬被壓縮。比較弱的專案會被擱置，邊際生產者會減產或退出，而還在提議新產能的廠商也愈來愈借不到錢。最終供給成長慢下來，需求追上來，報酬回升——如果沒有人記取教訓，整個循環就會重新開始。這也是為什麼資本週期思維常被濃縮成一句話：「好生意不等於現在適合往裡面加產能的產業」——這兩個問題的答案完全取決於這個產業目前站在循環的哪個位置。

A counterexample worth holding onto: not every high-return industry corrects quickly, and the framework does not claim it will. See's Candies, the confectioner Berkshire Hathaway bought in 1972, has earned returns on capital that would attract a flood of competitors in almost any other industry — and yet no meaningful new entrant has taken share from it in decades. The reason is not that capital chose not to chase those returns; it is that the return does not come from a scarce, replicable asset like a factory. It comes from a regional brand and distribution relationship built over generations, which new capital cannot simply buy or build on a normal project timeline. The capital cycle framework applies with force to industries where returns come from physical capacity — chips, ships, commodities, real estate — and applies much more weakly where returns come from something capital cannot manufacture quickly, a distinction this lesson returns to near the end.

值得記住的反例：不是每個高報酬的產業都會快速修正，這套框架也沒有這麼宣稱。時思糖果（See's Candies）——波克夏一九七二年買下的糖果公司——賺到的資本報酬率，如果放在幾乎任何其他產業，早該引來一波競爭者湧入。但幾十年過去，沒有任何有意義的新進者從它手上搶走份額。原因不是資本選擇不去追逐這個報酬，而是這個報酬並非來自一項稀缺、可複製的資產（像一座工廠），而是來自幾代人累積下來的區域品牌與通路關係——這種東西，新資本沒辦法照著正常的專案時間表直接買到或蓋出來。資本週期框架在報酬來自實體產能的產業（晶片、船運、大宗商品、房地產）裡效力最強，在報酬來自資本無法快速複製的東西的產業裡效力就弱得多——這一點，這一課的最後會回過頭來講。

Takeaway一句話收斂

High returns are not a stable state in a capital-intensive industry — they are an invitation that capital eventually accepts, with a delay.在資本密集型產業裡，高報酬不是穩定狀態——它是一張資本遲早會接受的邀請函，只是有時間差。

Think first: why does the correction always seem to arrive later than the warning signs suggest it should?先想一下：為什麼修正總是比警訊出現的時間晚很多？

Because the loop has two separate lags stacked on top of each other. The first lag is between a return signal and a capital-spending decision — boards need a few quarters of confirmed strong pricing before they commit, because nobody wants to be the one who greenlit a fab during a temporary spike. The second, usually much longer, lag is between the spending decision and the capacity actually coming online — two to three years for a semiconductor fab, five or more for a deepwater oil project, several years for a large mining expansion. So the gap you are trying to estimate is not "how long until returns fall," it is "how long from the return signal, through the decision lag, through the build lag, until the new supply actually lands" — and that full chain is almost always longer than the visible warning signs make it feel.

因為這個循環疊了兩段各自獨立的時間差。第一段時間差，是從報酬訊號到資本支出決策——董事會通常要看到好幾季確認過的強勁報價才敢下決定，因為沒有人想成為那個在短暫飆升期核准新蓋晶圓廠的人。第二段時間差通常更長，是從支出決策到產能真正上線——半導體晶圓廠要兩到三年，深海油田專案要五年以上，大型礦場擴建也要好幾年。所以你要估的落差，不是「報酬還要多久才會下滑」，而是「從報酬訊號出現，經過決策的時間差，再經過建置的時間差，到新供給真的落地」——而這整條鏈幾乎永遠比看得到的警訊讓人感覺的還要長。

Three Proxies — and Why You Need at Least Two to Agree

三個代理指標——為什麼至少要兩個一致才算數

Reading capital-cycle position off a narrative — "management sounds excited," "the trade press says shortage" — is unreliable, because narrative is the most easily manipulated and most lagging part of the whole picture. The framework instead runs on three quantitative proxies, each pulled from public filings, and treats a call as safe to make only when at least two of the three point the same direction. Requiring corroboration is not bureaucratic caution; each proxy has its own specific way of lying to you on its own, and the failure modes rarely overlap, so agreement across two independent measures screens most of them out.

單靠敘事來判斷資本週期位置——「管理層聽起來很興奮」、「產業媒體說缺貨」——並不可靠，因為敘事是整幅圖景裡最容易被操縱、也最滯後的一塊。這套框架改用三個從公開財報就能抓到的量化代理指標，並且只在至少兩個指向同一方向時，才敢把這個判斷當成穩的。要求相互印證不是官僚式的謹慎——每一個指標都有各自會騙人的方式，而且失效的模式很少重疊，所以要求兩個獨立指標一致，能篩掉大部分的誤判。

Proxy one — the capex/depreciation ratio. Depreciation is roughly a proxy for the capital spending needed just to maintain existing capacity as it wears out; capital spending above that level is, roughly, spending on growth. A ratio near 1.0x means an industry is mostly replacing what it already has. A ratio climbing toward 2x, 3x or higher — and staying there for several quarters — means new capacity is being built on top of the existing base, not just maintained. You find both lines on the cash-flow statement of every public filer in the industry (capital expenditures; depreciation and amortization); summing them across the two or three dominant players gives an industry-level read that is hard to fake, because it is audited spending, not a forecast.

指標一——資本支出／折舊比。折舊大致可以代表「光是要維持既有產能、抵銷設備自然耗損所需要的資本支出」；超過這個水準的資本支出，大致上就是在花錢擴產。比值接近 1.0 倍，代表這個產業大部分在替換既有設備。比值一路爬升到 2 倍、3 倍甚至更高，而且撐了好幾季——代表新產能正在既有基礎之上被蓋出來，而不只是維護。這兩條線在產業裡每一家上市公司的現金流量表都找得到（資本支出；折舊與攤銷），把該產業前二、三大廠商的數字加總，就能得到一個很難造假的產業級讀數，因為這是經過會計師查核的實際支出，不是預測。

Proxy two — industry ROIC versus the cost of capital. This is the return the capital cycle actually responds to. A spread of, say, 15-25 percentage points above the cost of capital (a rough weighted-average cost of capital for most industrial and commodity businesses runs somewhere around 8-10%) is the kind of gap that draws in aggressive expansion; a spread near zero or negative signals a trough where capital is being repelled, not attracted. The trap with this proxy alone is that it is backward-looking and can be distorted by one-off items — an asset sale, an insurance settlement, a currency swing — so a single quarter's spike or collapse should never be read as a phase change on its own.

指標二——產業 ROIC 與資金成本的利差。這是資本週期真正在回應的那個報酬。假設利差高出資金成本十五到二十五個百分點（多數工業與大宗商品類公司的粗略加權平均資金成本大約落在 8% 到 10% 之間），這種缺口足以吸引積極擴產；利差接近零或轉負，代表產業正處於資本被排斥、而非被吸引的谷底。只用這個指標的陷阱在於它是回顧性的，而且容易被一次性項目扭曲——資產出售、保險理賠、匯率波動——所以單一季度的暴衝或暴跌，絕對不該被單獨拿來當成階段轉換的證據。

Proxy three — new-capacity lead time. This is the least numeric of the three but arguably the most decision-relevant, because it tells you how much of the future is already locked in. It is estimated from disclosed construction timelines: fab groundbreaking-to-production announcements, mine permitting-to-first-ore schedules, shipyard order-to-delivery windows. A lead time that is shortening — because producers have learned to build faster, or are adding capacity in smaller, quicker increments — means the correction, when it comes, arrives sooner than the historical pattern would suggest. A lengthening lead time (permitting delays, equipment backlogs, skilled-labor shortages) buys the current high-return phase more time before new supply lands.

指標三——新產能前置時間。這是三者中最不量化的一個，但可能是對決策最有意義的一個，因為它告訴你「未來已經被鎖定了多少」。這個指標是靠已揭露的建置時程來估：晶圓廠動工到量產的公告、礦場取得許可到出第一批礦石的排程、造船廠接單到交船的時間窗。如果前置時間正在縮短——因為生產者學會蓋得更快，或改成用更小、更快的增量方式擴產——代表修正真的來臨時，會比歷史型態暗示的更早到。如果前置時間正在拉長（許可延遲、設備排隊、技術人力短缺），則會替目前的高報酬階段爭取到更多時間，讓新供給晚一點落地。

The Investment Clock: Four Phases, Four Recurring Mistakes

投資時鐘：四個階段，四種反覆出現的錯誤

Put the two proxies together over time and an industry traces a rough four-phase loop, sometimes called the investment clock. The phases are not calendar-fixed — a memory-chip cycle can run three to four years end to end, a shipping cycle five to ten — but the sequence and the mistake attached to each phase repeat with striking consistency across very different industries.

把兩個代理指標拉長時間一起看，一個產業大致會畫出一個四階段的循環，有時稱為投資時鐘。這幾個階段沒有固定的日曆長度——記憶體晶片的循環可能三到四年跑完一圈，航運循環可能要五到十年——但階段的先後順序，以及每個階段附帶的錯誤，卻在非常不同的產業之間，展現出驚人的一致性。

Phase I — trough. Returns are at or below the cost of capital, capex/depreciation is flat or falling, and capacity is being retired rather than added. The typical mistake here is giving up entirely — treating a cyclical trough as proof the industry is permanently broken, and missing the setup for the next leg because the pain of Phase I feels indistinguishable from genuine structural decline.

第一階段——谷底。報酬處於或低於資金成本，資本支出／折舊比持平或下滑，產能是在退場而不是在增加。這個階段最常見的錯誤是徹底放棄——把循環性的谷底當成產業已經永久壞掉的證據，因為第一階段的痛苦感受起來，跟真正的結構性衰退幾乎分不出來，於是錯過了下一波循環的鋪陳。

Phase II — shortage, capital pouring in. Demand has outrun existing supply, returns are spiking, and — this is the part people miss — capital is now flooding in to fix the shortage. This is the phase this lesson spends the most time on below, because it is the single most dangerous phase to misread, and the mistake is almost universal: treating the visible shortage as the buy signal, when the more important fact is that the correction is already being built.

第二階段——短缺，資金湧入。需求已經超過現有供給，報酬正在飆升，而——這正是多數人會漏掉的部分——資本已經開始湧入來修補這個短缺。這一課接下來會花最多篇幅講這個階段，因為它是最容易被誤讀、也是最危險的一個階段，而這個錯誤幾乎是通病：把看得到的短缺當成買進訊號，卻沒注意到更重要的事實是——修正這個短缺的力量，已經在被建置中了。

Phase III — capacity arriving, returns peaking or rolling over. The capital committed during Phase II starts showing up as actual output. Returns are usually still high — headline numbers look great, and consensus estimates are often still being revised upward — but the growth in supply is now outrunning the growth in demand. The mistake here is extrapolating the trailing numbers: using Phase III's still-strong reported returns to justify paying up for the stock, when the leading indicators (capex/depreciation still elevated, new supply visibly landing) already point the other way.

第三階段——產能到位，報酬見頂或開始回落。第二階段投入的資本，開始變成實際的產出。報酬通常還很高——表面數字看起來很漂亮，市場共識預估往往還在上修——但供給的成長速度已經超過需求的成長速度。這個階段最常見的錯誤是外推落後數字：拿第三階段仍然強勁的財報數字，來替追高股價找理由，卻沒發現領先指標（資本支出／折舊比仍居高不下、新供給正在明顯落地）早就指向反方向。

Phase IV — oversupply, returns falling toward or below the cost of capital. Prices are under visible pressure, weaker producers are cutting capacity or exiting, and capital spending is being slashed. The mistake here is the mirror image of Phase III's mistake: extrapolating the current pain forward and assuming the industry stays broken, when the capacity cuts happening right now are what seeds the next Phase I trough and eventual recovery.

第四階段——供給過剩，報酬滑向或跌破資金成本。價格承受明顯壓力，較弱的生產者開始減產或退出，資本支出被大砍。這個階段的錯誤，正好是第三階段錯誤的鏡像：把眼前的痛苦線性外推，假設這個產業會一直壞下去——卻沒發現此刻正在發生的產能削減，正是下一輪第一階段谷底、以及最終復甦的種子。

One more rule worth carrying forward: a phase transition should require two independent, conjoined signals, not one. "Capex growth breaks above its multi-year range" is a warning; "capex growth breaks above its range at the same time as order backlogs start shrinking" is a signal you can act on. Calling a phase change off a single data point is a common way to whipsaw yourself in and out of a position on noise.

還有一條值得帶著走的規則：判斷階段轉換，應該要求兩個獨立且同時出現的訊號，而不是一個。「資本支出成長率突破多年區間」只是警訊；「資本支出成長率突破區間，同時訂單能見度也開始縮短」才是可以據以行動的訊號。只憑單一數據點就喊階段轉換，是讓自己被雜訊反覆巴來巴去、進進出出的常見原因。

Why "Shortage Plus Capital Flooding In" Is the Most Dangerous Entry Point

為什麼「短缺加上資金瘋狂湧入」是最危險的進場點

This is the counterintuitive core of the lesson, so it is worth stating plainly: most investors treat a verified, visible shortage as close to the safest possible signal — demand clearly exceeds supply, pricing power is confirmed, management is bullish on every call. The capital cycle framework says the opposite. A shortage on its own tells you almost nothing about forward returns, because the question that actually determines forward returns is not "is there a shortage today," it is "how much capital has already been committed to end it, and how soon does that capital arrive as capacity." A shortage with capital pouring in is not a shortage that is safe to buy into — it is a shortage with its own expiration date already being built, and the more exciting the shortage narrative, the faster and larger that capital response tends to be.

這是這一課最反直覺的核心，所以值得講清楚：多數投資人會把一個經過驗證、看得見的短缺，當成接近最安全的訊號——需求明顯超過供給，定價權得到確認，管理層每一場法說都在喊多。資本週期框架的結論恰恰相反。單獨一個短缺，其實幾乎透露不出任何關於未來報酬的資訊，因為真正決定未來報酬的問題不是「今天是不是短缺」，而是「已經有多少資本被投入來終結這個短缺、這些資本多快會變成產能」。一個資金正在瘋狂湧入的短缺，不是一個可以放心買進的短缺——它是一個已經在被自己建置出保存期限的短缺，而且短缺敘事講得愈精彩、愈多人相信，資本回應的速度與規模通常也愈快愈大。

⚠War Story戰爭故事

Between 2023 and 2025, the buildout of AI computing infrastructure produced one of the cleanest real-time tests of this exact trap. Demand was not a story — it was visible in the numbers. A leading GPU maker's data-center revenue went from roughly $15 billion in fiscal 2023 to around $47 billion the following year, then to well over $100 billion the year after that. The hyperscalers buying that capacity — the handful of companies that run the largest cloud data centers — raised their combined annual capital-spending guidance repeatedly across those two years, from roughly $120-150 billion a year toward figures several times that. Chip foundries and packaging suppliers announced new advanced-packaging capacity on an accelerating schedule. Every proxy in this lesson — capex/depreciation, spiking returns on the hottest links in the chain, shortening lead times as producers rushed to add capacity faster — was flashing the same phase: shortage, with capital flooding in.

A retrospective look back at a large batch of industry-level calls made specifically inside that shortage-plus-inflow window found a striking pattern: across roughly two dozen such calls, only about seven in twenty-five turned out right in hindsight — a win rate of well under a third, in the single cell "verified shortage, Phase II capital inflow." The physical supply-and-demand read was not wrong on its own terms; what was missing was a second, orthogonal question that has nothing to do with tons or units or capacity: how much of the correct physical story was already reflected in the price before you acted on it. A shortage you can see is a shortage the market can see too, and the market prices in what it can see faster than the physical capacity itself arrives.

二〇二三年到二〇二五年間，AI 運算基礎設施的擴建，成為這個陷阱一次極乾淨的即時測試。需求不是故事——它清清楚楚寫在數字裡。一家主要 GPU 廠商的資料中心營收，從二〇二三財年約一百五十億美元，成長到隔一年約四百七十億美元，再到再隔一年超過一千億美元。買進這些運算力的雲端巨頭——那幾家經營最大規模雲端資料中心的公司——在這兩年裡一再調高合計年度資本支出指引，從每年約一千兩百億到一千五百億美元，一路上修到好幾倍於此的規模。晶圓代工廠與先進封裝供應商也以加速的節奏宣布新產能。這一課講的每一個代理指標——資本支出／折舊比、鏈上最熱環節的報酬飆升、生產者為了搶快擴產而不斷縮短的前置時間——全部指向同一個階段：短缺，加上資金瘋狂湧入。

事後回頭檢視一大批專門在這個「短缺＋資金湧入」窗口做出的產業層級判斷，發現一個很明顯的型態：在大約二十五個這類判斷裡，事後回顧只對了大約七個——命中率遠低於三分之一，就集中在「已驗證短缺、第二階段資本湧入」這一格。這不是說實體供需的判讀本身錯了；缺的是另一個和噸數、單位、產能完全無關的正交問題：這個正確的實體故事，在你根據它採取行動之前，已經有多少被反映進價格裡了。一個你看得見的短缺，市場也看得見——而市場把「看得見的東西」定價進去的速度，往往比實體產能真正到位還要快。

The operational takeaway is not "never buy into a shortage." It is: treat a verified shortage as the start of the analysis, not the end of it. Once you have confirmed the shortage, the next question is always the capital-cycle question — how fast and how large is the capital response — followed by the pricing question this framework revisits repeatedly in later lessons: how much of a correct call has the market already absorbed into the price. A shortage where capital response is still slow (long permitting delays, a genuinely scarce input, a technology only one or two suppliers can build) is a very different animal from a shortage where every producer in the industry is racing to add capacity within eighteen months.

操作上的結論不是「短缺絕對不能買」，而是：把一個已驗證的短缺，當成分析的起點，而不是終點。確認短缺之後，下一個問題永遠是資本週期的問題——資本回應的速度有多快、規模有多大——接著是這套框架後面會反覆回來談的定價問題：一個正確的判斷，市場已經把多少吸收進價格裡了。一個資本回應還很慢的短缺（許可延遲得很長、投入要素真的稀缺、只有一兩家供應商造得出來的技術），和一個產業裡幾乎每家生產者都在十八個月內搶著擴產的短缺，是完全不同的動物。

Why Supply Is Easier to Forecast Than Demand

為什麼供給比需求更容易預測

Demand forecasting depends on things nobody can observe directly — how many units a billion consumers will want two years from now, whether a new use case takes off, whether a competing product steals share. Supply, in a capital-intensive industry, depends on things that are, in large part, already written down somewhere: how many fabs are under construction and when they are scheduled to finish, how much a mining company has budgeted for a specific expansion and when permits were filed, how many ships are on order at which yards for delivery in which year. This is not a claim that supply forecasts are always right — projects slip, plans get cancelled — but the raw material for a supply forecast is closer to fact, while the raw material for a demand forecast is closer to guesswork multiplied by more guesswork.

需求預測仰賴一些沒有人能直接觀察到的東西——十億消費者兩年後到底想買多少台、一個新應用場景會不會真的起飛、有沒有競品把份額搶走。供給，在資本密集型產業裡，很大一部分仰賴的東西其實已經白紙黑字寫在某個地方：有幾座晶圓廠正在動工、預計什麼時候完工，礦業公司為某個特定擴建案編了多少預算、許可什麼時候送件，哪些船廠接了幾艘船的訂單、哪一年交船。這不是說供給預測永遠準——專案會延遲、計畫會取消——而是供給預測的原材料比較接近事實，需求預測的原材料則比較接近一層又一層疊起來的猜測。

The practical consequence is a rule of thumb worth internalizing: when a management team talks about demand, treat it as color, not evidence — they have every incentive to sound confident regardless of what they privately expect. When the same management team commits capital — breaks ground, signs an equipment order, files a permit — treat that as a real data point, because capital, once committed, is expensive to walk back. Watching what an industry's capital is doing, rather than what its executives are saying about demand, is the single most transferable habit this lesson can hand you.

這帶來一條值得內化的經驗法則：管理層在講需求的時候，把它當成語氣、氛圍，不是證據——無論他們私底下怎麼想，都有充分的誘因把話講得信心滿滿。同一個管理層真的投入資本的時候——動工、簽下設備訂單、送出許可申請——把它當成真正的數據點，因為資本一旦投入下去，要收回來的代價很高。盯著一個產業的資本正在做什麼，而不是它的高階主管正在講什麼需求故事，是這一課能交給你、最能跨產業套用的一個習慣。

⚑

Pitfall — treating order backlog as capital-cycle evidence. A long, growing order backlog feels like hard data, but it is a demand-side, not a capital-side, signal — and it can stay long and growing right up until the moment a wave of new capacity lands and the backlog starts clearing faster than new orders arrive. Backlog tells you demand has been strong recently; it tells you nothing about how much capacity is already being built to satisfy it. Read backlog alongside the three capital-cycle proxies, never as a substitute for them.

陷阱——把訂單能見度當成資本週期的證據。又長又持續增加的訂單能見度，感覺起來像是硬資料，但它是需求端訊號，不是資本端訊號——而且它可以一路又長又增加，直到新一波產能落地、訂單去化速度突然超過新單進來速度的那一刻為止。訂單能見度告訴你的是「最近需求一直很強」，卻完全沒告訴你「已經有多少產能正在被蓋出來滿足它」。訂單能見度要跟三個資本週期代理指標放在一起讀，絕對不能拿來取代它們。

Think first: why would a semiconductor fab and a piece of enterprise software follow completely different capital-cycle rhythms, even if both industries are currently growing fast?先想一下：為什麼一座半導體晶圓廠和一套企業軟體，即使兩個產業現在都在快速成長，資本週期的節奏卻會完全不同？

A fab costs tens of billions of dollars, takes two to three years to build, and locks in that capacity for a decade or more once it exists — so the lag between a return signal and the industry's response, and then between the response and the correction, is long and largely fixed. Software has close to none of that: a cloud provider can spin up more server capacity in weeks, a software company's biggest cost is often people, and adding staff or seats scales in months, not years. There is very little irreversible physical capital standing between "we see high returns" and "we have added capacity to compete for those returns," so the loop this lesson describes compresses almost to nothing — there is barely a gap left for a cycle to trace.

一座晶圓廠要花幾百億美元，得蓋兩到三年，一旦蓋好，這個產能就會被鎖定十年以上——所以從報酬訊號到產業回應、再從回應到修正之間的時間差，又長又相對固定。軟體幾乎完全沒有這回事：雲端業者可以在幾週內拉出更多伺服器容量，軟體公司最大的成本往往是人，加人力或加座位數是以月計算，不是以年計算。「看到高報酬」和「已經加了產能來競爭這個報酬」之間，幾乎沒有不可逆的實體資本擋在中間，所以這一課講的這個循環會被壓縮到幾乎不存在——幾乎沒有落差可以讓一個循環被畫出來。

The Time Scale Differs by Industry — and So Does Whether the Tool Even Applies

時間尺度因產業而異——這個工具適不適用，也因產業而異

The length of a full capital-cycle loop is set almost entirely by the physical lead time of the underlying capacity. Shipping — where a large vessel takes roughly two to three years to build and, once built, lasts twenty-five years or more, so an over-ordering wave takes a long time to work off — tends to run five- to ten-year full cycles, with the 2021 freight-rate spike and its subsequent collapse a recent example of the pattern compressed into an unusually sharp round trip. DRAM, where a fab takes two to three years to build but process upgrades and product mix shifts let producers adjust supply somewhat faster than shipping's fixed hull count, tends to run tighter, three- to four-year cycles, as the opening case in this lesson illustrated. Enterprise software, cloud infrastructure, and most other asset-light service businesses barely trace a cycle at all, for the reason worked through just above.

一個完整資本週期的長度，幾乎完全由底層產能的實體前置時間決定。航運——一艘大型船隻大約要花兩到三年造，造好之後可以用二十五年以上，所以一波超額訂船需要很長時間才能消化——傾向於跑五到十年的完整循環，二〇二一年運價飆升及其後的崩跌，就是這個型態被壓縮成一次特別劇烈的來回的近期例子。DRAM——晶圓廠要蓋兩到三年，但製程升級與產品組合調整讓生產者能比航運業固定的船隻數量更快地調整供給——傾向於跑得比較緊湊，三到四年一個循環，這一課開頭的案例就是示範。企業軟體、雲端基礎設施，以及多數其他資產輕的服務業，幾乎畫不出一個循環——原因就在前面剛講過的那個道理。

This is also where the tool's boundary sits, stated plainly: the capital cycle framework is a strong lens wherever returns are tied to physical, lead-time-heavy capacity — chips, ships, mines, refineries, commercial real estate, most heavy industry. It is a weak lens, close to irrelevant, wherever the business is asset-light and capacity can be added on a timescale of weeks or months — most software, most services, most platform businesses. Trying to force a capital-cycle phase call onto a software company by looking for "capex spikes" will mostly produce noise, because the real constraint on that industry's competitive response was never capital in the first place. Knowing when not to use a tool is as much a part of using it well as knowing how to use it.

這裡也是這個工具的邊界所在，講白了：資本週期框架在報酬與實體、前置時間長的產能綁在一起的地方——晶片、船運、礦業、煉油、商用不動產、多數重工業——是一把很強的尺。在資產輕、產能可以在幾週或幾個月內加上去的地方——多數軟體、多數服務業、多數平台型生意——是一把很弱、幾乎沒用的尺。硬要拿資本週期的階段判斷去套一家軟體公司，去找「資本支出暴增」，多半只會找到雜訊，因為那個產業競爭回應的真正限制，本來就從來不是資本。知道什麼時候不該用一個工具，跟知道怎麼用好它，同樣重要。

Takeaway一句話收斂

Longer physical lead times mean longer, more forecastable cycles; near-zero lead times mean the capital-cycle lens barely applies at all.實體前置時間愈長，循環愈長、也愈可預測；前置時間趨近於零，資本週期這把尺就幾乎派不上用場。

把其他滑桿都固定不動，只把前置時間從0.5年拉到5年：谷底利差會明顯惡化，復甦也要花上長得多的時間，即使資本追逐的是完全一樣的報酬、完全一樣的速度。就是這一個關係，解釋了為什麼一座晶圓廠、一座礦場或一艘船，帶著的資本週期風險，遠比一個能在一季內就加出產能的生意大得多——決定修正最終有多劇烈的，是前置時間，不是機會本身有多大。這是一個刻意做成方向性的簡化玩具模型，用來讓這層關係看得見，不是預測工具——真實世界的修正還牽涉到這個簡化模型沒有涵蓋的幾十個因素。

🛢️War Story戰爭故事

US shale oil, 2011-2016, is the classic pre-AI-era version of the same trap, and it adds a wrinkle: the correction can be accelerated deliberately by an outside player. Between 2011 and mid-2014, hydraulic fracturing turned marginal shale formations into some of the most attractive drilling economics in the world, and capital poured in — the US rig count climbed toward roughly 1,600 by October 2014, and shale producers raised and spent record amounts of debt and equity capital chasing wells that paid back in months, not years, when oil traded near $100 a barrel. That is Phase II in its purest form: a real resource discovery, real returns, and real capital racing to capture them.

The correction, when it came, was sharper than a typical capital cycle because a second actor made it sharper on purpose. Rather than cutting production to defend price — the traditional OPEC playbook — Saudi Arabia chose to keep pumping through the second half of 2014, explicitly to protect market share and push higher-cost shale producers out of the market. WTI crude fell from roughly $100 a barrel in mid-2014 to around $26 by early 2016, a decline of roughly 75%. US rig counts collapsed from near 1,600 to under 400 within about eighteen months, and dozens of shale producers filed for bankruptcy through 2015-2016. The lesson is not just "overcapacity corrects" — it is that a capital-cycle bust can be amplified by a rational incumbent choosing to make the correction happen faster and harder, rather than waiting passively for it to run its course.

二〇一一到二〇一六年的美國頁岩油，是同一個陷阱在 AI 時代之前最經典的版本，而且多了一個轉折：修正可以被一個外部玩家蓄意加速。二〇一一到二〇一四年年中之間，水力壓裂技術把邊際頁岩地層變成全世界最誘人的鑽井經濟效益之一，資本大舉湧入——美國鑽機數在二〇一四年十月一路衝到大約一千六百台，頁岩生產者募集並花掉創紀錄規模的債務與股權資本，追逐當原油交易在每桶接近一百美元時、幾個月而不是幾年就能回本的油井。這就是最純粹形態的第二階段：一個真實的資源發現、真實的報酬、以及真實的資本正在搶著把它拿到手。

修正真正來臨的時候，比一般資本週期更劇烈，因為有第二個玩家蓄意讓它變得更劇烈。沙烏地阿拉伯沒有走傳統歐佩克式的減產保價劇本，而是在二〇一四年下半年選擇維持產量，明白表示是要保護市場份額、把成本較高的頁岩生產者擠出市場。西德州原油從二〇一四年年中大約每桶一百美元，跌到二〇一六年初大約每桶二十六美元，跌幅大約75%。美國鑽機數在大約十八個月內，從接近一千六百台崩落到不足四百台，數十家頁岩生產者在二〇一五到二〇一六年間聲請破產。這個教訓不只是「產能過剩終究會修正」——而是資本週期的崩跌，可以被一個理性的既有業者刻意加速、加重，而不是被動等它自己走完全程。

Think first: if capacity cuts and bankruptcies in Phase IV are what seed the next cycle's recovery, why do so many investors still avoid an industry precisely when it is cutting capacity fastest?先想一下：如果第四階段的產能削減與破產潮，正是下一輪循環復甦的種子，為什麼那麼多投資人偏偏在產能削減最快的時候，反而避開這個產業？

Because Phase IV is, by construction, the point of maximum visible pain — falling prices, layoffs, bankruptcy headlines, and analysts cutting numbers — and pain is exactly what most people's intuition reads as "stay away." But the capacity cuts happening during that pain are the mechanism that eventually rebalances supply and demand; the industry cannot recover until enough marginal capacity has actually exited. The investors who do well from a trough are, almost by definition, the ones willing to look at capex/depreciation ratios and lead times while headlines are still bleak, rather than waiting for the headlines to improve first — by which point the trough is usually already behind them.

因為第四階段的本質，就是看得見的痛苦最大的時刻——價格下跌、裁員、破產頭條、分析師砍預估——而痛苦正是多數人的直覺會讀成「離遠一點」的東西。但正是這段痛苦期間發生的產能削減，才是最終讓供需重新平衡的機制；一個產業要等到足夠多的邊際產能真的退出，才可能復甦。能從谷底獲利的投資人，幾乎就是那些願意在頭條新聞還很黯淡的時候，去看資本支出／折舊比與前置時間的人，而不是等頭條新聞先變好——等到那時候，谷底通常早就已經走完了。

⚑

Pitfall — reading today's capacity utilization as tomorrow's price. Utilization is a coincident, sometimes even lagging, indicator: it tells you how tight the industry is right now, but it says nothing about capacity that has already been funded and is still under construction. An industry can show 95% utilization and record margins in the same quarter that a wave of new capacity — invisible in the utilization number because it has not opened yet — is scheduled to land within twelve months. If you are not separately tracking committed, funded capacity in the pipeline, high utilization today can be the exact moment right before the correction, not evidence that the good times will continue.

陷阱——用今天的產能利用率推斷明天的價格。產能利用率是一個同時性、有時候甚至是滯後性的指標：它告訴你這個產業現在有多緊繃，卻完全沒告訴你已經拿到資金、還在興建中的那些產能。一個產業可能在同一季展現出95%的利用率與創紀錄的毛利率，而一波新產能——因為還沒開出來，在利用率數字裡完全看不到——已經排定要在十二個月內落地。如果你沒有另外去追蹤那些已核准、已募資、正在興建中的產能，今天的高利用率，很可能正是修正到來前的那一刻，而不是好日子會繼續下去的證據。

⚑

Pitfall — reading a rising capex/depreciation ratio in isolation as proof of overcapacity forming. Some industries structurally run above 1x even at equilibrium, because heavy manufacturing routinely reinvests ahead of straight-line depreciation schedules, or because a generational technology upgrade (new process node, new equipment standard) forces a wave of replacement spending that looks like growth capex but is not adding net new capacity. Always compare the ratio to that specific industry's own multi-year historical range rather than to a universal number, and separate "spending to replace with better technology" from "spending to add units of capacity" wherever the disclosure allows it.

陷阱——單獨看資本支出／折舊比上升，就當成產能過剩正在形成的證據。有些產業即使在均衡狀態下，結構性地本來就會維持在1倍以上，因為重工業經常會超前直線折舊排程去做再投資，或者因為一次世代性的技術升級（新製程節點、新設備標準）逼出一整波看起來像擴產支出、實際上並沒有增加淨產能的替換性支出。永遠要把這個比值拿去跟這個特定產業自己的多年歷史區間比較，而不是套用一個放諸四海皆準的數字，並且只要揭露資訊允許，就把「花錢升級到更好的技術」跟「花錢增加產能單位數」分開來看。
