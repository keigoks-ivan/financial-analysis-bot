What You'll Learn這一課學什麼
  • Trace a single sale through the income statement, balance sheet, and cash flow statement, and explain why the three do not always move together.追蹤一筆銷售如何流過損益表、資產負債表、現金流量表，並解釋為什麼三張表有時候不會同步變動。
  • Tell gross profit, operating profit, net income, and free cash flow apart, and name one common way each of them gets dressed up.分辨毛利、營業利益、淨利、自由現金流的差別，並各自說出一種常見的美化手法。
  • Calculate ROIC as after-tax operating margin times invested capital turnover, and calculate incremental ROIC from two years of data.用稅後營業利益率乘以投入資本周轉率算出 ROIC，並用兩年資料算出增量 ROIC。
  • Spot the gap between per-share growth and total earnings growth, and explain how buybacks can create one without the other.看出每股數字成長和總額成長之間的落差，並解釋回購如何只製造前者。
  • Recognize when a stock price series needs adjusting for splits and dividends before any return can be trusted, and estimate a rough number instead of a false-precision one.判斷股價序列什麼時候需要還原分割與配息才能信任報酬率，並用「大概」的量級估算取代假精確的數字。

A Company That Almost Never Missed

一家幾乎從來不會「錯過」的公司

For a long stretch through the 1990s and into the early 2000s, General Electric reported quarterly earnings per share that matched or beat Wall Street's estimate almost every single time — often by exactly one cent. GE at that point spanned jet engines, medical imaging, television broadcasting, industrial equipment, and a large finance arm. Those businesses do not move in lockstep: engine orders, ad revenue, and loan losses do not all land on the same schedule quarter after quarter. A company that large hitting the same target that consistently, for that long, is not a coincidence you would expect from the underlying operations alone.

1990 年代到 2000 年代初，奇異公司（GE）連續多年，幾乎每一季公布的每股盈餘都精準命中或些微超過華爾街的預估值——而且經常剛好高出一美分。當時的 GE 橫跨飛機引擎、醫療影像、電視媒體、工業設備，還有一個規模龐大的金融子公司。這些事業的節奏完全不同步：引擎訂單、廣告營收、放款壞帳不會剛好每季都用同樣的幅度移動。一家這麼龐大、業務這麼分散的公司，連續多年都精準命中同一個目標，這不太可能只是業務本身自然對齊的結果。

This is a teaching case about how numbers get reported, not a verdict on GE as a business today. The mechanism worth learning from it: a large finance subsidiary carries reserves — money set aside for expected future losses — and management has real discretion over how much to set aside in any given quarter. Release a little more reserve than the loan book actually needs, and net income goes up this quarter with no change to the underlying business. Set aside a little more than needed, and you have a cushion to release later, in a quarter that needs help. None of this shows up as an obvious lie on the income statement. It shows up, if at all, as a mismatch between how smooth the reported profit line looks and how much the cash the business actually generates bounces around underneath it. That mismatch is exactly what this lesson teaches you to go looking for.

這是一個關於數字如何被呈現的教學案例，不是對 GE 這家公司現在的評價。值得學的機制是：一家大型金融子公司會提列準備金——為了未來可能發生的壞帳先預留一筆錢——而經營層對每一季要提列多少，其實有相當大的裁量空間。如果某一季實際需要的準備金比提列的少，多提列的部分被釋放回來，淨利當季就會上升，但底層業務其實什麼都沒變。反過來，某一季多提列一點，就等於先存了一筆緩衝，留到之後需要「撐一下」的季度再釋放出來。這整個過程不會在損益表上留下明顯的「說謊」痕跡。如果真的留下痕跡，通常是「帳面利潤看起來異常平滑」與「底層現金流量實際上起伏不小」之間的落差。這個落差，正是這一課要教你去找的東西。

Takeaway收斂

One number on one statement is an opinion shaped by real choices. Reading the three statements together, and reading cash against profit, is how you find out how much of that opinion is backed by something real.

單一報表上的單一數字，是一連串真實選擇形塑出來的意見。把三張表放在一起讀，把現金拿來對照利潤，才能看出這個意見有多少是真的有東西撐著。

One Sale, Three Statements

一筆銷售，流過三張表

The mechanism to internalize first: a company's three financial statements are not three separate report cards. They are three different camera angles on the same set of events, and a single sale moves through all three at once, usually at different speeds.

要先內化的機制是：一家公司的三張財務報表不是三張各自獨立的成績單。它們是同一組事件的三個不同攝影機角度，一筆銷售會同時流過這三張表，只是速度通常不一樣。

Say a manufacturer ships $1,000 of equipment to a customer on 60-day payment terms. On the income statementThe statement that records revenue when it is earned and costs when they are incurred, regardless of when cash actually changes hands — this is called accrual accounting., $1,000 of revenue is recognized immediately, along with the cost of the goods sold — say $600 — leaving $400 of gross profit. On the balance sheet, that $1,000 does not show up as cash; it shows up as accounts receivable, an asset representing money the company is owed but has not collected. At the same time, the $600 of inventory that walked out the door disappears from the inventory line. On the cash flow statement, nothing has happened yet — no cash moved. Sixty days later, when the customer pays, the receivable turns into cash: the balance sheet shifts $1,000 from receivables to cash, and the cash flow statement finally records that $1,000 as cash collected from operations.

假設一家製造商出貨 1,000 美元的設備給客戶，付款條件是 60 天後付款。在損益表依「應計基礎」記錄的報表：收入在賺得的那一刻就認列，成本在發生的那一刻就認列，不管現金什麼時候真的進出。上，1,000 美元的營收立刻被認列，連同銷貨成本（假設 600 美元）一起，留下 400 美元的毛利。在資產負債表上，這 1,000 美元不會變成現金，而是變成應收帳款——一項代表「公司該收但還沒收到」的資產；同時，離開倉庫的那 600 美元存貨，從存貨科目裡消失。在現金流量表上，此刻什麼都還沒發生——沒有任何現金移動。60 天後，客戶付款了，應收帳款才轉成現金：資產負債表上把 1,000 美元從應收帳款搬到現金，現金流量表這時候才終於把這 1,000 美元記成「營業活動收到的現金」。

This is why the same $1,000 sale can make a company look profitable on the income statement quarter after quarter while its cash keeps draining — if receivables keep growing faster than they get collected, or if the timing gap between shipping and collecting keeps stretching out. It is also why a company can report a net loss on the income statement (because of a large non-cash charge, more on that below) while its cash balance actually grows. Neither situation is automatically bad or good; the point is that no single statement tells you the whole story on its own, and the gap between them is itself information.

這就是為什麼同一筆 1,000 美元的銷售，可以讓公司在損益表上連續好幾季看起來很賺錢，現金卻不斷流失——如果應收帳款成長的速度一直快過收現的速度，或者出貨到收現之間的時間差一直在拉長。這也是為什麼一家公司可以在損益表上出現淨損（因為某項大額的非現金費用，下面會再談），現金餘額卻反而在成長。這兩種情況都不代表自動是壞事或好事，重點是：沒有任何一張報表能單獨講完整個故事，而三張表之間的落差本身就是資訊。

How to see it from public filings: look for the reconciliation at the top of the cash flow statement, which starts from net income and adds back non-cash items, then adjusts for the change in each working-capital line — receivables, inventory, payables. A company where "increase in accounts receivable" is a large and growing subtraction, quarter after quarter, faster than revenue itself is growing, is a company whose reported profit is running ahead of its collected cash. That single line is one of the highest-signal, most underused numbers on the entire filing.

如何從公開財報看出來：找現金流量表最上方那段「調節」——從淨利出發，加回非現金項目，再逐項調整營運資金科目（應收帳款、存貨、應付帳款）的變動。如果「應收帳款增加」這一行是一個很大、而且逐季擴大的扣減項，成長速度比營收本身還快，代表這家公司帳面上認列的利潤，跑得比實際收到的現金還快。這一行單獨看，是整份財報裡訊號密度最高、卻最少被讀者留意的數字之一。

Four Kinds of Profit, Four Ways to Dress Them Up

四種利潤，四種美化的方式

A single income statement produces at least four different profit numbers, and they answer different questions. Gross profit (revenue minus cost of goods sold) tells you how much pricing power and production efficiency the core product has. Operating profit — often called EBIT, earnings before interest and tax — subtracts the cost of running the business around that product: sales, marketing, R&D, administration. Net income subtracts interest, tax, and anything unusual that happened that year. Free cash flow is not on the income statement at all; it is cash generated by operations minus the capital spending needed to keep generating it.

同一張損益表至少會生出四種不同的利潤數字，各自回答不同的問題。毛利（營收減銷貨成本）告訴你核心產品有多少定價能力與生產效率。營業利益（常被稱為 EBIT，稅前息前利益）在毛利之上，再扣掉圍繞這個產品運作整間公司的成本：業務、行銷、研發、行政。淨利再扣掉利息、稅，還有當年度發生的各種一次性項目。自由現金流則完全不在損益表上；它是「營業活動產生的現金」減去「維持這個現金產生能力所需要的資本支出」。

Each one gets massaged differently. Gross margin can be flattered by reclassifying costs that arguably belong in cost of goods sold into operating expense instead — the gross margin line looks better, but the business has not actually gotten more efficient at making the product. Operating profit and net income are the two most exposed to one-time items: a gain from selling a building, a tax settlement, a restructuring credit — all can be tucked into a normal-looking line to make a soft quarter look fine. Net income specifically is the target of “adjusted” or “non-GAAP” presentations that add back stock-based compensation, amortization of acquired intangibles, and restructuring charges, on the argument that these are not real costs of running the business. Some of that argument is reasonable; some of it is a way to report a number well above what GAAP actually requires.

每一種利潤被動手腳的方式都不一樣。毛利率可以靠把原本該算進銷貨成本的費用，重新分類成營業費用來美化——毛利率這一行看起來變好了，但公司做這個產品其實並沒有變得更有效率。營業利益與淨利是最容易被一次性項目影響的兩個層次：出售一棟大樓的利得、一筆稅務和解、一筆重組費用的迴轉，都可以塞進看起來正常的科目裡，讓一個表現平庸的季度看起來還過得去。淨利尤其是「調整後」或「非公認會計原則（non-GAAP）」呈現方式最愛動手的地方——把股份基礎薪酬、併購產生的無形資產攤銷、重組費用通通加回去，理由是這些不算「經營本業的真實成本」。這個說法有部分合理，但也有一部分，只是一種把數字報得比公認會計準則實際要求還要高的手法。

Free cash flow is harder to dress up in the same way because it is built from cash, not accounting choices — but it has its own blind spot: it can look artificially strong or weak purely from timing. A company that slows down capital spending for one year, or stretches out payments to suppliers, or draws down inventory it built up the year before, will show a free cash flow spike that has nothing to do with the underlying business getting better. This is exactly why the earlier example about General Electric matters: smoothing shows up as an income statement that looks calmer than the cash underneath it, and the fix is always the same — check whether the calm profit line is confirmed by the messier cash line, not whether the profit line looks nice on its own.

自由現金流比較難用同樣的方式美化，因為它是從現金堆出來的，不是從會計選擇堆出來的——但它也有自己的盲點：純粹因為時間點的關係，可以看起來異常強或異常弱。一家公司如果某一年刻意放慢資本支出、拖延付款給供應商，或者把前一年囤積的存貨消化掉，都會讓自由現金流出現一次性的暴衝，跟底層業務有沒有變好完全無關。這正是前面 GE 案例重要的原因：帳面平滑，會表現成損益表看起來比底下的現金還要冷靜，而檢查的方法永遠一樣——去看這條看起來很平穩的利潤線，有沒有被那條比較顛簸的現金線印證，而不是只看利潤線本身漂不漂亮。

The Divergence Test: When Growth and Profit Stop Agreeing

背離測試：成長與利潤不再同步的訊號

One fast way to screen for margin trouble without building a full model: compare revenue growth year over year with operating profit growth year over year. If revenue grew 15% and operating profit grew only 5%, the gap — 10 percentage points — says costs are eating into the business faster than sales are expanding it. As a rough guide: a gap under 0% (operating profit growing faster than revenue) usually signals margin expansion; 0–3 points is a normal amount of noise; above 3 points is worth a closer look; above roughly 7 points is a real margin compression warning that deserves an explanation before you accept any story about "temporary" cost pressure.

要在不用建完整模型的情況下快速篩出毛利警訊，有一個很快的方法：拿營收年增率跟營業利益年增率相比。如果營收成長 15%，營業利益只成長 5%，這個 10 個百分點的落差，說明成本吃掉獲利的速度快過營收擴張的速度。粗略的參考值：落差小於 0（營業利益成長比營收還快）通常代表毛利正在擴張；0 到 3 個百分點屬於正常雜訊範圍；超過 3 個百分點值得多看一眼；超過大約 7 個百分點，就是真正的毛利壓縮警訊，任何「這只是暫時性成本壓力」的說法，都要先拿出解釋才能接受。

Where this breaks down: a company deliberately investing ahead of revenue — building out a new factory, hiring a sales team before the pipeline matures — will show the same divergence pattern for reasons that have nothing to do with losing pricing power. The number does not tell you which story is true; it tells you where to look. Read it alongside the gross margin line specifically: if gross margin is holding steady while operating margin falls, the pressure is coming from spending decisions, which can be a choice; if gross margin itself is falling, the pressure is coming from the product or from competition, which is harder to walk back.

這個方法失效的地方：一家刻意超前營收投資的公司——蓋新廠、業務團隊先養起來再等業績兌現——也會出現同樣的背離型態，但原因跟失去定價權完全無關。這個數字不會告訴你哪個故事才是真的；它只會告訴你該往哪裡看。要搭配毛利率一起讀：如果毛利率守住、營益率卻在下滑，壓力來自支出決策，這是一種選擇，可以收回來；如果連毛利率本身都在下滑，壓力來自產品或競爭本身，通常比較難逆轉。

⚠

Pitfall: treating EBITDA as cash. EBITDA (earnings before interest, tax, depreciation, and amortization) is an approximation of operating profit before non-cash accounting charges — it is not a cash flow measure at all. It ignores the capital spending needed to actually keep the equipment running and ignores the cash tied up or released by changes in working capital. A business with high EBITDA and heavy, unavoidable capital spending — an airline, a telecom network operator, a chip fabricator — can have thin or negative free cash flow even while EBITDA looks robust.

陷阱：把 EBITDA 當成現金。EBITDA（稅前息前折舊攤銷前利益）只是「扣掉非現金會計費用之前的營業利益」的近似值，它根本不是一個現金流量指標。它完全沒有考慮維持設備運轉所需要的資本支出，也沒有考慮營運資金變化占用或釋放的現金。一家 EBITDA 很高、但資本支出又重又躲不掉的公司——航空公司、電信網路營運商、晶圓代工廠——可以在 EBITDA 看起來很健壯的同時，自由現金流卻很薄甚至是負的。

⚠

Pitfall: forgetting stock-based compensation. Many companies add stock-based compensation (SBC) back to arrive at “adjusted” earnings, on the logic that it is a non-cash expense. It is non-cash, but it is not free — it dilutes existing shareholders by issuing new shares, which is a real economic cost that simply shows up as a slowly rising share count instead of a cash outflow. A company whose adjusted earnings look healthy but whose SBC runs at 8–10% of revenue or more is quietly transferring value from existing owners to employees every year, whether or not that shows up in net income.

陷阱：忘了股份基礎薪酬（SBC）。許多公司把股份基礎薪酬加回去算「調整後」盈餘，理由是它不是現金費用。它確實不是現金，但它不是免費的——它靠發行新股稀釋既有股東，這是一項真實的經濟成本，只是不會以現金流出的形式出現，而是以股數緩慢上升的形式出現。一家調整後盈餘看起來很健康、但 SBC 占營收 8% 到 10% 以上的公司，其實每年都在悄悄把價值從既有股東手上，轉移給員工，不管淨利表上有沒有反映出來。

Think first: why can net income grow while free cash flow falls?先想一下：為什麼淨利可以成長，自由現金流卻反而下滑？

A growing company needs more working capital to support that growth — more inventory to stock, more receivables outstanding as sales volume rises — and often needs more capital spending to add capacity ahead of the revenue actually landing. Both of those are uses of cash that sit below net income on the cash flow statement. If net income grows 15% but capital spending grows 40% and receivables grow with it, free cash flow can fall even as the income statement looks better every quarter. This is not automatically a red flag — it can be exactly what a business investing in a real growth opportunity looks like. The question worth asking next is whether the incremental capital being spent is earning a high return, which is the subject of the next section.

一家成長中的公司，要支撐這個成長需要更多營運資金——存貨要囤更多，隨銷量上升應收帳款餘額也跟著變大——通常還需要更多資本支出，先把產能建起來等營收落地。這兩者在現金流量表上都排在淨利之後、都是現金的用途。如果淨利成長 15%，但資本支出成長 40%、應收帳款也跟著長大，自由現金流可以一路下滑，即使損益表每一季看起來都在變好。這不自動是紅燈——它也可能正是一家公司投資在一個真實成長機會裡該有的樣子。接下來值得問的問題，是這筆新增資本能不能賺到高報酬，這正是下一節要談的。

ROIC: What a Dollar of Capital Earns

ROIC：一塊錢資本能賺多少

Every business needs capital tied up to operate — factories, inventory, money owed by customers. Return on invested capital (ROIC)After-tax operating profit divided by invested capital — how much profit the business generates per dollar tied up in it, before financing decisions. asks the single most important question this whole framework depends on: how much profit does the business generate per dollar tied up in it? Get the answer high and durable, and the business is worth compounding money into. Get it low, or worse, below the cost of that capital, and every dollar of growth is quietly destroying value even while revenue and even net income keep climbing.

每一門生意都需要綁住一些資本才能運轉——廠房、存貨、客戶還沒付的錢。投入資本報酬率（ROIC）稅後營業利益除以投入資本——公司每綁住一塊錢資本，能創造出多少利潤，還沒算進融資結構的影響。問的是這整套框架最核心的問題：這門生意每綁住一塊錢，能創造出多少利潤？答案又高又持久，這門生意就值得把錢一直投進去複利。答案很低，甚至低於資金成本，那即使營收、甚至淨利都在往上走，每一塊錢的成長其實都在悄悄摧毀價值。

Invested capitalEverything tied up to generate operating profit: net property, plant & equipment, plus inventory, plus receivables, plus other operating assets, minus non-interest-bearing operating liabilities such as accounts payable. is defined as: net property, plant & equipment, plus inventory, plus receivables, plus other operating assets, minus non-interest-bearing operating liabilities (mainly accounts payable and accrued expenses — money the company owes but is not paying interest on). That last subtraction matters: a company that gets its suppliers to finance its inventory — pay them 60 days after selling the goods those suppliers shipped — needs less of its own capital to run the same size business, and that shows up directly as a lower invested capital base and a higher ROIC, with nothing else changing.

投入資本為了創造營業利益而綁住的所有資源：淨廠房設備，加上存貨，加上應收帳款，加上其他營運資產，扣掉不計息的營運負債，主要是應付帳款與應計費用——公司欠著但不用付利息的錢。的定義是：淨廠房設備，加上存貨，加上應收帳款，加上其他營運資產，扣掉不計息的營運負債（主要是應付帳款與應計費用——公司欠著、但不用付利息的錢）。最後這個扣減項很重要：一家能讓供應商幫忙墊資的公司——先把貨賣掉，60 天後才付供應商錢——經營同樣規模的生意需要的自有資本就更少，這會直接反映成更低的投入資本、更高的 ROIC，其他什麼都沒變。

The formula worth memorizing splits ROIC into two levers: ROIC = after-tax operating margin × invested capital turnover. After-tax operating margin (NOPAT ÷ revenue) captures pricing power and cost discipline. Invested capital turnover (revenue ÷ invested capital) captures how much revenue the business squeezes out of each dollar tied up. A software company can post a modest 25% margin but turn its tiny invested capital base over five or six times a year, landing on a high ROIC through turnover. An airline can post a decent-looking margin in a good year but turn its enormous fleet of capital over barely once, landing on a ROIC that struggles to clear its cost of capital even in the good years. Same formula, completely different route to the same number — which is exactly why looking at margin alone, without turnover, misses half the picture.

值得記下來的公式，把 ROIC 拆成兩個槓桿：ROIC ＝ 稅後營業利益率 × 投入資本周轉率。稅後營業利益率（稅後營業利益 ÷ 營收）反映的是定價能力與成本紀律。投入資本周轉率（營收 ÷ 投入資本）反映的是公司從每一塊錢綁住的資本裡，能榨出多少營收。一家軟體公司可能只有 25% 左右的利益率，但一年把很小的投入資本基礎周轉五、六次，靠周轉率把 ROIC 拉高。一家航空公司在景氣好的年份，利益率看起來還可以，但龐大的機隊資本一年周轉不到一次，即使在景氣好的年份，ROIC 也很難清楚打敗資金成本。同一個公式，走完全不同的路徑到同一個數字——這正是為什麼只看利益率、不看周轉率，會漏看一半的畫面。

Where this tool does not apply cleanly: for banks, insurers, and other financial companies, "invested capital" and "revenue" as defined here do not mean the same thing they mean for an industrial or software business — a bank's core input is deposits and its core output is a spread, not a factory turning materials into product. Running this exact formula on a bank produces numbers that look meaningless or absurd. Financial companies need their own version built around return on tangible equity instead, which is a topic for a later lesson on matching the right yardstick to the right business.

這個工具不適用的地方：對銀行、保險公司等金融股來說，這裡定義的「投入資本」與「營收」，跟一般工業或軟體公司的意義並不一樣——銀行的核心投入是存款，核心產出是利差，不是一間把原料變成產品的工廠。把這個公式原封不動套在銀行上，算出來的數字會顯得沒有意義甚至荒謬。金融股需要用自己的一套版本，圍繞在有形股東權益報酬率上，這是後面一課討論「用對的尺量對的生意」時會再細談的主題。

Incremental ROIC: The Number That Actually Predicts Growth

增量 ROIC：真正能預測成長的數字

Average ROIC tells you how the whole existing capital base is doing. It does not tell you what happens to the next dollar the company reinvests, and the next dollar is what growth is made of. Incremental ROICThe change in after-tax operating profit divided by the change in invested capital between two periods — the return on the newest capital added, not the historical average. is calculated from the change between two periods: the increase in after-tax operating profit divided by the increase in invested capital. A company can have an average ROIC that looks merely fine while its incremental ROIC is excellent — meaning the newest investments are earning far more than the base did, and the average is simply being dragged down by older, lower-return assets still on the books. Multiply incremental ROIC by the reinvestment rate and you get a rough ceiling on how fast the business can grow itself without needing outside financing — the exact mechanic a later lesson on growth durability builds on directly.

平均 ROIC 告訴你整個既有資本基礎的表現如何。它不會告訴你公司再投入的下一塊錢會發生什麼事，而成長正是由這些「下一塊錢」堆出來的。增量 ROIC兩個期間之間，稅後營業利益的變動除以投入資本的變動——衡量的是最新增加的那筆資本的報酬率，不是歷史平均。是用兩期之間的變動算出來的：稅後營業利益的增加額，除以投入資本的增加額。一家公司的平均 ROIC 可能看起來只是普通，但增量 ROIC 卻很出色——代表最新投入的資本賺到的報酬，遠比舊有資本基礎好，平均值只是被帳上還留著的舊資產、低報酬拖累而已。把增量 ROIC 乘上再投資率，就能粗略估出這門生意不靠外部融資、單靠自己造血能長多快——這正是後面討論成長持續期那一課會直接沿用的機制。

Worked Example: Reading Two Years of Statements逐步範例：讀兩年的報表

The company below, Atlas Components, is an illustrative teaching case, not a real listed company — the numbers are built to be internally consistent and realistic in scale, not sourced from any actual filing.

下方的「亞特拉斯零組件」是虛構的教學案例公司，不是真實上市公司——數字是為了內部邏輯自洽、量級貼近現實而設計的，不是取自任何真實財報。

  1. 1

    Year 1: revenue $500M, operating income (EBIT) $80M → operating margin 16%. Apply a 25% tax rate: NOPAT = $80M × 0.75 = $60M.

    第一年：營收 5 億美元，營業利益（EBIT）8,000 萬美元 → 營業利益率 16%。套用 25% 稅率：稅後營業利益（NOPAT）= 8,000 萬 × 0.75 ＝ 6,000 萬美元。

  2. 2

    Year 1 invested capital: net PP&E $250M + inventory $70M + receivables $60M + other operating assets $20M − non-interest-bearing operating liabilities $80M = $320M.

    第一年投入資本：淨廠房設備 2.5 億 + 存貨 0.7 億 + 應收帳款 0.6 億 + 其他營運資產 0.2 億 − 不計息營運負債 0.8 億 ＝ 3.2 億美元。

  3. 3

    Year 1 ROIC = $60M ÷ $320M = 18.75%. Check via the decomposition: margin 12% ($60M NOPAT ÷ $500M revenue) × turnover 1.5625× ($500M ÷ $320M) = 18.75% — matches.

    第一年 ROIC ＝ 6,000 萬 ÷ 3.2 億 ＝ 18.75%。用分解式驗算：利益率 12%（6,000 萬 NOPAT ÷ 5 億營收）× 周轉率 1.5625 倍（5 億 ÷ 3.2 億）＝ 18.75%——吻合。

  4. 4

    Year 2: revenue $560M, EBIT $90M (16.07% margin — essentially flat vs. Year 1). NOPAT = $90M × 0.75 = $67.5M. Invested capital: net PP&E $266M + inventory $78M + receivables $68M + other $22M − liabilities $85M = $349M.

    第二年：營收 5.6 億，EBIT 9,000 萬（利益率 16.07%——跟第一年幾乎持平）。NOPAT ＝ 9,000 萬 × 0.75 ＝ 6,750 萬。投入資本：淨廠房設備 2.66 億 + 存貨 0.78 億 + 應收帳款 0.68 億 + 其他 0.22 億 − 負債 0.85 億 ＝ 3.49 億美元。

  5. 5

    Year 2 ROIC = $67.5M ÷ $349M = 19.34% — up slightly from 18.75%. On its own, this says "steady, maybe slightly improving capital efficiency." Nothing alarming, nothing exciting.

    第二年 ROIC ＝ 6,750 萬 ÷ 3.49 億 ＝ 19.34%——比 18.75% 略升。單看這個數字，只能說「穩定，資本效率或許略有改善」，沒什麼特別，也沒什麼好擔心。

  6. 6

    Incremental ROIC = ΔNOPAT ÷ Δinvested capital = ($67.5M − $60M) ÷ ($349M − $320M) = $7.5M ÷ $29M = 25.9%. This is meaningfully higher than either year's average ROIC — the newest capital Atlas put to work is earning noticeably better returns than its existing base, which the average-ROIC numbers alone completely hid.

    增量 ROIC ＝ ΔNOPAT ÷ Δ投入資本 ＝（6,750 萬 − 6,000 萬）÷（3.49 億 − 3.2 億）＝ 750 萬 ÷ 2,900 萬 ＝ 25.9%。這個數字明顯高於任一年的平均 ROIC——亞特拉斯最新投入的資本，賺到的報酬明顯優於既有資本基礎，而這件事光看平均 ROIC 完全看不出來。

  7. 7

    Now free cash flow. Year 1: net income $55M, D&A $30M, capex $40M, working capital increase $10M → CFO = $55M + $30M − $10M = $75M; FCF = $75M − $40M = $35M; FCF ÷ NI = 35/55 = 0.64. Year 2: net income $63M, D&A $34M, capex $50M, working capital increase $15M → CFO = $63M + $34M − $15M = $82M; FCF = $82M − $50M = $32M; FCF ÷ NI = 32/63 = 0.51.

    接著算自由現金流。第一年：淨利 5,500 萬，折舊攤銷 3,000 萬，資本支出 4,000 萬，營運資金增加 1,000 萬 → CFO ＝ 5,500 萬 + 3,000 萬 − 1,000 萬 ＝ 7,500 萬；FCF ＝ 7,500 萬 − 4,000 萬 ＝ 3,500 萬；FCF ÷ NI ＝ 35/55 ＝ 0.64。第二年：淨利 6,300 萬，折舊攤銷 3,400 萬，資本支出 5,000 萬，營運資金增加 1,500 萬 → CFO ＝ 6,300 萬 + 3,400 萬 − 1,500 萬 ＝ 8,200 萬；FCF ＝ 8,200 萬 − 5,000 萬 ＝ 3,200 萬；FCF ÷ NI ＝ 32/63 ＝ 0.51。

  8. 8

    Put it together and the naive reading and the correct reading diverge sharply. Naive reading: "FCF conversion fell from 0.64 to 0.51 — cash quality is deteriorating, be cautious." Correct reading: ROIC held steady to slightly up, capex stepped up to fund growth, and the capital funding that growth is earning an incremental return of roughly 26% — well above the company's existing base rate. The falling FCF/NI ratio here is not a warning sign; it is the visible cost of a business investing in unusually productive growth. The two readings would lead to opposite decisions, and only the incremental ROIC calculation tells you which one is right.

    把這些數字放在一起，直覺的讀法跟正確的讀法會分岔得很開。直覺讀法：「FCF 轉換率從 0.64 掉到 0.51——現金品質在惡化，要小心。」正確讀法：ROIC 持平略升，資本支出加碼是為了資助成長，而支撐這個成長的資本，賺到的增量報酬大約是 26%——明顯高於公司既有的基礎報酬率。這裡下滑的 FCF/NI 比率不是警訊；它是一門生意投資在異常高產出成長機會上，看得見的代價。這兩種讀法會導向完全相反的決定，只有算出增量 ROIC，才知道哪一種是對的。

Takeaway收斂

A high, durable ROIC is evidence of a good business — it is not, by itself, evidence of a good stock. Whether the price already pays for that quality is a separate question this lesson deliberately leaves open; it belongs to the valuation lessons much later in this framework.

又高又持久的 ROIC 是好生意的證據——它本身並不是好股票的證據。價格有沒有已經把這份品質算進去，是另一個問題，這一課刻意先留著不答；它屬於這套框架後面估值那幾課的範圍。

Per Share Is Not the Same as In Total

每股數字，不等於總額

Every number this lesson has covered so far — revenue, NOPAT, net income, free cash flow — is a total-company figure. The number printed in headlines, EPS, is that total divided by the share count. That denominator is not fixed. A company can grow EPS in exactly three ways: grow total profit, shrink the share count, or some mix of both — and only the first one requires the underlying business to actually get better.

這一課到目前為止談的每一個數字——營收、NOPAT、淨利、自由現金流——都是公司整體的數字。財經頭條印出來的那個數字，EPS（每股盈餘），是把這個總額除以股數。這個分母不是固定的。一家公司要讓 EPS 成長，其實只有三條路：讓總利潤成長、讓股數變少、或者兩者混合——而只有第一條路，真的需要底層生意變好。

BuybacksA company using its cash to repurchase and retire its own shares — this reduces the share count, which mechanically raises EPS even if total profit does not grow at all. reduce the share count directly. If a company earns a flat $500M in net income for two straight years but retires 15% of its shares along the way, EPS still rises by roughly 18% — a number that would look, on any headline scan, exactly like real earnings growth. This is not automatically deceptive: buying back stock at a price below what the business is actually worth is a genuinely good use of capital, and it is the mechanism that made per-share value compound for long-term holders of several well-known industrial and consumer companies over decades. It becomes a problem specifically when buybacks are used to paper over a stalled business, funded with debt, executed at prices well above intrinsic value, or timed to offset dilution from heavy stock-based compensation issued to employees rather than to actually shrink the float.

回購公司用現金買回並註銷自己的股票——這會讓股數變少，即使總利潤完全沒有成長，EPS 也會因此機械式地上升。直接讓股數變少。如果一家公司連續兩年淨利都持平在 5 億美元，但過程中買回並註銷了 15% 的股份，EPS 依然會上升大約 18%——任何一個掃頭條的人看到這個數字，都會以為這是真實的獲利成長。這不一定是騙人的：用低於企業真實價值的價格買回股票，是一種真正對股東有利的資本運用方式，也正是好幾家知名工業與消費品公司，讓長期股東的每股價值持續複利成長好幾十年的機制之一。它變成問題，具體是當回購被拿來掩蓋一門停滯的生意、靠舉債支撐、在遠高於內在價值的價格執行，或者只是拿來抵銷發給員工的大量股份薪酬造成的稀釋，而不是真的讓在外流通股數縮小的時候。

How to see it from public filings: compare EPS growth to total net income growth over the same period, and separately track the diluted weighted-average share count over three to five years. If EPS is consistently outrunning net income growth by several points a year, and the share count is falling steadily, the gap is buybacks, not operations — which is not a verdict, just a signal to go check what price those buybacks were executed at and whether debt funded them.

如何從公開財報看出來：把同一段期間的 EPS 成長率跟總淨利成長率放在一起比，並另外追蹤三到五年的稀釋加權平均股數。如果 EPS 每年持續跑贏淨利成長好幾個百分點，同時股數穩定下滑，這個落差來自回購，不是來自本業——這不是一個定論，只是一個訊號，提醒你去查那些回購是在什麼價位執行的、有沒有靠舉債撐著。

Think first: why can “EPS growth of 15%” fail to make the company more valuable?先想一下：為什麼「EPS 成長 15%」不能保證公司變得更值錢？

Company value is set by the total cash the business can generate for all its owners over time, discounted back to today — it does not care how many slices that total gets cut into. If EPS rises purely because the share count shrank while total profit and total cash generation stayed flat, the size of the pie has not changed; only the number of slices has. The per-share number the market watches went up, but nothing happened that a rational buyer of the entire company, rather than one share of it, would pay more for. Value only genuinely increases when the total pie itself grows, or when shares are retired at a price meaningfully below what the whole pie is worth — which shifts a larger share of the same-size pie to the remaining owners.

公司的價值是由這門生意未來能為所有股東創造的總現金，折現到今天所決定的——它不在乎這塊總額被切成幾片。如果 EPS 上升純粹是因為股數變少，而總利潤與總現金創造能力完全沒變，那塊餅的大小其實沒有改變，只是切法變了。市場盯著的每股數字上升了，但一個理性買下整間公司（而不是一股）的買家，並沒有任何理由願意多付錢。價值真正增加，只有在整塊餅本身變大的時候，或者股份是在明顯低於整塊餅真實價值的價格買回並註銷的時候——後者是把同樣大小的餅，讓更少的剩餘股東去分。

📜War Story戰爭故事

In August 2015, Valeant Pharmaceuticals — a pharmaceutical roll-up that grew mainly by acquiring other drugmakers and raising prices on their existing drugs — traded near $262 a share, one of the largest holdings in several well-known hedge funds. Valeant reported strong growth in "cash EPS," a company-defined adjusted measure that stripped out amortization of the intangible assets created by all those acquisitions, restructuring charges, and other items. Under that measure, the growth story looked intact. Under plain GAAP net income, and especially under free cash flow relative to the mountain of debt used to fund the acquisitions, the picture was far less comfortable — heavy debt service, price increases that drew regulatory and political scrutiny, and specialty pharmacy relationships that later drew fraud allegations. By early 2016 the stock had fallen more than 90% from its peak, and by 2017 it traded under $10 before the company was renamed Bausch Health years later. The GAAP-versus-adjusted gap had been visible in the filings the entire time; the adjusted number was simply the one most investors were reading.

2015 年 8 月，Valeant 製藥公司——一家主要靠併購其他藥廠、然後對既有藥品大幅漲價來成長的藥業集團——股價一度接近每股 262 美元，是好幾檔知名避險基金的重倉持股。Valeant 當時公布的是強勁成長的「現金 EPS」，一種公司自訂的調整後指標，把那一連串併購產生的無形資產攤銷、重組費用等項目全部剔除。用這個指標看，成長故事完好無缺。但用一般公認會計原則下的淨利，特別是拿自由現金流去對照支撐這些併購的龐大債務，畫面就沒那麼好看了——沉重的還債壓力、引來監管與政治關注的大幅漲價策略，以及後來被指控涉及詐欺的特約藥局合作關係。到 2016 年初，股價已經從高點跌掉超過 90%，2017 年一度跌破 10 美元，公司幾年後改名為 Bausch Health。GAAP 與調整後數字之間的落差，其實一直都攤在公開財報裡；只是多數投資人當時讀的，是那個調整後的數字。

📜War Story戰爭故事

Run the same reading in the opposite direction and it can lead to a mistake of the opposite kind. MongoDB, the database software company, reported GAAP net losses for years after its 2017 IPO — heavy R&D spending and substantial stock-based compensation kept accounting profit negative for a long stretch, which made its trailing P/E undefined and its EPS trend look, at a glance, like a company that was not making money. Read the cash side instead: revenue kept growing at a high rate, free cash flow margin turned positive and kept improving over time, and the combination — revenue growth rate plus FCF margin, a rough health check sometimes called the "Rule of 40" — stayed comfortably in healthy territory even in years when GAAP EPS was negative. A screen built purely around positive EPS would have thrown this company out for exactly the wrong reason. This is a teaching illustration of the pattern, not a current view on the stock — the specific numbers move every quarter and would need to be re-checked against the latest filing before they mean anything for a decision today.

把同一套讀法反過來用，也可能導向另一種相反方向的錯誤。資料庫軟體公司 MongoDB 在 2017 年上市後，連續好幾年 GAAP 淨利都是虧損——龐大的研發支出加上可觀的股份薪酬，讓帳面利潤長期為負，這讓它的過去 12 個月本益比失去意義，EPS 的走勢乍看之下就像一家不賺錢的公司。但如果改讀現金端：營收持續高速成長，自由現金流利潤率轉正並持續改善，兩者合起來——營收成長率加上 FCF 利潤率，一個有時被稱為「40 法則」的粗略健康檢查——在好幾個 GAAP EPS 依然為負的年份，都穩穩落在健康區間。一個純粹用「EPS 是否為正」當篩選條件的檢查表，會因為完全錯誤的理由把這家公司篩掉。這是一段用來說明型態的教學案例，不是對這檔股票現在的看法——實際數字每一季都在變動，要用在今天的決定之前，必須重新對照最新財報。

Adjusted Prices, and Thinking in Magnitudes

還原股價，以及用量級思考

One more number-reading habit belongs in this lesson because it quietly breaks more return calculations than almost anything else: whether the stock price series you are using has been adjusted for splits and dividends. On August 31, 2020, Apple executed a 4-for-1 stock split — a shareholder who owned one $500 share the day before owned four $125 shares the day after, with the total value unchanged. A raw, unadjusted price chart that does not account for that event shows the price falling roughly 75% overnight, which is nonsense; nothing about the business or the shareholder's wealth changed. A total-return or split-and-dividend-adjusted price seriesA price history recalculated so that stock splits and dividend payments do not create artificial jumps — the only kind of price series long-run return calculations should be built on. rescales the entire history before the split so the series stays continuous, and does the same for large special dividends. Any return calculated off an unadjusted series that spans a split date will be wrong, sometimes by a factor of several times, and the error is not random — it is a systematic distortion tied to the exact date of the corporate action.

這一課還要放進另一個讀數字的能力，因為它安靜地毀掉的報酬率計算，可能比其他任何一件事都多：你用的股價序列，有沒有還原分割與配息。2020 年 8 月 31 日，蘋果執行了 1 股分割為 4 股；一位分割前一天持有一股 500 美元股票的股東，分割後一天持有四股各 125 美元的股票，總價值完全沒變。一張沒有處理這個事件的原始未調整股價圖，會顯示股價一夜之間跌了大約 75%，這完全沒有意義；公司的business本身、股東的財富，什麼都沒變。還原股價（total-return / 拆股與配息調整後的股價序列）重新計算過的股價歷史，讓股票分割與配息不會製造出人為的跳空——長期報酬率的計算只應該建立在這種序列上。會把分割之前的整段歷史重新按比例縮放，讓序列維持連續，對大額特別股息也做同樣處理。任何一個橫跨分割日期、用未還原序列算出來的報酬率都是錯的，有時候錯到差好幾倍，而且這個誤差不是隨機的——它是綁定在那個公司行動確切日期上的系統性扭曲。

The last habit worth building here is thinking in magnitudes rather than false precision — sometimes called a Fermi estimate, after the physicist known for quickly estimating quantities from first principles. Financial numbers invite a false sense of exactness because they come printed to the cent, but a number derived from several uncertain inputs is only as reliable as its shakiest input. If a retailer discloses total revenue and store count but not per-store sales, dividing one by the other gives a rough per-store figure — and if that figure comes out at, say, $200M per store when comparable retailers in the same category run closer to $20M per store, the honest conclusion is not "this store must be extraordinary." It is "one of my assumptions is off by roughly a factor of ten, most likely the store count, and I need to find out which one before I trust anything downstream of it." Getting comfortable saying "roughly $30–35M" instead of a fabricated "$31.4M" is not sloppiness — it is honesty about how much the underlying data actually supports.

這裡值得養成的最後一個習慣，是用量級思考取代假精確——有時候被稱為費米估算，得名自那位擅長從第一原理快速估出量級的物理學家。財務數字很容易給人一種假的精確感，因為它們印出來都精確到小數點，但一個由好幾個不確定輸入推導出來的數字，可靠度只跟其中最不可靠的那個輸入一樣差。如果一家零售商只揭露總營收與門市數，沒有揭露單店營收，把兩者相除可以得到一個粗略的單店數字——如果算出來是每店 2 億美元，而同類別可比零售商大約是每店 2,000 萬美元，誠實的結論不是「這家店一定特別強」，而是「我某個假設大概錯了十倍左右，最可能是門市數這一項，在信任任何後續推導之前，得先找出是哪裡錯了」。習慣說「大概 3,000 萬到 3,500 萬美元」，而不是硬湊一個「3,140 萬美元」出來，不是偷懶，而是誠實面對底層資料實際能撐起多少精確度。

The lesson is that margin alone, or turnover alone, tells you almost nothing about capital efficiency; ROIC needs both.

把周轉率拉到接近 0.3 倍，同時把利益率維持在 12% 附近——這大致是重資產生意（航空公司、晶圓代工廠）的樣子：即使利益率相當體面，ROIC 依然很弱，因為卡住的是周轉率。接著把周轉率拉到 3 倍以上，同時把利益率維持在只有 3% 到 5%——這是低利益率、高周轉率的零售商或經銷商：即使利益率這條線本身看起來平淡無奇，ROIC 依然能撐出健康的兩位數。這裡的教訓是：單看利益率、或單看周轉率，幾乎看不出資本效率好不好，ROIC 兩者都要。

把資本支出拉高、淨利不動，看著比率往下掉——這正是上面亞特拉斯零組件範例的形狀。接著把淨利拉到接近零：輸出的比率應該維持在合理範圍（已經做了除以零的防呆），不會爆出一個讀不懂的數字，這件事本身也值得留意，因為一個真的拿這個比率做篩選的系統，同樣需要這層防呆。

⚠

Pitfall: computing a multi-year return off an unadjusted price series. If the stock underwent a split or a large special dividend anywhere inside the window you are measuring and you did not adjust for it, the number you get back is not approximately wrong — it can be wrong by a factor of two, four, or more, in whichever direction the corporate action distorts the series. Always confirm the data source labels its prices as split-and-dividend-adjusted before trusting any return calculated across more than a single quarter.

陷阱：用未還原的股價序列算多年期報酬率。如果你測量的區間裡，股票曾經分割過或發過大額特別股息，而你沒有還原，算出來的數字不是「大概錯了一點」，而是可能整整錯了兩倍、四倍甚至更多，方向取決於那次公司行動怎麼扭曲了序列。在信任任何跨越超過一季的報酬率計算之前，先確認資料來源有沒有標明股價已經還原分割與配息。

How This Connects

這一課接到哪裡

The first lesson in this framework (01 · The Framework: Five Questions) laid out the five questions a full analysis needs to answer, and argued that valuation belongs last. This lesson is the toolkit that makes every one of the other four questions answerable with numbers instead of impressions: reading whether a moat shows up in the figures runs on the same ROIC decomposition built here; judging how long high returns can last runs on the same incremental-ROIC calculation; deciding whether a company can survive a bad stretch runs on the same free-cash-flow read; and even the final valuation step depends on knowing which profit number is real before any of it gets discounted. None of that works without first getting comfortable with what a total-versus-per-share number is actually measuring, and with sizing an unfamiliar figure in rough magnitudes rather than false precision.

這套框架的第一課（01・框架總覽：五個問題）列出了一份完整分析需要回答的五個問題，並主張估值要放在最後一步。這一課，是讓另外四個問題能用數字、而不是印象去回答的工具箱：判斷護城河有沒有反映在數字上，用的是這裡建立的同一套 ROIC 分解；判斷高報酬能維持多久，用的是這裡的同一套增量 ROIC 算法；判斷一家公司撐不撐得過一段壞光景，用的是這裡的同一套自由現金流讀法；就連最後的估值步驟，也要先確定哪個利潤數字才是真的，才能拿去折現。這一切的前提，是先習慣總額與每股數字究竟在量什麼，並且習慣用大概的量級去估一個陌生數字，而不是硬湊出假精確。

The next lesson stays at the level of a whole industry rather than a single company's statements, and asks a different but related question: what is actually pulling demand, and how big is the runway. The same discipline learned here — trust a number only as far as the assumption underneath it, and prefer a defensible "roughly" over a fabricated decimal — is exactly what keeps that next step honest.

下一課把視角拉高到整個產業層級，而不是單一公司的報表，問的是一個不同但相關的問題：需求究竟被什麼拉動、跑道有多長。這一課學到的紀律——一個數字只能信任到它底層假設能撐住的程度，寧可用一個站得住腳的「大概」，也不要硬湊一個小數點——正是讓下一步不失真的關鍵。

Numbers only tell you how a business performed once you know how to read them — they say nothing yet about what pulls the industry it operates in forward or backward. That is where the next lesson starts: the demand engine underneath every revenue line.

數字只有在你懂得怎麼讀的時候，才會告訴你一門生意表現如何——它們還完全沒有回答，這門生意所在的產業，究竟被什麼拉動、又被什麼拖累。這正是下一課要開始的地方：藏在每一行營收數字底下的需求引擎。
