A Shortage Nobody Made Money From一場沒有人賺到錢的缺貨潮

In 2006 the memory-chip industry looked like a can't-miss trade. Every PC, and soon every phone, needed more DRAM, and demand was growing at a double-digit pace every year. And yet within two years, two of the world's largest memory makers were headed for bankruptcy, and DRAM contract prices had fallen more than 90% from their peak. Qimonda — the memory business spun out of Infineon, once one of the world's top-five DRAM makers — filed for insolvency in January 2009. Elpida, the Japanese maker backed by NEC and Hitachi, held on a few years longer before filing for bankruptcy in February 2012 and being bought out by Micron the following year. Strong demand. Collapsing prices. Bankrupt suppliers. If you had only asked "does the world need more of this," you would have gotten the direction completely wrong.

二〇〇六年，記憶體晶片產業看起來是穩賺不賠的題材。每一台個人電腦、很快連每一支手機都需要更多 DRAM，需求每年以兩位數的速度成長。但短短兩年內，全球兩家最大的記憶體廠都走向破產，DRAM 合約價從高點崩跌超過九成。奇夢達（Qimonda，英飛凌分拆出來的記憶體事業，曾是全球前五大 DRAM 廠之一）在二〇〇九年一月聲請破產保護；爾必達（Elpida，由日本 NEC 與日立支持的記憶體廠）多撐了幾年，終究在二〇一二年二月申請破產，隔年被美光收購。需求強、價格崩、廠商倒——如果你只問「這個世界需不需要更多這個東西」，方向會完全判斷錯。

What actually happened: through the mid-2000s boom, essentially every major DRAM maker had been adding capacity at the same time, each betting the others would blink first and slow down. None did. By the time all those new fabs came online together, supply grew faster than even that strong demand could absorb, and prices collapsed under the sheer weight of it. The lesson is not "avoid growing industries." It is that a good demand story only becomes a good investment once you also know how the supply side of the same industry is going to behave. That is this chapter: not whether people want to buy, but who can sell, at what cost, and how fast new sellers can show up.

實際發生的事是：二〇〇〇年代中期景氣好的時候，幾乎每一家主要 DRAM 廠都同時在擴產，各自賭的是「其他人會先退縮放慢腳步」。結果沒有人退縮。等到新蓋的晶圓廠一起量產，供給成長的速度連再強的需求都吸收不了，價格就在這股重量下崩潰。這裡的教訓不是「不要碰成長型產業」，而是一個好的需求故事，要搭配你知道這個產業的供給端會怎麼行動，才會變成一筆好投資。這正是這一課要講的：不是有沒有人想買，而是誰能賣、成本多少、新的賣家能多快冒出來。

Supply Elasticity: How Fast a "Yes" Turns Into a Shipped Unit供給彈性：一句「好，我來擴產」要多久才變成貨

Every industry has a gap between "it would now be profitable to make more of this" and "more of this is actually for sale." Call that gap the lead timeThe time between the decision to add capacity and that capacity actually shipping product - for a chip fab, often 2-3 years; for a cloud server rack, often weeks.從「決定要擴產」到「新產能真的能出貨」中間隔的時間——晶圓廠常要二到三年，雲端伺服器機櫃常常只要幾週。. A cloud provider can rack up more servers in weeks. A chip fab takes two to three years from breaking ground to volume output. A copper mine can take a decade from discovery to first shipment. This one number - how long it takes new supply to actually show up - is what determines whether a demand shock turns into a brief price blip or a multi-year pricing window. The industry-level version of this idea is called supply elasticityHow much an industry's total output can grow in response to a price signal within a given time window. Low elasticity plus long lead time means a demand surge shows up almost entirely as price, not volume, for years.在特定時間內，整個產業的總產出能對價格訊號反應多少。彈性低、前置時間長，代表需求爆發會有好幾年幾乎全部反映在價格上，而不是產量上。: how much total output an industry can grow in response to a price signal within a given window. When elasticity is low and lead time is long, a demand surge shows up almost entirely as price, not volume, for years, because nobody can physically make more of it, fast.

每一個產業，「現在多生產這個東西就有利可圖」和「這個東西真的多了、能賣了」之間都存在一段落差，這段落差就叫前置時間The time between the decision to add capacity and that capacity actually shipping product - for a chip fab, often 2-3 years; for a cloud server rack, often weeks.從「決定要擴產」到「新產能真的能出貨」中間隔的時間——晶圓廠常要二到三年，雲端伺服器機櫃常常只要幾週。。雲端業者幾週內就能多架幾台伺服器；晶圓廠從動土到量產常要兩到三年；一座銅礦從探勘到第一批出貨甚至可能要十年。這一個數字——新供給到底多快能真的出現——決定了一次需求衝擊會變成短暫的價格波動，還是撐好幾年的定價窗口。這個概念放到整個產業層級，叫做供給彈性How much an industry's total output can grow in response to a price signal within a given time window. This is also why "the world needs more of X" is not, by itself, an investable fact. Two industries can face the exact same demand surge and end up in completely different places three years later, purely because of how long it takes their respective supply sides to respond. A useful public-data proxy for lead time: read the capex disclosure and earnings-call commentary of the largest 3-4 players and look for how they themselves describe "time to first production" for a new project - it is almost always stated explicitly, because it is the number equity analysts ask about most.

這也是為什麼「這個世界需要更多 X」這句話本身，不能直接當成一個能拿來投資的事實。兩個產業可能面對一模一樣的需求爆發，三年後的結局卻完全不同，純粹是因為各自供給端反應的速度不一樣。一個好用的前置時間公開資料代理指標：讀前三、四大廠商的資本支出揭露與法說會發言，看他們自己怎麼形容新專案「從動工到第一批量產」要多久——這幾乎一定會被明講出來，因為這是分析師最常追問的數字。

Takeaway重點

Lead time, not the size of the demand number, is usually the single variable that decides whether a shortage is a footnote or a multi-year story.

前置時間，而不是需求數字本身有多大，通常才是決定一次缺貨只是個小插曲、還是會撐好幾年的那個關鍵變數。

Concentration: Reading CR3 and HHI Off Public Market-Share Data集中度：從公開市占率數字讀出 CR3 與 HHI

How many players actually control an industry's output is the second piece of the puzzle, because it determines whether the players who are there can behave in a coordinated, disciplined way when supply gets tight - or whether there are simply too many independent decision-makers for any restraint to hold. Two simple, calculable numbers do most of the work. CR3The combined market share of the three largest players in an industry. A quick, standard proxy for how concentrated an industry is.產業裡前三大廠商的合計市占率，是判斷一個產業集中度最常用、最快的代理指標。 is just the sum of the top three players' shares - simple, and a reasonable first cut. HHIHerfindahl-Hirschman Index: the sum of every player's market share squared. Rewards true dominance more than CR3 does, because squaring punishes a lopsided distribution (one 60% player) more than an even one (three 20% players).赫芬達爾指數：把每一家廠商的市占率平方後加總。因為是平方，會比 CR3 更重視「誰特別大」——一家獨大 60% 跟三家各 20%，CR3 可能一樣，HHI 差很多。 - the sum of every player's squared market share - is a slightly richer version: because it squares each share before adding, it rewards true dominance more than an even split. A common rule of thumb, borrowed from U.S. antitrust merger guidelines and used here only as an investing heuristic, treats HHI above roughly 2,500 as highly concentrated, 1,500-2,500 as moderately concentrated, and below 1,500 as competitive.

一個產業實際上由幾家公司控制，是拼圖的第二片，因為這決定了在場的玩家有沒有辦法在供給吃緊時做出協調、有紀律的行為，還是根本有太多各自為政的決策者，讓任何克制都撐不住。兩個算得出來的簡單數字，就能做大部分的判斷工作。CR3The combined market share of the three largest players in an industry. A quick, standard proxy for how concentrated an industry is.產業裡前三大廠商的合計市占率，是判斷一個產業集中度最常用、最快的代理指標。就是前三大廠商市占率直接加總，簡單，也是不錯的第一刀判斷。HHIHerfindahl-Hirschman Index: the sum of every player's market share squared. Where this data actually comes from in practice: company investor decks and 10-K/annual-report segment disclosures, third-party trackers (semiconductor-industry associations, shipping-industry data providers, commodity consultancies), or a simple sum-of-public-comps-over-total-market-estimate when no single source has the whole picture. None of this requires anything proprietary - it is the same public arithmetic any analyst can run.

實務上這些數字從哪裡來：公司給投資人的簡報、10-K 或年報裡的分部揭露、第三方追蹤機構（半導體產業協會、航運業資料供應商、大宗商品顧問公司），或者當沒有單一來源看得到全貌時，把所有上市可比公司的營收加總、除以估計的總市場規模。這些都不需要任何內線資訊，是任何分析者都能做的公開算術。

Think first: why might CR3 = 90% NOT guarantee pricing power, even though it looks like a textbook oligopoly?先想一下：為什麼 CR3 等於 90%，未必保證有定價權，儘管看起來就是教科書等級的寡占？

Concentration is a structural condition, not a behavior. Three players controlling 90% of an industry can just as easily use that scale to fight a price war as to keep discipline - DRAM's own 2007-2009 chicken game happened inside a fairly concentrated industry at the time. The number to check is whether margins moved together with concentration, or whether players kept adding capacity anyway, each betting the others would blink first. High CR3 tells you consolidation happened; it does not by itself tell you what the survivors chose to do with it.

集中度是結構條件，不是行為本身。三家廠商掌控九成市場，一樣可以用這個規模去打價格戰，也可以拿來維持紀律——DRAM 二〇〇七到二〇〇九年的那場價格戰，發生時產業集中度其實已經不低。真正要查的是：利潤率有沒有跟著集中度一起走高，還是玩家還在拚命擴產、各自賭別人會先退縮。CR3 高只告訴你整併已經發生，不會告訴你倖存下來的那幾家決定拿這個結構做什麼。

The fragmented structure has a nastier variant worth naming on its own: fragmented plus high exit barriersCosts or obligations that make it hard for a losing competitor to actually leave an industry - long-term debt, union contracts, leases, sunk regulatory value - so capacity stays in the market long after it stops earning its cost of capital.讓一個虧錢的競爭者難以真正退出產業的成本或義務——長期債務、工會合約、租約、已投入的監理資源——導致產能早就賺不到資金成本了，卻還留在市場上。. An industry can have terrible economics for decades not because demand is weak - air travel demand has grown almost every decade since commercial aviation began - but because too many independent operators compete for the same seats, and none of them can afford to actually leave. Evidence of Discipline, and the False Signals That Fake It供給紀律的證據，以及會騙你的假訊號

You cannot see collusion, and in most jurisdictions coordinated price-fixing is illegal anyway - so real supply discipline, in a legal and investable sense, has to show up as independently rational behavior that merely happens to look coordinated from the outside. Four places to look for it in public data. First, synchronized capacity cuts: when multiple independent players announce production or capex cuts within the same few quarters, as Samsung and SK Hynix both did in 2023, that is evidence every major player independently reached the same conclusion about oversupply - a stronger signal than any single company's guidance alone. Second, the trend in capex divided by depreciation: a ratio consistently below 1 means the industry is not even replacing the capital base it already has, let alone expanding it; a ratio well above 1 and still rising means capital is still flooding in. Third, where each major producer sits relative to the marginal producerThe highest-cost supplier still operating. 你看不到勾結，而且在多數司法管轄區，協調定價本來就違法——所以真正的供給紀律，在合法且能拿來投資的意義上，必須表現成「各自獨立的理性行為，剛好從外部看起來像是協調一致」。有四個地方能在公開資料裡找到這種證據。第一，同步減產：當好幾家各自獨立的廠商在同幾個季度內宣布減產或砍資本支出，就像三星與 SK 海力士在二〇二三年做的那樣，代表每一家主要廠商都各自獨立得出了同樣的產能過剩結論——這比任何單一公司自己的財測，訊號都更強。第二，資本支出除以折舊的比率走勢：這個比率持續低於 1，代表這個產業連自己既有的資本存量都沒在更新，更別提擴張；比率遠高於 1 而且還在上升，代表資本還在持續湧入。第三，每一家主要廠商相對於邊際生產者The highest-cost supplier still operating. The price level at which they lose money and would rather stop producing than keep selling is roughly where a price war has to bottom.還在生產、但成本最高的那個供應商。他們開始虧錢、寧可停產也不想再賣的價格，大概就是一場價格戰會跌到的底部。的位置：來自設備商、產業協會或公司自己揭露單位成本的公開成本曲線估計，能大致告訴你一場價格戰要跌到哪個價位，才會逼最弱的生產者出局。第四，產能利用率Output actually produced divided by maximum output an industry's installed capacity could produce. High and rising utilization is necessary but not sufficient evidence of a real shortage - see the pitfall below.實際產出除以現有裝置產能能生產的最大值。利用率高且上升，是判斷真短缺的必要條件，但不是充分條件——見下方的陷阱。搭配通路或客戶端庫存水位：利用率單獨看可能會誤導人（見下方陷阱），但利用率維持高檔、同時客戶端庫存也在同步去化，會是比利用率單獨一個數字好得多的真短缺訊號。

The false signal to watch for is the opposite of discipline dressed up as discipline: a chicken game. In 2006-2007, DRAM makers were making almost identical public statements about supply "rationality" while every single one of them kept raising capex - nobody wanted to be the one who blinked first and ceded share, so the public restraint language and the private capacity decisions pointed in opposite directions. The check is simple: read what companies say, but verify it against what they are actually ordering and building. Capex guidance, not commentary, is the number that matters.

要留意的假訊號，是紀律的相反面偽裝成紀律：一場價格戰。二〇〇六到二〇〇七年，DRAM 廠商公開發言時幾乎口徑一致地在談供給「理性化」，同一時間每一家都還在拉高資本支出——沒有人想成為那個先退縮讓出份額的人，於是公開的克制發言，和私下的擴產決策，方向完全相反。查證的方法很簡單：聽公司怎麼說，但要拿他們實際下單和動工的數字去對照。真正有意義的是資本支出財測，不是發言內容。

⚠

When you see utilization running near 95-100%, the instinct is "this will keep supporting high prices." But check what is happening on the capex side in the same window - if capacity announcements or capex guidance are also spiking, you are usually looking at the shortage's peak, not a stable plateau. High utilization plus a wave of new project announcements is a leading indicator of the reversal, not of more of the same.

當你看到利用率逼近 95% 到 100%，直覺會覺得「這會繼續撐住高價」。但要同時看同一段時間資本支出端在發生什麼事——如果擴產宣布或資本支出財測也同步暴增，你看到的通常是短缺的高峰，不是一個穩定的高原期。利用率高再加上一波新專案宣布，是反轉的領先訊號，不是「還會繼續一樣」的訊號。

⚠

When you see CR3 above 70-80%, the instinct is "oligopoly, pricing power, safe to assume discipline." But if margins across the top players are falling at the same time concentration is high, that combination usually means the top few are fighting over share, not cooperating on restraint - concentration describes how many seats are at the table, not what the people sitting there are actually doing.

當你看到 CR3 超過 70% 到 80%，直覺會覺得「寡占、有定價權、可以放心假設有紀律」。但如果前幾大廠商的利潤率在集中度高的同時反而在下滑，這個組合通常代表龍頭們正在搶份額，不是在合作克制——集中度描述的是桌上有幾張椅子，不是坐在椅子上的人實際在做什麼。

This whole toolkit assumes physical or logistical capacity is the thing constraining supply, and it has real limits. For a software or platform business, the marginal unit of supply is close to free to produce - adding another customer does not require years of fab construction - so CR3, lead time, and utilization barely explain that business's economics at all; the moat chapters later in this course, on structural advantages like switching costs and network effects, do far more of the work there. Similarly, in industries where entry is blocked by regulation or licensing rather than by cost or lead time - banking charters, broadcast spectrum, casino licenses - the relevant constraint is a legal one, not a supply-elasticity one, and the analysis has to start from the regulatory framework instead.

這整套工具都假設限制供給的是實體或物流上的產能，而它確實有適用邊界。對一家軟體或平台型公司來說，多生產一單位供給的成本接近零——多接一個客戶不需要花好幾年蓋工廠——所以 CR3、前置時間、利用率這套工具，幾乎完全解釋不了這種公司的經濟體質；本課程後面談護城河的幾課，講轉換成本、網路效應這類結構性優勢的地方，才會真正做到大部分的判斷工作。同樣地，在一些進入門檻是靠法規或執照而不是成本或前置時間擋住的產業裡——銀行執照、廣播頻譜、賭場執照——真正的限制是法律上的，不是供給彈性上的，分析要從監理架構開始，而不是這一課的工具。

From the Gap to Either Profit or Glut缺口如何變成利潤，或變成過剩

Put Chapter 03's demand growth number and this chapter's supply elasticity together, and you get the actual outcome that matters to an investor: does a demand tailwind turn into years of excess profit, or does it get absorbed so fast that nobody captures anything durable? The mechanical answer: when demand growth outruns an industry's maximum supply growth, bounded by lead time, for long enough, the gap has to show up somewhere - and because you cannot store the world's actual output of most goods for free, it shows up as price and margin, for roughly as long as the lead time takes to close. When supply can respond within months, the same demand growth shows up mostly as more units sold at roughly the same price, and margins stay unremarkable no matter how exciting the growth number looks.

把第 03 課的需求成長數字，和這一課的供給彈性放在一起看，才會得出對投資人真正有意義的結果：一波需求順風，最後會變成好幾年的超額利潤，還是被吸收得太快，沒有人留住任何持久的東西？機制上的答案是：當需求成長的速度，超過受前置時間限制的產業最大供給成長速度，而且撐得夠久，這個缺口就一定要在某個地方表現出來——而因為多數商品的實際產出沒辦法免費囤積，它會表現在價格與利潤率上，大概會撐到前置時間補上缺口為止。當供給能在幾個月內反應，同樣的需求成長大部分會變成「多賣一點、價格差不多」，利潤率再怎麼看都不會特別亮眼，不管成長數字本身有多驚人。

Here is the part that catches most people off guard: the more visible and widely reported a shortage becomes, the closer it usually is to its own expiration date - not because the shortage was fake, but because visibility is exactly what triggers the capacity response that ends it. The DRAM supercycle and the container-shipping spike you will read about below are the same mechanism, twice: a real, headline-worthy shortage was the direct trigger for a wave of capacity that, arriving years later, ended the shortage - and in the shipping case, pushed the industry into the opposite problem. A shortage is not proof that prices will keep rising. It is frequently the birth announcement of the oversupply that follows it; how much lead time separates the two is the only real question.

這裡是最容易讓多數人措手不及的部分：一個缺貨故事被講得越大聲、傳得越廣，通常離它自己的到期日就越近——不是因為這個缺貨是假的，而是因為「被廣泛看見」正是觸發終結它的擴產反應的那件事。下面會讀到的 DRAM 超級週期和貨櫃航運運價暴衝，其實是同一個機制講兩次：一個真實、上得了頭條的缺貨，直接觸發了一波新產能，而這波產能幾年後到位，結束了缺貨——在航運的案例裡，甚至把整個產業推向了相反的問題，也就是過剩。缺貨不是價格會繼續漲的證明。它經常正是接下來那場產能過剩的出生公告；兩者之間只隔了多長的前置時間，才是真正該問的問題。

Think first: why would a shortage everyone is excited about right now often be the worst moment, from the industry's own long-run economics, to be the one building new capacity - and yet also the moment the most new capacity actually gets ordered?先想一下：為什麼一個現在人人都興奮的缺貨，從產業長期經濟體質的角度看，往往是最不該去擴產的時刻——但偏偏也是最多新產能真的被下單的時刻？

Because every individual producer is reasoning from their own vantage point, not the industry's: from inside one company, an elevated price looks like a durable repricing worth chasing, not a temporary imbalance that a dozen competitors are about to chase at the very same time. Nobody privately believes they are the one adding the capacity that tips the whole industry into glut. That is exactly the mechanism the capital cycle chapter formalizes - watch the pattern repeat in both of this chapter's war stories below.

因為每一個生產者都是從自己的角度在推理，不是從整個產業的角度：從一家公司內部看出去，一個墊高的價格看起來像是值得追的持久重新定價，不像是一個十幾個對手同時間都在追的暫時性失衡。沒有人私底下會覺得，自己就是那個把整個產業推向過剩的人。這正是資本週期那一課要正式講的機制——下面這一課的兩個戰爭故事，會讓你看到同一個模式重演兩次。

And even when you get all of this right - real shortage, long lead time, genuine discipline - one more question remains before it becomes an investable idea rather than just a correct industry read: has the market already priced it? A shortage that is common knowledge, with the obvious beneficiary stocks already up several-fold, may be a completely correct read that is simply no longer actionable. A correct forecast and a cheap stock are not the same claim; a later chapter in this course, on price and expectations, covers how to check the difference.

而就算你把上面全部判斷對了——真短缺、前置時間長、真的有紀律——在它變成一個能拿來投資的想法之前，還有最後一個問題：市場是不是早就定價進去了？一個已經眾所皆知的缺貨，明顯受惠的股票也已經漲了好幾倍，可能是一個完全正確的判斷，卻早就沒有操作空間了。「判斷正確」和「股票便宜」是兩件不一樣的事；本課程後面談價格與市場預期的一課，會講怎麼分辨這兩者的差別。

⚠

When an order backlog looks enormous relative to what a supplier can ship this year, the instinct is "multi-year visibility, safe to underwrite." But in long-lead-time industries, customers sometimes place duplicate orders with more than one supplier just to guarantee a place in line, a practice sometimes called double-booking, inflating the reported backlog well beyond what will actually be delivered and paid for. Check shipped and delivered volume against the backlog number over a few quarters before trusting it as a demand signal.

當一份訂單積壓（backlog）看起來遠遠超過供應商今年能出的貨量，直覺會覺得「有好幾年的能見度，可以放心買單」。但在前置時間長的產業裡，客戶有時候會同時跟一家以上的供應商下重複訂單，只是為了搶到排隊的位置，這種做法有時候叫做重複下單，會讓帳面上的訂單積壓遠高於實際會出貨、會被付款的量。要拿幾個季度的實際出貨量去對照訂單積壓數字，確認過了再把它當成需求訊號來信。

Takeaway重點

Strong demand plus elastic supply usually means a growth story without much of a profit story. Strong demand plus inelastic, disciplined supply is where the actual money tends to be made - and where the risk that it is already priced in is highest.

強勁需求加上有彈性的供給，通常只是一個成長故事，沒有太多利潤故事。強勁需求加上缺乏彈性、有紀律的供給，才是真正容易賺到錢的地方——同時也是「早就被定價進去」風險最高的地方。

  4. 4

    Classify: an HHI near 3,000 sits well above the ~2,500 highly-concentrated line, consistent with what a 91% CR3 already implied - this is a textbook oligopoly by structure.

    判斷分類：HHI 接近 3,000，明顯高於「高度集中」約 2,500 的門檻，跟 CR3 高達 91% 講的是同一件事——就結構而言，這是教科書等級的寡占。

  5. 5

    Now add a demand assumption: AI-accelerator demand for high-bandwidth memory is growing roughly 20%/yr, and new fab capacity for this category takes roughly 3 years to reach volume. Cumulative demand growth over that window: (1 + 20%)³ ≈ 1.73×, i.e. about +73% over 3 years.

    接著加入一個需求假設：AI 加速器對高頻寬記憶體的需求，大約每年成長 20%，而這一類的新晶圓產能大約需要 3 年才能量產。這段期間累積的需求成長：(1 + 20%)³ ≈ 1.73 倍，也就是 3 年大約成長 73%。

  6. 6

    Current utilization is already running above 90%. Demand growing +73% over 3 years against roughly flat near-term capacity means utilization would need to exceed 100% well before any new fab announced today actually ships - the arithmetic itself, not a forecast, says the constraint has to bind.

    目前利用率已經超過 90%。需求 3 年內成長 73%，對上近期幾乎持平的產能，代表利用率會在今天宣布的任何新廠真正出貨之前，就必須超過 100%——這是算術本身的結論，不是預測，代表這個限制一定會被撞上。

  7. 7

    Conclusion: high concentration + long lead time + a real demand surge is a supply story structurally capable of holding pricing power for roughly the length of the lead time (about 3 years), unless demand growth undershoots this assumption. Whether that is still a good stock, per the previous section, is a separate question about what price is already paying for it.

    結論：高集中度加上長前置時間、再加上真實的需求爆發，這個供給結構理論上能撐住定價權大約一段前置時間那麼久（約 3 年），除非需求成長不如這個假設。至於這是不是一檔好股票，如同上一節講的，是另一個關於現在的價格已經付了多少的問題。

📉War Story戰爭故事

DRAM, 2013-2024: what discipline actually looks like once you only have three players left. After Qimonda and Elpida exited, the DRAM industry consolidated to three players - Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron - controlling roughly 90%+ of global output ever since. What changed was not that memory chips stopped being cyclical. It is that the cycles became survivable. From about 2016 to mid-2018, DRAM contract prices roughly tripled, driven by strong smartphone and cloud-server demand meeting capacity that only three disciplined players controlled - none of them rushed to flood the market the way six or seven competitors had in 2006. All three posted some of the highest operating margins in the semiconductor industry during that stretch.

DRAM，二〇一三至二〇二四年：只剩三個玩家之後，「紀律」長什麼樣子。奇夢達與爾必達出場之後，DRAM 產業整合成三家：三星、SK 海力士、美光，此後長年合計掌控全球出貨量九成以上。改變的不是記憶體不再有循環，而是這個循環變得撐得過去了。約二〇一六年到二〇一八年中，DRAM 合約價大約翻了三倍，強勁的智慧型手機與雲端伺服器需求，對上只有三家有紀律的玩家控制的產能——沒有人像二〇〇六年那六、七家玩家一樣搶著把市場灌爆。三家在這段期間都繳出半導體業數一數二的營業利益率。

Then, as new capacity from that boom came online and smartphone unit growth flattened, contract prices fell more than 40% from late 2018 into 2019 - supply still eventually caught up, just over a longer and less destructive cycle than 2007-2009. The pattern repeated again in miniature in 2020-2021 (pandemic PC and server demand), and then in a much sharper down-leg in 2022-2023, when DRAM prices fell by roughly half and both Samsung and SK Hynix posted memory-segment losses for multiple quarters. What is notable is what happened next: in 2023, both Korean makers publicly announced coordinated production and capex cuts - the kind of synchronized restraint that six independent, undercapitalized players in 2007 were structurally unable to pull off. By 2024, prices recovered sharply, helped by an entirely new source of demand: AI accelerators' need for high-bandwidth memory. The mechanism did not change - long lead times, high capital intensity, real cyclicality. But with three players instead of eight, the amplitude of the crash and the credibility of the recovery both changed. Concentration did not eliminate the cycle. It made the cycle survivable, and made the recoveries stronger.

接著，那波景氣帶來的新產能陸續上線，加上手機出貨成長趨緩，合約價從二〇一八年底到二〇一九年跌了超過四成——供給還是追上了，只是這次循環拉得比較長，破壞力也比二〇〇七到二〇〇九年小。同樣的模式在二〇二〇到二〇二一年（疫情帶動個人電腦與伺服器需求）小規模重演一次，接著在二〇二二到二〇二三年出現更劇烈的下跌，DRAM 價格幾乎腰斬，三星與 SK 海力士都連續多季在記憶體部門出現虧損。值得注意的是接下來發生的事：二〇二三年，兩家韓廠公開宣布同步減產與砍資本支出——這種同步克制，是二〇〇七年那六家各自為政、資本又不夠雄厚的玩家在結構上做不到的事。到了二〇二四年，價格在一個全新需求來源（AI 加速器需要的高頻寬記憶體）帶動下強勁回升。機制本身沒有變——前置時間長、資本密集、確實有循環——但玩家從八家變成三家，崩跌的幅度與復甦的可信度都不一樣了。集中度沒有消滅循環，而是讓這個循環撐得過去，也讓復甦更有力。

🚢War Story戰爭故事

Container shipping, 2020-2023: when a "shortage" is actually a bottleneck, and the fix overshoots. Trans-Pacific container shipping rates spent most of 2019 around $1,500-2,000 per 40-foot container. By late 2021 the same route was pricing above $15,000-20,000 - a ten-fold-plus spike that looked, on the surface, exactly like a capacity shortage. It mostly was not. Global container-ship capacity was growing at its normal single-digit annual pace throughout 2020-2021; what actually broke was the flow, not the stock. Ships were queuing for weeks outside ports like Los Angeles and Long Beach because trucking, warehousing, and labor everywhere in the chain were overwhelmed at once. That distinction matters enormously for how long a shortage should be expected to last: a true capacity shortfall in a 2-3-year-lead-time industry takes years to resolve, because you have to build the ships. A logistics bottleneck resolves whenever the bottleneck clears, which can happen much faster - and did, through 2022-2023.

貨櫃航運，二〇二〇至二〇二三年：當「缺貨」其實是瓶頸，而解方反而擴產過頭。二〇一九年多數時間，泛太平洋航線一個四十呎貨櫃的運價大約落在一千五百到兩千美元。到了二〇二一年底，同一條航線的報價衝上一萬五千到兩萬美元以上，漲幅超過十倍，表面上看起來完全就是產能短缺。但大部分其實不是。二〇二〇到二〇二一年，全球貨櫃船總運力仍然以正常的個位數年成長率在增加；真正出問題的是「流量」不是「存量」——船隻在洛杉磯、長灘這類港口外排隊等了好幾週，因為整條鏈上的卡車、倉儲、人力同一時間全部塞爆。這個區別對「短缺應該撐多久」極為關鍵：一個前置時間二到三年的產業如果真的產能不足，要花好幾年才能解決，因為你得真的把船蓋出來；而物流瓶頸只要瓶頸解開就能解決，速度可以快得多——二〇二二到二〇二三年也的確如此。

But container-ship owners, watching the same headline rates everyone else was watching, placed a record wave of newbuild orders in 2021 - at the time, reportedly equal to somewhere around a quarter of the entire existing global fleet. Those ships mostly were not needed for the actual problem (port congestion), but they got ordered anyway, because from the deck of any individual shipping line, $18,000 a container looks like a permanent repricing, not a temporary jam. When that orderbook started delivering from 2023 onward, spot rates on many routes fell back toward, and at times below, 2019 levels. The shortage that triggered the order wave was mostly a bottleneck. The overcapacity that followed the order wave was real - and it landed on a fragmented industry with dozens of independent owners, none coordinating with each other, which is exactly the structure from the table above where a glut, once triggered, has nowhere disciplined to go.

但貨櫃船東們看著跟所有人一樣的頭條運價，在二〇二一年下了一波破紀錄的新船訂單——當時報導稱這批訂單量約占全球現有船隊的四分之一左右。這些船大部分根本解決不了真正的問題（港口壅塞），卻還是被下單了，因為對任何一家單獨的航運公司來說，一個貨櫃一萬八千美元看起來像是永久重新定價，不像暫時塞車。等這批訂單從二〇二三年起陸續交船，許多航線的即期運價就跌回、有時候甚至跌破二〇一九年的水準。觸發這波下單潮的短缺，大部分其實只是瓶頸；而跟在這波下單潮後面的產能過剩，才是真的——而且降臨在一個由幾十家各自獨立、彼此互不協調的船東組成的碎片化產業上，正好就是上面表格裡那種「過剩一旦被觸發，就沒有紀律可以收拾」的結構。

How This Connects這一課接到哪裡

The supply verdict you just learned to build - concentrated or fragmented, disciplined or fighting, short lead time or long - is the second half of an equation whose first half was Chapter 03's demand multiple. Put the two together and you get where the value chain actually ends up: Chapter 05 takes this industry-level supply/demand read and asks which single link in the chain, not which whole industry, actually captures the profit. And the mechanism you saw twice in this chapter's war stories - "a shortage attracts new capital, which eventually creates the next glut" - has a name and a fuller toolkit of its own: that is Chapter 06, the capital cycle.

你剛剛學會判斷的供給結論——集中還是碎片化、有紀律還是在打價格戰、前置時間短還是長——是一個方程式的後半段，前半段是第 03 課教的需求倍數。把兩邊放在一起，才看得出整條鏈的錢最後真正流向哪裡：第 05 課會拿這一課的產業供需判斷，去問鏈上哪一個環節——不是哪一整個產業——真正把利潤收進口袋。而這一課戰爭故事裡出現兩次的機制，「短缺吸引新資本進場，最後催生下一輪過剩」，有自己的名字與更完整的工具箱，那就是第 06 課的資本週期。
